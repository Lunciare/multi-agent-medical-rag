Resistant Hypercalcemia
KEYWORDS: hypercalcemia, calcium, levels, serum, calcium levels, serum calcium, elevated, parathyroid, hyperparathyroidism, renal, cases, vitamin, include, pth, bone

Introduction Hypercalcemia is a serum calcium (Ca) level over two standard deviations above the average mean values. That equates to a total serum calcium level greater than 10.5 mg/dl or an ionized calcium level greater than 5.3 mg/dl. Normal serum calcium levels often range from 9.0 to 10.5 mg/dl. However, values may vary across laboratories. Only about 45% of serum calcium is in the free or ionized state; almost half of the serum concentration is bound to albumin, and about 10% is partially bound to small ions, including phosphate and citrate. Any state of dehydration or the process of hemoconcentration that occurs during venipuncture can falsely elevate total serum albumin levels and falsely account for elevated total serum calcium levels, whereas ionized calcium may remain normal.
