Preoperative Preparation No randomized studies exist to guide medical optimization approaches before surgery. However, it is well-recognized that initiating medical therapy with α- and β-blockade in the preoperative period can significantly reduce the risk of uncontrolled hypertension, hypertensive crises, tachycardia, and volume expansion during the perioperative period. A typical regimen involves initiating phenoxybenzamine 7 to 14 days before surgery, starting at a dosage of 10 mg orally twice daily and carefully titrating up to a maximum of 1 mg/kg/d. Doxazosin, an α-1 selective agent, is an acceptable alternative due to its favorable adverse effect profile. At least 3 to 4 days after starting α-blockade, β-blockade with propranolol, atenolol, or metoprolol should be initiated to manage tachycardia. These agents should not be started without prior α-blockade, as doing so may precipitate a hypertensive crisis due to unopposed α-receptor stimulation. Calcium channel blockers, such as amlodipine and nifedipine, can serve as alternative or add-on agents to effectively control hypertension in the preoperative period.
