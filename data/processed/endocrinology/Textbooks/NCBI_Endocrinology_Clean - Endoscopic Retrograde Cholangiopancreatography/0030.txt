Sometimes luminal perforations can be closed primarily with endoscopically placed sutures or clips. However, if the lumen is too narrow for the duodenoscope to pass, there will likely be too little room for a primary closure. In those cases, a fully covered esophageal stent can seal the rent if it can be positioned and secured above the major duodenal papilla. If that is not possible due to the type of perforation, if the patient becomes clinically unstable, if the perforation is too large, or if endoscopic expertise is not available to perform a primary closure, surgery may be necessary. In fact, surgery is necessary to repair most esophageal perforations and open gastric or duodenal perforations. When surgery is delayed because of failure of nonoperative therapy, there is greater morbidity and mortality, and hospitalizations are prolonged.
