In these situations, using a side-viewing duodenoscope may be problematic, and a forward-viewing endoscope facilitates afferent loop intubation and biliary cannulation. Where available, some endoscopists use a double-balloon enteroscope to advance a forward-viewing endoscope through the afferent loop by alternately inflating and deflating the balloons. The duodenoscope features an elevator at its distal tip, which can be raised or lowered to direct the cannula, sphincterotome, balloon, or other accessory into the major or minor papilla, thereby accessing the desired duct and performing the necessary procedures (see Image. Distal Tip of Duodenoscope). The areas around and beneath the elevator act as a reservoir for bacteria that cannot always be adequately reprocessed with current technology.
