Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Foramen of Winslow (Omental Foramen, Epiploic Foramen)
KEYWORDS: foramen, winslow, lesser, foramen winslow, hepatic, ligament, omentum, stomach, lesser omentum, hepatoduodenal, duodenum, greater, artery, posterior, hepatoduodenal ligament

Introduction The foramen of Winslow constitutes the only natural communication between the greater and lesser sacs of the peritoneal cavity. The caudate lobe of the liver marks the superior boundary, and the 1st part of the duodenum forms the inferior border. The hepatoduodenal ligament, the free edge of the lesser omentum containing the hepatic portal vein, hepatic artery, and common bile duct, forms the anterior boundary. The inferior vena cava constitutes the posterior border. Also referred to as the “epiploic foramen” or “omental foramen,” this structure was first described by Jacob Winslow in his 1732 publication, " Exposition anatomique de la structure du corps humain " (see Image . Foramen of Winslow). Jacob Winslow, born in Denmark and later naturalized as a French citizen, converted to Catholicism. Winslow is regarded alongside William Cheselden in London and Alexander Monro in Edinburgh as a leading anatomist of the 18th century. His publication is notable for reliance solely on direct anatomical observation rather than the opinions of contemporaneous anatomists.
