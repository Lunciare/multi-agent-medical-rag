SOURCE: Reference 13, copyright © 2003 American Diabetes Association, reprinted with permission from The American Diabetes Association The presence of diabetes complications is associated with elevated depression symptoms in adults with type 2 diabetes (39) and type 1 diabetes (40). Severity of complications may also increase risk for depression symptoms over time. For example, severity of diabetic peripheral neuropathy, along with physical symptoms and functional impairments secondary to neuropathy, are associated with increased symptoms of depression, both cross-sectionally (41) and over 18 months (42) in adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes. NHIS data showed that the 12-month prevalence of MDD, diagnosed by structured clinical interview, was considerably higher among those with major complications of diabetes compared to those who did not have complications (Table 33.6) (13). Perceived worsening of health status over the prior year was also an independent predictor of risk for MDD (Table 33.6) (13).
