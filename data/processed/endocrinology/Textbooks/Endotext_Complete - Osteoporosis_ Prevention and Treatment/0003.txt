The Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation (BHOF, previously the National Osteoporosis Foundation) currently recommends DXA for women ≥65 years and men ≥70 years, or earlier if clinical risk factors are present. Physicians should routinely perform height measurements preferably with a stadiometer as there is an association between height loss and spinal fractures. The BHOF Clinical Guide recommends vertebral imaging for spinal fractures in the presence of height loss of 1.5 inches or more and longitudinal height loss of 0.8 inches or more for postmenopausal women and men age 50-69. Vertebral imaging is also recommended in women and men age 70 and 80 years and older, respectively ( 7 ). When the diagnosis of a low bone density compared with age-adjusted controls or osteoporosis is made, a work-up to look for secondary causes of osteoporosis should be considered. See Table 1 .
