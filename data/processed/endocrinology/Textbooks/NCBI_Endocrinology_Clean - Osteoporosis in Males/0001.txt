Diagnostic tools include bone mineral density testing and laboratory evaluations to identify secondary causes. Screening is recommended for men aged 70 and older or younger men with risk factors, although screening guidelines vary internationally. Management involves lifestyle modifications and pharmacological treatments. Regular monitoring is essential to evaluate treatment efficacy and progression. Despite advancements in awareness and treatment options, osteoporosis in men remains underdiagnosed and inadequately managed. This activity for healthcare professionals is designed to enhance the learner's competence in recognizing risk factors for osteoporosis in males, performing the recommended osteoporosis screening and diagnostic evaluation, and implementing an effective interprofessional management approach to improve patient outcomes. Introduction Osteoporosis is a disease of weakened bones caused by low mineral density and distortion of the architecture, predisposing them to low-impact, fragility fractures. This condition is often referred to as the "silent disease," as frequently no manifestations are clinically apparent until a fracture has occurred. Osteoporotic fractures are associated with decreased quality of life, increased morbidity, disabilities, and mortality. Osteoporosis translates to "porous bones" derived from the Greek root word poro, which means porous or passage, and osis , meaning condition.
