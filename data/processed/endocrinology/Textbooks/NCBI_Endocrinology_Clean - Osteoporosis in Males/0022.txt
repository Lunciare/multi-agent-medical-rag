However, there is also some controversial evidence that calcium supplementation can increase cardiovascular disease (CVD) frequency in females and males. The NIH-AARP Diet and Health Study assessed calcium intake at baseline in 388,229 men and women aged 50 to 71. Patients were followed for 12 years, and cardiovascular events were identified. The authors found that men on calcium supplementation had a 20% higher risk of CVD death, which was not true for women. Therefore, the NOF recommends a diet with adequate amounts of total calcium (at least 1000 mg/day for men 50–70 years old and 1200 mg/day for men 71 years and older) and adding calcium supplements only if dietary intake is insufficient. Vitamin D intake (800–1000 IU/day), including supplements if necessary, is also advised for individuals age 50 and older to achieve a vitamin D level of 30 ng/ml or greater. Pharmacologic Management The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved several antiresorptive medications for treating osteoporosis in males. Bisphosphonates Bisphosphonates are synthetic analogs of inorganic pyrophosphate deposited in the bone matrix.
