The HALT trial furthermore demonstrated a significant reduction in the incidence of new vertebral fractures in men with nonmetastatic prostate cancer receiving androgen deprivation therapy. In males, it is FDA-approved for the treatment of osteoporosis at high risk for fractures with primary osteoporosis and for those with prostate cancer on androgen deprivation therapy who are at high risk of fractures. Furthermore, denosumab is FDA-approved for men and women at risk for glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis. It may cause hypocalcemia, which has to be corrected before starting this medication. Denosumab can increase the risk of serious skin infections (cellulitis) and skin rash. Like bisphosphonates, it has also been rarely associated with the development of ONJ, although it is more common when used in patients with cancer at higher doses. Denosumab has also rarely been associated with the development of atypical femur fractures.
