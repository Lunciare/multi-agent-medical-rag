One meta-analysis specifically assessing smoking and fracture risk in men demonstrated a relative risk of 1.37 (95% CI: 1.22-1.53). Prior studies have shown a 32% increased risk of spine fractures and a 40% increased risk of hip fractures in male smokers. The underlying pathophysiology is not entirely understood, but it is believed to be a combination of nicotine and cadmium effects upon bone tissue, reduction of calcium absorption, vitamin D deficiency, and interference with tissue repair processes. Diet : Balanced diet with adequate protein and dairy intake. Physical activity : Regular physical activity, at least 30 to 40 minutes of weight-bearing exercises 3 to 4 times per week, is recommended. Numerous studies demonstrated the reduction of osteoporotic fractures in men with physical activity. Examples include: The PASSWORD study: Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity was associated with a smaller decline (4 mg/cm2) in BMD of the femoral neck.
