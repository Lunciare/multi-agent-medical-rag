However, these types of procedures are not without with complications, such as damage to neurovascular structures and infections. Importantly, if the splitting of the gluteus medius muscle is greater than a few centimeters (typically 5cm) superior to the tip of the greater trochanter, then the risk of injury to the superior gluteal nerve and superior gluteal vessels is increased. The incidence of injury to the superior gluteal nerve is dependent mainly on its course and branching pattern. During approaches to the hip joint, it is essential to define the nerve’s distance from the greater trochanter and the safe area for the superior gluteal nerve. The safe area for the superior gluteal nerve in hip surgeries is defined as 4cm from the tip of the greater trochanter for the anterior third, and 5cm for the posterior and middle third of the gluteus medius.
