Numbers were too small to estimate hip fracture rates by race/ethnicity for all groups. The prevalence of any hip, spine, or wrist fracture, the most common fractures among older adults, was also higher among those with diabetes (14.6%, p<0.05) and prediabetes (14.9%, p<0.05) compared to those with normal glucose levels (12.0%) (Figure 32.1, Table 32.2). The prevalence of any fracture was not higher in those with diabetes (39.3%) or prediabetes (40.4%) compared with normal glucose levels (40.7%), consid­ ering all participants together (Table 32.3). When results were considered separately by sex, women had a higher prevalence of any fracture with diabetes (39.2%) compared with normal glucose levels (35.6%), but this difference was not statistically significant.
