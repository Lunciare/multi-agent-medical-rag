A meta-analysis of the association between diabetes and hip fracture incidence, based on 12 studies from Europe, the United States, and Australia, reported a combined relative risk of hip fracture of 1.7 (95% CI 1.3–2.2) comparing those with and without type 2 diabetes (23). Type 2 diabetes was also associated with a modest increase in the risk of all non-spine fractures (RR 1.2, 95% CI 1.01–1.5), based on a meta- analysis of eight studies (23). Bone Mineral Density BMD is usually measured in the clinic using DXA, an x-ray imaging tool, and is the basis for identifying increased frac­ ture risk in older adults. Femoral neck BMD T-score is an index that compares measured BMD to the average BMD in healthy 20–29-year-old non-Hispanic white women.
