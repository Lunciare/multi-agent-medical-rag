Indications for surgery include the development of optic neuropathy, excessive proptosis resulting in corneal complications, aesthetic indications, relief of pain, and severe inflammation or progressive eye disease that does not respond to other measures. Orbital decompression surgery usually involves either removal of the roof, lateral wall, or the medial wall and the floor (transantral approach). In addition, other surgical techniques have been described to correct various aspects of Graves orbitopathy. Some of these include removal of retroocular adipose tissue, which can assist cosmetically in patients with moderate Graves orbitopathy with proptosis. Other surgical modalities include surgery to correct the lid retraction and restrictive myopathy. Regarding external beam radiation, its role is generally limited to those patients who do not respond to other treatment modalities. Side effects such as chronically dry eyes and a theoretical risk of tumor induction restrict its use. Its proposed mechanism of action is on modulating the immune system.
