Infants with imperforate hymen may not demonstrate a perineal bulge on physical exam. The imperforate hymen may be subtle, even if ultrasound demonstrates bilateral hydronephrosis with a bladder distended to the umbilicus. Therefore, the history and physical examination should be performed diligently with suspicion of this disorder. The common redundancy of the newborn hymenal tissue explains why hymenal abnormalities may be missed on the newborn exam. During the interview of the mature adolescent, clarifying the menstrual and coital history is important since patients may report regular menses with a microperforate hymen. Cyclic monthly pain is often reported due to menstrual blood that expands the vaginal canal and uterus with resultant hematometra. Expansion can cause numerous pelvic symptoms, including pelvic and back pain, constipation, radiated pain secondary to sacral plexus irritation, and urinary retention. Up to 3 liters of retained blood have been reported in cases in the literature.
