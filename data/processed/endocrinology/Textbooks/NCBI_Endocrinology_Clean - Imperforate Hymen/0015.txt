Rarely the imperforate hymen has been associated with neonatal death due to respiratory failure from abdominal distension. Even with postoperative infection following hymenectomy, hymenal patency remains satisfactory. The theoretical risk of dyspareunia is not well-elucidated in the literature. Adolescents treated with Foley catheter insertion to preserve the hymenal structure did not complain of severe dyspareunia at first coitus on follow-up. At an average follow-up period of 8.5 years, women who underwent hymenectomy reported dysmenorrhea without complaints of sexual dysfunction. Successful live birth was reported among those that attempted pregnancy. In another study, 86% of women experienced no dyspareunia and conceived after hymenectomy. Dysmenorrhea is likely due to pelvic endometriosis from retrograde menstruation in women with imperforate hymen. A long-term follow-up of 40 patients with either imperforate hymen or transverse vaginal septa found that patients with imperforate hymen had significantly better rates of successful term pregnancies. Vaginal adenocarcinoma is rare and was reported in a patient with vaginal adenosis and a prior history of imperforate hymen.
