As the influence of maternal estrogen subsides, the hymenal opening expands and may adopt a crescentic appearance. Hymenal tags may spontaneously resolve and may also appear where there were no previous tags. A study of 468 newborns showed a significant difference in hymenal characteristics by race but not gestational age or weight. At birth, the most common normal hymenal types are the annular and fimbriated forms. Other hymenal variations that are considered abnormal include microperforated, septate, and cribriform hymens. Microperforate hymen is thought to result when epithelial cells in the center of the developing hymen do not normally degenerate. As the hymen is not a Müllerian structure, there is not a high likelihood of urinary tract anomalies associated with imperforate hymen. Exceptions to this speculation are reported in the literature in case reports. However, these instances are likely due to random embryologic events. While many imperforate hymen cases are considered isolated incidences, there are reports of familial occurrence of the imperforate hymen in autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, and polygenic inheritance patterns.
