Established pituitary provocation tests do not show a consistent strong OT increase to test for a suspected deficiency ( 79 ). New stimulation tests such as Kisspeptin or Melatonin have shown a limited increase ( 79a , 79b ). A single MDMA 100 mg dose resulted in an eightfold increase in plasma OXT in healthy adults but had no effect in patients with AVP-Deficiency ( 79c ). This differentiation makes MDMA a strong candidate for OXT-deficiency testing. However, its potential side effects (e.g., cardiovascular) and regulatory restrictions limit its widespread use. Lower doses may be explored for a safer alternative. OT-R expression is widespread in the CNS of many species and OT has widespread roles as a neurotransmitter, including neural networks that mediate a range of complex behaviors. In some cases, these overlap those involving AVP ( 32 , 33 ). CLINICAL PROBLEMS SECONDARY TO DEFECTS IN THE HYPOTHALAMO-POSTERIOR PITUITARY AXIS Defects in the production or action of AVP manifest as clinical problems in maintaining plasma sodium concentration and fluid balance, reflecting the key role of the hormone in these processes. A further group of related clinical conditions reflect primary defects in thirst. In some cases, the two may coincide, reflecting the close anatomical and functional relationship of both processes. AVP Deficiency CLASSIFICATION AVP deficiency (AVP-D) is characterized by production of dilute urine in excess of >40-50 ml/kg/24 hours in adults. AVP-D arises through one of four mechanisms ( Figure 7 and Table 2 ). Deficiency of AVP (formerly known as central diabetes insipidus). Inappropriate, excessive water drinking: primary polydipsia. Renal resistance to the antidiuretic action of AVP: AVP resistance (formerly known as nephrogenic Diabetes insipidus). Increased vasopressinase expression in pregnancy. Figure 7.
