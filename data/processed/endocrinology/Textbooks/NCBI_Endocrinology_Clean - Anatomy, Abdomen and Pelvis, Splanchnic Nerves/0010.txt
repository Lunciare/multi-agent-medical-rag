Surgical Considerations Splanchnicectomy is a procedure frequently used for controlling severe visceral pain, as in pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer cases. Knowledge of the anatomy and variations of the thoracic splanchnic nerve is essential for properly performing this procedure. Surgeries within the pelvis risk injury to the pelvic and sacral splanchnic and hypogastric nerves and the inferior hypogastric plexus. Injury to these structures could affect normal bowel, bladder, and sexual function. Clinical Significance Evidence shows that greater thoracic splanchnicectomy can effectively reduce chronic pain from chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer. Left flank pain control is attainable by transecting the left greater thoracic splanchnic nerves. Notably, bowel motility is controlled by multiple branches of the greater thoracic splanchnic nerve. Thus, bowel dysfunction is a likely complication of this procedure.
