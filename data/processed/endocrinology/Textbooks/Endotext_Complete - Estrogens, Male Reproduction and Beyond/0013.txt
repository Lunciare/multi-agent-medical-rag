Of particular interest is the demonstration of differential expression in the human testis of wild type ERβ (ERβ1) and of a human variant form of ERβ, the latter arising from alternate splicing (known as ERβcx, or ERβ2), ( 95 , 96 ). ERβ2 expression seems to be associated with prevalent, negative inhibition of ER action by inhibiting ERα–induced transactivation ( 97 ); it is highest in spermatogonia and Sertoli cells in adult men, suggesting that these cells may be "protected" from estrogen action ( 95 , 96 ). Wild type ERβ1 was mostly present in pachytene spermatocytes and round spermatids, which have been proposed to be more estrogen sensitive ( 26 ), yet ERβ1 was low in less mature germ cells ( 95 ). In addition, the discovery of several splice variants of ERβ (including ERβ4) in human testicular cells suggests a specific and more complex estrogen action on spermatogenesis ( 96 ). Besides, the cellular distribution of non-genomic GPR30 estrogen receptor in human testicular biopsies was examined ( 98 ).
