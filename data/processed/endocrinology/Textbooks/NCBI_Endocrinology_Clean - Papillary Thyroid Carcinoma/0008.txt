Microcalcifications are highly specific for this type of cancer. Other imaging modalities, including CT, MRI, and FDG-PET/CT, may be needed to assess the extent of extra-thyroidal extension, evaluate the presence of substernal masses, detect recurrent tumors, and improve diagnostic accuracy. Treatment / Management Surgery Choices depend on the location and extent of identifiable disease and the risk that unidentifiable disease foci are also present. With increasing emphasis on risk-stratified management, the treatment approaches recommended by the 2015 ATA guidelines are more conservative than in the past. Small-size papillary cancers can be followed conservatively with active surveillance and without any intervention, and surgery can be pursued only if there is a significant change in size or internal features of the tumor. A recent study showed that there is still an adjustment period among physicians who are still recommending more aggressive treatment choices despite the current changes. A lobectomy is an option for unifocal tumors smaller than 4 cm with no evidence of extrathyroidal extension or lymph node metastasis.
