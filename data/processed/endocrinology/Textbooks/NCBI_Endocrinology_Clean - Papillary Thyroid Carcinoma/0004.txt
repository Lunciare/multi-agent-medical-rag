Pathophysiology Chromosomal rearrangements have been identified in PTC, including the RET protooncogene, NTRK1, and MET gene overexpression. Further, mutations in the BRAF gene have also been identified and correlated with prognosis, particularly BRAFV600E mutation. A clear association between PTC and radiation exposure is now well established. Histopathology Gross Findings Grossly, PTC typically presents as an invasive neoplasm with poorly defined margins, a firm consistency, and a granular white-cut surface. Calcifications may be present. The size is widely variable, with a mean diameter of 2-3 cm. PTC has 3 classification categories based on the size and extent of the primary lesion. Minimal carcinoma or occult carcinoma/micro-carcinoma tumors are 1.5 cm or smaller and show no evidence of invasiveness through the thyroid capsule or cervical lymph nodes. These lesions are typically nonpalpable and usually found as incidental findings during operative or autopsy examination. Intra-thyroid tumors are greater than 1.5 cm in diameter but remain confined to the thyroid gland with no evidence of extra-thyroid invasion.
