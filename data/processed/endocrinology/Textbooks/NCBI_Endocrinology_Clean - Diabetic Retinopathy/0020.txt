Refractory Diabetic macular edema - The definition of refractory or persistent DME varies, including macular edema not responding to multiple injections of anti-VEGF and, at least one sitting of laser, and suboptimal response (central macular thickness on OCT of more than 250 microns with associated visual loss) to 6 consecutive monthly injections of anti-VEGF agents. A possible approach to such cases includes evaluating systemic control, higher dosing or frequency of anti-VEGF medications, use of steroid implants (according to protocol U of DRCR.net), rechecking of the diagnosis, ruling out any unfavorable anatomical causes, and ultrawide field angiography to look for extreme peripheral ischemic areas and to laser as far in the periphery as possible. Ischemic type of diabetic macular edema is associated with poor visual prognosis. The patient can be considered for an extreme peripheral laser to kill out the ischemic areas responsible for VEGF release. Steroid implants could be tried. However, the prognosis is guarded despite all the measures.
