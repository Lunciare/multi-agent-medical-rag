Its primary function is to regulate serum osmolarity. The ADH levels are lower when the osmolality is below 280 mOsm/kg in a normal individual. This results in water excretion. Conversely, when the plasma osmolality rises above 280 mOsm/kg, the ADH levels increase, resulting in water reabsorption. In addition to osmoreceptor stimulation, volume-sensitive receptors can also trigger ADH release. Volume-sensitive receptors increase ADH if there is a sudden and significant drop in pressure. Small incremental decreases are insufficient to activate ADH – renin and norepinephrine handle these smaller changes instead. ADH increases water retention and raises blood pressure via 2 different receptors. In the distal nephron, V2 receptors help increase water reabsorption by increasing the aquaporin channels in the principal cells of the collecting duct. Increased ADH also stimulates V1 receptors, which increase vascular resistance throughout the body. This topic is discussed in detail.
