A review of the literature found that the most common symptoms of granulomatous hypophysitis at presentation were headache (61%), visual changes (40%), polyuria/polydipsia (27%) and cranial nerve palsies (27%); panhypopituitarism and AVP deficiency were found in 49% and 27% of cases, respectively ( 12 ). Cases of compression of the cavernous part of the internal carotid artery have also been described ( 40 ). Clinical data regarding xanthomatous and IgG4-related hypophysitis are less robust due to the rarity of these variants. AVP deficiency A review of 36 published cases showed that the most common symptoms of xanthomatous hypophysitis were headache (69%), menstrual disorders in women (67%), visual disturbances (34%), polyuria (36%), and fever (8%). The most common pituitary hormone abnormalities were GH deficiency (64%), AVP deficiency (55%), and hyperprolactinemia (55%), while hypothyroidism (42%), adrenal insufficiency (36%), and hypogonadism (36%) were less prevalent ( 41 ). IgG4-related hypophysitis involves frequently both the pituitary and the stalk (~65%) and usually manifests with headaches (~50%), panhypopituitarism (~50%), anterior hypopituitarism (25-47%), and AVP deficiency (18-36%) ( 20 , 42 ). Cases of intrachiasmal abscess and spreading to the cavernous sinus have also been reported ( 43 , 44 ). Primary hypophysitis is rare in children, with less than 100 cases reported in the literature, of which only a few were biopsy-proven ( 45 - 47 ). The clinical presentation, however, seems to differ from adults. A review of the literature showed that the most common presenting symptoms in children are caused by antidiuretic hormone (ADH) deficiency (85%) ( 47 ). GH deficiency is found in 76% of cases, while FSH/LH, TSH and ACTH deficiencies were less common than in adults (32%, 29% and 20%, respectively). Headaches and visual disturbances were also rarely reported (17% and 8% of cases, respectively) ( 47 ). As AVP deficiency and growth retardation are the most common presenting symptoms in children with primary hypophysitis, the more frequent intracranial germinomas and Langerhans cell histiocytosis, as well as craniopharyngiomas, must be considered in the differential diagnosis ( 48 ). Moreover, children with a presumptive diagnosis of hypophysitis are at risk of developing germinomas later in life (up to 3 years after the initial diagnosis) and require extended follow-up ( 47 , 49 ). Germinomas are also a documented cause of secondary hypophysitis (see “Hypophysitis secondary to sella and parasellar disease” below).
