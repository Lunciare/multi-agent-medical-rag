A recent prospective study involving 822 cancer patients evaluated the incidence of pituitary dysfunction in those treated with either PD-1/CTLA-4 combination therapy or PD-1 monotherapy (165). ACTH and cortisol levels were monitored every six weeks following treatment initiation. If both hormones were found to be below the normal range, patients underwent pituitary function assessment and MRI. Pituitary dysfunction was identified in 16 of 74 patients on combination therapy compared to 25 of 748 on PD-1 monotherapy (21.6% vs. 3.3%). Multiple hormone deficiencies, including ACTH deficiency, were more common in the combination group (12.2% vs. 0.3%), occurring in nine and two patients, respectively. Isolated ACTH deficiency was detected in seven patients receiving combination therapy and 23 patients on PD-1 monotherapy (9.5% vs. 3.1%). Pituitary enlargement (37.5% vs. 0%) and multiple hormone deficiencies (56.3% vs. 8.0%) were also significantly more prevalent in the combination group. Additionally, the median time to diagnosis was shorter among those receiving combination therapy (88 days vs. 168 days). These results highlight the need for vigilant monitoring of pituitary function, especially in patients treated with combination ICI.
