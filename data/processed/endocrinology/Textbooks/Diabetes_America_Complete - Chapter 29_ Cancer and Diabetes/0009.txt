Mortality is clearly an index of the severity of a disease from both clinical and public health standpoints. Epidemiologic studies often use cancer mortality as a surrogate for cancer incidence, because mortality data are easier to collect in large populations. However, mortality is influenced by factors other than incidence alone. For instance, cancer progression and cancer treatment contribute substantially to cancer mortality. When a disease is not fatal, mortality is not a good index of incidence. Furthermore, the cause of death may be incorrect on some death certificates (15). These factors should be considered when evaluating evidence based on mortality data. SHARED RISK FACTORS BETWEEN DIABETES AND CANCER Shared risk factors represent one likely explanation for the higher prevalence of cancer in diabetic adults. Many of these factors are strong, well-established, and common. Non-Modifiable Risk Factors Non-modifiable risk factors common to both cancer and diabetes include age, sex, and race/ethnicity. Similar to diabetes, cancer incidence and mortality increase with age
