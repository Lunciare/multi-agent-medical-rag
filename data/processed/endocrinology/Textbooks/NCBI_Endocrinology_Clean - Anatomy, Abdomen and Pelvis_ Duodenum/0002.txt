Precise understanding of duodenal structure and physiology supports timely diagnosis, procedural safety, and prevention of life-threatening complications. Structure and Function Structure The duodenum is the initial C-shaped segment of the small intestine and represents the direct continuation of the pylorus of the stomach. Distally, this segment transitions into the jejunum and ileum, with the proximal portion constituting the shortest and widest region of the small intestine. Located inferior to the stomach, the duodenum measures approximately 25 to 30 cm in length. The designation derives from the Latin term duodenum , meaning “12 fingers,” reflecting the segment's approximate length. Four anatomically distinct segments are recognized. The duodenal bulb forms the proximal segment and connects to the undersurface of the liver via the hepatoduodenal ligament, which encloses the portal vein, hepatic artery, and common bile duct. The 2nd, or descending, segment lies anterior to the IVC and right kidney, with the head of the pancreas occupying a C-shaped concavity. The 3rd segment courses from right to left anterior to the aorta and IVC, with the superior mesenteric vessels positioned anteriorly. The 4th segment ascends to continue as the jejunum.
