Routine aggressive potassium-lowering therapy is generally unnecessary and may increase the risk of hypokalemia after Fab administration. Calcium administration, once avoided due to concerns about worsening toxicity, is no longer considered contraindicated when clinically indicated; however, Fab therapy remains the definitive treatment. Clinicians should promptly correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia to reduce the risk of arrhythmias. Supportive management includes administering atropine for symptomatic bradycardia, providing intravenous fluids to treat hypotension and improve renal perfusion, and using lidocaine or phenytoin to manage ventricular arrhythmias. Clinicians should avoid antiarrhythmics that worsen atrioventricular conduction or increase digoxin levels, such as amiodarone and procainamide. Continuous cardiac monitoring is required. Clinicians should not use digoxin levels to guide management after Fab administration because assays detect both free and Fab-bound digoxin. Patients with symptomatic toxicity require hospital admission, and those with severe arrhythmias or hemodynamic instability require intensive care monitoring. Differential Diagnosis Toxicologic and non-toxicologic conditions can mimic digoxin toxicity. Toxicologic mimics include calcium channel blockers, ß-blockers, and clonidine overdose, which may cause bradycardia and hypotension. Hyperglycemia supports calcium channel blocker overdose, whereas clonidine toxicity more commonly produces CNS and respiratory depression
