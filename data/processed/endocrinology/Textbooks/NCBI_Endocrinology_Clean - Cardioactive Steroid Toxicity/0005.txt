Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for CAS toxicity in patients presenting with unexplained arrhythmias, particularly in the setting of renal impairment, polypharmacy, or herbal supplement use. Pathophysiology The chemical structure of a CAS contains a steroid nucleus and a 5- or 6-membered lactone ring. Cardenolides, which are 5-membered rings, are mostly from plants, while bufadienolides, containing a 6-membered ring, are primarily animal-based. Digitoxin and digoxin, which originate from foxglove, belong to the cardenolide class, whereas bufadienolides such as bufalin originate from mammals, amphibians, certain insects (eg, lightning bugs of the Photinus species), and plant sources such as red squill. CAS primarily serve as inotropic agents in therapeutic settings. In addition to myocytes, they affect vascular smooth muscle and the sympathetic nervous system. Their primary mechanism of action is the reversible inhibition of the Na + , K + -ATPase, which increases intracellular sodium and decreases intracellular potassium, thereby inactivating the sodium-calcium antiporter and causing intracellular calcium accumulation.
