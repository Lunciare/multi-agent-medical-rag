Clinicians should obtain a thorough medication history, including prescription drugs, herbal supplements, and potential exposure to plant-derived CAS such as foxglove or oleander. A history of renal disease, recent illness, dehydration, or medication changes increases the risk of toxicity. Physical Examination Vital sign abnormalities are common. Bradycardia due to increased vagal tone and atrioventricular nodal suppression is a frequent finding. Hypotension may occur in severe cases. General examination findings may include lethargy, confusion, or altered mental status, particularly in chronic toxicity. Signs of dehydration, such as dry mucous membranes or decreased skin turgor, may be present, increasing the risk of toxicity. In addition to bradycardia and arrhythmia, the cardiovascular examination may reveal signs of reduced cardiac output. Severe toxicity may result in syncope or signs of hemodynamic instability. Neurologic examination may demonstrate confusion, delirium, or visual disturbances. Visual symptoms are often subjective but can be important diagnostic clues. Evaluation In acute ingestion, the clinician should obtain digoxin or digitoxin levels at presentation and at least 6 hours after ingestion.
