For patients suspected of chronic toxicity, 1 level should suffice. Experts consider levels above 2 ng/ml for digoxin and above 40 ng/ml for digitoxin 6 hours after the last dose to be clinically toxic. Clinicians should monitor electrolytes soon after presentation, as hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and hypercalcemia increase susceptibility to the toxic cardiac effects of digoxin. Hyperkalemia correlates with mortality. Studies indicate that serum potassium levels above 5.5 mEq/L are associated with significantly increased mortality. Assessment of renal function is of paramount importance given the renal clearance of digoxin. Patients suspected of intentional ingestion should also undergo an assessment of acetaminophen and salicylate levels to assess for possible co-ingestants. Patients who can get pregnant and are of childbearing age should undergo pregnancy testing, and a fingerstick glucose level is necessary to exclude hypoglycemia as a potential cause of altered mental status. Serial ECGs and continuous cardiac monitoring are necessary to detect arrhythmias.
