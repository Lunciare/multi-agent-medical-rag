Analysis of U.S. surveillance data from 2005 to 2010 estimated approximately 5,156 emergency department visits annually for digoxin toxicity, with 78.8% resulting in hospitalization. Notably, rates of emergency department visits and hospitalizations remained relatively stable during this period despite declining digoxin prescribing, suggesting that toxicity risk persists among exposed patients. CAS toxicity from herbal supplements and traditional medicines is increasingly recognized. Toxicity may result from intrinsic CAS content, contamination, adulteration, or misidentification of plant materials. Plant-derived poisoning remains a significant cause of morbidity and mortality in certain regions. Yellow oleander ( Thevetia peruviana ) poisoning is particularly prevalent in South Asia, including India and Sri Lanka, where it represents a common method of intentional self-poisoning and is associated with substantial mortality. Animal-derived CAS, such as bufadienolides found in Bufo toad secretions used in traditional remedies and aphrodisiacs, can also cause life-threatening cardiac toxicity. The true incidence of supplement-related CAS toxicity is likely underreported due to incomplete exposure histories, inconsistent supplement regulation, and limitations of standard immunoassays, which may not detect all CAS compounds.
