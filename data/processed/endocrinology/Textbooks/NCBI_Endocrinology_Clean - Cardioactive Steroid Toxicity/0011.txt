Multidose activated charcoal may be used in severe poisoning, particularly with digitoxin, to interrupt enterohepatic recirculation. Though rarely used in clinical practice, cholestyramine and colestipol may also reduce gastrointestinal reabsorption. Antidotal therapy with Digoxin-Specific Antibody Fragments Definitive treatment involves administration of digoxin-specific antibody fragments (digoxin immune Fab), which bind free digoxin and reverse its toxic effects. Indications include life-threatening arrhythmias, potassium greater than 5.0 to 5.5 mEq/L in acute toxicity, hemodynamic instability or end-organ dysfunction attributable to toxicity, symptomatic chronic toxicity with arrhythmias or neurologic symptoms, acute ingestion with digoxin concentration above 10 ng/mL at 6 or more hours post ingestion, or suspected toxicity from non-digoxin CAS such as oleander. Asymptomatic patients with mildly elevated digoxin levels generally do not require Fab therapy. One vial of digoxin immune Fab binds approximately 0.5 mg of digoxin. Clinicians calculate digoxin immune Fab dosing based on the amount ingested, the serum digoxin concentration, or empiric dosing when these data are unavailable or plant-derived CAS ingestion is suspected. Serum digoxin concentrations do not reliably reflect the severity of toxicity from naturally occurring CAS.
