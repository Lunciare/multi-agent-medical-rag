Hyperkalemia serves primarily as a marker of severe toxicity rather than the direct cause of death, and correction of serum potassium alone does not reverse toxicity. Definitive treatment requires administration of digoxin immune Fab to neutralize circulating toxin and restore cellular ion homeostasis. Patients who present with chronic digoxin toxicity and remain untreated have a mortality ranging from 5% to 13%. Complications Clinicians must maintain vigilance for cardiac arrhythmias and electrolyte abnormalities in patients with CAS toxicity. These agents produce a characteristic combination of impaired atrioventricular nodal conduction, attributable to increased vagal tone, and increased myocardial automaticity, attributable to intracellular calcium accumulation, thereby allowing virtually any type of arrhythmia to occur. Hyperkalemia reflects severe toxicity. Patients may also develop neurologic symptoms such as confusion, weakness, or altered mental status, most often secondary to cerebral hypoperfusion caused by arrhythmias and reduced cardiac output. In rare cases, severe hypoperfusion may result in end-organ ischemia, including mesenteric ischemia.
