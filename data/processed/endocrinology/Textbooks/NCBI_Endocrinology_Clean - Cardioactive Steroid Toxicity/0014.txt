Hyperglycemia supports calcium channel blocker overdose, whereas clonidine toxicity more commonly produces CNS and respiratory depression with miosis; however, these features are not sufficient to exclude digoxin toxicity. Non-toxicologic mimics include hypothermia, hypothyroidism, myocardial infarction, sinus node dysfunction (including sick sinus syndrome), and hyperkalemia. Interpretation of serum digoxin concentrations requires caution, as plant-derived CAS can cause digoxin-like toxicity; however, standard digoxin assays may be unreliable, and exposure may not be recognized immediately. Additionally, endogenous digoxin-like immunoreactive substances (DLIS) in volume-expanded states (eg, uremia and renal failure, liver disease, and preeclampsia) and certain exogenous agents (eg, spironolactone, canrenone, potassium canrenoate, and some traditional herbal preparations) may cross-react with some digoxin immunoassays, falsely elevating results. When clinically necessary and available, measuring free digoxin in protein-free ultrafiltrate can reduce interference from protein-bound digoxin. Prognosis The prognosis of CAS toxicity varies substantially based on the source of exposure, severity of presentation, and access to specific antidotal therapy. However, overall survival can be favorable with appropriate treatment, particularly when digoxin-specific Fab antibodies are available.
