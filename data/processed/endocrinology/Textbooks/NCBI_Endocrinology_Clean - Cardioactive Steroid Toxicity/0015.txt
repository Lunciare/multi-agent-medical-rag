For digoxin and digitoxin poisoning, observational studies show response rates of 50% to 90% after digoxin-Fab administration, with dysrhythmia resolution typically occurring within 30 to 45 minutes. An observational study among patients presenting in cardiac arrest from digoxin toxicity reports survival rates of 54% in patients treated with digoxin-Fab. Acute accidental poisoning from plants containing cardiac glycosides (lily of the valley, oleander, and foxglove) is common due to their narrow therapeutic index. Death occurs from ventricular fibrillation or tachycardia. The prognosis depends heavily on access to digoxin immune Fab, which may be limited in resource-poor countries where plant-based poisoning is more prevalent. Hyperkalemia is a critical prognostic marker in acute CAS toxicity and reflects the severity of Na + , K + -ATPase inhibition. Higher potassium levels correlate with increased risk of life-threatening arrhythmias and mortality. Historically, potassium levels greater than 5.5 mEq/L were associated with very high mortality rates in untreated patients; however, the availability of digoxin-specific antibody fragments has dramatically improved survival.
