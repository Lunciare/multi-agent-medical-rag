Cardioactive Steroid Toxicity
KEYWORDS: digoxin, toxicity, cas, patients, renal, levels, clinicians, ingestion, digitoxin, risk, cas toxicity, fab, including, chronic, serum

Introduction Cardioactive steroids (CAS), also known as cardiac glycosides, are pharmacologically active compounds that have been used for centuries to treat heart failure and edema, historically referred to as “dropsy.” Therapeutic use dates back to the 18th century, when William Withering described the effects of foxglove ( Digitalis purpurea ). These compounds occur naturally in several plants, including oleander ( Nerium oleander) , foxglove ( Digitalis species), lily of the valley ( Convallaria majalis ), red squill ( Drimia maritima ), dogbane ( Apocynum species), and milkweed ( Asclepias species). Digoxin is primarily derived from Digitalis lanata , whereas Digitalis purpurea contains digitoxin and related glycosides. Some traditional herbal remedies and supplements may also contain CAS and pose a risk of toxicity. Digoxin is the most widely used CAS in modern medicine and is indicated for symptomatic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and for ventricular rate control in atrial fibrillation. Available in tablet, capsule, and oral solution forms, the capsule formulations demonstrate slightly greater bioavailability.
