Several medications increase digoxin levels by reducing renal clearance or altering distribution, including quinidine, amiodarone, verapamil, diltiazem, spironolactone, and carvedilol. The intestinal bacterium Eggerthella lenta (formerly Eubacterium lentum ) metabolizes digoxin into inactive compounds in some individuals, reducing drug bioavailability. Antibiotics that suppress this bacterium may increase digoxin absorption and serum concentrations, thereby increasing the risk of toxicity. History and Physical History Patients with acute CAS toxicity often present within hours of ingestion, commonly due to intentional overdose, accidental ingestion, or consumption of plant- or animal-derived CAS. Early symptoms frequently include gastrointestinal complaints such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and anorexia. Neurologic symptoms, including fatigue, weakness, dizziness, and confusion, may follow. Chronic CAS toxicity typically develops gradually and often occurs in older adults receiving therapeutic digoxin, particularly in the setting of renal impairment, dehydration, electrolyte abnormalities, or drug interactions. Patients may present with vague symptoms such as fatigue, lethargy, anorexia, nausea, confusion, or functional decline. Visual disturbances, including blurred vision, diplopia, photophobia, scotomas, and color vision abnormalities such as xanthopsia, are characteristic and may suggest chronic toxicity.
