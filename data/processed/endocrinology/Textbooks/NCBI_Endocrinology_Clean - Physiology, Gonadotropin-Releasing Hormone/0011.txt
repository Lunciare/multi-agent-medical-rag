Protein kinase C causes phosphorylation of intracellular proteins. Inositol triphosphate binds to receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum membrane and causes the release of calcium into the cytoplasm. The proteins phosphorylated by Protein kinase C, serve as the intracellular signaling pathway for mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK) pathway, including ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK. Calcium released into cytosol binds to calmodulin, causing further phosphorylation of proteins via activation of another set of protein kinases, performing functions of intracellular transport, enhancement of cell metabolism, and exocytosis of vesicles storing hormones. Another point worth remembering is that although most GnRH receptors are Gq-protein coupled, few of them are also Gs protein-coupled. This Gs/cAMP pathway is involved in the initial response of pituitary gonadotroph to GnRH or GnRH analogs and further regulation of synthesis and secretion of pituitary gonadotroph. Differential regulation of LH and FSH: Although GnRH acts on pituitary cells to enhance gonadotropes release, its action is not equal to both FSH and LH.
