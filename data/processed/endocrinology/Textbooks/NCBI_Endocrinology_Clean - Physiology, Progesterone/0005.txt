PR-A and PR-B are similar in composition except that PR-B has an additional 164 amino acids (it is also known as the AF-1), which gives PR-B a unique function. Although PR-A and B both share the AF-1 domain, it appears that the addition of AF-3 domain inhibits the AF-1 antagonist capabilities, allows for increased activity of PR-B. PR-C lacks both the AF-1 and DNA binding ability; however, it can still dimerize with other receptors and can bind to its ligand. Development Progesterone plays a significant role during the normal menstruation cycle. The menstrual cycle is broadly divided into two sequential phases, beginning with the follicular phase, also known as the proliferative phase, and followed by the luteal phase. Each phase of menstruation requires a distinct set of hormones to allow for normal progression through a given cycle. The increase of progesterone, specifically in the menstrual cycle, occurs due to the initiation of a mid-cycle luteinizing hormone (LH) surge near the end of the follicular phase.
