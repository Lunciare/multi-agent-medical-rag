One small study of type 1 diabetes patients that included African Americans did not find a high frequency of CD (Table 27.6) (96). Many studies have been cross-sectional, based on a single screening test either at the time of diag­ nosis of diabetes or at some unspecified time point later. A few studies have followed children over time. One such study from Denver had one of the highest cumulative frequencies of TTG (>11%) in a large cohort of nondiabetic high-risk children (97). While a substantial propor­ tion of the CD is detected at the time of diabetes diagnosis, as many as 40% of total cases occur in the years following diagnosis (98). The mean age of diagnosis of CD in children is approximately 10 years, while diagnosis of type 1 diabetes occurs significantly earlier.
