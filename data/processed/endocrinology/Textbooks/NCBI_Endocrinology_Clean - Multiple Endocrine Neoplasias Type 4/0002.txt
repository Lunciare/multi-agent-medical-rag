Multiple Endocrine Neoplasias Type 4
KEYWORDS: pituitary, tumors, gene, hyperparathyroidism, patients, parathyroid, disease, mutation, gland, primary hyperparathyroidism, primary, neuroendocrine, syndrome, hormone, reported

CDKN1B result in MEN4. In humans, the CDKN1B gene is located on chromosome 12p13.1. The CDKN1B gene encodes a protein called p27, a CDK inhibitor (CDKI), a well-known cell-cycle regulator. CDKN1B functions as a tumor suppressor gene, and germline mutations in CDKN1B lead to reduced expression of p27, resulting in uncontrolled cell-cycle progression. Conversely, in MEN1, there is an inactivation of menin (encoded by the MEN1 gene) that affects cell division and gene transcription of the CDKN1B regulator p27. This mutation in CDKN1B was first identified in rats. Subsequently, Franklin et al tested their theory that CDKIs may work as tumor suppressor genes in mouse models. They found that the loss of function of p18 and p27 leads to a wide range of multiple endocrine gland tumors that predominantly affect the pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, and parathyroid glands. In 2002, Fritz et al described a MEN-like syndrome in rats with negative mutation for MEN1 or RET genes. This syndrome, involving multiple glands, was thought to be intermediate, or a combination of MEN1 and MEN2, and was termed "MENX."
