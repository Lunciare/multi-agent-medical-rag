Genetically, Temple syndrome is caused by dysregulation of imprinted genes on chromosome 14q32.2. The imprinted region responsible for TS ( figure 7 ) contains three differentially methylated regions- DMRs (IG-DMR, MEG3-DMR, MEG8-DMR). Protein-coding genes (DLK1, RTL1/Retrotransposon Gag Like 1, DIO3/ Iodothyronine Deiodinase 3 ), long ncRNAs (MEG3/ maternally expressed 3, MEG8/ maternally expressed 8, RTL1as/ retrotransposon Gag like 1 antisense transcript, DIO3OS/ DIO3 opposite strand upstream RNA) and short ncRNAs (SNORDs/ small nucleolar RNA C/D box, and miRNAs) are transcribed based on parent-of-origin. The paternal allele express the DLK1 and RTL1 genes, which are silenced in the maternal allele ( 24 ). The three primary molecular mechanisms for TS14 are: ( 1 ) maternal uniparental disomy of chromosome 14 (UPD(14)mat) (60-75%); ( 2 ) hypomethylation of the IG-DMR controlling the DLK1–MEG3 domain on the paternal allele , leading to a maternal chromosome-like expression pattern (10-20%); and ( 3 ) microdeletions in the paternal copy of the DLK1–MEG3 domain (5-15%) ( 24 , 45 ). All three mechanisms lead to reduced or absent expression of paternally expressed genes such as DLK1. As previously mentioned, DLK1 plays a critical inhibitory role in the regulation of GnRH secretion and energy metabolism ( 14 ).
