MECP2—A Potential Common Factor Between Puberty and Neurodevelopment Methyl-CpG-binding protein 2 (MECP2) gene, located on the X-inactivation zone of chromosome X (Xq28) , encodes a nuclear methylated DNA binding protein functioning as a repressor or an activator of gene transcription ( 36 ). MECP2 belongs to the methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD) family, which also includes MBD3, a protein previously shown to interact with MKRN3 in pubertal regulation ( 36 ). Loss-of-function mutations in MECP2 are associated with Rett syndrome, an X-linked dominant regressive neurodevelopmental disorder that primarily affects girls. Precocious puberty (CPP) has been described in up to 15% of girls with MECP2 mutations, and early thelarche in approximately 25%. Moreover, boys with MECP2 duplication and severe intellectual disability have also rarely presented with CPP ( 23 ). In a recent multicenter genetic study involving 404 girls with idiopathic CPP, rare potentially damaging heterozygous variants in MECP2 were identified in seven girls, four of whom had de novo mutations ( 36 ). Some of the affected girls displayed additional features of neurodevelopmental delay such as microcephaly or autism spectrum disorder, while others showed isolated CPP, suggesting variable phenotypic expression. Experimental studies further support a role for MECP2 in puberty regulation. In mice, MECP2 protein co-localized with GnRH-expressing neurons in hypothalamic nuclei involved in pubertal control ( 36 ). In ewes, MECP2 mRNA expression decreased at the time of puberty onset, while GnRH and KISS1 expression increased, and immunohistochemistry demonstrated MECP2 protein co-expression with GnRH and kisspeptin in the hypothalamus ( 23 ). Together, these findings suggest that MECP2 may act as an epigenetic regulator of pubertal timing. Given its established role in neurodevelopment, the identification of MECP2 variants in girls with CPP, with or without cognitive symptoms, highlights a potential overlap between the molecular mechanisms regulating neurodevelopment and reproductive axis activation.
