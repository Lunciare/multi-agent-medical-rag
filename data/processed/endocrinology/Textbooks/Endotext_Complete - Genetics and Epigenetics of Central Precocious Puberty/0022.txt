Furthermore, syndromic forms of CPP, including those associated with imprinting disorders such as Temple syndrome, neurodevelopmental conditions like Rett and DDX3X-related syndromes, and tumor-predisposing disorders such as Pallister-Hall and tuberous sclerosis, highlight the diverse clinical contexts in which early pubertal onset may arise. These syndromes also emphasize the interplay between genetic mutations, epigenetic dysregulation, and structural or functional brain abnormalities in the pathogenesis of CPP. Ultimately, elucidating the genetic and epigenetic underpinnings of CPP offers critical insights not only into the neuroendocrine control of puberty, but also into broader biological processes involving growth, development, and metabolism. As molecular diagnostic tools continue to evolve, they will improve the accuracy of diagnosis, inform prognosis, and permit targeted interventions in children with disorders of early puberty.
