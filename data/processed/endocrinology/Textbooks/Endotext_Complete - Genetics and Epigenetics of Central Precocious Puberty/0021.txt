subunit 2) (16p13) Hamartomas in multiple organs, seizures, cognitive impairment Autosomal dominant CNS hamartomas may affect HPG axis Very rare SUMMARY Over the past two decades, significant advances in genomic and epigenomic research have transformed our understanding of central precocious puberty (CPP). Once considered to be idiopathic in most cases, CPP is now increasingly recognized as a heterogeneous condition with a complex molecular basis. Monogenic causes—particularly mutations in imprinted genes such as MKRN3 and DLK1—have emerged as key contributors to familial forms of CPP, underscoring the importance of parent-of-origin–specific gene regulation in pubertal timing. In parallel, genome-wide association studies and epigenetic profiling have identified additional candidate genes and regulatory pathways that modulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis activation.
