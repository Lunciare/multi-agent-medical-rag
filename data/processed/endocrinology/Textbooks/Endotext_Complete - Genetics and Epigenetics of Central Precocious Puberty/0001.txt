Genetics and Epigenetics of Central Precocious Puberty
KEYWORDS: puberty, cpp, precocious, precocious puberty, pubertal, pubmed, central, gene, central precocious, mutations, genetic, syndrome, girls, gnrh, genes


ABSTRACT Central precocious puberty (CPP) is defined as the early activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, occurring before age 8 in girls and 9 in boys and leading to the early appearance of secondary sexual characteristics. While multiple environmental and pathological factors may contribute to the condition, genetic and epigenetic mechanisms have emerged as major determinants in both familial and sporadic cases. This chapter reviews current knowledge on the genetic architecture of CPP, including well-established monogenic causes such as mutations in MKRN3, DLK1, KISS1, KISS1R and MECP2, and other candidate genes. The role of genomic imprinting is discussed in detail. In addition, recent insights into epigenetic regulation—including differential methylation patterns in hypothalamic genes and the impact of imprinted gene networks—highlight emerging mechanisms that may bridge genetic susceptibility with environmental influences. The chapter also explores syndromic forms of CPP and cases in which CPP arises secondarily to other genetic disorders. These include children with neurodevelopmental disabilities and those with congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH), in which chronic peripheral androgen excess may lead to premature activation of the central HPG axis. Together, this chapter provides a comprehensive and up-to-date overview of the molecular and genetic mechanisms underlying CPP, offering clinicians and researchers a framework for understanding its pathogenesis, refining diagnostic approaches, and identifying potential targets for future therapies. INTRODUCTION Central precocious puberty (CPP), an earlier than normal activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, with its clinical manifestation of secondary sexual characteristics in response to androgens in males and estrogens in females, as well as reproductive capacity, is a relatively common clinical condition. CPP is caused by a wide range of etiologies, including central nervous system abnormalities, genetic mutations, peripheral hormone production, exposure to endocrine-disrupting chemicals, as well as idiopathic causes. Sex, geographic location, and certain environmental exposures may also affect its occurrence. In recent years, increasing attention has been directed toward understanding the genetic and epigenetic mechanisms that regulate the timing of pubertal onset. These investigations have provided crucial insight into the molecular control of HPG axis activation. We describe the normal sequence of puberty, followed by focus on the genetic and epigenetic etiologies of precocious puberty, with the aim of elucidating key regulatory pathways involved in pubertal timing, and their implications for diagnosis and for management.
