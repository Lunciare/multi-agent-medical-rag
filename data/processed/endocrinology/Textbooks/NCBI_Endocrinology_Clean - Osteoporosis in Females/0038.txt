Treatment can involve both nonpharmacological and pharmacological options. Nonpharmacological Treatments Alcohol : The AACE advises postmenopausal women to limit alcohol intake to no more than 2 units of alcohol daily, as it has been shown that consuming more than 3 units per day is associated with a 38% increase in major osteoporotic fractures and a 68% increase in hip fractures. Conversely, numerous studies, such as Epidémiologie de l'Osteoporose (EPIDOS), noted that moderate drinking was associated with a protective relationship and a higher trochanteric BMD in older women. Smoking cessation : Women who smoke are noted on average to have lower BMD and an estimated 30% increase in risk of fractures, which is independent of BMD. On average, smokers are thinner, experience menopause at a younger age, and have lower estradiol levels. Studies have furthermore noted that smoking cessation can lead to an increase in BMD and reduce fracture risk; similarly, smoking cessation appears to be associated with increased bone formation marker levels, such as osteocalcin.
