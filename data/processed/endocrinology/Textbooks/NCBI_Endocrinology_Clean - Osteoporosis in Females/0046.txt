Risedronate is a third-generation bisphosphonate approved in 2000, which is administered orally. Prevention dosages include 5 mg oral daily, 35 mg oral weekly, or 150 mg oral monthly; similar to ibandronate, the treatment dosages for risedronate are the same as prevention doses. Risedronate does have a special formulation that can be taken with or without food and is delayed-release (past the stomach), which is beneficial in patients with preexisting upper gastrointestinal disease. However, the rates of adverse gastrointestinal events (including upper) are not lowered. Risedronate was studied in the Vertebral Efficacy with Risedronate Therapy North America (VERT NA), Vertebral Efficacy with Risedronate Therapy Multinational (VERT MN), Hip Intervention Program (HIP), and the Risedronate and Alendronate (REAL) studies. Zoledronate is a third-generation bisphosphonate approved in 2007, which is administered intravenously. The prevention dosage is 5 mg every 2 years, whereas the treatment dosage is 5 mg yearly.
