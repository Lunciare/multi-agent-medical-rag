Differential Diagnosis When evaluating osteoporosis in females, it is essential to consider other conditions that can mimic or contribute to decreased bone density. The following is a list of differential diagnoses that should be ruled out during assessment. The differential diagnosis for osteoporosis is as follows: Osteopenia Osteogenesis imperfecta Osteomalacia Vitamin D deficiency or insufficiency Hyperparathyroidism Hyperthyroidism Paget disease of the bone Mineral and bone disorders of chronic kidney disease Primary bone malignancies Metastases to the skeleton Avascular necrosis Hematologic malignancies Mimickers of vertebral osteoporosis (such as osteoarthritis, scoliosis, or Scheuermann disease) Prognosis In the European Union, 43,000 deaths related to fractures were reported in 2010, with around 50% due to hip fractures, 28% to vertebral fractures, and 22% to other types of fractures. An estimated 50% of postmenopausal women will experience a fragility fracture.
