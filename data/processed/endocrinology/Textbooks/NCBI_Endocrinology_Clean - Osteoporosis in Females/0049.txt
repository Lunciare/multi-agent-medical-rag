Uveitis has rarely been linked to bisphosphonate therapy, and conflicting evidence has suggested an increased risk of esophageal cancer; however, the FDA has concluded there is no definitive association. Bisphosphonates have been associated with osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femur fractures. Despite the beneficial effects of bisphosphonates, due to the long list of adverse effects, approximately 50% of patients will discontinue bisphosphonate therapy within 1 year of use. Denosumab Denosumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody, approved in 2010 for the treatment of osteoporosis. This drug's mechanism of action involves targeting the receptor activator of the nuclear factor Kappa B receptor ligand (RANKL), which prevents its attachment to the RANK. Denosumab is administered as a 60 mg subcutaneous injection every 6 months and does not have a limit of duration. However, the Endocrine Society recommends reassessing fracture risk after 5 to 10 years of use and considering continuing or transitioning to other therapy if fracture risk remains high.
