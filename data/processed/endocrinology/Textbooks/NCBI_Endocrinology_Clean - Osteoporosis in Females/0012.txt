the Norwegian Tromso Study noted 3% of vertebral fractures in females younger than 60, compared to 19% in females older than 70. The osteoporosis ‘treatment gap’ is the number of patients who meet the indication for therapy minus the number of patients who receive treatment. The treatment gap is a global concern, with the International Osteoporosis Foundation recording a treatment gap of 73% among women from France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the United Kingdom. In 2019, approximately 15 million women in Europe who were eligible did not receive treatment for osteoporosis. Up to 95% of patients who are discharged following the repair of a hip fracture do not receive treatment or a management plan, with men far less likely to receive treatment compared to women. When treatment is commenced, however, it is also estimated that around 70% of patients will not continue treatment past the first year. In addition to the absence of treatment, screening for osteoporosis is not prioritized. Less than one-third of patients with a fragility fracture have either a bone density test or have received treatment for underlying osteoporosis. An estimated 91% of women older than 65 with a previous fragility fracture are not screened for osteoporosis.
