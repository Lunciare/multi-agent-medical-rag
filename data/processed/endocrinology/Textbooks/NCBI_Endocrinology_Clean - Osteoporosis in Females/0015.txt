Osteoprotegerin (also known as osteoclastogenesis inhibitory factor) is released from osteocytes and binds to RANKL to inhibit its function, leading to an inhibition of bone resorption (see Image. Role of RANKL/RANK/OPG Axis on Bone Homeostasis and Immune System). BMD depends upon 2 principal factors: the peak bone mass and the rate of decline. Peak bone mass is defined as the maximal skeletal mass and strength attained. Around 50% of peak bone mass is obtained in the adolescent years in females ('peaks'), followed by slow building continuing until the third decade, where peak bone mass is reached. Estimates suggest that around 60% to 80% of peak bone mass is genetically predetermined. Compared to men, however, peak bone mass is around 10% lower in females, meaning women start resorption from a lower bone density. An increase in peak bone mass in children and adolescents can reduce the risk of an osteoporotic fracture during adulthood by nearly 50%. Compared to males, females have less periosteal bone formation, but more endocortical apposition, largely due to estrogen inhibition of periosteal apposition.
