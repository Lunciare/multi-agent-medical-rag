Wrist fractures occur earlier in life than other fractures [International Osteoporosis Foundation- Epidemiology. https://www.osteoporosis.foundation/health-professionals/fragility-fractures/epidemiology ]. This differs from men, who account for 15% of wrist fractures and do not demonstrate an increased incidence with age. Around 326,838 wrist fractures occur annually, with a 16% lifetime risk for a Colle’s fracture in a 50-year-old woman in the United States. In the United Kingdom, the incidence of distal forearm fractures is 39.7 per 10,000 person-years in women, compared to 8.9 per 10,000 person-years in men, for individuals older than 50. Contrary to hip and vertebral fractures, however, there does not appear to be an associated increased risk for mortality following distal forearm fractures. Vertebral compression fractures are the most common fractures, but are typically asymptomatic and are often incidentally found on imaging. Existing vertebral fractures increase the risk of a subsequent fracture by 5-fold. Only about one-third of vertebral fractures come to clinical attention when they can be diagnosed radiographically.
