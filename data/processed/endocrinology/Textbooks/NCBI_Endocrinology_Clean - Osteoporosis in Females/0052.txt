However, bazedoxifene (with and without conjugated estrogen) has only been shown to reduce vertebral fractures. Adverse events of bazedoxifene include leg cramps and deep venous thromboses; unlike raloxifene, the impact on breast cancer prevention is unknown. Caution is furthermore advised in initiating hormone therapy in women older than 60. Candidates for bazedoxifene with conjugated estrogen are typically younger postmenopausal women with a uterus who require symptomatic vasomotor relief and prevention of bone loss. Raloxifene Like bazedoxifene, raloxifene also belongs to the class of selective estrogen receptor modulators. Unlike bazedoxifene, however, raloxifene was approved as monotherapy in 1997 for prevention and in 1999 for treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis, administered as 60 mg orally once daily. Raloxifene has only been shown to reduce vertebral fractures, as noted in the Multiple Outcomes of Raloxifene Evaluation Study (MORE), the Continuing Outcomes Relevant to Evista (CORE), and Selective Treatment of Osteoporosis with Raloxifene (STAR) trials. Once raloxifene has been discontinued, the skeletal benefit is lost within the subsequent 1 to 2 years; furthermore, raloxifene may increase bone loss when administered to premenopausal women.
