In contrast, a “false” tripod is present when at least 1 of these arteries arises separately before the remaining 2 along the course of the celiac trunk. The celiac artery typically measures 1.5 to 2 cm in length and provides the primary arterial supply to the foregut and foregut-derived structures, including the distal esophagus, stomach, proximal 1/3 of the duodenum, liver, pancreas, gallbladder, and spleen. Adequate blood flow through the celiac trunk is essential for maintaining the physiological function of these organs. Embryology The arterial system undergoes numerous modifications in utero . During the intraembryonic period, paired dorsal aortae form the primitive arterial system and eventually fuse to constitute the abdominal aorta. Paired ventral segmental arteries develop from the dorsal aorta, with some fusing to form median vessels and temporarily interconnected by a ventral longitudinal anastomosis. The 10th, 13th, and 21st ventral segmental arteries persist to form the celiac trunk and the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries, respectively.
