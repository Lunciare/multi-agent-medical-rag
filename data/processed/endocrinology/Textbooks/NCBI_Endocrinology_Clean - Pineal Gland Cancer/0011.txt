Always attempt an endoscopic biopsy when performing an ETV Final management is based on pathology Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy ETV is performed by fenestrating the floor of the third ventricle, thereby creating a passage between the third ventricle and the prepontine cistern. In most cases, a right frontal burr hole is made anterior to Kocher's point, and the neuroendoscope is advanced through the brain parenchyma into the ipsilateral lateral ventricle. From there, the floor of the third ventricle near the tuber cinereum is fenestrated and expanded to permit adequate CSF flow. If abnormal tissue is visualized, a biopsy is obtained from the posterior aspect of the third ventricle. Should ETV fail, definitive treatment involves placement of a ventricular shunt. The ETV success score is calculated using 3 variables—age, cause of hydrocephalus, and prior ventricular shunt placement. Each variable is assigned a success percentage, which is added to yield a total score ranging from 0% to 90% (see Table. Endoscopic Third Ventriculostomy Success Score).
