Prognosis Indicators Findings associated with a worse prognosis include local invasion, CSF seeding, and extensive peritumoral edema. These features can significantly impact survival outcomes and influence the treatment approach. Local invasion is most often observed in pineoblastomas and many germ cell tumors. The presence of local invasion reflects aggressive biological behavior and can complicate surgical resection. CSF seeding is another indicator of poor prognosis. Pineoblastomas most frequently demonstrate distal dissemination, whereas germinomas are more likely to exhibit proximal seeding. Treatment / Management Following established treatment algorithms is essential for the evaluation of pineal region tumors. These protocols ensure both accurate diagnosis and appropriate management. If hydrocephalus is absent: Obtain tumor markers in serum and CSF If positive, treatment should proceed based on the most likely pathology If negative, tissue diagnosis is required Biopsy (stereotactic, endoscopic, open) Resection (guided by radiographic or preliminary pathology findings) The confirmed pathology determines final management If hydrocephalus is present: CSF diversion should be performed, with endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV) being the preferred approach.
