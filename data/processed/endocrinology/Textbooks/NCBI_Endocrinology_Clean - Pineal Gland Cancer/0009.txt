Because the gland lacks a blood-brain barrier, it shows avid enhancement with gadolinium. Calcifications are also frequently visualized on MRI. Brain MRI shows homogeneous enhancement in pineal region tumors, except in cases with heterogeneous cellularity. Dense tumors, such as pineoblastomas and germinomas, often exhibit restricted diffusion on diffusion-weighted imaging. In contrast, pineal cysts typically lack enhancement and are characterized by a thin wall. Therefore, the presence of a thick lesion wall excludes the diagnosis of a cyst. Calcifications are more readily identified on head CT and can sometimes assist in establishing an accurate diagnosis. Pineal parenchymal tumors such as pineocytoma and pineoblastomas typically demonstrate peripheral calcifications, whereas germ cell tumors tend to encompass the calcified pineal tissue. Many tumors of the pineal region may spread locally or seed distantly. Therefore, a complete spinal MRI is often recommended as a part of the initial evaluation. Evidence of local invasion or remote dissemination carries important prognostic implications and can significantly alter both initial management and the selection of adjuvant therapy.
