Pineal Parenchymal Tumors with Intermediate Differentiation These tumors display 2 characteristic microscopic growth patterns—lobulated or diffuse. The lobulated pattern shows poorly defined lobules separated by large fibrous blood vessels. In contrast, the diffuse pattern resembles that of oligodendrogliomas or central neurocytomas, and the large pineocytomatous rosettes typical of pineocytomas are absent. Immunohistochemical staining typically demonstrates positivity for synaptophysin, variable expression of neurofilament protein, and negativity for neuronal nuclei. Papillary Tumor of the Pineal Region Papillary tumors of the pineal region exhibit a variable morphology, ranging from solid to predominantly papillary growth patterns. The histological appearance of these tumors often resembles that of ependymomas and may include ependymal rosettes. Foci of necrosis are sometimes observed within the tumor. Immunohistochemical staining typically shows positivity for cytokeratins, such as AE1/3, CAM5.2, KL1, and CK18; S100; vimentin; transthyretin; neuron-specific enolase; and microtubule-associated protein 2. Glial fibrillary acidic protein demonstrates variable expression. Pineoblastoma Pineoblastomas are composed of densely packed small, round blue cells with a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio.
