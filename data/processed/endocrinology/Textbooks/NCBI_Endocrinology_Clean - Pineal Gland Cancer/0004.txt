Pineal region tumors are generally classified into the following groups: Pineal parenchymal tumors Germ cell tumors Tumors arising from adjacent cells, such as astrocytes and meningiomas from arachnoid cap cells Metastatic tumors Histopathology The histopathology of pineal region tumors is highly heterogeneous, reflecting the diverse cellular origins within and around the gland. Tumors may arise from pineal parenchymal cells, germ cells, or adjacent structures, each with distinct morphologic and immunohistochemical profiles. Accurate histopathological classification is essential for guiding prognosis and therapeutic strategies. Pineocytoma Pineocytomas are comprised of small cells resembling normal pinealocytes, typically arranged in sheets. A characteristic histological feature is the presence of pineocytomatous pseudorosettes, which are absent in normal, healthy pineal gland tissue. Pineocytomas lack a well-formed blood-brain barrier, resulting in vivid contrast enhancement on imaging. Immunohistochemical staining demonstrates positivity for synaptophysin, neurofilament protein, and neuron-specific enolase. Other neuronal markers, including Tau protein, chromogranin-A, and 5-hydroxytryptamine, may show variable positivity, while neuronal nuclei staining is characteristically negative.
