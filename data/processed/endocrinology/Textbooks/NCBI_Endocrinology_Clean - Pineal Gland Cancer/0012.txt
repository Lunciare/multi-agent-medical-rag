Surgical resection is the mainstay for all pineal parenchymal tumors, mature teratomas, and for residual disease following chemotherapy and radiotherapy, in what is termed second-look surgery. Radiotherapy is primarily used in the treatment of germinomas but is generally reserved for patients older than 3. In younger patients, alternative approaches are preferred to minimize long-term neurocognitive effects. Chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy represents the standard for most other pineal region tumors. This approach is indicated for all nongerminomatous germ cell tumors and for germinomatous germ cell tumors in children younger than 3. Adjuvant chemotherapy with radiotherapy is also recommended for all tumors, except pineocytomas and mature teratomas, when complete resection has been achieved. The surgical approach to the pineal region is determined based on the tumor's location and the surgeon's preference. The paramedian infratentorial supracerebellar approach is considered the most flexible option for resection of the most pineal region tumors, except for large lesions that extend laterally and inferiorly.
