Pathophysiology The pineal gland develops during the seventh week of gestation. In humans, its principal function is the production of melatonin, a hormone that regulates circadian rhythms and sleep-wake cycles. The gland is also involved in regulating puberty and reproductive functions. Tumors of the pineal region are diverse in origin due to the proximity to multiple anatomical structures. The pineal gland commonly sequesters embryonic germ cells, which explains why germ cell tumors represent some of the most common pathologies in this location. Germ cell tumors typically occur sporadically.
