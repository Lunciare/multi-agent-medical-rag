CSF analysis can be performed via lumbar puncture, provided there is no obstructive hydrocephalus, or during endoscopic procedures. CSF evaluation helps diagnose many tumors of the pineal region and can provide complementary information to serum markers and imaging findings (see Image. Tumor Markers). Imaging Studies Brain MRI with and without gadolinium enhancement is the gold standard for evaluating pineal region tumors. When MRI is not available, alternative imaging modalities such as head computed tomographic (CT) scan, angiography, and, in infants, cranial ultrasound can play a role in the assessment of pineal region mass lesions. Normal neuroimaging findings of the pineal gland: CT scan: Calcification of the pineal gland is a common finding in adults but is typically absent in children younger than 5. The prevalence of this calcification peaks around the age of 30. MRI: On MRI, the pineal gland demonstrates signal intensity similar to that of gray matter and appears as a small nodule of tissue located in the posterior aspect of the third ventricle.
