This technique involves a left-sided suboccipital craniotomy, which protects the torcula and the typically more dominant right-sided veins and dural sinuses, including the transverse sinus. The midline infratentorial supracerebellar approach has traditionally been used to expose pineal region tumors. This technique is performed via a bilateral midline suboccipital craniotomy but is limited by restricted lateral and inferior visualization due to the tentorial angle and the obstructive apex of the culmen. In addition, nearly all midline bridging vermian veins must be sacrificed. The occipital transtentorial approach is beneficial for large pineal region tumors that extend laterally and inferiorly. This technique is performed through a unilateral occipital craniotomy. Limitations include challenges with anatomic orientation, the need to divide the tentorium, and the risk of homonymous hemianopia resulting from occipital lobe retraction. Screening of the entire neuroaxis is essential in all patients with pineal region tumors. Prognosis and treatment vary dramatically depending on whether CSF dissemination is present. Distant CSF seeding, except germinoma, and drop metastases are associated with poor outcomes.
