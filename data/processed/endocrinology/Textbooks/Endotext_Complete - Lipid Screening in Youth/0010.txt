Compelling data from Braamskamp et al. compared offspring with FH who were treated with statins from an early age, followed through age 30, with their parents in the first 30 years of their lives. A dramatic separation in freedom from coronary event curves was seen with a cumulative coronary event incidence of 25% in parents, while only one offspring had an event. The presumed difference between these two genetically comparable groups is the early initiation of 3-hydroxy-3-methyl-glutaryl-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins). Indeed, the one event in the offspring occurred in a youth who self-discontinued therapy. These results suggest long-term LDL-C reduction is beneficial in delaying events ( 68 ). That data has recently been extended to show protection through age 40 ( 69 ). The effect of statins in treating LDL-C levels has been examined in randomized, placebo-controlled, clinical trials of FH children and found to be safe and efficacious. RCTs in children with FH aged 8 to 17 years show that those in the statin group had regression of CIMT while those in the placebo group were stable or worsened. In young FH adults, statin use has led to a substantial reduction in coronary mortality ( 36 , 37 , 46 , 47 , 70 ). In the CHARON study, children aged 6-9, 10-13, and 14-17 treated with rosuvastatin showed reductions in LDL levels of 43%, 45%, and 35%, respectively. There were no serious adverse events related to treatment and no deleterious effects on growth or sexual maturation ( 71 ). An elegant combined meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of statin therapy compared to meta-analyzed LDL-lowering genetic mutations on ASCVD events ( 72 ).
