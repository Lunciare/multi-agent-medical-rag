Integrating these previously documented features into ongoing or future programs would be highly useful, as they may be cost-effective and provide decision support. Moving forward, additional data are needed on the broader effects of universal screening. These effects may include the number of cases of dyslipidemia detected, referrals to lifestyle modification practitioners, initiation of pharmacotherapy, and the impact of therapy on lipid levels. Further, lifestyle modification counselling creates opportunities to build relationships with families. It provides another avenue for assessing social determinants of health that may affect individuals’ ability to adhere to diet and exercise recommendations, including food insecurity, stable housing, and a safe built environment. Longer-term studies are needed to document changes in ASCVD event risk resulting from early-life modification of ASCVD risk factors. Determinants of lipid testing, dyslipidemia identification, and lipid therapy need to be determined, including at the patient, family, provider, practice, and geographic levels.
