The corpus luteum is then known as the corpus luteum graviditatis. The corpus luteum does not have this job for the remainder of the pregnancy. Instead, the placenta maintains the pregnancy through progesterone secretion, and the corpus luteum degenerates around week 12. The alternate fate of the corpus luteum occurs if the egg does not undergo fertilization. It stops secreting progesterone, decays and turns into a corpus albicans. Without progesterone maintaining the endometrium, females shed the lining, resulting in menstruation. Embryology Oogenesis occurs in the female embryo before birth but does not complete until puberty. Therefore, an embryo does not form a corpus luteum. Once a female begins ovulating and menstruating, a corpus luteum forms in the ovary once the secondary oocyte is released from the follicle during monthly ovulation.
