Drug-Drug Interactions CYP1A2 inhibitors: As melatonin is metabolized by CYP1A2, caution should be exercised when using it concurrently with potent CYP1A2 inhibitors such as fluvoxamine. Sedatives or hypnotics: Melatonin should not be combined with other drugs, including benzodiazepines, zolpidem, or eszopiclone, as this combination may result in excessive sedation. Contraindications Despite being generally well-tolerated, melatonin supplements are rarely associated with angioedema. As melatonin is a naturally produced hormone, allergic reactions can also be caused by unregulated excipients in some formulations. Clinicians should exercise caution when considering melatonin supplementation for patients with autoimmune diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis or post-organ transplant. Melatonin stimulates the function of the immune system via the production of interleukins (ILs), including IL-1, IL-2, IL-6, and IL-12, interferon-gamma, helper T cells, cytotoxic T cells, and B- and T-cell precursors. However, the clinical significance of this effect remains undefined. Monitoring Clinicians should monitor for improvements in insomnia in individuals and watch for adverse drug reactions, such as daytime sleepiness and headaches, associated with melatonin therapy when prescribing melatonin.
