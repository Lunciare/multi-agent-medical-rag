Although the discrete threshold of pituitary adenoma has not been identified yet, a tumor size larger than 6 millimeters is highly indicative of Cushing disease as the cause of Cushing syndrome. Ectopic ACTH secretion, mostly due to paraneoplastic causes, is the second-most common cause of ACTH-dependent cortical hyperplasia. ACTH-independent hypercortisolism, or adrenal-originating Cushing syndrome, may occur due to conditions including primary pigmented nodular adrenal disease (PPNAD) or ACTH-independent macronodular adrenal hyperplasia. PPNAD is categorized as with or without association with Carney complex. ACTH-independent macronodular adrenal hyperplasia presents in the late 40s, is more common in men, and is characterized by extremely enlarged adrenal glands—up to 20 to 25 times normal. Carney Complex and Adrenal Hypercorticoplasia Carney complex (CNC) is a rare genetic mutation caused by inactivating mutations of PRKAR1A (CNC1) localized on the long arm of chromosome 17.
