However, this 10 percent rule is oversimplified and incorrect as the prevalence of extra-adrenal tumors (paragangliomas) is as high as 20%, and about 20 to 30% of these tumors are hereditary . Familial syndromes associated with pheochromocytomas include multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A and 2B, neurofibromatosis (Von Recklinghausen disease), and Von Hippel-Lindau syndrome . Indications The classic triad of symptoms in patients with PCC consists of episodic headaches, diaphoresis, and tachycardia . High blood pressure is the most common sign of pheochromocytoma, though 10 to 20 percent of patients may present with normal blood pressure . High blood pressure is caused by the release of noradrenaline and adrenaline from the tumor. The release of catecholamines can be persistent or episodic; therefore, the patient may have constant hypertension or episodic hypertension. Between 10 and 20 % of patients may not have any clinical features, and the tumor is sometimes found incidentally during a workup. The prevalence of PCC in patients with hypertension is less than 0.2% .
