AIDS patients frequently develop several different types of malignancies, such as lymphoma and Kaposi sarcoma, in part due to profound destruction of host immune system by HIV ( , ). Glucocorticoids are among the central compounds for the treatment of the patients harboring these malignancies ( , ). Glucocorticoids are also pivotal for the treatment of HIV-associated nephropathy, which is observed in about 10% of AIDS patients ( ). Use of glucocorticoids is further considered for the patients with HIV-associated tuberculosis and other opportunistic infections as part of the immunoadjuvant therapy ( , ). In this chapter, we will explain known interactions between HIV infection and the HPA axis, particularly focusing on glucocorticoid hormones. We also present our understanding on some emerging concepts of such interactions, and discuss their possible mechanisms and relevance to HIV pathogenesis.
