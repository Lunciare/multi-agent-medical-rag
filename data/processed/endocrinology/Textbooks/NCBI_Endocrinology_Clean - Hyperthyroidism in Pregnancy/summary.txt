Hyperthyroidism in Pregnancy
KEYWORDS: thyroid, hyperthyroidism, pregnancy, fetal, disease, graves, graves disease, levels, treatment, trabs, used, tsh, atds, risk, hormone

Introduction Hyperthyroidism is uncommon during pregnancy. The condition is characterized by elevated levels of circulating thyroid hormones, including thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), and a decreased thyroid-stimulating hormone, thyrotropin. Though relatively rare, identification and treatment of overt hyperthyroidism are essential to mitigate maternal and fetal complications. Ideally, hyperthyroidism is diagnosed before conception, and treatment is started to achieve euthyroid status. However, up to half of all pregnancies in the United States are unplanned, making an early diagnosis of thyroid dysfunction imperative. This review examines the etiology, epidemiology, pathophysiology, and initial evaluation of hyperthyroidism during pregnancy, followed by a discussion of treatment options, management strategies, and associated complications.
