Normohormonal Primary Hyperparathyroidism Normohormonal primary hyperparathyroidism (NH-PHPT) is defined by elevated serum calcium levels with PTH concentrations that remain within the normal reference range. NH-PHPT results from autonomous parathyroid overactivity, most frequently due to a single adenoma, although multigland disease is more common in this variant than in classic hyperparathyroidism. PTH levels are described as “inappropriately normal” because they do not suppress in response to hypercalcemia. The pathophysiology of NH-PHPT is similar to that of classical PHPT, involving clonal proliferation of parathyroid cells and altered CaSR responsiveness, which leads to impaired calcium-mediated feedback inhibition. Patients typically exhibit milder biochemical abnormalities but may still develop osteoporosis, nephrolithiasis, or other end-organ complications. Epidemiology PHPT represents the most common identifiable cause of hypercalcemia in ambulatory patients, particularly among postmenopausal women. The female-to-male ratio is approximately 3:1 to 4:1, with peak incidence occurring between ages 50 and 60. In the United States, the age-adjusted prevalence is estimated at 233 per 100,000 women and 85 per 100,000 men. Incidence rates are higher among Black individuals compared to White populations, and lower among Asians and Hispanics.
