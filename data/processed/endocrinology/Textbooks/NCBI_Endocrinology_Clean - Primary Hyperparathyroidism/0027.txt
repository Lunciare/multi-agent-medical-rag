The severity of hypercalcemia influences long-term outcomes, the presence of complications, and the chosen treatment strategy. Most asymptomatic individuals remain stable for extended periods and have a normal life expectancy. In contrast, patients with moderate to severe hypercalcemia (serum calcium ≥10.8 mg/dL) or complications, eg, nephrolithiasis, osteoporosis, or fragility fractures, face a higher risk of morbidity and mortality. In these cases, surgical intervention substantially improves survival rates. Parathyroidectomy achieves a cure in more than 95% of cases when performed by experienced endocrine surgeons. This procedure results in normalization of calcium and PTH levels, enhanced BMD, and decreased incidence of kidney stones and fractures. These skeletal benefits are sustained for over a decade after successful surgery. Among patients managed conservatively, most maintain stable calcium levels and BMD; however, approximately 25% to 40% experience disease progression or develop new indications for surgery within 10 to 15 years, particularly younger patients or those with higher initial calcium concentrations. In general, the long-term prognosis is excellent for patients who receive timely diagnosis, appropriate surgical intervention when indicated, and consistent monitoring
