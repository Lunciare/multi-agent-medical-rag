Normohormonal Primary Hyperparathyroidism In NH-PHPT, patients have hypercalcemia with PTH values within the laboratory reference range. In this presentation, PTH is inappropriately normal given the degree of hypercalcemia. Evaluation follows the same principles as classic PHPT: confirm persistent hypercalcemia, rule out secondary causes (eg, medications, malignancy, vitamin D intoxication, or granulomatous disease), and assess renal and skeletal involvement. Parathyroid Crisis Bone turnover markers and neurocognitive testing are not routinely recommended. However, when PHPT presents with severe or symptomatic hypercalcemia (>14 mg/dL), urgent evaluation for parathyroid crisis is necessary. Such cases often warrant inpatient management, aggressive medical therapy, and early surgical consultation. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Resistant Hypercalcemia ," for further information. Treatment / Management Surgical intervention remains the sole definitive treatment for PHPT and is recommended for all eligible patients, irrespective of age or symptomatology, once diagnostic criteria are fulfilled. Parathyroidectomy achieves cure rates above 95% in experienced centers and improves bone mineral density, renal function, and overall quality of life.
