Primary Hyperparathyroidism
KEYWORDS: calcium, pth, hypercalcemia, parathyroid, hyperparathyroidism, phpt, patients, renal, disease, bone, elevated, levels, normal, surgical, vitamin

Introduction Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is an endocrine disorder defined by autonomous overproduction of parathyroid hormone (PTH), resulting in hypercalcemia and disrupted calcium homeostasis. The most common etiology is a single benign adenoma, while multigland hyperplasia, multiple adenomas, and parathyroid carcinoma are less frequent. Variants, eg, normocalcemic and normohormonal PHPT, represent milder biochemical manifestations of the same disease process. PHPT is the leading cause of hypercalcemia in the outpatient setting, predominantly affecting postmenopausal women. The incidence of PHPT is rising due to increased routine biochemical screening. The underlying pathophysiology involves a shift in the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) set point, causing persistent PTH secretion despite elevated serum calcium.
