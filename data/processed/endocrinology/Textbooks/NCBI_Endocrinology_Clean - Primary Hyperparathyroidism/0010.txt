The reference range for intact PTH varies by assay but is generally 10 to 65 pg/mL (1.1–6.9 pmol/L). As approximately half of serum calcium is protein-bound, total calcium should be corrected for albumin (corrected calcium = measured calcium + 0.8 × [4 – albumin]). Direct measurement of ionized calcium is preferred when albumin levels are abnormal or total calcium is borderline. In PHPT, serum phosphorus is usually low or low-normal due to PTH's phosphaturic action. After biochemical confirmation, further evaluation should assess end-organ involvement and exclude secondary causes (see Table 2 ).
