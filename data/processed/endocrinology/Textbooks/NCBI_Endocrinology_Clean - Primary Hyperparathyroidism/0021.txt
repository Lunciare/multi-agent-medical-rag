Tertiary hyperparathyroidism develops following extended secondary hyperparathyroidism, particularly in patients undergoing dialysis, when parathyroid glands begin to secrete PTH autonomously. This condition leads to hypercalcemia and multiglandular hyperplasia. The simultaneous presence of elevated PTH and hypercalcemia distinguishes tertiary hyperparathyroidism from secondary hyperparathyroidism (elevated PTH, low or normal calcium) and from primary hyperparathyroidism (elevated PTH, hypercalcemia, but without preceding renal disease). Normocalcemic Primary Hyperparathyroidism NPHPT is defined by persistently elevated PTH with normal total and ionized calcium levels, confirmed on repeated measurements. Diagnosis requires exclusion of all secondary causes, eg, vitamin D deficiency, renal insufficiency, inadequate calcium intake, hypercalciuria, magnesium deficiency, or medication effects. Patients may present with subtle skeletal or renal manifestations. Longitudinal studies demonstrate that up to 20% of cases progress to classical hypercalcemic PHPT.
