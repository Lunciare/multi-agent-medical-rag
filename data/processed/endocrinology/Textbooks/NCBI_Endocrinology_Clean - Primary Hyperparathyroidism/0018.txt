Bilateral neck exploration Bilateral neck exploration remains the gold standard for cases where localization studies are negative, inconclusive, or suggest multiglandular hyperplasia. Bilateral neck exploration is also indicated in patients with familial syndromes (eg, MEN1, MEN2A) or lithium-associated PHPT, which are associated with multigland disease. During bilateral neck exploration, all 4 parathyroid glands are identified and evaluated for enlargement or nodularity with the goal of excising all hyperfunctioning tissue while preserving at least 1 viable normal gland to prevent permanent hypoparathyroidism. Although bilateral neck exploration is more extensive than MIP, it remains a safe and curative option in experienced hands. Special surgical considerations Special consideration is required in the following clinical settings: Pregnancy : Parathyroidectomy can be performed safely in the second trimester when medically indicated. Reoperative surgery : Should be performed only by experienced endocrine surgeons and requires positive preoperative localization. Parathyroid carcinoma : En bloc resection with ipsilateral thyroid lobectomy and surrounding soft tissue is required to achieve negative margins. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Parathyroid Cancer ," for further information.
