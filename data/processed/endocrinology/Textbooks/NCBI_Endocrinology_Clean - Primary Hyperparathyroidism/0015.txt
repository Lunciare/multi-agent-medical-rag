The following localization studies help guide the choice between focused or bilateral exploration and minimize operative time: Neck ultrasonography: High-resolution neck ultrasound provides anatomical visualization of the parathyroid glands and is widely available. The sensitivity of ultrasound for identifying abnormal parathyroid glands typically ranges from 70% to 81%, with specificity between 90% and 95%. Sensitivity is reduced in cases of multiglandular disease, small adenomas, posteriorly located glands, or ectopic mediastinal lesions. Ultrasound is also operator-dependent, yields no functional information, and is less reliable for ectopic, mediastinal, or posteriorly situated glands. Technetium-99m sestamibi (MIBI) scintigraphy: MIBI scans (planar scintigraphy or SPECT/CT) assess enriched mitochondrial activity in hyperfunctioning parathyroid tissue, giving functional information. Reported sensitivity ranges from 64% to 91%, depending on the imaging protocol, gland size, and presence of multigland disease, while specificity ranges from 83% to 96%. Planar MIBI imaging alone has lower sensitivity (approximately 42%–79%). SPECT/CT, which combines functional and anatomic imaging, significantly improves localization, achieving sensitivities up to 97% and high specificity.
