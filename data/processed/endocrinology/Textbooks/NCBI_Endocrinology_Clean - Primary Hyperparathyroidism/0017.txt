A final PTH value falling into the normal range further supports biochemical cure. If the expected drop does not occur, exploration continues to identify additional hyperfunctioning glands. Routine intraoperative PTH monitoring is particularly useful in minimally invasive and reoperative surgeries, as it allows real-time decision-making and minimizes unnecessary dissection. Minimally invasive parathyroidectomy Minimally invasive parathyroidectomy (MIP) is the preferred surgical approach when preoperative imaging localizes a single adenoma. The technique uses a small incision (2–3 cm) and is explicitly directed at the abnormal gland, allowing less tissue dissection, reduced operative time, a shorter hospital stay, and a faster recovery. MIP may be performed under local, regional, or general anesthesia, depending on the patient's and surgeon's preference. Intraoperative PTH monitoring (see below) enhances success rates and confirms cure in real time. Conversion to bilateral exploration may be required if intraoperative findings suggest multiglandular disease or if intraoperative PTH levels fail to fall appropriately.
