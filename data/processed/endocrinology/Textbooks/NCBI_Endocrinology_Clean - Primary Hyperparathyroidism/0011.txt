If patients are taking biotin, lithium, or thiazide diuretics, these medications should be discontinued for several weeks before retesting to prevent laboratory interference or drug-induced alterations in calcium metabolism. Skeletal and Renal Imaging Skeletal and renal assessments are not required for diagnosis but may provide valuable adjunctive information. Dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DXA) should be performed at the lumbar spine, hip, and distal one-third radius to evaluate bone mineral density (BMD). Imaging for vertebral fractures, eg, vertebral fracture assessment or plain x-rays, may also be indicated. Renal evaluation includes 24-hour urinary calcium measurement and renal imaging, eg, ultrasound, noncontrast computed tomography (CT), or KUB x-ray, to screen for nephrolithiasis or nephrocalcinosis, which may be clinically silent in up to 20% of patients. Localizing Imaging Localization imaging is not necessary for diagnosis, but is essential for preoperative planning and should be ordered by the operating surgeon.
