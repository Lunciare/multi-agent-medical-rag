Vitamin A toxicity increases bone resorption, leading to hypercalcemia and low PTH. Both are PTH-independent causes that can be distinguished by elevated 25-hydroxyvitamin D or retinol levels. Milk-alkali syndrome : Excessive intake of calcium carbonate or calcium supplements can cause milk-alkali syndrome, characterized by hypercalcemia, metabolic alkalosis, and renal insufficiency. PTH is typically suppressed, distinguishing it from PHPT unless an underlying parathyroid disorder coexists. Granulomatous diseases (eg, sarcoidosis, tuberculosis): Extrarenal 1α-hydroxylase activity converts 25-hydroxyvitamin D to active calcitriol, stimulating calcium absorption. This leads to elevated calcium and calcitriol, suppressed PTH, and normal or elevated phosphate levels. Endocrine mimickers (hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, and rebound hypercalcemia) : Hyperthyroidism accelerates bone turnover and calcium release, causing mild hypercalcemia with low PTH. Adrenal insufficiency may cause transient hypercalcemia due to volume depletion. Rebound hypercalcemia after denosumab withdrawal, or use of PTH analogs (teriparatide, abaloparatide), can also raise calcium but are PTH-independent and typically transient.
