In advanced cases, osteitis fibrosa cystica may develop, which is characterized by fibroblast proliferation, multinucleated osteoclast-like giant cells, and brown hemosiderin deposits, known as “brown tumors.” Parathyroid carcinoma is histologically defined by capsular, vascular, or perineural invasion, a trabecular growth pattern, and increased mitotic activity. These features are often accompanied by dense fibrous bands that separate tumor lobules. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Parathyroid Cancer ," for further information. History and Physical Historically, primary hyperparathyroidism was most often diagnosed in patients presenting with nephrolithiasis, osteoporosis, or pathologic fractures. Since the 1970s, the widespread adoption of automated serum chemistry panels has shifted detection toward asymptomatic disease, with most cases now identified through incidental findings of hypercalcemia. Diagnosis currently relies on biochemical confirmation, demonstrated by elevated or inappropriately normal parathyroid hormone levels in the presence of hypercalcemia. Clinical History During medical history assessment, clinicians should evaluate for renal calculi, bone pain, myalgias or muscle weakness, neurocognitive symptoms (eg, fatigue, memory changes, or depression), gastrointestinal discomfort, and medication use, including thiazide diuretics, lithium, calcium supplements, vitamin B supplements, and vitamin D preparations.
