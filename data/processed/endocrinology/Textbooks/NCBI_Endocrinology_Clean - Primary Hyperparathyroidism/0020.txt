When both hypercalcemia and osteoporosis coexist, combination therapy with cinacalcet and an antiresorptive agent is appropriate. Patients managed medically require regular surveillance, including annual serum calcium, creatinine, or eGFR, and vitamin D testing. PTH should be repeated as clinically indicated. DXA scans of the lumbar spine, hip, and distal radius every 1 to 2 years should be used to monitor osteoporosis. Renal imaging or 24-hour urinary calcium should be obtained if nephrolithiasis is suspected. Surgery should be reconsidered if the patient develops symptoms, worsening hypercalcemia (>1 mg/dL above normal), nephrolithiasis, significant bone loss (T-score ≤ –2.5), or declining renal function. Differential Diagnosis The differential diagnosis of PHPT includes numerous disorders characterized by hypercalcemia or elevated PTH levels. Accurate diagnosis relies on a comprehensive biochemical evaluation to differentiate PTH-dependent from PTH-independent causes of hypercalcemia. Secondary and Tertiary Hyperparathyroidism Secondary hyperparathyroidism arises as a compensatory mechanism in response to chronic hypocalcemia or vitamin D deficiency.
