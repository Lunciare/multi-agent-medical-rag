severe hyperparathyroidism: Homozygous calcium-sensing receptor ( CaSR ) mutations causing life-threatening hypercalcemia in infancy Iatrogenic and Endocrine Causes Precise differentiation from true PHPT is essential to avoid unnecessary surgical intervention. Several medications and metabolic disorders can mimic or exacerbate PHPT by inducing hypercalcemia with elevated or inappropriately normal PTH levels, including: Lithium therapy : Lithium reduces CaSR sensitivity, increasing the calcium threshold required for PTH suppression. This may result in parathyroid hyperplasia and, over time, autonomous PTH secretion that biochemically resembles PHPT. In most cases, hypercalcemia resolves after discontinuation of lithium; however, persistent cases may necessitate surgical intervention. Thiazide diuretics : Thiazides enhance distal tubular calcium reabsorption, which can result in mild hypercalcemia. This effect may reveal previously unrecognized or subclinical PHPT rather than directly causing the condition. In most cases, PTH levels are normal or only slightly elevated. Biotin interference : High-dose biotin supplementation (greater than 5 mg per day) may result in falsely low PTH measurements, potentially leading to misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis of primary hyperparathyroidism. Biotin should be discontinued at least 48 hours before testing.
