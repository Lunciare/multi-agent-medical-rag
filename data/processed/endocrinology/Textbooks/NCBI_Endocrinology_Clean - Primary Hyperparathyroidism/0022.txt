Normohormonal Primary Hyperparathyroidism NH-PHPT is characterized by biochemical hypercalcemia with PTH levels that remain within the normal range but are inappropriately unsuppressed in the context of elevated calcium. In nonparathyroid causes of hypercalcemia, PTH is typically suppressed. Patients often present with single-gland disease, although the incidence of multiglandular involvement is higher than in classic PHPT. Intraoperative assessment frequently requires bilateral neck exploration, and preoperative localization is less sensitive. Despite these challenges, surgical cure rates exceed 90%. Diagnosis relies on recognizing that normal PTH levels in the setting of hypercalcemia are physiologically inappropriate. Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcemia FHH is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by inactivating mutations in the CaSR gene. These mutations result in an increased calcium set point and reduced urinary calcium excretion. Clinical features include lifelong mild hypercalcemia, low urinary calcium excretion (<100 mg/24 hours), a calcium–creatinine clearance ratio below 0.01, and normal or mildly elevated PTH levels.
