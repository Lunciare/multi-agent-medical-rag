High-resolution neck ultrasonography and technetium-99m sestamibi scans, often combined with single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), are standard first-line modalities. If these studies are inconclusive, 4-dimensional CT or F-18 fluorocholine positron emission tomography (PET)/CT may be utilized for greater sensitivity, particularly in cases of ectopic or multigland disease. Selective venous sampling is reserved for complex or reoperative cases. Additional information on localizing imaging techniques is provided below under surgical management. Preoperative and Perioperative Evaluation Laryngoscopy is recommended both before and after surgery to evaluate the function of the recurrent laryngeal nerve, particularly in reoperative cases or when voice changes are present. Baseline serum calcium and PTH levels should be measured on the day of surgery for comparison with intraoperative values. Vitamin D deficiency should be corrected preoperatively to reduce the risk of postoperative hypocalcemia or hungry bone syndrome. Normocalcemic Primary Hyperparathyroidism NPHPT is characterized by persistently elevated PTH levels with normal calcium concentrations. Diagnosis requires exclusion of all secondary causes of hyperparathyroidism, such as vitamin D deficiency, renal insufficiency (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²), calcium or magnesium deficiency, malabsorption, hypercalciuria, and specific medications (bisphosphonates, denosumab, lithium, proton pump inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors).
