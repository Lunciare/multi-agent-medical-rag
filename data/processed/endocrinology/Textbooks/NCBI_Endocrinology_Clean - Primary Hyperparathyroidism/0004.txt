Although most adenomas arise in orthotopic parathyroid glands, up to 10% are found in ectopic locations. Common ectopic sites include the thymus, thyroid gland, pericardium, retroesophageal space, and superior mediastinum. Rare sites comprise the pharynx, lateral neck, and esophagus. Normocalcemic Primary Hyperparathyroidism Normocalcemic primary hyperparathyroidism (NPHPT) is a variant of primary hyperparathyroidism defined by persistently elevated PTH levels with normal total and ionized serum calcium concentrations, confirmed by repeated measurements over at least 3 months. This disorder results from autonomous parathyroid overactivity, most often due to a single adenoma or, less frequently, multigland hyperplasia, but without sufficient PTH excess to induce overt hypercalcemia. The underlying pathophysiology mirrors that of classical hypercalcemic PHPT, involving clonal dysregulation of parathyroid cells and reduced CaSR expression. While many patients are asymptomatic, some may present with nephrolithiasis or osteoporosis. The clinical course is variable, and only a minority of cases progress to hypercalcemia over several years. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Normocalcemic Hyperparathyroidism ," for further information.
