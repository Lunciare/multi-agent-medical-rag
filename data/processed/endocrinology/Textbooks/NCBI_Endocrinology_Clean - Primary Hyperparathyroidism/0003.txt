Under normal physiology, activation of the CaSR on parathyroid chief cells suppresses PTH secretion, PTH gene expression, and cellular proliferation. In PHPT, the set point for calcium-induced inhibition of PTH release is shifted upward, resulting in higher circulating calcium levels required to suppress PTH secretion. PTH hypersecretion subsequently promotes bone resorption, renal tubular calcium reabsorption, and increased intestinal calcium absorption via enhanced renal production of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, while simultaneously reducing renal phosphate reabsorption. PHPT ranks as the third most prevalent endocrine disorder, following diabetes mellitus and thyroid disease. The etiological distribution is as follows: Single parathyroid adenoma: 80% to 85% Multigland disease (hyperplasia or multiple adenomas): 10% to 15% Parathyroid carcinoma: <1% Atypical parathyroid adenomas exhibit histologic features suggestive of malignancy but lack evidence of invasive growth and are therefore classified within the benign disease spectrum. Multigland disease occurs more frequently in hereditary syndromes, eg, multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1), multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2A (MEN2A), and CDC73 -related disorders, as well as in lithium-associated PHPT.
