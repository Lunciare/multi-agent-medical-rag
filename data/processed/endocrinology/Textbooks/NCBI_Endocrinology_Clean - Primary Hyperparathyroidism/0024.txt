Parathyromatosis Parathyromatosis is an uncommon cause of persistent or recurrent hyperparathyroidism, most frequently resulting from seeding of parathyroid tissue during surgical procedures or ablation, although it may rarely be developmental. The condition is characterized by multiple parathyroid tissue nodules embedded within fibrotic tissue, which can mimic carcinoma but lack true malignant invasion. Management is challenging; complete excision is potentially curative but technically demanding. Calcimimetics and denosumab may be used to control symptoms. Genetic Syndromes Associated with Primary Hyperparathyroidism Approximately 10% of primary hyperparathyroidism cases are genetic in origin and often involve multiple glands. Genetic testing is advised for patients younger than 30 years, those with a family history, or individuals with recurrent or multiglandular disease, including: MEN1 ( MEN1 mutation): Pituitary and pancreatic tumors; >90% develop PHPT MEN2A ( RET mutation): Medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma, and parathyroid hyperplasia Neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism: Homozygous calcium-sensing receptor ( CaSR ) mutations causing life-threatening hypercalcemia in infancy Iatrogenic and Endocrine Causes Precise differentiation from true PHPT is essential to avoid unnecessary surgical intervention.
