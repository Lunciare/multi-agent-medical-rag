Postoperative Considerations Postoperative care includes monitoring for hypocalcemia and hungry bone syndrome. Hungry bone syndrome occurs in up to 10% of cases, particularly after long-standing disease or severe hypercalcemia. Management includes high-dose oral or IV calcium and active vitamin D (calcitriol) replacement. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Hungry Bone Syndrome ," for further information. Transient hypocalcemia occurs in approximately 50% of patients and should be managed with supplementation as needed. Medical Management and Surveillance For patients with PHPT who are not surgical candidates or who elect nonoperative management, the primary goals are to control hypercalcemia, preserve bone density, and monitor for renal complications. Calcium intake should be maintained within the normal dietary range (1,000–1,200 mg/day) to prevent secondary PTH stimulation. Vitamin D deficiency should be corrected to achieve a 25-hydroxyvitamin D level >30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L), which can reduce PTH levels and mitigate bone loss. Cinacalcet, a calcimimetic that activates the calcium-sensing receptor, effectively lowers serum calcium in over 70% of patients but has minimal effect on BMD. Bisphosphonates (eg, alendronate) and denosumab can increase BMD, particularly at cortical sites, but do not normalize calcium.
