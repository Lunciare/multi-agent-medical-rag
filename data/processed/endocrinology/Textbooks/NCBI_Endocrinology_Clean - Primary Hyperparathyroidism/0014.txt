Observation and medical management are reserved for asymptomatic individuals, those with mild biochemical abnormalities, or patients deemed unsuitable for surgery due to comorbidities or elevated anesthesia risk (see Table 3 ). Surgical Management Indications for surgery Surgical intervention is indicated for all symptomatic patients, including those presenting with nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, fragility fractures, or osteoporosis. Additionally, surgery may be considered for individuals exhibiting neurocognitive or neuropsychiatric symptoms attributable to PHPT, as well as for those who prefer definitive management. Asymptomatic patients who meet any of the following criteria are also eligible for parathyroidectomy: Age <50 years Serum calcium >1.0 mg/dL (0.25 mmol/L) above the upper limit of normal Creatinine clearance <60 mL/min or eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² Bone mineral density T-score ≤–2.5 at any site (eg, lumbar spine, hip, or distal radius) Presence of vertebral fracture on imaging Renal stones or calcifications on ultrasound or CT Hypercalciuria (>250 mg/24 hours in women and >300 mg/24 hours in men) Preoperative localization Preoperative imaging is performed only for surgical planning, not for diagnosis, as biochemical testing confirms the disease.
