Sensitivity is reduced in multiglandular hyperplasia, small (<500 mg) adenomas, and cystic or normohormonal disease variants. When combined with neck ultrasound, the sensitivity for localization exceeds 90%, and in high-volume centers can reach as high as 96%, with specificity between 83% and 94%. Four-dimensional (4D) CT : This modality provides detailed anatomic and perfusion data, especially as a second-line modality after inconclusive or discordant ultrasound or sestamibi scans. It has an average sensitivity of 80% and a specificity of 84%. F-18 fluorocholine positron emission tomography (PET/CT) : PET/CT offers high sensitivity for small or ectopic adenomas and may be used in reoperative or complex cases. Large prospective and retrospective studies report sensitivity values of 93% to 99% and specificity of 78% to 91%. Intraoperative PTH monitoring Intraoperative PTH monitoring provides biochemical confirmation of surgical success and reduces the risk of persistent or recurrent disease. PTH has a half-life of 3 to 5 minutes. Therefore, a 50% or greater reduction from baseline within 10 to 20 minutes after gland excision indicates successful removal of all hypersecreting tissue.
