Malignancy-Related Hypercalcemia Malignancy is a major PTH-independent cause of hypercalcemia, affecting 20% to 30% of cancer patients. Most cases are due to the secretion of parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP) by squamous cell, renal, breast, or bladder carcinomas. Other mechanisms include osteolytic metastases and cytokine-mediated bone resorption, as seen in multiple myeloma. Laboratory findings typically show elevated calcium, suppressed PTH, increased PTHrP, and normal or elevated phosphate. Diagnosis is confirmed by identifying low intact PTH and high PTHrP levels. Please see StatPearls' companion resource, " Malignancy-Related Hypercalcemia ," for further information. Parathyroid Carcinoma Parathyroid carcinoma is a rare malignancy, representing less than 1% of primary hyperparathyroidism cases. Patients typically present with markedly elevated calcium levels (>14 mg/dL) and PTH concentrations 5 to 10 times the upper normal limit, often with neck masses, hoarseness, or recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement. Clinical severity is primarily due to severe, refractory hypercalcemia rather than local tumor invasion. Histological confirmation requires evidence of capsular or vascular invasion.
