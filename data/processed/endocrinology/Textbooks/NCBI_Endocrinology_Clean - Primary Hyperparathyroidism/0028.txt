In general, the long-term prognosis is excellent for patients who receive timely diagnosis, appropriate surgical intervention when indicated, and consistent monitoring of calcium levels, renal function, and bone health. Complications Complications of Untreated Primary Hyperparathyroidism Complications of PHPT result from chronic hypercalcemia and excessive PTH activity, impacting the skeletal, renal, neurocognitive, cardiovascular, and gastrointestinal systems. Skeletal complications are particularly significant and include progressive loss of BMD, especially at cortical sites. This loss leads to osteopenia, osteoporosis, fractures, and osteitis fibrosa cystica, which manifests as bone pain and “brown tumors.” Parathyroidectomy can reverse these skeletal effects, with BMD increasing by up to 10% within the first year and continuing to improve for more than a decade following surgery. Renal complications arise from PTH-mediated calcium mobilization and hypercalciuria, which increase the risk of nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, and chronic kidney disease. Up to 50% of symptomatic patients develop kidney stones, and 20% may progress to nephrocalcinosis without treatment. Surgical intervention significantly reduces the recurrence of kidney stones and helps stabilize renal function.
