Pregnancy in Women with Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease Women with diabetic nephropathy have poor pregnancy outcomes ( 255 ). They remain at increased risk of hypertension, preeclampsia, abnormal fetal growth, and preterm delivery ( 256 ). In a recent series, the prevalence of diabetic nephropathy and moderately elevated albuminuria in early pregnancy was similar in women with T1D or T2D, and pregnancy outcomes were comparable regardless of the type of diabetes ( 257 ). Women with any evidence of CKD therefore should be counselled pre-pregnancy. RAS inhibitors should be stopped and therapies safe in pregnancy, such as methyldopa, labetolol, and nifedipine, used as substitutes. In women with T1D, maintenance of BP <135/85 mmHg and proteinuria <300 mg/24 h with methyldopa improves outcomes ( 208 , 258 ). REFERENCES 1. Federation ID.
