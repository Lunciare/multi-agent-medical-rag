The adrenal cortex is much larger than the smaller medulla, accounting only for approximately 15% of the gland. It is composed of 3 distinct zones: Zona Glomerulosa The zona glomerulosa (outer layer) synthesizes mineralocorticoids, the most important of which is aldosterone. This hormone plays an important role in electrolyte balance and blood pressure regulation. Zona Fasciculata The zona fasciculata (middle layer) produces glucocorticoids, the predominant hormone being cortisol. This hormone regulates blood sugar via gluconeogenesis, modulates the immune system, and regulates the metabolism of fat, protein, and carbohydrates. Cortisol secretion is regulated by the adrenocorticotropic hormone, which is released from the pituitary gland. Zona Reticularis The zona reticularis (inner zone) produces androgens and plays a role in the development of secondary sexual characteristics. The primary androgen produced in the zona reticularis is dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), which is the most abundant hormone in the body. It serves as a precursor for synthesizing many other hormones the adrenal gland produces, such as progesterone, estrogen, cortisol, and testosterone.
