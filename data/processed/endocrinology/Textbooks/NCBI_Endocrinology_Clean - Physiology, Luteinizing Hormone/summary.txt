Physiology, Luteinizing Hormone
KEYWORDS: levels, ovulation, testosterone, art, gnrh, patients, hcg, development, increase, cells, fsh, secretion, women, hormone, pituitary

Introduction Luteinizing hormone (LH) is a glycoprotein hormone that is co-secreted along with follicle-stimulating hormone by the gonadotrophin cells in the adenohypophysis (anterior pituitary). Luteinizing hormone is a part of a neurological pathway comprised of the hypothalamus, the pituitary gland, and the gonads (see Image. Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal Axis). In this pathway, LH release is stimulated by gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) and inhibited by estrogen in females and testosterone in males. LH has various functions, which differ between women and men. In both sexes, LH contributes to the maturation of primordial germ cells.
