GnRH itself undergoes regulation by multiple neurotransmitters like dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, glutamate, opiate, and galanin. Kisspeptin is a crucial regulator of GnRH; it is an important GnRH secretagogue encoded by the KISS1 gene. Gonadal steroids, estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone exert negative feedback, thus decreasing the secretion of LH. Function In males, LH stimulates testosterone release by the Leydig cells of the testes. In females, LH stimulates steroid release from the ovaries, ovulation, and the release of progesterone after ovulation by the corpus luteum. Ovulation Ovulation is made possible by the combined actions of the hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovary. The hypothalamus begins the ovulation process by releasing GnRH in a pulsatile fashion. This pulsatile release causes the anterior pituitary to release LH and FSH, which then act on the ovarian follicle. This follicle comprises 3 essential cells: theca, granulosa, and oocyte. LH causes the theca cells to make androstenedione. Androstenedione then converts to estradiol via aromatase, which FSH stimulates.
