In agreement with this notion, activating mutations of the TSHR have been identified in hyperfunctioning areas of multinodular goiter (70,19,65,23). The independent occurrence of two activating mutations in a patient may seem highly improbable at first. However, the multiplicity of the possible targets for activating mutations within the TSHR makes this less unlikely. It is also possible that a mutagenic environment is created in glands exposed to a chronic stimulation by TSH, resulting in H2O2 generation (71,72). Finally the involvement of TSHR mutations in thyroid cancers has been implicated in a limited number of follicular thyroid carcinoma (73-81).
