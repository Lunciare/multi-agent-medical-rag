Update on Pancreatic Transplantation in the Management of Diabetes
KEYWORDS: transplantation, pancreas, pubmed, diabetes, patients, transplant, diabetic, pancreas transplantation, kidney, ptx, type, recipients, renal, spk, graft

ABSTRACT Pancreas transplantation is the most effective therapeutic option that can restore insulin independence in beta-cell penic recipients with diabetes. Because of life-long immunosuppression and the initial surgical risk, pancreas transplantation is a therapeutic option only in selected patients with diabetes. Based on renal function, candidates for pancreas transplantation can be classified into three categories: uremic patients, post-uremic patients (following a successful kidney transplantation), and non-uremic patients. Uremic patients are best treated by a simultaneous kidney-pancreas transplantation. Post-uremic patients can receive a pancreas after kidney transplantation. Non-uremic patients can receive a pancreas transplant alone, if diabetes is poorly controlled resulting in hypoglycemia unawareness, and in the presence of evolving chronic complications of diabetes.
