Patients with bilateral or idiopathic hyperaldosteronism are typically treated medically with spironolactone or eplerenone to control blood pressure. However, they tend to experience higher long-term rates of cardiovascular events compared to other hypertensive patients, as elevated serum aldosterone levels are associated with increased risk. This has prompted investigations into alternative therapies for cases where surgery is not feasible. Additional medications Other medications, particularly amiloride and triamterene—potassium-sparing diuretic antihypertensives—help lower blood pressure and increase serum potassium levels. These medications help maintain normal serum potassium levels and also allow a reduction of the required dosage of the mineralocorticoid antagonist drug. They are also the recommended medical therapy for patients who do not tolerate spironolactone or eplerenone. Amiloride is generally preferred, as triamterene may increase the risk of urinary calculi formation. Please see StatPearls' companion resources, " Amiloride " and " Triamterene ," for more information. Patients with primary hyperaldosteronism are sensitive to salt intake and typically experience a more pronounced beneficial response to dietary sodium restriction compared to other hypertensive patients.
