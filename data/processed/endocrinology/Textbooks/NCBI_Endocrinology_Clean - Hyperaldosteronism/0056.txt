Untreated hyperaldosteronism is linked to substantial morbidity and mortality, primarily due to uncontrolled hypertension, stroke, congestive heart failure, and cardiac arrhythmias. Additional risks include coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, increased proteinuria, chronic kidney disease, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and osteoporosis secondary to hypercalciuria. Complications Myocardial fibrosis has also been observed in patients with long-standing hyperaldosteronism. Secondary adrenal insufficiency requiring glucocorticoid supplementation may develop in 30% to 50% of patients following unilateral adrenalectomy, particularly in those with mild autonomous cortisol secretion before surgery. Some patients may experience severe hyperkalemia immediately following unilateral adrenalectomy due to prolonged suppression of the adrenal zona glomerulosa and insufficient aldosterone production from the remaining adrenal gland. Residual or recurrent hyperaldosteronism may occur after adrenalectomy surgery due to many factors.
