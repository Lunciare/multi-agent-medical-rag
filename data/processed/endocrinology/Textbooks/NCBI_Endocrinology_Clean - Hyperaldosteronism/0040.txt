If primary hyperaldosteronism is diagnosed and confirmed, the next step is to differentiate between unilateral disease, which is optimally treated surgically, and bilateral disease, which is best managed medically. Unilateral disease is traditionally confirmed with bilateral selective adrenal venous sampling; however, CT scans and other imaging modalities may also be used and can be sufficient for lateralization in selected cases. Successful surgical outcomes depend on accurate lateralization. Secondary hyperaldosteronism, characterized by elevated serum renin levels, is best treated by eliminating the underlying etiology and using appropriate medical therapy. Treatment / Management Surgical Management of Unilateral Primary Hyperaldosteronism Total adrenalectomy surgery This is the preferred treatment of choice for unilateral primary hyperaldosteronism. Minimally invasive techniques, such as laparoscopic or robotic approaches, are preferred over partial adrenalectomy, as they offer better symptom resolution and treatment efficacy. Complete adrenalectomy is preferred to partial gland removal due to greater efficacy and resolution of symptoms. Notably, primary hyperaldosteronism is the most common surgically curable cause of hypertension. Preoperative spironolactone is recommended for 4 to 6 weeks to help control blood pressure.
