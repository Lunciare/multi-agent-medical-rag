However, it carries a significant risk of incomplete control of hyperaldosteronism due to residual abnormal aldosterone-secreting tissue. Total adrenalectomy remains the preferred approach in most cases, as partial adrenal resections require technically challenging selective segmental adrenal venous sampling and are associated with lower rates of postoperative biochemical success and blood pressure normalization, with limited supporting data currently available. However, several studies suggest that partial adrenalectomy may be appropriate in selected cases of unilateral surgical hyperaldosteronism and can yield satisfactory outcomes. Percutaneous and transcatheter ablation of unilateral adrenal adenomas In a randomized controlled trial, 27% of patients achieved a complete response, 54% had a partial response, and over half showed biochemical success. These new procedures are currently recommended only for patients who are unsuitable for surgery or long-term medication or who decline these options. Their long-term outcomes compared to standard surgery or extended medical therapy remain unclear.
