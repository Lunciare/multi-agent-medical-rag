Similarly, adrenal vein sampling is unnecessary in patients aged 40 or older who have marked hyperaldosteronism and a clearly defined solitary, unilateral adrenal adenoma on CT imaging. This is also not required in individuals who are not surgical candidates, those with suspected adrenal cancer, or patients with known familial hyperaldosteronism. Adrenal vein sampling remains the gold standard for differentiating unilateral from bilateral adrenal hyperaldosteronism. This technique should ideally be performed in all potential adrenalectomy patients to avoid inappropriate procedures and to identify the 40% of patients with primary hyperaldosterone who would benefit from surgery but might otherwise be missed based on imaging studies alone. However, expertise in this technically demanding procedure is often limited, and it may be unnecessary in clearly defined surgical cases. (The technique is best reviewed in detail elsewhere. ) The adrenal veins are relatively small, making the interpretation of the results difficult. The procedure is expensive, carries a significant risk of complications, and many institutions lack experience in this challenging technique.
