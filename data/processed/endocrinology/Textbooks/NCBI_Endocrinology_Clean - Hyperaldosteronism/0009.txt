In newly diagnosed hypertensive patients, primary hyperaldosteronism is found in approximately 11,200 per 100,000 individuals. Earlier studies significantly underestimated the incidence of hyperaldosteronism due to variations in patient selection, diagnostic methods, definitions of both hyperaldosteronism and hypertension, and a general lack of screening in patients who met the recommended screening criteria. Black individuals may exhibit increased sensitivity to aldosterone compared to the general population, which can result in hyperaldosteronism even without significantly elevated plasma aldosterone levels. The reported prevalence largely depends on the incidence of screening tests performed and the criteria used to determine a positive result warranting further investigation. In a study, lowering the threshold aldosterone-to-renin ratio from above 30 to above 20 increased the detection rates of hyperaldosteronism from 13.8% to nearly 33%. Tertiary care centers that treat patients with refractory hypertension have reported that almost half of their referrals could potentially have primary hyperaldosteronism. Secondary Hyperaldosteronism This condition is diagnosed less frequently than primary hyperaldosteronism. Both forms are more prevalent in women.
