However, HBS has been found to have a sensitivity of 38% and specificity of 89% for sphincter of Oddi dysfunction, performing poorly compared to manometry. FMU involves performing focussed abdominal ultrasound before and after ingestion of a standardized fatty meal which stimulates CCK and should demonstrate a dynamic change in the diameter of the common bile duct in the setting of obstruction secondary to sphincter of Oddi dysfunction. Compared to SOM, the sensitivity of FMU is 21%, and its specificity is 97%. Thus there may be a role for FMU in patient selection for sphincterotomy in the Type II SOD group, where treatment choice can cause a dilemma.
