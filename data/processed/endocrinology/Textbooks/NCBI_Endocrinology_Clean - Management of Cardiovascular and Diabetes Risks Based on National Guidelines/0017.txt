In patients with peripheral arterial disease, aspirin or clopidogrel use is recommended to prevent the risk of cardiovascular death, especially in symptomatic PAD (Class I indication). In asymptomatic PAD (ABI ≤0.9), aspirin/clopidogrel use is recommended as a class IIa indication. There is no added benefit of dual antiplatelet agents in symptomatic or asymptomatic PAD. However, dual antiplatelet agents might be reasonable in patients with symptomatic PAD who underwent revascularization (Class IIb indication). All of the above patients should receive statin therapy. Hyperlipidemia When discussing hyperlipidemia, the values of LDL-C and triglycerides matter the most, not the total cholesterol levels. Statins are always the first-line treatment for dyslipidemia because they confer the highest cardiovascular protection. Statins are classified as low intensity (<30% reduction of LDL from baseline), moderate-intensity (30 to 49%), and high intensity (>50%). Only two commercially available statins are high-intensity statins, atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg and rosuvastatin 20-40mg. The addition of ezetimibe, bile acid sequestrant, or PCSK-9 inhibitors to statins reduces LDL-C values by a further 15%, 25%, and 50%, respectively.
