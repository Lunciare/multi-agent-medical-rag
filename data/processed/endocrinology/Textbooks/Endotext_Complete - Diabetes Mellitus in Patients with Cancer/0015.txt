MANAGEMENT RECOMMENDATIONS Despite the effects of hyper- and hypoglycemia in people with diabetes (PWD) and those without known diabetes in cancer, there is a sparsity of guidance on the specific management considerations of these individuals. To address this, collaborative guidelines have recently been produced by the UK Chemotherapy Board (UKCB) and Joint British Diabetes Society for Inpatient Care (JBDS) ( 131 , 132 ). The scope of these guidelines are to provide advice for the oncology/hemato-oncology and diabetes multidisciplinary teams to manage people with diabetes, commencing anti-cancer/ steroid therapy, as well as identifying individuals without a known diagnosis of diabetes who are at risk of developing hyperglycemia and new onset diabetes. These guidelines are intended for the outpatient management of people with cancer, particularly in the setting of the oncology/hemato-oncology clinic, and provision of advice for individuals at home, but where necessary, may be applied to inpatients as well. Whilst covering these guidelines in detail is beyond the scope of this chapter, key management considerations are summarized in tables 4 - 9 . Table 4. At Baseline View in own window 1) HbA 1c and venous plasma glucose should be checked in all people with cancer at baseline clinic visit 2) Provide high risk individuals with capillary blood glucose (CBG) meter and glucose testing strips, or if baseline plasma glucose is ≥12 mmol/L (216 mg/dl) 3) Individuals with raised baseline HbA 1c (>47 mmol/mol [6.5%]) should be referred to primary care for management of hyperglycemia prior to next follow up visit 4) When initiating SACT/glucocorticoids individuals must be informed of the risk of developing hyperglycemia/diabetes and potential symptoms to expect 5) The recommended glucose target level is 6.0-10.0 mmol/L (108 – 180 mg/dl), allowing a range of 6.0-12.0 mmol/L (108 – 216 mg/dl) 6) There are differences in opinion at where the threshold for intervention should be drawn - 12.0 mmol/L (216 mg/dl) is a pragmatic threshold Table 5. Commencing Glucocorticoids (GC) /Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy View in own window 1) Check baseline HbA 1c and random venous plasma glucose before starting therapy 2) Monitor random plasma glucose at each treatment visit 3) Educate patients in symptoms of hyperglycemia 4) Consider commencing gliclazide 40mg if raised blood glucose ≥12mmol/L (216 mg/dl) on two occasions 5) Gliclazide may require frequent and significant increases in dose to reduce glucose levels, particularly on high dose steroids 6) Inform diabetes care provider if persistently raised
