Subacute Thyroiditis
KEYWORDS: thyroiditis, thyroid, subacute, subacute thyroiditis, elevated, graves, patients, painful, usually, pfsat, sat, low, table, disease, diagnosis

CLINICAL RECOGNITION Subacute thyroiditis (SAT) is an inflammatory condition of the thyroid with characteristic presentations and clinical course. Patients with the classic, painful (DeQuervain’s; Granulomatous) thyroiditis, (PFSAT) typically present with painful swelling of the thyroid. Transient vocal cord paresis may occur. At times, the pain begins and may be confined to the one lobe, but usually spreads rapidly to involve the rest of the gland ("creeping thyroiditis"). Pain may radiate to the jaw or the ears. Malaise, fatigue, myalgia and arthralgia are common.
