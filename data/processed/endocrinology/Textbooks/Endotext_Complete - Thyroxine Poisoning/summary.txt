Thyroxine Poisoning
KEYWORDS: ingestion, levels, children, thyroid, tablets, serum, charcoal, probably, thyroxine, symptoms, total, lt-, activated, l-thyroxine, gastric

CLINICAL RECOGNITION A massive L-Thyroxine (T4) overdose may be accidentally and unintentionally ingested, most commonly by children and adolescents. It may occur intentionally in young and older adults in an attempt to lose weight, with suicidal intentions, or for undeclared purposes. In some localities thyroxine may be obtained at drugstores without prescription (mostly in the generic form). In some reports thyroxine preparations by a pharmacist had an erroneous LT4 dosage. Thyroid hormone pills used to treat hypothyroid dogs typically contains a much higher dose of thyroid hormone and if mistakenly taken by humans can lead to thyroxine poisoning. Ingested thyroxine, which is itself probably of modest physiologic significance, is rapidly partially converted to triiodothyronine (T3), the active form of thyroid hormone.
