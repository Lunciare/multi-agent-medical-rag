Circumportal pancreas is an annular variant that occurs when pancreatic tissue envelops the portal vein or its immediate tributaries, the superior mesenteric and splenic veins. This anomaly is believed to arise when the developing uncinate process (of the ventral bud) fuses with pancreatic body tissue (of the dorsal bud), thereby entrapping the portal venous confluence. The annular segment classification is by its relation to the splenic vein—suprasplenic, infrasplenic, or combined subtypes, with a further description describing whether the main pancreatic duct courses: anteportal or retroportal. Circumportal pancreas significantly increases the technical difficulty of pancreatic head resection. Moreover, this anatomic variant is often an intraoperative discovery not revealed by preoperative imaging.
