For instance, a baseline assessment of renal function, as indicated by blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, and electrolyte levels, may be helpful in symptomatic patients or those who have consumed a significantly large amount (ie, greater than 6 grams in an adult or greater than 400 mg/kg in a child) of the drug. If a patient presents with bleeding likely induced by NSAID use, a complete blood count will allow for monitoring of hemoglobin and platelet counts. An arterial blood gas can help determine the acid-base status of a patient if there is concern for massive ingestion, and an electrocardiogram can provide evidence of QT interval prolongation in the setting of potential coingestant consumption that may result in adverse effects on the cardiac conduction system. Last, clinicians must always consider other possible etiologies of altered mental status and may order tests to assess for differential diagnoses such as hypoglycemia. By conducting these targeted laboratory assessments, healthcare providers can more effectively identify and manage the multifaceted effects of NSAID toxicity, thereby ensuring comprehensive patient care.
