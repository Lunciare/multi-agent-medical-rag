Interestingly, men who did not participate in resistance training also gained muscle mass, albeit at a slower rate than those with resistance training and testosterone supplementation. The effects of testosterone supplementation have shown to be dose-dependent and associated with changes in muscle pennation angle. Fascicle length and muscle pennation angle increase is needed to increase muscle surface area and the subsequent rise of the force of contraction. Interestingly, low levels of testosterone in geriatric males correlate with an increased risk of atrial fibrillation (AF). Low levels of testosterone also correlate to coronary artery disease. However, it is not clear whether coronary artery disease (CAD) preceded low testosterone levels or followed; this is an inverse relationship between CAD and testosterone levels, suggesting that testosterone supplementation may decrease the severity of CAD.
