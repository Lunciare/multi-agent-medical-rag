A study of 42 AAS users and 50 nonuser controls was performed to see how AAS affects periodontal health. Both participant groups were 19 to 40 years old. Periodontitis was present in a more significant percentage of AAS users (32%) vs. controls (16.7%). Dental plaque was also present more frequently in AAS users than controls. Clinical attachment loss was seen almost 2.4 times more often in AAS users than in control nonusers. Researchers also examined the microbiota in AAS users vs. controls. Infection with P. intermedia was 4.9 times more likely in AAS users vs non-users. Similarly, AAS users were 3.5 times more likely to be infected with A actinomycetemcomitans than the control group. Finally, fungal cultures showed that AAS users were more likely to have Candida pocketing than nonusers. Histopathology Myocyte histopathological changes include testosterone and other AAS-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation. They, in turn, induce cell apoptosis. Testosterone also increased Bax (apoptosis promoter) to Bcl-2 (apoptosis inhibitor) ratio.
