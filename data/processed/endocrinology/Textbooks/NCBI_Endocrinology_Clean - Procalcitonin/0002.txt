PCT should not be used as the sole determinant for antimicrobial therapy. Pathophysiology Under normal homeostasis, pre-procalcitonin undergoes initial synthesis by thyroid C cells. Later this peptide is transformed into procalcitonin via cleavage of a 25-amino acid signal sequence by endopeptidases. The end product calcitonin, the 32-amino acid hormone responsible for serum calcium regulation, is formed following conversion by the enzyme prohormone convertase. Typically, physiological conditions result in very low serum PCT levels of less than 0.05 ng/mL. However, the synthesis of PCT can be increased up to 100 to 1000 fold due to circulating endotoxins or cytokines such as interleukin (IL)- 6, tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-alpha, and IL-1b, which act on various tissues. The extra-thyroid synthesis of PCT occurs in the liver, pancreas, kidney, lung, intestine, and leukocytes; notably, the synthesis of PCT is suppressed within these tissues in the absence of bacterial infection. In contrast, cytokines released following viral infection, such as interferon (INF)-gamma, will lead to the down-regulation of PCT, thus highlighting another advantage of PCT assays.
