Dopamine from the hypothalamus suppresses prolactin secretion. Histopathology Corticotroph and lactotroph carcinomas represent the most common subtypes of pituitary carcinoma. Some carcinomas resemble benign pituitary tumors and lack distinct pathologic indicators, eg, cellular atypia or dedifferentiation. No single pathologic marker has been currently found to define pituitary gland malignancy. The concept of “high-risk adenomas” has been introduced to describe tumors demonstrating rapid growth, an elevated Ki67 index, and radiologic evidence of invasion. Numerous ongoing and completed studies continue to explore the molecular mechanisms of underlying pituitary tumor behavior, although validation of reliable molecular markers remains limited. History and Physical Clinical Features The clinical presentation of pituitary tumors divides into functional (secreting) and nonfunctional (nonsecretory) categories. Functional tumors typically appear at a younger age because of symptoms linked to excessive hormone production and physiologic effects of hypersecretion. Nonfunctional tumors, unless incidentally discovered on imaging that is performed for unrelated reasons, often remain undetected until they enlarge enough to cause neurologic deficits from compression of adjacent structures.
