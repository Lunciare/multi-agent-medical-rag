Evaluation Laboratory Tests Laboratory evaluation relies on both blood and urine testing. Selection of specific hormonal tests depends on the patient’s symptoms, although broad hormonal panels are often ordered for incidental or nonfunctioning pituitary tumors. Endocrinologic assessment primarily focuses on excluding hypersecretory tumors. Commonly tested hormones include prolactin and insulin-like growth factor. Hyposecretory adenomas cause reduced secretion of 1 or more pituitary hormones and require a comprehensive hormonal profile that includes TSH, FSH, LH, free T4, total testosterone in men, and early morning cortisol levels. Evaluation of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism involves magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain at diagnosis to detect pituitary tumors as the possible cause. Stimulatory tests are then performed to confirm hormonal deficiencies, with protocols tailored to the specific hormone involved. Imaging A sellar MRI is the preferred imaging modality for assessing pituitary gland pathology. Tumors appear as macroadenomas when their diameter exceeds 1 cm and as microadenomas when they are smaller than 1 cm (see Image . Pituitary Macroadenoma on Head Computed Tomography).
