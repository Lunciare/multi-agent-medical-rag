Interestingly, “Satchmo” also represents the well-known nickname of the legendary jazz musician Louis Armstrong, providing an easy association to aid memory when evaluating pituitary lesions. The following differential diagnoses are associated with the mnemonic: S: Sarcoidosis, sellar tumor (pituitary adenoma, pituitary carcinoma) A: Aneurysm (typically superior hypophyseal) T: Teratoma, tuberculosis (other granulomatous diseases) C: Craniopharyngioma, chordoma, cleft cyst (Rathke) H: Hypothalamic glioma, hamartoma, histiocytosis M: Meningioma, metastasis O: Optic nerve glioma Prognosis The overall prognosis of benign pituitary tumors remains fair in terms of mortality compared to other neurohormonal tumors. The 5-year survival rate is approximately 82%. The prognosis is much less favorable for pituitary carcinoma. The mean survival time for pituitary carcinoma is less than 4 years once metastases have been identified. Complications Blindness represents one of the most devastating complications of pituitary tumors. Tumor extension into the optic chiasm or optic nerves can lead to partial or complete vision loss, and even tumors initially lacking visual symptoms may progress rapidly to cause blindness.
