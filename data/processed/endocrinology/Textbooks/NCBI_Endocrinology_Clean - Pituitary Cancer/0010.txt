Antisecretory Medications Managing hormone hypersecretion remains essential in pituitary carcinoma, paralleling treatment strategies for functional pituitary tumors. High-dose dopamine agonists (eg, cabergoline) can be used for lactotroph tumors. In hypercortisolism, steroidogenesis inhibitors (eg, metyrapone or ketoconazole) help reduce excess cortisol production and prevent associated morbidity and mortality. For somatotrophic tumors, therapy may involve somatostatin analogs (octreotide), growth hormone receptor antagonists (pegvisomant), or dopamine agonists administered at maximally tolerated doses to achieve hormonal control. Future Therapies Emerging therapeutic approaches under investigation include immunotherapy and molecularly targeted agents. Potential candidates include bevacizumab, an anti-VEGF antibody; everolimus, an mTOR inhibitor; and several tyrosine kinase inhibitors. These agents represent promising directions for future management strategies in patients with aggressive or treatment-resistant pituitary carcinoma.
