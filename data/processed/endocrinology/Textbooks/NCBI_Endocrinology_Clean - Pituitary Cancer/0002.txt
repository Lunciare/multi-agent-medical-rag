Ongoing research continues to examine the molecular mechanisms that influence pituitary tumor behavior. Transcriptional and posttranscriptional regulation through methylation, histone modification, and altered expression of long noncoding RNAs or specific microRNAs have demonstrated correlations with malignant potential in several studies. Tumors harboring specific germline mutations, including MEN1, AIP , and GPR101 , tend to exhibit greater invasiveness and resistance to treatment. Certain hereditary disorders also increase the likelihood of pituitary tumor formation. Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) syndrome frequently accompanies pituitary adenomas, highlighting a genetic predisposition. No environmental or lifestyle factors have shown a definitive link to the pathogenesis of pituitary tumors, leaving no established preventive measures at the current time. Epidemiology The prevalence of pituitary carcinoma is extremely low and estimated at 1/1,000,000, which is less than 0.01% of all detected anterior pituitary gland tumors. Pituitary carcinomas usually originate from functioning pituitary gland tumors and are primarily represented by lactotroph and corticotroph carcinomas, with each type accounting for around one-third of cases.
