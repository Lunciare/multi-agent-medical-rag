Conformal radiotherapy may be employed to achieve antitumor and antisecretory effects, although available data primarily involve aggressive, rapidly growing tumors and pituitary carcinoma. Case reports describe attempts to use 2 different radiation modalities for refractory cases, but outcomes remain suboptimal. Chemotherapy Temozolomide, an oral alkylating agent, induces deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) methylation, leading to irreversible DNA damage and tumor cell death. This drug represents the recommended first-line chemotherapeutic option for pituitary carcinoma. Clinical studies demonstrate improved progression-free and overall 5-year survival among responders. Despite its use as standard therapy, complete radiologic response occurs in only 6% of patients, partial response in 31% (tumor reduction >30%), stable disease in 33% (tumor size change <10%), and progressive disease in 30% (tumor growth >10% or new metastasis noted). Superior responses have been observed in 3 specific situations: concurrent radiotherapy, clinically functioning tumors, and tumors showing low O6-Methylguanine-DNA Methyltransferase (MGMT) expression. For tumors progressing during or after temozolomide therapy, no proven alternative treatment exists, emphasizing the need to optimize and extend temozolomide efficacy.
