Computed tomography angiography of the head can aid preoperative planning by defining relationships with nearby vascular structures, eg, the internal carotid artery. In the absence of histopathologic evidence of malignancy, a diagnosis of pituitary carcinoma depends on radiographic confirmation of systemic or craniospinal metastasis, typically identified on spinal MRI when clinically suspected. Treatment / Management Surgery Approximately 80.5% of patients with pituitary carcinoma undergo at least 2 surgical procedures, and 27.9% require 4 or more surgeries. Surgical debulking provides clinical benefit when tumor growth produces mass effect on surrounding structures, eg, the optic pathways, third ventricle, or, in advanced cases, the brainstem. Reducing tumor burden can alleviate symptoms and improve outcomes when combined with other modalities in a multimodal treatment plan. Radiotherapy About 42.9% of patients with pituitary carcinoma receive at least 2 courses of radiotherapy. The selection of the optimal technique requires collaboration with specialists in radiotherapy and radiosurgery, with individualized planning for each patient. Conformal radiotherapy may be employed to achieve antitumor and antisecretory effects, although available data primarily involve aggressive, rapidly growing tumors and pituitary carcinoma.
