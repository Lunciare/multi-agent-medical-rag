Prolactin: Excess prolactin may lead to impotence in men, while in women it can cause amenorrhea-galactorrhea syndrome. Growth hormone: Overproduction results in gigantism in prepubertal children and acromegaly in adults. Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH): Overproduction leads to Cushing’s disease, characterized by hypertension, purple striae, decreased libido, skin atrophy with easy bruising, depression, muscle wasting, and weight gain with centripetal fat distribution, supraclavicular fat pads, a rounded face, and slender extremities. Thyrotropin (TSH): Hypersecretion causes secondary hyperthyroidism, producing symptoms such as sweating, heat intolerance, palpitations, increased anxiety, diarrhea, and weight loss. FSH and LH: Symptoms of hormone deficiency Compression of normal pituitary tissue by large tumors can lead to hormone deficiencies. The sequence of hormone loss, from most to least sensitive, follows the order below: growth hormone first, gonadotropins (FSH and LH) next, then TSH, ACTH, and last the prolactin—remembered by the mnemonic G o L ook F or T he A denoma P lease. Pituitary apoplexy may develop from rapid tumor enlargement due to necrosis or hemorrhage, characteristically producing sudden headache, neurologic impairment, and endocrine dysfunction. Unlike typical pituitary tumors, pituitary apoplexy more often presents with ophthalmoplegia rather than visual pathway deficits.
