Macroadenomas may induce diabetes insipidus by disrupting vasopressin secretion, leading to excessive thirst and polyuria. Pituitary apoplexy, characterized by hemorrhage into the gland, constitutes a medical emergency and typically presents with sudden-onset severe headache, ophthalmoplegia, and visual disturbances. Patients with recurrent disease often require multiple surgeries, contributing to a higher incidence of surgical complications, including hydrocephalus that necessitates ventricular shunt placement. To minimize serious complications, surgical intervention should be performed by neurosurgeons with extensive experience managing pituitary tumors. Rare postradiotherapy cases of sellar fibrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma have been documented, typically developing after an average of 10.5 years. Debate continues regarding whether radiotherapy contributes to the malignant transformation of benign pituitary adenomas. However, a large cohort study of 641 patients followed for 8 years found no statistically significant increase in malignant transformation compared to the general population. Perioperative complications may also include infection, eg, meningitis or pituitary abscess, CSF leak, and unintentional entry into the cavernous sinus with possible damage to its contained structures.
