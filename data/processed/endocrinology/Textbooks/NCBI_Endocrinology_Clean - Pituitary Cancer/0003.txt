Furthermore, the median latency period from the time of diagnosis to the occurrence of the first metastasis is approximately 5 to 7.5 years. A change from a nonfunctioning pituitary tumor to a functioning tumor has been documented in cases where pituitary adenomas have progressed to pituitary carcinomas. Pathophysiology The pathophysiology of pituitary cancer involves genetic mechanisms, although most causative mutations remain unidentified and continue to undergo active investigation. Ongoing research aims to define the associations between specific gene mutations and tumor development. The anterior pituitary gland synthesizes 6 primary hormones: luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), prolactin, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and growth hormone. The posterior pituitary gland stores and releases oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone. Regulation of pituitary hormone secretion depends on hypothalamic control. Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus governs the release of LH and FSH. Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) stimulates ACTH secretion, while thyrotropin-releasing hormone triggers TSH release. Growth hormone secretion responds to stimulation by growth hormone–releasing hormone and inhibition by somatostatin. Dopamine from the hypothalamus suppresses prolactin secretion.
