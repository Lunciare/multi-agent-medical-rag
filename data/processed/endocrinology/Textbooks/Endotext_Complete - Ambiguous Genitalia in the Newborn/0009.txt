Accurate molecular diagnosis aids in managing affected individuals and provides families with information concerning prognosis and the risk of recurrence. This information is often sourced from literature and comprehensive data registries, such as the I-DSD ( 18 - 24 ). For clinical purposes, DSD (including ambiguous genitalia in newborns) is typically classified based on the affected individual's karyotype. The consensus statement addressing the approach and care of DSD (originally published in 2006 and updated in 2016) suggests the following broad classification for DSD: (A) Sex Chromosome DSD, (B) 46,XY DSD, and (C) 46,XX DSD ( Table 3 ) ( 1 , 20 ).
