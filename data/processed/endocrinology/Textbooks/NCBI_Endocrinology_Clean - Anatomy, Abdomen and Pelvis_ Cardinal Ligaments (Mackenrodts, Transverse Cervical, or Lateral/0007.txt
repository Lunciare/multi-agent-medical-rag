Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Cardinal Ligaments (Mackenrodts, Transverse Cervical, or Lateral Cervical Ligaments)
KEYWORDS: ligament, cardinal, cardinal ligament, section, pelvic, support, ureter, ligaments, cervical, uterus, average, tissue, lateral, connective, fibers

a distal section of the cardinal ligament along with the CUSC. The presence of ureter and other neural and vascular structures make the intermediate section unsuitable for surgical use. During a vaginal hysterectomy, the traction and cutting of the cardinal ligaments leads to the movement of the ureter out of the operative field. This action guards the ureter against possible iatrogenic injury. After a hysterectomy, suspension of the vaginal vault to the CUSC provides surgical support of the vaginal vault and may reduce the chances of pelvic prolapse. Clinical Significance Lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes in cardinal ligaments may be involved in cervical cancer, even up to the most lateral region near the pelvic sidewall. Therefore, in this condition, extensive resection of the cardinal ligament is a must to ensure a disease-free state.
