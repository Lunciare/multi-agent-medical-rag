The cardinal ligaments of women with a prolapsed uterus showed a higher expression of collagen III and tenascin and lower quantities of elastin. Altered connective tissue distribution in the cardinal ligaments with fewer and thinner collagen fibers has been reported in women with prolapse of the pelvic organ. Functions In the standing position, the cardinal ligament has a vertical orientation, while the uterosacral ligaments are dorsally oriented, and together they provide apical support for the uterus and vagina. It gives hammock-like support to the uterus that holds the cervix in position and prevents its downward displacement through the vagina. Embryology The lateral cervical ligament appears during the tenth week of intrauterine life. Surgical Considerations The cardinal ligament is one of the pedicles the surgeon must secure during a hysterectomy. Due to the absence of basic neurovascular structure, the first pedicle of transvaginal hysterectomy involves a distal section of the cardinal ligament along with the CUSC.
