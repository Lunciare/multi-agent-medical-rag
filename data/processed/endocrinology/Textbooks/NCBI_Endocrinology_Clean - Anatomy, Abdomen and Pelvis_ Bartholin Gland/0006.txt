Bartholin Gland Abscess Bartholin gland abscesses may result from either an infected cyst or a primary gland infection. These lesions typically present with severe pain and swelling, making sitting, walking, and sexual intercourse difficult. Other presenting signs and symptoms include acute, painful, unilateral vulvar swelling, erythema and edema surrounding a fluctuant vulvar mass, and sudden symptom relief following spontaneous mass discharge or rupture. Pyrexia is not a common feature in otherwise healthy patients. Results from a study by Kessous et al describe the most common microbial pathogens associated with Bartholin abscesses. Escherichia coli was the most commonly found pathogen (43.6%), followed by Staphylococcus a ureus (6.4%), group B Streptococci (4.8%), and Enterococcus spp (4.8%). Less than 10% of cases were polymicrobial in origin. E coli -positive cultures were more common in recurrent infections (56.8%) than in primary infections (37%).
