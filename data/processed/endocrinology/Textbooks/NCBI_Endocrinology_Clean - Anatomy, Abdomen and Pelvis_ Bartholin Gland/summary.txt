Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Bartholin Gland
KEYWORDS: bartholin, gland, cyst, bartholin gland, glands, vulvar, cysts, bartholin glands, abscess, primary, abscesses, carcinoma, incision, drainage, pain

Introduction The Bartholin glands, also known as the greater vestibular glands, are important organs of the female reproductive system. Danish anatomist Caspar Bartholin Secundus first described these structures in 1677. The glands primarily produce a mucoid secretion that aids vaginal and vulvar lubrication. The Bartholin glands are located in the vulvar vestibule on either side of the vaginal introitus (see Image. These structures are homologous to the bulbourethral (Cowper) glands in men. Bartholin gland pathology may present as an asymptomatic mass, causing only vulvar asymmetry.
