Neurotensinomas
KEYWORDS: neurotensin, bloom, tumors, syndrome, hypotension, plasma, features, clinical, pancreatic, levels, secretion, cases, peptide, patients, endocrinol

ABSTRACT Neurotensin (NT) is a 13 amino acid polypeptide first extracted from bovine brain subsequently isolated from the human GI tract. Neurotensin has a number of interesting pharmacologic effects that include hypotension, tachycardia, and cyanosis and stimulation of secretion from the small intestine. It also has been reported to inhibit the interdigestive myoelectric complex and stimulate insulin release. NT also increases venous vascular permeability, raises blood glucose, and lowers blood pressure. High concentrations of NT-like immunoreactivity (NTLI) are localized to a specific “N” cell in the jejunum. Plasma concentrations rise after food ingestion, and high circulating levels have been found after surgery for duodenal ulcer and jejunoileal bypass for obesity.
