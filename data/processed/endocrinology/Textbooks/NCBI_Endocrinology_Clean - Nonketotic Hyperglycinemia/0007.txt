These include: Organic acidurias Pyridoxine dependent epilepsy Disorders of intracellular cobalamin metabolism GLYT1 encephalopathy Lipoate deficiency Prognosis The prognosis depends on the amount of glycine cleavage enzyme system activity. Severe deficiency in this enzyme results in the presentation of the disease during the neonatal period, which is associated with a poor prognosis due to life-threatening apnea. In neonates where spontaneous breathing is reestablished, these patients may develop progressive lethargy, encephalopathy, or death. Other patients might be marred by a lifetime of severe developmental impairment and intractable epilepsy. Patients who present later in life (3 months or later) are more likely to have attenuated glycine cleavage enzyme activity.
