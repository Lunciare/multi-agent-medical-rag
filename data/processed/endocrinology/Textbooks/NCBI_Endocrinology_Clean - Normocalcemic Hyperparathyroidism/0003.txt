In CKD, reduced vitamin D activation leads to decreased intestinal absorption of calcium. Lower levels of serum calcium then induce a secondary increase in the secretion of parathyroid hormone. Treatment of hyperparathyroidism in CKD can include vitamin D supplements, sufficient to maintain a 25-hydroxyvitamin D level of at least 30 ng/mL, calcitriol (ie, activated vitamin D), phosphate binders, and calcimimetics to reduce PTH secretion. Parathyroid surgery is sometimes necessary in patients with severe or refractory disease. Other causes of SHPT include older anticonvulsants (eg, phenytoin and phenobarbital), celiac disease, and medications including bisphosphonates, denosumab, diuretics, and lithium. Drug-related SHPT can be verified by stopping the drug for several months and rechecking the PTH level. If a drug elevated the PTH level, then levels should normalize when the medication is withheld. If PTH remains elevated, then another cause is responsible. In 1988, Rao et al first proposed a biphasic progression of hyperparathyroidism.
