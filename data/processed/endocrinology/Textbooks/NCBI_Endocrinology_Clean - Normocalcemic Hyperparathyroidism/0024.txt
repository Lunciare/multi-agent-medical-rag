Vitamin D should be supplemented if the 25-hydroxyvitamin D is <30 ng/mL based on studies showing that vitamin D can lower blood parathyroid hormone and improve bone mineral density without significantly increasing serum or urinary calcium levels. Operative Management Parathyroid surgery is the only definitive treatment for normocalcemic or hypercalcemic PHPT. Minimally invasive surgical techniques have been developed for this procedure. No known medical treatments can fully reproduce the benefits of a successful parathyroid surgery. Some studies have shown bone mineral density (BMD) improvement after parathyroidectomy. The recommendations and indications for parathyroid surgery by the expert panel on Asymptomatic Primary Hyperparathyroidism, including patients aged younger than 50 years, osteoporosis, creatinine clearance <60 ml/min, urinary calcium >300 mg/24 hours in men and >250 mg/24 hours in women, and nephrolithiasis, can be equally applied to NHPT. Although the risk for recurrent renal calculi is not eliminated, the risk is substantially decreased, and renal function is preserved. The recurrence rate of nephrolithiasis and nephrocalcinosis can be further reduced by performing a 24-hour urine test for kidney stone prevention and optimizing any remaining risk factors.
