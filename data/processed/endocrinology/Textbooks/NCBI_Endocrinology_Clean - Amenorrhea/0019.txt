Interpretation of Diagnostic Study Findings A positive urine pregnancy test indicates a pregnancy, an abnormal TSH indicates thyroid disease, and an elevated prolactin identifies hyperprolactinemia. The combination of low FSH and estradiol levels identifies hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and indicates that the abnormality affects hypothalamic or pituitary function. The most common causes of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism are CDGP in primary amenorrhea and FHA in secondary amenorrhea. In patients with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and other abnormalities in nonhormonal serum testing (eg, abnormal renal or liver function tests), further evaluation for chronic disease is warranted. The combination of elevated FSH and low estradiol levels identifies hypergonadotropic hypogonadism, indicating the abnormality affects ovarian function.
