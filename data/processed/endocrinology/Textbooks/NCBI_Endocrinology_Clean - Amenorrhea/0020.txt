Patients with PCOS typically have elevated serum testosterone levels and evidence of polycystic ovaries appearing on ultrasound. Other serum tests differentiate PCOS from other less common causes of hyperandrogenemia: an elevated fasting 17-OH-P suggests NCCAH, while an elevated DHEAS suggests an adrenal androgen-secreting tumor. A pelvic ultrasound can detect many Müllerian anomalies, including Müllerian agenesis. If the patient has an outflow tract obstruction, such as an imperforate hymen, transverse vaginal septum, or cervical or vaginal agenesis, the uterus can appear distended with heterogeneous material representing the retained menstrual fluid. Patients lacking estrogen exposure, such as those with FHA, POI, and natural menopause, often will have a thin endometrial stripe. Treatment / Management The treatment for amenorrhea is based on correcting the underlying etiology and monitoring the patient for complications. Outflow Tract Abnormalities Patients with some types of outflow obstructions (eg, imperforate hymen, cervical stenosis) can be treated with a surgical correction to relieve the obstruction.
