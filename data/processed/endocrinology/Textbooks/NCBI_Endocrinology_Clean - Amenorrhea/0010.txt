Although the typical age of the menopausal transition is in the early 50s, many women can experience this change in their 40s, which is still considered to be within normal limits. Medication-induced amenorrhea can result from several drugs (eg, chemotherapy, illicit substances). Since synthetic progestins, found in hormonal contraceptives, cause endometrial atrophy, prolonged use can lead to secondary amenorrhea, even during a typical hormone-free interval. Some medications (eg, antipsychotics) may also cause hyperprolactinemia resulting in amenorrhea. Epidemiology In the US, only about 2% of adolescent girls have not reached menarche by age 15 years. The most common causes of primary amenorrhea are gonadal dysfunction in 43% of patients, müllerian agenesis in about 10% to 15%, and CDGP in about 14% of patients. Abnormal karyotypes are found in approximately 50% of patients with primary amenorrhea and no associated comorbidities. The prevalence of secondary amenorrhea not caused by physiologic changes is approximately 3% to 4% of reproductive females. The most common causes of secondary amenorrhea include PCOS, FHA, hyperprolactinemia, and POI.
