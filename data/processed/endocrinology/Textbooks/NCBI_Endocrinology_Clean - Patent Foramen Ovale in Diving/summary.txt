Patent Foramen Ovale in Diving
KEYWORDS: decompression, foramen, ovale, foramen ovale, patent, patent foramen, sickness, decompression sickness, divers, diving, gas, diver, pressure, closure, lungs

Introduction Patent foramen ovale (PFO), also known as persistent foramen ovale, is a condition in which the foramen ovale, a naturally occurring opening in the atrial septum of the developing fetus, fails to close after birth. Among individuals who participate in aquatic diving, this condition has been associated with severe, sudden-onset neurological decompression sickness, inner ear decompression sickness, and cutis marmorata. Etiology During fetal development, the foramen ovale enables blood to flow from the right atrium to the left atrium, bypassing the fetal lungs. This opening typically closes within a few years after birth. If it fails to close, venous blood will continue to be shunted into the left heart without passing through the lungs. The shunt is not clinically significant in most individuals.
