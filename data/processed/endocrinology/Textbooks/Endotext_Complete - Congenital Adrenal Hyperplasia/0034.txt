Crinecerfont (Crinessity, Neurocrine Biosciences, San Diego, CA USA) was tested versus placebo in prospective, randomized trials while study subjects were maintained on standard treatment with glucocorticoids (hydrocortisone or predniso(lo)ne) and mineralocorticoids. Both primary and secondary endpoints were met. In the pediatric trial, 69 participants receiving crinecerfont had lower mean morning serum androstenedione levels at 4 weeks (p<0.001 versus 34 in the placebo group) reflecting improved androgen control while being able to decrease glucocorticoid doses by an average of 18% at 28 weeks. In contrast, the placebo group increased glucocorticoid doses by 5.6% ( 175 ). Similarly, the larger adult trial (n=182) showed 97% retention at week 24, and week 4 mean serum androstenedione levels decreased by 345 ng/dl (p<0.001 versus placebo group). Physiologic glucocorticoid dosing was achieved with androstenedione control in 63% of the crinecerfont group at week 24 ( 176 ) . Phase 4 clinical trials are being initiated to determine long-term efficacy and safety for CAH patients. Other novel agents such as atumelnant (an MC2R antagonist) and Lu AG13909 (an anti-ACTH monoclonal antibody) are being trialed to balance adrenal hormone perturbations in CAH. Gene therapy for CAH has not yet been implemented successfully in humans. CONCLUSION The pathophysiology of CAH involves autosomal recessive inheritance of pathogenic variants in any one of several genes encoding enzymes for adrenal steroidogenesis. Clinical presentations and hormonal profiles are specific for each type of CAH. Treatment aims to replace the insufficient adrenal hormones and to suppress precursors produced in excess. Individualized hormone replacement therapy and careful medical monitoring permits normal growth and development. Beyond standard oral glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement new treatment strategies continue to be developed and studied to improve outcomes for CAH patients. REFERENCES 1.
