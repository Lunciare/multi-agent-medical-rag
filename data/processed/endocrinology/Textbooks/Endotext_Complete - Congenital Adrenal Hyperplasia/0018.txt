The severity improves with age, attributed in part to higher dietary salt intake in older children and adults, and in part to improved mineralocorticoid sensitivity of the kidney ( 82 ). Aldosterone synthase deficiency was historically divided into two subtypes termed corticosterone methyl-oxidase, deficiency types I and II, differing only in that a steroid intermediate, 18-hydroxycorticosterone, is elevated in type II deficiency, but low in type I deficiency( 81 ). The distinction is not clinically important. Glucocorticoid Remediable Aldosteronism Glucocorticoid- remediable aldosteronism (glucocorticoid- or dexamethasone-suppressible hyperaldosteronism, familial hyperaldosteronism type I) is an autosomal dominant form of low-renin hypertension in which hyperaldosteronism is rapidly suppressed by glucocorticoid administration ( 83 , 84 ). This unusual effect of glucocorticoids suggests that aldosterone secretion in this disorder is regulated by ACTH instead of the renin-angiotensin system. In addition to abnormally regulated secretion of aldosterone, there is marked overproduction of 18-hydroxycortisol and 18-oxocortisol, steroids that require both 17-hydroxylase (CYP17A1) activity, which is expressed only in the zona fasciculata, and aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2) activity, which is normally expressed only in the zona glomerulosa. Together, these features imply that CYP11B2 is being regulated in a manner similar to the closely related enzyme steroid 11-hydroxylase (CYP11B1). The disorder is caused by unequal meiotic crossing-over events between the CYP11B1 and CYP11B2 genes ( 84 - 86 ) (see below) GENETICS There are 2 CYP11B genes located at chromosome 8q24.3 in a tandem duplication reminiscent of that of the 21-hydroxylase ( CYP21A ) genes. If the genes are pictured as being transcribed from left to right, CYP11B2 (encoding aldosterone synthase) is on the left, and CYP11B1 (steroid 11β-hydroxylase) on the right. The encoded proteins are 95% identical; a very few amino acids, particularly positions 288 in exon 5 (serine in CYP11B1, glycine in CYP11B2) and 320 in exon 6 (valine in CYP11B1, alanine in CYP11B2) are responsible for the extra enzymatic activities in CYP11B2 ( 87 , 88 ). Inactivating variants in CYP11B1 cause 11β-hydroxylase deficiency. A stereotypical mutation, p.Arg448His (R448H) is found in Jews of North African origin, in whom 11OHD occurs relatively frequently.
