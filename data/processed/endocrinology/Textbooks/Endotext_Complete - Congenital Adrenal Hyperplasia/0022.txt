Older children may present with normal serum electrolytes and elevated plasma renin, and some cases present in adulthood ( 99 , 101 , 102 ). Males have normal external genitalia but may have azoospermia and hypergonadotropic hypogonadism. Females have irregular menses, and ovarian ultrasound in 46,XX females reveals multilocular cysts ( 102 , 103 ). CYP11A1 Deficiency Deficiency of cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme (CYP11A1) resembles lipoid CAH but affected individuals do not show adrenal enlargement or lipid accumulation. At least 70 patients with CYP11A1 mutations have been described ( 104 , 105 ), most of whom had milder, ‘non-classic’ disease with late-onset adrenal insufficiency and normal 46,XY male genital development, similar to non-classic lipoid CAH ( 106 , 107 ). Severe pathogenic variants of CYP11A1 are thought to be lethal because placental progesterone synthesis should be impaired, interfering with maintenance of a normal pregnancy. Laboratory Findings and Diagnosis of Lipoid CAH and CYP11A1 Deficiency Concentrations of all adrenal steroids are low or absent and basal concentrations of ACTH and plasma renin are elevated. In patients with lipoid CAH, grossly enlarged adrenal glands may be visible on ultrasound or MRI and are laden with cholesterol and cholesterol esters. Genetics Lipoid CAH is caused by mutations in the STAR gene on 8p11.23 ( 91 , 93 , 108 ). It is the second-most common form of CAH in Japan and Korea, where about one in 300 people carries the nonsense mutation Glu258Stop (Q258X), so that one in every 250,000 to 300,000 newborns is affected ( 109 ). CYP11A1 deficiency is especially common in Eastern Turkey, where the homozygous mutant Arg451Trp (R451W) appears to be due to a founder effect ( 110 ). DISORDERS OF ACCESSORY PROTEINS Cytochrome P450 Oxidoreductase Deficiency (PORD) PATHOPHYSIOLOGY P450 Oxidoreductase Deficiency (PORD) is a rare form of CAH that presents with signs and symptoms resembling 21-hydroxylase deficiency, 17-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase deficiency, or a combination of these enzyme deficiencies ( 111 - 113 ). The exact prevalence of this condition is unknown; a systematic review in 2022 found 119 published cases ( 114 ). As discussed above, POR is a flavoprotein that transfers electrons from NADPH to all microsomal P450 enzymes, including CYP17A1 (17-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase), CYP21A2 (21-hydroxylase) and CYP19A1 (aromatase), as well as hepatic drug-metabolizing P450 enzymes ( 115 ).
