GENETICS PORD is caused by pathogenic variants in the P450 oxidoreductase ( POR) gene located at chromosome 7q11.2. Known pathogenic variants retain some enzyme activity, and homozygous null variants are believed to be incompatible with life ( 121 ). Two POR mutations predominate: p.Ala287Pro (A287P) among patients of European ancestry ( 112 , 122 ), and p.Arg457His (R457H) among patients of Japanese ancestry ( 113 , 123 , 124 ), yielding somewhat different clinical findings. POR A287P reduces activity of CYP17A1 more than it impairs CYP21A2 activity. Although this biochemical profile might be expected to affect male sexual development more than female development, homozygosity for p.A287P is, in fact, associated with 46,XX virilized genitalia and normal 46,XY genitalia ( 122 ). R457H disrupts CYP19A1 (aromatase) activity but A287P does not ( 125 ). Thus, pregnant women carrying a fetus with the POR mutation R457H (but not POR A287P) may experience virilization during pregnancy from the alternative (backdoor) androgen pathway and/or from ( 112 , 113 ), as occurs in women carrying fetuses with CYP19A1 deficiency ( 126 ). Skeletal abnormalities characteristic of the Antley-Bixler syndrome are most commonly found in patients with p.R457H or p.A287P on one allele, and a null variant on the other ( 113 ) whereas mutations retaining ∼20% to 40% of activity may cause disturbed steroidogenesis without Antley Bixler syndrome. The polymorphic variant p.Ala503Val (A503V), which mildly affects many P450 activities, is normally found in ~28% of POR alleles, varying from ~19% in African Americans to ~37% in Chinese Americans ( 127 ). Ferredoxin and Ferredoxin Reductase Deficiencies As previously discussed, mitochondrial P450 enzymes like CYP11A1, CYP11B1 and CYP11B2 require 2 accessory electron transport proteins: ferredoxin reductase (FDXR) accepts pairs of electrons from NADPH and transfers them singly to ferredoxin, a small iron-sulfur protein, which in turn transfers them to the P450 ( 128 , 129 ). FDXR mutations impair formation of iron/sulfur centers and cause iron overload and mitochondriopathy (also known as Auditory Neuropathy and Optic Atrophy (ANOA) syndrome); two reviews summarize 73 cases ( 130 , 131 ).
