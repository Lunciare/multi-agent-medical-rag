Adapted from ( 1 ). Over 90% of mutations causing 21OHD are the result of either of two types of recombination events between CYP21A2 and CYP21A1P. The first results from misalignment and unequal meiotic crossing over, resulting in large-scale (30 kb) DNA deletions (~22% of the total) ( 54 ). The functioning of the remaining chimeric CYP21A gene varies depending on the breakpoint of the crossover. Breakpoints originating in STK19 or C4A lead to a net deletion of 1 of the C4 genes and CYP21A1P but leave CYP21A2 unaffected. This occurs on at least 5% of normal chromosomes. Breakpoints originating in CYP21A1P yield a deletion of C4B and a single chimeric CYP21A gene that has a 5′ end corresponding to CYP21A1P and a 3′ end corresponding to CYP21A2. If the breakpoint is in or before intron 2, the resulting chimera encodes an active or partially active enzyme with decreased expression, and functions as an allele for mild, non-classic CAH ( 55 ). This may be referred to as an attenuated CAH allele ( 56 ). If the breakpoint is at or after the 3’ end of intron 2, the chimeric gene includes CYP21A1P mutations that prevent synthesis of a functional protein, so that it represents a null allele and therefore is usually referred to as a CYP21A2 deletion. Occasionally a breakpoint occurs in the TNX genes, leading to complete deletion of C4B and CYP21A2 , and a TNXB/TNXA chimeric gene. These are referred to as CAH-X alleles, and may comprise as many at 5% of classic 21OHD alleles ( 56 ) associated with hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (EDS), which is presumed to be dominantly expressed. Thus, EDS may be detected in CAH patients carrying a CAH-X allele if the CYP21A2 gene on the other chromosome also carries a pathogenic variant.
