Affected females are born with normal external genitalia but may be recognized at puberty when they fail to develop secondary sex characteristics or when they have primary amenorrhea( 141 ). Both 46,XY and 46,XX patients fail to undergo normal pubertal development and have sparse pubic and body hair. Partial forms of this enzyme defect permit aromatization of androgens to estrogens, resulting in some degree of breast development. Affected males may have low testosterone levels with mildly elevated gonadotropins and/or oligospermia. Male gynecomastia may develop in this setting. Females with partial 17OHD have irregular menses, decreased fertility, and cystic ovaries on ultrasound ( 142 , 143 ). LABORATORY FINDINGS The diagnosis of 17OHD depends on finding elevated precursor-to-product ratios with cosyntropin stimulation. Elevated serum levels of deoxycorticosterone (DOC, >100 ng/dL [>3 nmol/L]) and corticosterone (>4000 ng/dL [>116 nmol/L]) with low cortisol (<5 mcg/dL [<138 nmol/L]) and low in all sex steroids are characteristic. Progesterone is also elevated, whereas renin is suppressed ( 144 ). Gonadotropins and corticotropin (ACTH) are elevated indicating primary gonadal and adrenal insufficiency, even in childhood. A characteristic feature of selective lyase deficiency is an elevated ratio of 17-OHP/androstenedione (>50), with low levels of sex steroids. As with other types of CAH, genotyping may clarify the diagnosis when hormone measurements are inconclusive. CYTOCHROME B5 (CYB5) DEFICIENCY Cytochrome b5 is a small, 14 kDa heme-containing electron transfer protein that is principally involved in the reduction of methemoglobin, but it also plays a key role in facilitating the 17,20 lyase activity of CYP17A1 ( 145 , 146 ). CYB5 is expressed in the zona reticularis and increases with the onset of adrenarche ( 147 ). In patients with CYB5 deficiency, 17-hydroxylase activity and cortisol synthesis are normal, but 17,20 lyase activity, and the synthesis of adrenal and gonadal androgen precursors, are impaired, causing under-virilization of 46,XY fetuses, often associated with elevated methemoglobin levels but without clinical methemoglobinemia ( 148 , 149 ). GENETICS 17OHD is caused by pathogenic variants in the CYP17A1 gene located on chromosome 10q24.3. Over 180 pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants have been identified; however, populations in which 17OHD is prevalent have specific common variants as a consequence of founder effects. Recurring pathogenic variants have been found in Brazil (p.W406R (Trp406Arg) and p.R362C (Arg362Cys)) ( 150 ) and China (c.985_987delTACinsAA, also known as p.Y329LfsX90, Y329fs, or p.Y329Kfs*90)( 151 ).
