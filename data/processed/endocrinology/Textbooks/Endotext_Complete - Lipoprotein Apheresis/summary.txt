Lipoprotein Apheresis
KEYWORDS: patients, levels, apheresis, ldl-c, pubmed, cardiovascular, group, events, cardiovascular events, ldl-c levels, coronary, treatment, study, decrease, studies

ABSTRACT Lipoprotein apheresis (LA) involves the physical removal of lipoproteins from the blood and is employed only in patients where lifestyle and pharmacologic treatment is not capable of decreasing lipoproteins to acceptable levels. There are a number of different guidelines for the use of LA. The FDA has approved the use of LA for patients with homozygous or heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) and LDL-C levels >300mg/dL, patients with heterozygous FH with LDL-C > 100mg/dL with coronary artery disease or peripheral artery disease, or patients with FH who have LDL-C ≥ 100 mg/dL and established CAD or PAD and an Lp(a) above a threshold (commonly ~ 60 mg/dL). In other countries the guidelines are more liberal. Lipoproteins may be removed from the circulation by precipitation, adsorption, or filtration. A number of different systems are currently available for LA (heparin precipitation, binding to polyacrylate anions or dextran sulfate, filters that remove lipoproteins based on size, and columns containing antibodies to apolipoprotein B or lipoprotein (a)).
