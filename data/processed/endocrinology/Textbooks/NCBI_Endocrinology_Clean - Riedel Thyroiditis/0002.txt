Etiology The etiology of Riedel thyroiditis is not definitively known. Various theories have suggested that it could be a variant of autoimmune thyroiditis or part of a systemic fibrosing disorder, or an immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) related disease. The presence of anti-thyroid antibodies, eosinophilic infiltrate, and response to glucocorticoid therapy are suggestive of autoimmune pathology. However, the lack of other antibodies, association with other autoimmune disorders, normal lymphocyte counts, and complement levels suggest otherwise. Riedel thyroiditis has been known to be associated with orbital fibrosis, primary sclerosing cholangitis, and mediastinal and retroperitoneal fibrosis, giving rise to the suspicion that it may be a part of a multifocal idiopathic fibrosclerosing disorder. However, the lack of a consistent association of Riedel thyroiditis with other fibrosing disorders does not support this cause. Epidemiology Out of the disorders that cause thyroiditis, Riedel thyroiditis is extremely rare compared to Hashimoto thyroiditis or subacute granulomatous thyroiditis.
