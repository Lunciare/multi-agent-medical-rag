Other rarer causes include congenital anomalies such as pancreatic divisum, infections, radiation therapy, and ischemic insults leading to pancreatic tissue necrosis and fistulization. Epidemiology Pancreatic fistulas, particularly postoperative pancreatic fistulas (POPFs), are a significant complication in patients having pancreatic surgery. Using the International Study Group for Pancreatic Surgery criteria, the rates of POPFs range from approximately 17% to 39% following pancreatic surgery, making them the most frequent harmful complication of these procedures. The incidence varies based on factors such as surgical technique, pancreatic tissue quality, and patient comorbidities. A systematic review of 40 studies found the rate of clinically relevant pancreatic fistulas to be 22% to 26% in patients after a Whipple procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy). The overall incidence of pancreatic fistulas ranges from 5% to 30% following pancreaticoduodenectomy and 30% to 40% after distal pancreatectomy, with soft pancreatic tissue and altered pancreatic anatomy being significant risk factors.
