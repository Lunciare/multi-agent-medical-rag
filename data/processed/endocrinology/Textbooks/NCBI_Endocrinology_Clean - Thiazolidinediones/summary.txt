Thiazolidinediones
KEYWORDS: tzds, patients, insulin, risk, increased, tzd, heart, ppar-gamma, liver, edema, therapy, weight, cancer, pioglitazone, bladder cancer

Indications Thiazolidinediones (TZDs), also called "glitazones," in managing type 2 diabetes can help with glycemic control and insulin resistance. There are 2 TZDs, rosiglitazone and pioglitazone, currently approved by the FDA as monotherapy or combined with metformin or sulfonylureas to manage type 2 diabetes mellitus. These medications should be combined with lifestyle modifications such as diet, exercise, and weight reduction. TZDs may also be used to treat polycystic ovarian syndrome, as these may lead to improved endothelial function, improved ovulation, and reduced insulin resistance. Pioglitazone specifically reduces hepatic fat and may improve liver fibrosis in patients with nonalcoholic steatohepatitis; however, additional variables and risks require assessment in nonalcoholic steatohepatitis patients. The most significant advantage of TZDs is that they do not cause hypoglycemia as monotherapy and are not contraindicated in patients with renal disease.
