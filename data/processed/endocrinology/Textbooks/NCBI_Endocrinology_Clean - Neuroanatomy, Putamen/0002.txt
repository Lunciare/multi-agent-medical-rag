Traditionally, the basal ganglia structures are known for their motor functions. However, it is well established that the basal ganglia are not only involved in purely motor functions but also associated with more complex goal-directed behaviors, including components of emotion, motivation, and cognition that contribute to the expression of a particular movement. The putamen is also involved in modulating both sensory and motor aspects of pain. Embryology From cephalic to caudal, the primary 3 primitive brain vesicles differentiate into the prosencephalon (forebrain), mesencephalon (midbrain), and rhombencephalon (hindbrain). By the end of the fifth week of gestation, the prosencephalon differentiates into the telencephalon (cerebral hemispheres) and the diencephalon (see Image. Horizontal Section of the Right Cerebral Hemisphere). The telencephalon gives rise to most of the components of the basal ganglia, which include the caudate and the putamen. The globus pallidus originates from neuroblasts in the wall of the 3rd ventricle of the diencephalon.
