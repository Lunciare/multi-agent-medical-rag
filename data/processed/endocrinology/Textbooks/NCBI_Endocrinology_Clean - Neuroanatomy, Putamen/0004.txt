The 3 primary afferent input sources to the striatum are the cerebral cortex, the thalamus, and, primarily, dopaminergic cells of the brain stem. Accordingly, outputs from the striatum travel to the pallidum complex and the substantia nigra, pars reticulata, and pars compacta. Physiologic Variants Volumetric changes of the putamen have been linked to different neurologic and psychiatric disorders. Researchers have conducted large imaging meta-analyses to establish the physiologic variation of the putamen, making it comparable to its pathologic condition and enabling determination of its physiological state. Almost all studies have shown that the volume of the putamen declines in both genders with aging. However, the effects of gender and hemispheric asymmetry remain controversial. Surgical Considerations The putamen, a common structure affected by hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage, exhibits a wide range of presentations depending on the magnitude of the initial blood extravasation. Despite the controversy, surgical evacuation of the intracerebral hemorrhage is a mainstay therapeutic approach to decompress the mass effect and also eliminate the cytotoxic edema resulting from the ischemia and the degraded blood products.
