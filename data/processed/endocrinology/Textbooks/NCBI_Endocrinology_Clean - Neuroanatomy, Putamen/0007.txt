The presence of a conjugate eye deviation (CED) has been regarded as a poor prognostic marker in these bleeds. The putamen correlates with a broad spectrum of movement disorders and psychiatric diseases. The most well-known movement disorder related to the putamen is Parkinson disease, which is the result of dopamine depletion in the posterior putamen and presents with rigidity, tremor, ataxia, and impairment of balance. Changes in putamen volume are associated with a wide range of diseases. While some disorders increase the volume of the putamen, including bipolar disorder, Tourette syndrome, attention-deficit-hyperactivity disorder, researchers have seen a decline in volume in major depressive disorder, Williams syndrome, autism, schizophrenia, and suicide attempters. Interestingly, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) prevents putaminal volume loss associated with normal aging.
