The effects of angiotensin II are mediated by 2 receptor subtypes, type 1 and type 2, which elicit distinct and often opposing physiological responses. Angiotensin II t ype 1 receptor The angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1-R) is a G-protein-coupled receptor. This receptor is widely expressed in multiple cell types, including the heart, vasculature, kidneys, adrenal glands, pituitary, and central nervous system. Angiotensin II mediates vasoconstriction and sodium and water reabsorption through AT1-R activation. Pathological activation of AT1-R promotes inflammation, fibrosis, oxidative stress, tissue remodeling, and elevated blood pressure. Dysregulation of AT1-R is central to the pathophysiology of cardiac and renal diseases. Angiotensin II type 2 receptor The angiotensin II type 2 receptor (AT2-R) is a G-protein-coupled receptor predominantly expressed in fetal tissues, with expression declining in adulthood. In adult tissues, AT2-R is present in the heart, kidneys, adrenal glands, and brain. AT2-R mediates effects opposing those of AT1-R, providing protective actions that inhibit inflammation, fibrosis, and central sympathetic outflow while promoting vasodilation. Activation of AT2-R by angiotensin II induces vasodilation and natriuresis, counteracting the vasoconstriction and anti-natriuretic effects mediated by AT1-R.
