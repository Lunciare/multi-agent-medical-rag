Introduction The renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) functions as a primary regulator of blood volume, electrolyte homeostasis, and systemic vascular resistance. Short-term decreases in arterial pressure elicit responses via the baroreceptor reflex, whereas the RAAS mediates both acute and chronic adjustments. Classical RAAS comprises 3 principal components: renin, angiotensin II, and aldosterone. These components increase arterial pressure in response to reduced renal perfusion, decreased salt delivery to the distal convoluted tubule, and β-adrenergic stimulation. Recent research has expanded the understanding of RAAS through the identification of additional system components over the past decades.
