Physiology, Renin Angiotensin System
KEYWORDS: angiotensin, renal, raas, renin, aldosterone, cardiovascular, receptor, effects, type, sodium, heart, hypertension, patients, outcomes, heart failure

Renal benefits include reduced microalbuminuria and slower progression of kidney disease, including in patients with type 2 diabetes. Unlike ACEIs, ARBs do not affect bradykinin metabolism, which mitigates the risk of cough. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists Spironolactone, eplerenone, and finerenone improve outcomes in patients with a history of heart failure by inhibiting myocardial fibrosis. Spironolactone and eplerenone reduce hospitalizations and mortality in patients with heart failure with both preserved and reduced ejection fraction. Finerenone decreases hospitalizations due to heart failure and improves renal outcomes in patients with diabetic kidney disease. These medications serve as 1st-line therapy for medically managed cases of primary hyperaldosteronism. Aldosterone Synthase Blocker Baxdrostat, a selective aldosterone synthase inhibitor, demonstrates promising results in patients with resistant hypertension, producing dose-dependent reductions in blood pressure in a recent phase 2 clinical trial. The mechanism of action involves inhibition of CYP11B2, thereby blocking aldosterone synthesis in the zona glomerulosa. Epithelial Sodium Channel Blockers Amiloride and triamterene inhibit ENaCs without affecting mineralocorticoid receptors. These agents exert mild diuretic effects and may be combined with other diuretics to reduce potassium loss resulting from enhanced renal excretion (kaliuresis).
