Common causes of decreased ACTH include pituitary insufficiency due to an adenoma compressing the pituitary gland, pituitary apoplexy, the sudden hemorrhage into the pituitary gland causing loss of ACTH, or Sheehan syndrome, a condition of pituitary infarction after blood loss during childhood. Thyroid-stimulating Hormone A TSH assay is the recommended screening test for thyroid disease. It is the test of choice in patients suspected of having a thyroid hormone deficiency (hypothyroidism) or excess (hyperthyroidism). Abnormal levels indicate the pathological functioning of the thyroid gland. When TSH levels are below normal levels, the primary diagnosis is hyperthyroidism. Low levels of TSH are present in patients on steroids, dopamine, and somatostatin analogs. Patients with thyroid cancer who receive treatment with thyroxine can also have decreased levels of TSH. Graves disease is another common pathology that presents with low TSH levels and is due to an autoimmune disorder, which stimulates the thyroid gland to make excess thyroid hormones. The increased thyroid hormones cause feedback inhibition of TSH secretion by the anterior pituitary gland.
