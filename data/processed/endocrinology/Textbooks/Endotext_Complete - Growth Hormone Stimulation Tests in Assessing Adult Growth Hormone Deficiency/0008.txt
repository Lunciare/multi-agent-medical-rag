SAMPLING AND MEASUREMENTS: Baseline Blood is drawn for glucose measurement with a glucometer. Blood draw for baseline glucose, GH and IGF-I (cortisol and ACTH, if HPA axis is assessed simultaneously) levels will be sent to the laboratory for further analysis. During the test Blood samples are drawn from the IV line every 5-10 mins for measurement of glucose levels using a glucometer. Signs and symptoms of neuroglycopenia are recorded. When blood glucose levels from the glucometer approaches 45 mg/dL (2.5 mmol/L), blood samples are sent to the laboratory for measurements of blood glucose levels. When symptomatic hypoglycemia is achieved (laboratory blood glucose < 40 mg/dL or 2.2 mmol/L), additional blood samples are collected to measure glucose and GH (+/- cortisol if the HPA axis is assessed simultaneously) levels at 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 60 and 90 min. The patient can begin drinking orange juice and eat to raise his/her blood glucose levels (IV 100 ml of 5% Dextrose can be administered if the patient cannot tolerate oral intake due to nausea or vomiting). At the end of the test Blood glucose levels measured from the glucometer should increase to levels > 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) before the patient is discharged from the testing unit. INTERPRETATION: If adequate (symptomatic) hypoglycemia is not achieved (< 40 mg/dL or 2.2 mmol/L), then adult GHD cannot be diagnosed. Peak serum GH levels ≤ 5 µg/L at any time point during the hypoglycemic phase of the test is diagnostic of adult GHD. CAUTION: If adequate (symptomatic) hypoglycemia is not achieved (< 40 mg/dL or 2.2 mmol/L), then adult GHD cannot be diagnosed.
