Treatment decisions are based on tumor size, histologic grade, proliferative index (Ki-67), functional status, and the extent of metastatic spread. Surgical resection is the primary modality for localized disease and may also be appropriate in select patients with limited metastatic involvement. Systemic therapies, including somatostatin analogs, targeted molecular agents such as everolimus or sunitinib, cytotoxic chemotherapy, and PRRT, are utilized for advanced or unresectable tumors. The choice of systemic therapy depends on tumor differentiation, receptor status, and overall disease burden. Although pNETs have malignant potential, their prognosis is often favorable, particularly in cases identified at an early stage or in tumors that are well-differentiated and low-grade. With timely and appropriate interprofessional management, many patients experience extended survival, frequently spanning many years or even decades.
