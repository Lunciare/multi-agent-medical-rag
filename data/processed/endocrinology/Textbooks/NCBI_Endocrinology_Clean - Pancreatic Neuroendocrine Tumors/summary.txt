Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors
KEYWORDS: pnets, tumors, tumor, pancreatic, patients, functional, disease, neuroendocrine, imaging, metastatic, well-differentiated, treatment, somatostatin, surgical, ki-

Introduction Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (pNETs) arise from endocrine islet cells of the pancreas and occur sporadically or in the context of hereditary syndromes such as multiple endocrine neoplasia Type 1 (MEN1), von Hippel-Lindau disease, neurofibromatosis type 1, or tuberous sclerosis complex. They account for only 1% to 2% of pancreatic malignancies, although their incidence has increased over recent decades. These tumors are classified as functional when they secrete bioactive hormones (for example, insulin in insulinoma) or nonfunctional when hormone secretion is absent. Tumorigenesis involves activating mutations in genes, eg, MEN1 , DAXX , ATRX , or components of the mTOR signaling pathway, that drive uncontrolled proliferation. Functional neoplasms produce characteristic clinical syndromes, whereas nonfunctional lesions often remain asymptomatic until they generate mass effect or metastasize, most commonly to the liver and regional lymph nodes. The tumors range from well-differentiated NETs to poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas.
