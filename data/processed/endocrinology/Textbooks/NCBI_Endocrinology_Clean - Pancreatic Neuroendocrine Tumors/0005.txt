Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors

Tumors Insulinomas : The most common type; results from excessive insulin secretion, leading to recurrent episodes of hypoglycemia. Gastrinomas : These tumors cause Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (ZES) due to excess gastrin, resulting in peptic ulcers and severe acid hypersecretion. Glucagonomas : Excessive glucagon production leads to diabetes, weight loss, and necrolytic migratory erythema. VIPomas : These tumors secrete vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP), causing severe watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, and dehydration. Somatostatinomas : Secretes excess somatostatin, leading to diabetes, hypochlorhydria, gallstones, and steatorrhea. These tumors are often small and diagnosed earlier due to their pronounced clinical symptoms. Nonfunctional Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors Nonfunctional pNETs, which constitute 60% to 90% of cases, do not produce clinically significant hormone levels and typically present with nonspecific symptoms, eg, abdominal pain, weight loss, or mass effects leading to biliary or gastric obstruction. Due to their silent nature, these tumors are often diagnosed at an advanced stage. Molecularly, they share genetic alterations with functional pNETs, particularly in MEN1 , DAXX , and ATRX , but do not exhibit hormone overproduction. Higher tumor grade and proliferation indices, such as Ki-67, correlate with increased aggressiveness and metastatic potential.
