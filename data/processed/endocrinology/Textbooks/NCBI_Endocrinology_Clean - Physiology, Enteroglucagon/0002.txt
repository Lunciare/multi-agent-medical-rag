Physiology, Enteroglucagon
KEYWORDS: glp-, intestine, cells, dpp-, glicentin, glucose, intestinal, small, release, effects, endogenous, glucagon-like, pancreatic, enzyme, l-cells

L-cells of the intestine. Within the L-cells, the processing of preproglucagon polypeptide forms glicentin, glucagon-like peptides 1 and 2 (GLP-1 and GLP-2), and glicentin is then further cleaved to produce oxyntomodulin, with a leftover fragment called glicentin-related polypeptide (GRPP). GLP-1 undergoes further processing, with the removal of amino acids from the N-terminus, to form the molecule GLP-1 (7-36), which significantly promotes insulin release from the pancreatic islets. This insulinotropic effect is active even at lower blood glucose levels. It is much more potent than the glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP, previously known as a gastric-inhibitory peptide). Exenatide, a synthetic GLP-1 receptor agonist, was developed after the discovery of Exendin-4, a molecule of the glucagon superfamily of hormones, in the saliva of the Gila monster, which showed potent pancreatic insulinotropic effects in studies on animals, thus motivating the development of a new class of anti-diabetic medication. The gene that codes for glicentin is on chromosome 2 (6 exons, 5 introns) and is found in the alpha cells of the pancreas and the intestinal or intestinal L cells.
