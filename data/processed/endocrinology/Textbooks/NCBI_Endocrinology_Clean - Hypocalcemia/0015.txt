Recombinant human parathyroid hormone: For the treatment of chronic hypoparathyroidism and certain genetic diseases related to low PTH levels, recombinant human parathyroid hormone has been approved by the FDA, but its use is limited by cost and availability. There are also concerns for long-term adverse effects such as bone pain and iatrogenic hyperparathyroidism. Other parathyroid hormone preparations are in development and are expected to be approved for treating chronic hypocalcemia due to hypoparathyroidism. Disease-specific treatment Postsurgical hypoparathyroidism: Most patients will develop hypocalcemia after thyroidectomy or parathyroidectomy due to hypoparathyroidism, which is usually transient. Prophylactic treatment with calcium after surgery is sometimes recommended. This usually prevents severe symptomatic hypocalcemia. Calcium levels should be monitored closely after surgery, and replacement calcium should be tapered off as indicated. Hypomagnesemia: Serum magnesium should be corrected before correcting hypocalcemia. Vitamin D deficiency: Hypocalcemia due to vitamin D deficiency cannot be corrected unless vitamin D is repleted first. CKD: Hypocalcemia in CKD is usually a result of vitamin D deficiency.
