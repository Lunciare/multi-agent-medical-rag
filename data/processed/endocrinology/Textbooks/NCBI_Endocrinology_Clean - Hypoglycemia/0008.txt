Elevated C-peptide levels and insulin levels can be seen with secretagogue agents such as sulfonylureas or insulin secretagogues since both classes of agents stimulate endogenous insulin secretion. Once exogenous insulin administration is ruled out, sources of endogenous hyperinsulinemia need to be considered. Localization is usually performed via abdominal computed tomography (CT) or MRI. Treatment / Management Identification of a hypoglycemic patient is critical due to potential adverse effects, including coma or death. Severe hypoglycemia can be treated with intravenous (IV) dextrose followed by an infusion of glucose. For conscious patients able to take oral (PO) medications, readily absorbable carbohydrate sources (such as fruit juice) should be given. For patients unable to take oral agents, glucagon should be administered. Glucagon can be given intramuscularly or intranasally using the newest available formulations. Once the patient is awake, a complex carbohydrate food source should be given to achieve sustained euglycemia.
