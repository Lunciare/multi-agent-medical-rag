Complete ulceration through the duodenal wall can cause peritonitis and damage the surrounding organs. Helicobacter pylori infection is the most significant duodenal ulcer risk factor. Wilkie's syndrome (SMA syndrome) arises from the compression of the duodenum's inferior portion (3rd segment), which lies between the SMA and abdominal aorta. This condition creates duodenal obstruction, often leading to nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. Wilkie's syndrome most commonly presents in individuals with insufficient intraabdominal fat, such as those with anorexia nervosa or cancer. The ileocecal valve is an important landmark for Meckel disease. The ileal or Meckel diverticulum, when present, is often located close to the ileocecal valve. Meckel disease classically presents in a young male patient with hematochezia and periumbilical pain, though the condition is often asymptomatic.
