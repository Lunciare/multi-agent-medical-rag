Face and neck: 75 g Front of the trunk: 210 g Back of the trunk: 210 g One arm: 90 g One hand (front and back): 30 g Specific Patient Populations Pregnancy considerations: A systemic review suggests that maternal use of topical corticosteroids, regardless of potency, is not associated with significant adverse pregnancy outcomes. However, there could be a potential link between cumulative doses of potent corticosteroids and low birth weight; additional research is required. Breastfeeding considerations: Extensive use of potent corticosteroids may cause systemic effects in the mother, but short-term application is unlikely to pose a risk to the breastfed infant. To minimize potential risk, the least potent corticosteroid should be applied to the smallest skin area, ensuring the infant's skin does not directly contact treated areas. Lower potency corticosteroids should be used on the nipple or areola, and only water-miscible creams or gels should be applied to the breast. Any topical corticosteroid should be thoroughly wiped off before nursing. Pediatric patients: Pediatric patients may be more vulnerable to HPA axis suppression and Cushing syndrome caused by topical corticosteroids due to their higher skin surface area relative to body weight than adults.
