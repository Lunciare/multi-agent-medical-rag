Therefore, further advancement in the knowledge of gene-environment interactions may assist in the development of targeted prevention strategies for type 2 diabetes in the future. In addition, novel biomarkers and interme­ diate conditions associated with diabetes risk offer potential to detect diabetes risk at an early stage (4,5). Although type 2 diabetes cannot be cured at this time, the onset of type 2 diabetes may be delayed in many individuals at risk and may be prevented in some at-risk individuals through identifying metabolic risk factors at an early stage and intervening in the progression of the disease through modi­ fication of behavioral and lifestyle risk factors. Continued work to improve the understanding of type 2 diabetes risk may assist in the development of optimal strat­ egies for type 2 diabetes prevention with a long-term goal of addressing this major public health concern.
