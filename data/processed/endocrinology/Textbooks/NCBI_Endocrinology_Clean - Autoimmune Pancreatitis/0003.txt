Most patients with type 3 AIP are asymptomatic. Etiology The presence of lymphocytic infiltration on histologic examination demonstrates the autoimmune etiology of AIP. Various immune-mediated mechanisms could initiate the inflammatory response in this IgG4-related disease. Potential initiating mechanisms include bacterial infection and molecular mimicry in the setting of genetic predisposition and autoimmunity. The IgG4 antibodies can cause tissue destruction locally and systemically, although the etiology of IgG4-related diseases remains poorly understood. Subsequently, regulatory T cells mediate an immune reaction, and the shift of peripheral blood T lymphocytes toward a T helper 2 cell response causes the release of cytokines and interleukins, inducing inflammation and fibrosis. In the case of type 3 AIP, immune checkpoint inhibitors are triggered by a nonspecific inflammatory T-cell (mainly CD8+) response. Epidemiology Autoimmune pancreatitis is an uncommon cause of chronic and recurrent pancreatitis but is often underdiagnosed. Approximately 2% of all cases of chronic pancreatitis are attributed to autoimmune pancreatitis, and the prevalence is less than 1 per 100,000 population.
