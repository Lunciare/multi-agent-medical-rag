Alternative options studied for the treatment of type 1 AIP relapse include resumption of prednisone or substituting or adding azathioprine (either of which is typically highly effective). Rituximab, 6-mercaptopurine, mycophenolate mofetil, cyclophosphamide, or cyclosporine have also been studied, but have a lower response rate. Although corticosteroid therapy induces remission in as many as 99%, relapse or steroid dependence can occur in up to one-third of patients, especially in IgG4-related AIP. These patients may require long-term steroids or steroid-sparing agents. If there is no response to steroid treatment after 4 to 6 weeks, pancreatic cancer must be excluded. If the diagnosis of AIP is confirmed, an immunomodulator such as azathioprine can facilitate steroid tapering, , though it is not effective as monotherapy. Rituximab is a second-line therapy for type 1 AIP in patients with a contraindication to glucocorticoids or if a complete course of steroid therapy is not feasible. Colchicine and various biologic therapies can be effective for both treatment and maintenance therapy, primarily in type 2 AIP.
