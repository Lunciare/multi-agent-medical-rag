History and Physical Clinical Features of Hyperprolactinemia Symptoms of hyperprolactinemia may arise from the direct effects of elevated prolactin or from compression caused by a structural lesion in the pituitary region. Typical manifestations occur in premenopausal women and men but are uncommon in postmenopausal women. Reproductive dysfunction and galactorrhea represent hallmark features of hyperprolactinemia. However, in children and adolescents, hyperprolactinemia can lead to growth arrest, delayed puberty, and primary amenorrhea.
