Pediatric Diabetic Ketoacidosis
KEYWORDS: dka, insulin, diabetes, blood, glucose, patients, type, ketones, levels, type diabetes, serum, urine, children, potassium, ketone

Presents at any time before, during, or after treatment, but typically onset is within 12 hours of treatment. Signs/Symptoms: Altered mental status, new headache, recurrent vomiting, urinary incontinence, Cushing Triad (bradycardia, irregular respirations, hypertension) Cerebral Edema may not initially be seen on CT imaging of the brain, therefore may still require starting treatment even if the CT head is normal. Treat if high suspicion: Mannitol (0.5-1 g/kg IV over 15 minutes): Osmotic diuretic causing withdrawing water from the brain parencyma. May give a second dose if there is no initial response. Hypertonic (3%) saline 2.5 mL/kg over 30 minutes Neurosurgical consultation Other Complications include: Cognitive impairment Venous thrombosis Pancreatic enzyme elevations Acute kidney injury Hypokalemia Hypoglycemia Rhabdomyolysis Pulmonary edema Multiple organ dysfunction syndrome Cardiac arrhythmias
