This condition presents with numbness in the anterior thigh and weakness with hip flexion and knee extension. Obturator nerve entrapment usually occurs as a result of an obturator hernia. The elderly female population has an anatomic predisposition to this injury due to ligament laxity, minimal adipose tissue in the obturator canal, and wider obturator canals. This condition presents with decreased sensation in the medial thigh and weakness of adduction of the thigh. Ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerve damage can occur during gynecologic surgeries, resulting in abdominal muscle weakness and decreased sensation in the groin and suprapubic region. Injury to the ilioinguinal nerve is also evident in inguinal herniorrhaphy and open appendectomy. In extreme cases, it can lead to radiating pain and a burning sensation, which can be annoying. Here, the pain and burning are referred to the inguinal region and, sometimes, to the genitalia, based on their sensory distribution. Ilioinguinal nerve entrapment syndrome is characterized by a triad of radiating pain to the iliac fossa, altered sensation in its distribution, and a trigger point medial to the anterosuperior iliac spine.
