Despite previous pharmacovigilance reports suggesting a link, a randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving 30 healthy volunteers found that HCTZ 25 mg daily for 15 days did not increase photosensitivity to UVA or UVB radiation or cause phototoxic or carcinogenic effects. In vitro studies with human keratinocytes also found no adverse effects related to phototoxicity or carcinogenesis due to HCTZ administration combined with UVB exposure. A systematic review and meta-analysis on antihypertensive drugs concluded that diuretics (especially hydrochlorothiazide) are associated with an increased risk of non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC). However, this risk was predominantly observed during studies that did not adjust for sun exposure or smoking. Cohort studies and studies that corrected these factors did not find a significantly increased risk of NMSC. Furthermore, significant publication bias was identified in some studies. In conclusion, while observational data suggest a link between HCTZ and skin cancer, the evidence from well-controlled studies correcting for confounding factors is less conclusive.
