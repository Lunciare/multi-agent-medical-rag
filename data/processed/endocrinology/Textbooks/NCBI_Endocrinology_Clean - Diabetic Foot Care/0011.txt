The primary objectives of interprofessional care are to medically optimize comorbidities, prevent foot complications, and manage existing pedal pathology. Preparation Patients with diabetes should remove both shoes and socks prior to examination. Feet should be thoroughly cleaned and dried before any foot assessment or dressing change. Caregivers should wash their hands before and after performing a dressing change for a diabetic foot ulcer. A clean and sanitary environment should be maintained to reduce the likelihood of contamination and infection. Technique or Treatment Dermatological Assessment The dermatological examination of the diabetic foot should encompass a comprehensive evaluation of the entire foot and ankle, including interdigital spaces and nails. Hyperkeratotic areas should be debrided. Preulcerative and ulcerative lesions require detailed documentation of location, size, depth, wound base characteristics, periwound skin, undermining, sinus tracts, probing to bone, exudate quality, and signs of infection, such as erythema, warmth, malodor, or crepitus. A temperature difference exceeding 3 °C to 4 °C compared to the contralateral foot may indicate infection or acute Charcot neuroarthropathy.
