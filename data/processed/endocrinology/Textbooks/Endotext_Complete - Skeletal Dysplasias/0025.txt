Radiographic findings include eleven pairs of ribs, short, bowed limbs (most commonly femur and tibia), clubfeet, cervical spine abnormalities, scapular hypoplasia, vertical, narrow iliac bones, and hypoplasia of the vertebral pedicles ( 108 ). Campomelic dysplasia is often lethal during the first year of life, due respiratory insufficiency from trachea-bronchomalacia or cervical spine instability. There is one report which describes the phenotype of individuals who have survived infancy ( 109 ). Due to the role of SOX9 in male gonadal development, Campomelic dysplasia is also associated with dysgenetic 46,XY disorder of sex development (DSD) ( 110 ). The appearance of the external genitalia ranges from ambiguous genitalia to normal female genitalia. Internal structures are also variable, may include both Mullerian and Wolffian duct structures, and do not always correlate with the external genitalia ( 110 , 111 ). CONCLUSION For the pediatric endocrinologist, a foundational understanding of these conditions is essential for early recognition, accurate diagnosis, and comprehensive longitudinal management. Recent advances in molecular genetics have transformed diagnostic paradigms, allowing classification based on underlying pathogenic mechanisms. The 2023 revision of the International Nosology of Constitutional Disorders of Bone now delineates over 770 distinct entities across 42 diagnostic groups, reflecting this molecular framework and supporting more precise clinical correlations. Clinically, bone dysplasia may present across a broad spectrum—from perinatal lethality to incidentally noted disproportionate short stature or skeletal deformities. Pediatric endocrinologists are often consulted to distinguish these conditions from more common etiologies of growth failure and metabolic bone disease. A systematic approach that integrates clinical phenotype, radiographic features, biochemical data, and molecular diagnostics is essential for accurate classification and optimal patient care. Management is inherently multidisciplinary. Emerging therapies, including targeted biological agents and recombinant growth hormone in select contexts, are promising and will expand the pediatric endocrinologist’s role in the management of pediatric patients with skeletal dysplasia.
