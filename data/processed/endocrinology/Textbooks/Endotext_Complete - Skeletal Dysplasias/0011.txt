Spondylo-epi-metaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD), PAPSS2 related (formerly called SEMD, Pakistani type, MIM No. 612847) is due to autosomal recessive (AR) mutations in PAPSS2. The PAPSS2 gene helps sulfonate PGs in cartilage, which is necessary for bone formation. SEMD is characterized by short-trunk dwarfism, short and bowed lower extremities with enlarged knee joints, brachydactyly, and kyphoscoliosis ( 43 ). Short trunk dwarfism is typically apparent after age 2. Genu varum, genu valgum, and early onset osteoarthritic changes in the hands and knees have been described ( 44 ). Facies and intelligence are normal. Radiographic findings include delayed ossification of the epiphyses, rectangular vertebral bodies with irregular endplates and narrow intervertebral discs, precocious calcification of rib cartilage, short femoral neck, mildly shortened metacarpals, and mild metaphyseal and epiphyseal changes of tubular bones ( 45 ). Metaphyseal longitudinal striations, mild epiphyseal flattening, and irregular metaphyses are also described ( 46 ).
