Skeletal Dysplasias
KEYWORDS: pubmed, dysplasia, short, skeletal, bone, height, growth, osteopetrosis, disorders, free, stature, short stature, pmc, article pubmed, pmc free


ABSTRACT Skeletal dysplasias represent a complex and heterogeneous group of over 770 genetic disorders characterized by abnormalities in the development, growth, and maintenance of the skeleton. Though individually rare, these conditions collectively present a significant burden in pediatrics, particularly within endocrinology. Disproportionate growth failure, metabolic bone disease, bone fragility, and developmental delays frequently prompt investigation. Advanced molecular diagnostics, including next-generation sequencing and targeted gene panels, have revolutionized the classification and understanding of skeletal dysplasias. Contemporary frameworks integrate radiographic phenotypes with genotypic data, as reflected in the most recent (2023) Nosology and Classification of Genetic Skeletal Disorders. This dual approach not only refines diagnostic accuracy but also facilitates prognostic insight and genetic counseling. Pediatric endocrinologists are often involved in the longitudinal care of affected individuals, addressing complications such as bone fragility, short stature, delayed bone age, endocrine comorbidities (e.g., hypothyroidism, pubertal disturbances), and emerging therapeutic considerations—including fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) pathway inhibitors and enzyme replacement therapies. Early recognition and multidisciplinary coordination are essential to optimize outcomes, particularly given the lifelong implications of many skeletal dysplasias. This chapter provides an overview of the diagnostic approach to bone dysplasias with emphasis on molecular mechanisms, phenotype-genotype correlations, and management strategies. The reader should also refer to the dedicated chapters of osteogenesis imperfecta, osteoporosis/bone fragility, fibrous dysplasia, and disorders of vitamin D and phosphate metabolism in Endotext.
