Once the sphenoid bone is broached (a fracture is created), the sellar floor can be penetrated with a durotomy to view the sellar region. The area is enhanced visually with an operative microscope. The advantages of going the transsphenoidal route allow there to be minimal surgical trauma, less blood loss, avoidance of generic complications of craniotomy, and direct visualization of the gland. Complications of transsphenoidal surgery include persistent cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) rhinorrhea, panhypopituitarism, transient DI, postoperative meningitis, cranial nerve injury, vascular damage, and stroke due to vasospasm or thromboembolism. Clinical Significance Rathke’s Pouch Any remnants of Rathke’s pouch can produce mass-effect signs and symptoms. Rathke’s cleft cysts can accumulate fluid and start to expand, allowing compression of nearby structures. Vestigial remnants of the pouch can also form craniopharyngiomas. These benign tumors are slow-growing and are seen in young or very old people. These benign tumors are supersellers. They are mostly cystic but can be solid and encapsulated.
