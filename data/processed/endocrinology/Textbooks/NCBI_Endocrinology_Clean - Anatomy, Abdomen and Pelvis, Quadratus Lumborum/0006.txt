The first 4 pairs of somites appear in the occipital region, contributing to the formation of the occipital and facial bones and the eye and tongue muscles. Eight pairs of cervical somites follow, with the 1st contributing to the formation of the occipital bone, while the remaining 7 form the cervical vertebrae, muscles, and associated dermal structures. In succession, 12 pairs of thoracic somites form the vertebrae, musculature, and rib cage bones, contributing to upper limb development. Five pairs of lumbar somites form the lumbar vertebrae, muscles, and abdominal dermis. Five pairs of sacral somites form the sacrum along with its associated muscles and dermal elements. About 3 to 5 pairs of coccygeal somites form the coccyx. Blood Supply and Lymphatics The quadratus lumborum gets its vascular supply via the lumbar arteries and a lumbar branch of the iliolumbar artery. The 4 lumbar arteries on each side arise from the posterior surface of the aorta at the level of the 1st 4 lumbar vertebrae.
