The first 3 arteries pass posteriorly to the quadratus lumborum, while the 4th passes anteriorly. The iliolumbar artery is the 1st tributary of the internal iliac artery (or hypogastric artery). The iliolumbar artery rises behind the psoas and iliacus (in the iliac fossa), where the individual branches feed the iliac muscle. Branches from this artery extend to the psoas, quadratus lumborum, and transverse abdominal muscles. Another branch supplies the spinal canal between the 5th lumbar vertebra and the sacrum. The lumbar veins on each side connect through longitudinal anastomotic vessels that merge to form a small vertical trunk known as the "ascending lumbar vein." This vessel communicates with the iliolumbar vein at the inferior end and, sometimes, the common iliac vein. At the superior end, the ascending lumbar vein gives rise to the azygos vein on the right and the hemizygous vein on the left, creating an important anastomotic pathway between the inferior and superior vena cavae.
