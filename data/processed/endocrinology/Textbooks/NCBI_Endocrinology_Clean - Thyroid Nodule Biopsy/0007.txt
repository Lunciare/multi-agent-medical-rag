The needle is advanced to the nodule and moved to and fro multiple times through the nodule. Nonaspiration technique involves performing this maneuver without suction, with capillary action allowing the collection of the specimen within the needle hub. Aspiration technique allows for specimen acquisition via operator application of gentle suction during the to and fro maneuver. Although nonaspiration technique has occasionally been shown to be superior to the aspiration technique, two meta-analysis studies revealed no significant difference between the two. The needle-syringes may be delivered to a cytopathologist present during the procedure for further processing to ensure adequate acquisition. Multiple aspiration attempts are performed until a satisfactory tissue sample is confirmed by the cytopathologist. Complications The vast majority of thyroid FNAs are performed with ease and without complication. Development of a neck hematoma remains a concerning, yet rare, complication of thyroid FNA procedures.
