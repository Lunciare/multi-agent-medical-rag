Preconditions for Unsafe Acts • Environmental Factors: - Tools/technology: Obsolete drill systems, missing angled endoscopes, malfunctioning suction, or poorly designed OR ergonomics that contribute to poor visualization or delayed responses - Physical environment: Inadequate lighting in the operating room, uncomfortable temperature conditions, or distraction from noise can impact concentration during precise tumor dissection. - Task factors: Reoperations, anatomy near optic nerves or cavernous sinus, instances of intraoperative hemorrhage can involve high cognitive • Individual Factors: - Mental state: Surgeon’s stress due to fatigue, personal issues, or high case load. Emotional pressure to complete case rapidly. - Physiological state: Surgeon fatigue from overnight calls, dehydration, skipped meals, or illness • Team Factors: - Communication: Failure to verbally share surgical plan or miscommunication regarding hemostasis status during closure. Insufficient handover between surgical and recovery personnel. - Coordination: Inadequate planning for intraoperative sampling, or lack of coordination among multiple teams regarding post-operative instructions. - Leadership: Lead surgeon fails to delegate roles clearly, neglects to lead safety briefing, or undermines team input during complication management. Unsafe Acts • Errors: - Decision errors: Incorrectly assuming that a tumor can be fully resected using a standard approach when extended exposure is required - Skill- based errors: Inadvertent injury to optic nerve or carotid artery during dissection due to momentary lapses in attention or hand control. - Perceptual errors: The arachnoid boundary may be incorrectly identified as a result of bleeding, limited endoscopic visibility, or visual misperception within a two-dimensional field. • Violations: - Routine violations; Skipping pre-op imaging review meeting or proceeding without finalizing hormonal workup because ‘the case looks straightforward’ - Exceptional violations : Proceeding with surgery despite clear contraindications or without obtaining full patient consent during emergency situations represents rare but high-risk deviations from standard practice norms.
