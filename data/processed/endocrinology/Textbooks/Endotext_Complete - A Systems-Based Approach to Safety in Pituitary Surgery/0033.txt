Human Factors Analysis and Classification System for Pituitary Surgery View in own window Category Description / Examples Organizational Influences • Organizational culture: Absence of structured safety culture — irregular mortality and morbidity reviews, missing team briefings, and weak prioritization of complications — leads to normalization of deviance and unaddressed risks • Operational process: Failure to standardize WHO checklists, imaging review, and endocrine follow-up introduces variability. • Resource management: Limited access to experienced staff, neuronavigation, intraoperative imaging, specialized nursing, and ICU beds increases perioperative risk and reduces response capacity. Supervisory Factors • Inadequate supervision: Junior residents are allowed to manage critical steps (e.g., sella opening, hemostasis) without direct oversight. • Planned inappropriate operations: Scheduling complex tumors (e.g., giant adenomas with cavernous sinus invasion) at end of day without backup or assigning underprepared teams for high-risk patients. • Failed to correct known problems: Equipment issues (such as a faulty endoscope light source) or recurring complications (like delayed DI recognition) remain unresolved despite multiple incidents • Supervisory violations: Skipping
