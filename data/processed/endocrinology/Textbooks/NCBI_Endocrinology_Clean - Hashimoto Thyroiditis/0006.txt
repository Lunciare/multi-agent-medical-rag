B Cells Individuals with Hashimoto thyroiditis have the following polyclonal antibodies specific to thyroid antigens: Thyroid peroxidase antibody : The most common antibody in Hashimoto thyroiditis. These are found in over 90% of individuals with Hashimoto thyroiditis. Thyroglobulin antibody : Present in 50% to 80% of individuals with Hashimoto thyroiditis. TSH receptor antibody, TSHR Ab : Various types have been identified. These can be stimulating, blocking, or neutral. The vast majority of TSHR Abs in Hashimoto thyroiditis are blocking. Rarely, individuals with Hashimoto thyroiditis may have TSHR-stimulating antibodies, which can also cause thyroid eye disease. This might also explain why few individuals with Hashimoto thyroiditis switch to Graves disease. Role of T Cells CD8 + T cells are a central part of the immune dysfunction underlying the pathogenesis of Hashimoto thyroiditis. These cytotoxic cells infiltrate thyroid tissue, causing inflammation and the destruction of follicular cells. CD4 + T cells activate other immune cells, particularly macrophages and B cells, thereby promoting the production of autoantibodies. Different subsets of CD4 + T cells, including Th1, Th2, Th17, and Tregs (regulatory T cells), have various functions, and their imbalance contributes to the pathogenesis of Hashimoto thyroiditis.
