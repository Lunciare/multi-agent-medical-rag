Luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG) very much resemble TSH. Specifically, they all have the same alpha subunit as TSH but different beta chains that confer biological specificity. Since TSH, LH, FSH, and HCG share the same alpha subunit, they all function through the same cyclic adenine monophosphate (cAMP) second messenger system. The cAMP second messenger system entails adenine monophosphate (AMP) conversion to cAMP. In addition to cAMP, TSH also activates the IP3 signaling cascade. The IP3 second messenger system involves calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Both cAMP and IP3/Ca2+ cascades lead to downstream physiological effects that enhance thyroid hormone synthesis and thyroid gland growth. Organ Systems Involved The primary target of TSH is the thyroid gland. Specifically, TSH modulates the release of T3 and T4 from thyroid follicular cells. Around 80% of the thyroid hormone is released as T4. T4 is de-iodinated to T3, which is a more potent thyroid hormone.
