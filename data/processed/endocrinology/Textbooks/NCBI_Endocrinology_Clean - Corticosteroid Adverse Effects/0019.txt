Psychosis can occur but does so almost exclusively at doses of prednisone above 20 mg per day given for a prolonged period. Disturbances in sleep are reported, especially with split doses that may interfere with the normal pattern of diurnal cortisol production. Akathisia (motor restlessness) is a common glucocorticoid side effect. The risk of developing a given neuropsychiatric disorder following glucocorticoid therapy may increase among patients with a history of the condition. Rare cases of pseudotumor cerebri have also correlated with glucocorticoid use. There is specific documentation of neuropsychiatric adverse effects with glucocorticoid therapy in children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) receiving dexamethasone or prednisone for the induction and maintenance of treatment. The risk is higher in preschool-age children, and the symptoms typically present during the first week of glucocorticoid therapy. Glucocorticoid-induced acute neuropsychiatric impairment may present with a wide variety of behavioral symptoms, including euphoria, aggression, insomnia, mood fluctuations, depression, manic behavior, and even frank psychosis.
