Long-term oral corticosteroid therapy may be necessary for chronic illnesses such as polymyalgia rheumatica, SLE, RA, vasculitis, myositis, IgG4-related disease, chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML), lymphoma, leukemia, multiple sclerosis, organ transplantation, etc. Clinicians must make every effort to use the glucocorticoids at the lowest possible dose and for the shortest possible duration in these cases. A slow taper shall be attempted in patients with prolonged exposure to glucocorticoids to prevent adrenal crisis. Local Administration Glucocorticoid administration can be via several non-systemic routes, including intra-articular joint injections for joint inflammation, inhalational for asthma, topical for dermatological problems, ocular drops for eye conditions, and intra-nasal for seasonal rhinitis. Clinicians generally avoid intramuscular (IM) glucocorticoids due to the risk of local muscle atrophy due to depot effect, and the only indications for intramuscular glucocorticoids are for IM triamcinolone acetonide for specific inflammatory disorders and IM injection of betamethasone to a pregnant mother less than 37 weeks of gestation to stimulate fetal lung maturity.
