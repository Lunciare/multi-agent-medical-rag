Calcitonin should be administered at a dose of 4 IU/kg alongside normal saline infusion to help prevent bone resorption and increase urinary calcium excretion. Calcitonin is a very fast-acting medication, but its effects are limited in duration. Bisphosphonates, such as zoledronic acid (4 mg IV over 15-30 minutes) or pamidronate (60-90 mg IV over 2 hours), are recommended for patients without kidney dysfunction. Zoledronic acid is preferred for hypercalcemia secondary to malignancy due to its greater potency and shorter infusion time. Bisphosphonates are also commonly used in patients with bone metastases to prevent skeletal complications. The main adverse effects of bisphosphonates include osteonecrosis of the jaw and nephrotoxicity. Denosumab works by inhibiting RANKL and was previously considered only for patients who did not respond to zoledronic acid or those with kidney impairment, as it is not cleared by the kidneys. However, the latest guidelines from the Endocrine Society now recommend using denosumab early, as it has shown strong efficacy in lowering calcium levels and reducing recurrent episodes of hypercalcemia.
