A diagnosis of GH deficiency should only be made when biochemical GH deficiency is documented in the appropriate clinical context; multiple factors can influence the hormonal response to provocative testing. This is of particular importance in HIV-infected patients in whom reduced GH secretion may be functional. When imaging reveals pituitary or hypothalamic injury or disease and laboratory testing reveals biochemical GH deficiency, true GH deficiency is highly likely. HIV- and AIDS-Wasting Syndromes The laboratory evaluation of patients with HIV- or AIDS-wasting syndromes is comprehensive. After the laboratory evaluation, imaging studies may be indicated to evaluate opportunistic infections and hormonal deficiencies. Recommended laboratory testing includes: CD4 lymphocyte count and HIV viral load to establish overall disease control and underlying risks for opportunistic infections Screening for nutritional deficiencies, including but not limited to iron, vitamin D, calcium, vitamin B12, and folate Testing for gastrointestinal opportunistic infections, as suggested by a comprehensive medical history and physical examination Evaluation for hypogonadism and growth hormone deficiency.
