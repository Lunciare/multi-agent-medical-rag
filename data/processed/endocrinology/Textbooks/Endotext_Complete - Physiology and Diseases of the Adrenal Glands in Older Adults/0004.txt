The largest dose is administered upon waking, and the final dose should be taken at least 4–6 hours before bedtime to reduce the risk of sleep disturbances and metabolic complications. Prednisone or prednisolone (3–5 mg once daily) may be an appropriate alternative for elderly patients, as their longer half-life and simpler dosing schedule can improve adherence—particularly in individuals with cognitive impairment or complex medication regimens. In this population, special attention is needed to avoid over-replacement, which can worsen common comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, and cognitive decline. Even mild glucocorticoid excess may accelerate catabolic effects and increase cardiovascular risk. Conversely, under-replacement heightens the risk of adrenal crisis, particularly during intercurrent illness or physiological stress, both of which occur more frequently in older adults due to increased vulnerability to infection and acute disease. Mineralocorticoid supplementation is needed in patients with PAI, as evidenced by high renin and low serum aldosterone. In these patients, salt intake should not be restricted, but caution should be exercised when co-administered with grapefruit juice and licorice, as these can enhance mineralocorticoid activity. Fludrocortisone should be initiated at 0.1 mg/day, administered orally in the morning. The dose may be adjusted to a maintenance dose of 0.05 to 0.2 mg/day ( 13 , 15 , 18 , 19 ). Licorice, which has been employed for centuries as both an herbal medicinal agent and a flavoring compound, can contain glycyrrhizic acid (GA). GA is metabolized to glycyrrhetinic acid, which inhibits 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11β-OHSD2) enzyme in the kidney. Inhibition of this enzyme impairs the conversion of cortisol to cortisone, resulting in elevated cortisol concentrations capable of activating mineralocorticoid receptors. This process ultimately leads to the development of pseudoaldosteronism with possible hypertension and hypokalemia ( 21 ). Interestingly, bioflavonoids present in grapefruit juice can also inhibit 11β-OHSD2 causing a similar effect than licorice ( 22 ). Some studies have demonstrated that North American licorice plants contain relatively low concentrations of glycyrrhizic acid. Moreover, many products marketed as “licorice” in the United States contain anise oil rather than true licorice ( 23 , 24 ). In Europe the licorice usually contains glycyrrhizic acid.
