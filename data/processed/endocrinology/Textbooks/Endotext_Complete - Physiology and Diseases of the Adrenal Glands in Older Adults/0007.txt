If oral intake is compromised—such as in cases of vomiting, gastroenteritis, trauma, or surgery—100 mg of hydrocortisone should be administered IM immediately. In the setting of minor to moderate surgical stress, hydrocortisone doses should be increased to a total of 25–75 mg per day for 1–2 days. For major surgery requiring general anesthesia, trauma, or critical illness requiring ICU admission, a 100 mg IV bolus should be followed by a continuous infusion or divided doses totaling 200 mg over 24 hours. Interestingly, emerging evidence suggests that elderly patients may require lower postoperative doses of IV hydrocortisone—such as 25 mg every 6 hours—due to age-related reductions in hydrocortisone clearance ( 13 , 14 ). Adrenal Insufficiency and COVID-19 Multiple pathological changes have been identified, primarily through autopsy studies of patients who died from SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 infections. These include degeneration and necrosis of adrenal cortex cells, likely due to the direct cytopathogenic effects of the virus and local inflammation leading to microthrombosis, as well as decreased cortisol and ACTH secretion. Although AI is uncommon, it may occur depending on the severity and acuity of the infection. Autopsy series identified higher rates of adrenal damage (20-40% of cases), suggesting the true frequency of AI in COVID may be masked by glucocorticoid therapy used for treatment of respiratory failure ( 33 ). Recommendations include the prompt initiation of high-dose corticosteroids to manage the exaggerated cytokine response, and the use of heparin to prevent thrombosis, given the procoagulant state and reports of AI caused by adrenal vein thrombosis ( 13 , 14 ). ADRENAL INCIDENTALOMA IN THE ELDERLY An adrenal incidentaloma is defined as an adrenal mass measuring ≥1 cm, typically asymptomatic and discovered incidentally during imaging studies such as CT or MRI. In the general population, the prevalence is approximately 4%, whereas in older adults, it can reach up to 10% ( 34 ). If an adrenal mass is identified in individuals under 30 years of age, adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) should be considered. Autopsy studies have shown that adrenal incidentalomas may be present in up to 20% of elderly patients with hypertension or diabetes mellitus, often exceeding 1.5 cm in diameter. Arteriopathy is considered a likely underlying cause, leading to local ischemia and subsequent regenerative changes ( 35 – 37 ).
