Laboratory abnormalities can reveal hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypercalcemia, metabolic acidosis, hypoglycemia, altered cellular immunity (neutropenia, eosinophilia, lymphocytosis), and mild normocytic anemia ( 25 , 26 , 28 – 30 ). If there is a high suspicion of adrenal insufficiency, treatment should be initiated immediately; however, blood for serum glucose, electrolytes, basal cortisol, and ACTH levels should be obtained before initiating treatment if feasible. If subsequent results rule out adrenal insufficiency, empiric treatment can be discontinued. The mainstays of treatment for AAI include fluid resuscitation, intravenous (IV) hydrocortisone, supportive care, and identification and management of underlying triggers. For fluid resuscitation, 5% glucose in isotonic saline is recommended. In young adults, an initial bolus may be administered, while in elderly patients, a slower infusion is advised—for example, 1 liter over the first 5 hours, followed by 2–3 liters over the next 12–24 hours, as clinically indicated ( 13 , 19 , 25 , 26 , 28 , 29 ). Hydrocortisone is equally effective when administered as a bolus or continuous infusion. A commonly recommended regimen includes an initial 100 mg IV bolus, followed by a total of 200 mg over the next 24 hours, then tapering to 100 mg per day based on the patient’s clinical response ( 13 ). In patients with PAI, hydrocortisone doses greater than 50 mg/day provide adequate mineralocorticoid activity, so additional mineralocorticoid supplementation is not necessary at that stage ( 31 ). Following successful treatment of an adrenal crisis, hydrocortisone should be gradually tapered over approximately three days until the patient reaches their usual maintenance dose ( 32 ). Preventive strategies for managing PAI include comprehensive education for patients and their families. Key topics should cover the nature of the condition, indications for increased glucocorticoid dosing or intramuscular (IM) administration, and instructions on how to administer emergency injections. Patients should be equipped with a medical alert bracelet and an emergency kit containing a 100 mg hydrocortisone ampule, syringe, and needles. Annual review and reinforcement of this education is recommended. The “sick day rules” must be clearly explained: during febrile illness, the patient should double the daily dose if the temperature exceeds 100.4°F (38°C) or triple it if above 102.2°F (39°C), continuing for 2–3 days or until recovery.
