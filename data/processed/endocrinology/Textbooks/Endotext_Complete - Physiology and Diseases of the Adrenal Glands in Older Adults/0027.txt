The indications for the robot-assisted adrenalectomy are the same as for conventional laparoscopic surgery; functional and oncologic outcomes are similar. Some technical modifications are required for placement of laparoscopic ports. This procedure is performed only in highly specialized centers by experienced surgical teams ( 98 , 113 ). Partial Adrenalectomy Partial adrenalectomy, defined as adrenalectomy with preservation of adrenal tissue, was first proposed by Irvin et al. in 1983 using an open technique. In 1996, Walz achieved favorable outcomes with a minimally invasive retroperitoneal approach. This procedure is considered for bilateral adrenal tumors or for small, unilateral, and sporadic tumors—such as functioning adenomas smaller than 3 cm in size—particularly in young patients. It may also serve as a surgical option in patients with bilateral or hereditary adrenal tumors, such as pheochromocytomas associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) or von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) syndrome, in order to preserve adrenocortical function. However, the presence of a large pheochromocytoma or multiple small tumors within the same gland necessitates a total adrenalectomy, as one quarter to one third of a normal adrenal gland is required to maintain adequate adrenocortical function.
