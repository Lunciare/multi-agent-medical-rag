These include a diameter greater than 4 cm, irregular borders, high radiological density (>20 Hounsfield Units) that does not attenuate with contrast, non-homogeneous post-contrast enhancement, and delayed contrast wash-out (e.g., less than 50% wash-out at 10 minutes post-administration). Some lesions may show heterogeneous density due to central necrosis. ACC is typically unilateral, and up to 30% of cases may demonstrate tumoral calcification. In contrast, adrenal metastases tend to be bilateral ( 35 , 37 , 39 , 40 ). On MRI, ACC usually appears hypointense on T1-weighted images (compared to liver) and exhibits high to intermediate signal intensity on T2-weighted images. Adrenal metastases are typically iso- or slightly hypointense on T1 and demonstrate high/intermediate signal on T2, often due to their higher aqueous content. On 18F-FDG PET-CT, malignant masses generally show increased uptake of 18F-FDG, except in areas of intratumoral necrosis, which may demonstrate reduced or absent uptake. It is important to evaluate for local invasion or distant MTS. A common indication for this study is the assessment of an adrenal incidentaloma in a patient with a known primary malignancy. If there is suspicion of metastatic disease from a known primary malignancy, CT-guided fine needle aspiration (FNA) may be considered—once catecholamine hypersecretion has been ruled out—or an 18F-FDG PET-CT may be used to help differentiate metastatic disease from a primary adrenal malignancy. Management also depends on the size of the mass. For lesions >4 cm, close monitoring may be considered based on the patient’s age and symptoms, or surgery may be pursued. For masses >10 cm, open surgery is typically the preferred approach ( 35 , 37 , 39 , 40 ). Adrenal incidentalomas should be monitored, even in elderly patients. A follow-up CT scan is recommended 6–12 months after the initial diagnosis, especially if there are any concerning features. The need for further repeat CT imaging beyond this period remains controversial ( 35 , 37 , 40 , 41 ). Criteria for considering adrenalectomy based on CT monitoring include: an adrenal mass >4 cm, growth of >1 cm/year or an increase of ≥5 mm in any dimension, and the presence of Cushing syndrome in patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), or cardiovascular disease.
