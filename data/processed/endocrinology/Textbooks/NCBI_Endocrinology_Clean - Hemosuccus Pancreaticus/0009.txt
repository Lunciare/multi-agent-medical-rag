Interventional Radiologist It is often performed in hemodynamically stable patients considered the initial management line and usually done in HP diagnosed through angiography. IR has a success rate of about 67% to 100% of the cases. Techniques include angiography with embolization via prosthetic material, balloon tamponade, or stent placement. Coil embolization is the most commonly used method which induces thrombosis within the aneurysmal vessels. Nevertheless, it can induce ischemia within the vessel if it has a poor collateral supply. IR intervention has a recurrence rate of 30% of bleeding following the embolization. Balloon tamponade and stent placement can be put as a bridge to perform elective surgery. No major complication was noticed with IR interventions other than femoral artery cellulitis in about 0.5%. Failure of embolization may result from a failure to isolate the causative artery, spasm of the bleeding vessel, or the bleeding vessel's misidentification.
