It is often difficult to be diagnosed at early stages due to the paucity of cases, anatomic location, and intermittent nature of bleeding. The unfamiliarity of HP to physicians makes it a diagnostic challenge. It must be considered in patients with a history of chronic pancreatitis presenting with acute GI bleeding onset. Etiology Many potential causes behind HP are: Pancreatic inflammation : Whether acute, chronic or hereditary, pancreatitis considered being the most common cause of HP, responsible for about 80% of the cases. Pancreatitis promotes inflammatory alterations within the pancreatic ducts, which eventually leads to vascular wall rupture. Moreover, gallstones within the duct and pseudocysts' development of the pancreas might trigger an inflammatory reaction within the duct. Ultimately, the pancreatic pseudocyst plays a potential pathological role through the activation of the lytic enzymes (especially elastase), which lyse the elastic component of the vessels, results in hemorrhage.
