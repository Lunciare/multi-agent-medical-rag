Parathyroidectomy: When the etiology of hypercalcemia is primary hyperparathyroidism, the patient should be evaluated to determine whether they meet the surgical criteria for a parathyroidectomy. These criteria include serum calcium greater than 1.0 mg/dL above the upper limit of normal, evidence of osteoporosis on DEXA scan, fragility or vertebral fracture, 24-hour urine calcium greater than 400 mg/d, presence of renal stones, or age less than 50. If these criteria are met, the patients should pursue a parathyroidectomy to reduce calcium levels for a more long-term approach. Cinacelcet: Cinacelcet is a calcimimetic medication that is currently approved by the Food and Drug Administration for treating secondary hyperparathyroidism due to renal failure. This medication is also used in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism who are not candidates for surgery and has recently been reported in case studies as a treatment for hypercalcemia of malignancy. Cinacelcet works by increasing the sensitivity of the calcium-sensing receptors on the surface of the parathyroid cells, resulting in decreased parathyroid hormone release.
