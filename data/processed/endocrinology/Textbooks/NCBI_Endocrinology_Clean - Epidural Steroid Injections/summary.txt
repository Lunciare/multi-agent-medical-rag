Epidural Steroid Injections
KEYWORDS: epidural, steroid, spinal, epidural steroid, injections, pain, steroid injections, nerve, injection, needle, cervical, radicular, transforaminal, intervertebral, spinal nerve

Introduction Epidural steroid injections have been used to treat pain caused by lumbar disc injuries since the 1950s. These injections are often used in treating radicular pain, a neuropathic pain syndrome that radiates down a dermatome of 1 or more spinal nerves. Typically, the pain is due to nerve root irritation from mechanical compression by an intervertebral herniated disc, leading to inflammation. Additional etiologies leading to mechanical spinal nerve compression include spondylosis, spondylolisthesis, and ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, resulting in neuroforaminal stenosis. In patients with lumbosacral radiculopathy, more than half of the patients report interference in their activities of daily living, and a quarter continue to have severe pain that does not respond well to oral pharmacological treatments. Approximately 14% of patients with lumbosacral radiculopathy eventually require surgery for severe pain that may or may not be associated with a neurological deficit.
