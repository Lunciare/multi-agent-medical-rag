The standard of care involves using fluoroscopy or, less commonly, computed tomography guidance to ensure proper needle placement with the aid of contrast flow. The efficacy of epidural steroid injections in lumbosacral radiculopathy has been widely studied, and based on a recent systematic review including 8 randomized controlled trials, a strong recommendation on moderate-quality evidence demonstrates that transforaminal epidural steroid injections can be used to reduce pain at 3 months. However, a clear improvement in physical disability and reduction in surgery was not consistently observed in the literature. Cervical epidural steroid injections have similarly been shown to provide short-term relief for radicular cervical pain, but long-term outcomes have not been well studied. Limited high-quality evidence suggests that cervical epidural steroid injections can be beneficial for radiculitis secondary to disc herniation and discogenic pain. Most studies focus on interlaminar cervical epidural steroid injections instead of transforaminal cervical epidural steroid injections.
