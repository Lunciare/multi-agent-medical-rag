Near the liver, both layers attach firmly to the anterior surface, constituting the base of the ligament. The right layer continues horizontally along the anterior margin as the peritoneal lining of the anterior abdominal wall. This portion extends as parietal peritoneum along the right anterolateral abdominal wall and forms the right paracolic gutter before transitioning into the visceral peritoneum covering the ascending colon. Approaching the liver, the right layer becomes the visceral peritoneum, which lines the anterosuperior surface of the liver. This layer then forms the superior layer of the coronary ligament and reflects onto the diaphragm as the right triangular ligament. The region between the superior and inferior layers of the coronary ligament lacks peritoneal attachment, creating the bare area of the liver (see Image .
