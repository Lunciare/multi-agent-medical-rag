In patients with unilateral goiter and with no suspicion of malignancy, thyroid lobectomy can be performed with low recurrence rates. On the other hand, hyperthyroid patients and patients with thyroid cancer should be treated with total thyroidectomy. To achieve a substernal goiter excision, an extended Kocher incision is usually performed in the lower cervical region, providing sufficient exposure to the goiter. The excision can be extended through a midline sternotomy. Should a lateral neck dissection be performed, a modified, extended Kocher incision is executed. During a total thyroidectomy, it is often useful to commence with mobilization of the smaller lobe to facilitate the excision of the whole gland and the identification of the recurrent laryngeal nerve and the parathyroid glands. Preservation of the parathyroid glands, especially the superior ones, is of great importance. In cases of very large goiters, the sternothyroid muscles can be divided unilaterally or bilaterally to help identify and ligate the middle thyroid veins, preserve the anatomical structures, and mobilize the goiter.
