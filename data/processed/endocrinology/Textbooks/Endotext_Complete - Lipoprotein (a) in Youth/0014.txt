Given that a child’s medical history is often forgotten with time, it is essential that youth appreciate and articulate the importance of Lp(a) as a risk factor to their future health care providers and be aware that their children may acquire this risk factor. While the relative merits of screening and treating Lp(a) in the pediatric population may be debatable, what is irrefutable is that youth who enter adulthood with the lowest possible burden of ASCVD risk will have a much lower risk of developing ASCVD than those with multiple risk factors, including elevated Lp(a).
