This type is characterized by an increase in ductal tissue and vascularity. Fibrous gynecomastia is seen after a year duration and is noted to have more stromal fibrosis and few ducts. After one year, intermediate gynecomastia is present which is thought to be a progression from florid to fibrous. Histopathology Typical features include Proliferation of the stroma and ductules Loose stroma seen in acute cases and dense stroma with few ductules seen in chronic cases History and Physical Important factors in the history from the patient include onset and duration of gynecomastia, associated symptoms, problems with certain organ systems such as liver, renal, adrenal, prostate, pulmonary, testicular or thyroid. In some cases, the patient is found to have gynecomastia after undergoing an MRI or CT scan of the chest. A careful review of family history, genetic history, medications, and recreational drug use should also be taken into consideration. A complete and thorough physical exam should be done.
