Studies have shown that there are numerous risk factors for the development of high responsiveness, which include: diagnosis or family history of primary open-angle glaucoma (possibly due to the different expression of steroid receptors and isoforms in the TM), diabetes mellitus, high myopia, and, connective tissue disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis. The elderly population tends to be more susceptible, as are children less than six years of age. Studies have shown the odds ratio of developing SG in older patients to be as high as 1.72 after using topical cortisone eye drops. Pathophysiology SIG is considered a type of secondary open-angle glaucoma caused by increased resistance to the aqueous outflow at the TM level. In this condition, there is increased production and decreased destruction of the extracellular matrix of the TM. There is increased deposition of glycosaminoglycans, fibronectin, elastin, and type IV collagen and reduced activity of matrix metalloproteinases.
