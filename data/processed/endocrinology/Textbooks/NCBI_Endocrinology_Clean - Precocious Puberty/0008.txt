Precocious puberty is much more common in girls, with a female-to-male ratio of about 10:1. An early study published in 2005, based on data from a Danish national registry, estimated that 0.2% of females experienced some form of precocious puberty (CPP, PPP, or benign variants). In comparison, less than 0.05% of males were affected. The study showed a female predominance, roughly 20 to 23 cases per 10,000 girls, compared to fewer than 5 cases per 10,000 boys. More recent research indicates that CPP is around 15 times more common in girls than boys, with rates ranging from 29 per 100,000 girls in the United States to 92 per 100,000 girls in Denmark. Notably, PPP is diagnosed much less frequently than CPP; an Iranian case series (2012) found PPP in about 23% of children with precocious puberty. A Spanish observational study (2010) estimated the annual incidence of CPP to range from 0.02 to 1.07 cases per 100,000 people.
