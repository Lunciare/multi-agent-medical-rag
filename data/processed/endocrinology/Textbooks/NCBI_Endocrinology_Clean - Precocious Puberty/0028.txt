Outcomes depend on several factors, including the advancement of bone age, the age at which precocious puberty begins, the timing of treatment start, and the length of therapy. Two systematic reviews in the recent past suggested a height benefit of 0.63 (0.17-1.08) SDS and a mean difference (MD) of 4.83 cm (2.32-7.34 cm), compared to pretreatment predictions and untreated girls, respectively. The HPG axis returns to normal after the cessation of therapy, and these children typically experience a normal progression of puberty upon discontinuation of treatment. GnRH analog therapy is considered safe in terms of its long-term effects on fertility, weight, body mass index, and metabolic bone health outcomes. More data and longer-term follow-up are needed for some concerns regarding possible PCOS and the psychological effects of treatment versus no treatment. For patients with PPP, the prognosis depends on the underlying cause of the sex hormone exposure and whether it is treated effectively. Complications Complications can arise from precocious puberty and its associated conditions or the treatment itself.
