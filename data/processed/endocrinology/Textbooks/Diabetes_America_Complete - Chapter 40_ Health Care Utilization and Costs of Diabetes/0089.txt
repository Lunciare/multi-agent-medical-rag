Outpatient care accounted for the second largest propor­ tion of total direct medical costs in the United States (nearly $446 billion) (Figure 40.70) (20). Outpatient care includes visits to physician offices, hospital outpatient clinics, and emergency departments, as well as ambulatory services, home health care, and podiatry. Approximately 16% of the total dollars spent for outpatient care were incurred by people with diabetes ($70 billion), and 46% ($32 billion) of those costs were attributable to diabetes. People with diabetes spent about $43 billion for outpatient physician visits ($31.4 billion for physician office visits and $11.4 billion for hospital outpatient visits), $453 million for ambulance services, >$14 billion for emergency department visits, >$11 billion for home health care, and $458 million for podiatry (Table 40.48) (20). The proportion of outpatient costs attributable to diabetes increased with age, but the overall pattern was not as marked as it was for institutional care, as shown in Table 40.47 (20). People age <45 years were much more likely to incur costs related to emergency department use and podiatric care compared to other outpatient services use for that age group.
