In these cases, performing a CT with IV contrast optimized for imaging the pituitary (sella protocol) is the next best option. It is important to note that CT has the additional disadvantage of ionizing radiation, whereas MRI does not. Thus, patients screened for pituitary abnormalities should receive an MRI rather than a CT whenever possible. Furthermore, precontrast CT imaging offers little additional diagnostic value; thus, avoiding precontrast pituitary imaging is recommended. It is noteworthy to mention that patients with intracranial lesions often require multiple follow-up imaging studies, for which clinicians should consider the patient’s anticipated cumulative radiation dose. Magnetic Resonance MRI is the ideal imaging modality for assessing nearly all pituitary lesions. MRIs do not require ionizing radiation like CT or traditional radiography use and instead can generate tissue contrast based on differences in the magnetic properties of hydrogen atomic nuclei. MRI can easily differentiate between solid and cystic lesions and also between different soft tissues, such as gray and white matter.
