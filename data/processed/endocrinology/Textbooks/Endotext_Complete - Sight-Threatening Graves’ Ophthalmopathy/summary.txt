Sight-Threatening Graves’ Ophthalmopathy
KEYWORDS: corneal, graves, orbital, optic, treatment, don, eye, ophthalmopathy, closure, patients, severe, table, vision, surgery, disease

CLINICAL RECOGNITION Ophthalmopathy may develop any time in the course of Graves’ disease, or infrequently in association with primary thyroid failure or apparent Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, and is infrequently accompanied by thyroid dermopathy. Graves’ ophthalmopathy (GO) is usually mild to moderately severe, and about 75% of Graves’ patients apparently have no ocular involvement. However, GO may be sight threatening in 1-2% of cases. The latter represent an emergency requiring immediate treatment. GO-related sight loss may be due to corneal breakdown or, more frequently to dysthyroid optic neuropathy (DON). Corneal involvement and/or DON require an urgent referral to specialists.
