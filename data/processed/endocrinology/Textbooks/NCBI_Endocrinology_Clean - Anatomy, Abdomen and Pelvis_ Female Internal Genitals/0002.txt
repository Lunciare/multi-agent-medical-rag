Anteversion is the angulation between the long axis of the cervix and the vagina, which nears a right angle. Anteflexion is the angulation between the body's long axis and the cervix, an obtuse angle of around 120° to 125°. In an anteverted-anteflexed position, the uterine body sits within the vesicouterine space. In rare cases, the uterus may be retroverted-retroflexed, causing the uterine body to be within the pouch of Douglas. The shape of the uterus has been described as "pyriform" on examination in a sagittal plane. The uterus has 4 parts: the fundus, body, isthmus, and cervix. The uterine body is the largest portion, while the uterine isthmus is where the uterine body meets the cervix. The fundus is the part of the uterus above the opening of the fallopian tubes into the uterine cavity. The uterine cavity has a lining membrane called the endometrium.
