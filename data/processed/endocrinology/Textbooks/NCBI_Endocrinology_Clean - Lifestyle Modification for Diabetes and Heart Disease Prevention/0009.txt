A meta-analysis of 6 studies that used the DASH diet determined that the risk of coronary artery disease was reduced by 21%, and the risk of stroke was also reduced by 21%, demonstrating that the DASH diet has the potential to improve the risk of developing these 2 conditions significantly. Recent trials have demonstrated beneficial effects on blood pressure and improvements in other cardiovascular risk factors; and although the DASH diet was initially targeted toward patients with hypertension, a meta-analysis of various studies using it has determined that patients following it had a 20% risk reduction for developing T2DM. Overall, vegetarian diets of various types are associated with a lower prevalence of diabetes. A subset of the Adventist Health Study-2, which included over 60,000 subjects, showed a lower prevalence of T2DM in vegetarians than nonvegetarians. Vegans (2.9%), ovo-lacto (3.2%), pesco (4.8%), and semi (6.1%) all had a lower prevalence than nonvegetarians (7.6%).
