Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease T2DM is closely associated with other medical conditions, such as hypertension, obesity, and hypercholesterolemia, which together form portions of what is known as metabolic syndrome. Patients with metabolic syndrome are more likely to develop cardiovascular conditions and, therefore, have a higher mortality risk. As one of the leading causes of death in the United States, CVD prevention methods have been studied consistently over time. Various studies have determined that the lifestyle factors that influence cardiovascular disease the most are an individual's diet, physical activity, and tobacco use. Diet greatly influences the management and potential progression of CVD. Research has found that once coronary artery disease has been established, diet plays an integral role in furthering plaque deposition and influences endothelium reactivity, where both of these factors can largely predict cardiac events in the future.
