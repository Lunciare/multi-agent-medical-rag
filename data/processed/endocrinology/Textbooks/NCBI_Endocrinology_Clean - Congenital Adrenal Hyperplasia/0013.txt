Certain variants are associated with a syndromic presentation, such as Antley–Bixler syndrome, which involves skeletal abnormalities in both sexes. These features may include craniosynostosis, brachycephaly, midface hypoplasia, proptosis, choanal stenosis, radiohumeral or radioulnar synostosis, bowed femora, and arachnodactyly, appearing in various combinations. Antenatal maternal virilization is another potential manifestation. G=glucocorticoids, M=mineralocorticoids, S=sex hormones. Histopathology The histological features of CAH include: Adrenal cortex hyperplasia. Disorganized architecture of the adrenal medulla and cortex without distinct zones. Lipoid deposits representing cholesterol and cholesterol esters that fail to enter mitochondria for steroid synthesis (observed in lipoid CAH cases). Hypertrophy of the juxtaglomerular apparatus in cases of salt-wasting. History and Physical The symptoms of CAH are mentioned below.
