Newer Treatment Options Current research is focused on developing more physiological glucocorticoid replacements, as standard therapies often fail to control excess androgen production and pose the risk of supraphysiological steroid exposure. This highlights an unmet need for new treatments that can prevent excessive androgen production without requiring higher doses of steroids. Modified-release hydrocortisone was evaluated in a recent phase-3 trial in adults. Although the study did not achieve its primary end point of improved control of 24-hour 17-OHP levels at 24 weeks, it demonstrated potential for dose reduction during an 18-month extension, as well as patient-reported benefits, including restoration of menses and a higher pregnancy rate. Continuous subcutaneous hydrocortisone infusion (CSHI) was evaluated in a phase-2 trial involving difficult-to-treat adults. After 6 months of CSHI, there was a reduction in morning and 24-hour levels of 17-OHP, androstenedione, ACTH, and progesterone compared to baseline.
