Etiology The etiology of SPENs remains unclear, but the hypothesis with the most convincing evidence thus far is that SPENs originate from the pluripotent cells of the genital ridges that adhere to the pancreas during early embryogenesis. Features supporting this theory include the sex and age distribution, the absence of pancreatic markers, and the expression of sex hormone receptors. Some SPENs tend to regress after menopause, further supporting this hypothesis. An overwhelming majority of SPENs carry mutations in CTNNB1 , which is the gene encoding ß-catenin, thus resulting in its nuclear accumulation. A rare association between SPEN and familial adenomatous polyposis has also been documented in existing literature. Inactivation of the adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) gene and subsequent activation of the Wnt/ß-catenin pathway likely explain this association. Epidemiology Solid pseudopapillary epithelial neoplasms account for 1% to 2% of exocrine pancreatic lesions and approximately 5% of cystic pancreatic lesions in adults. In pediatric populations, these tumors comprise between 6% and 17% of all pancreatic neoplasms. Recent literature describes a 7-fold increase in incidence over the past 2 decades.
