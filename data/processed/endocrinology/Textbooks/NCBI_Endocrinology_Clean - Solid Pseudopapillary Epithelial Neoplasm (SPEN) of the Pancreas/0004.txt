Cercariform cells represent a consistent cytologic finding and serve as a key distinguishing feature from pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. Mitotic activity remains uncommon. Features suggesting malignant transformation include exophytic growth pattern, incomplete encapsulation, calcifications, invasion of pancreatic parenchyma or neurovascular structures, and metastases. Immunohistochemical analysis typically demonstrates positivity for ß-catenin, the dot-like pattern of CD99, vimentin, neuron-specific enolase, alpha-1-antitrypsin, and alpha-1-antichymotrypsin. Identification of CTNNB1 mutations alone often suffices for diagnostic confirmation. These neoplasms do not demonstrate elevated tumor markers, eg, cancer antigen 19-9 or carcinoembryonic antigen. History and Physical SPENs typically have a long asymptomatic period before developing any signs or symptoms. Most often, due to the indolent nature of these tumors, they are incidentally diagnosed on physical examination or upon obtaining imaging studies for other medical reasons. When patients are symptomatic, their symptoms result from the SPEN compressing adjacent organs. Symptoms, when present, are nonspecific and include abdominal pain or discomfort, diarrhea, or nausea. Rarely, jaundice or gastric outlet obstruction may develop depending on the location of the SPEN. To date, no endocrine functional imbalances have been associated with these tumors.
