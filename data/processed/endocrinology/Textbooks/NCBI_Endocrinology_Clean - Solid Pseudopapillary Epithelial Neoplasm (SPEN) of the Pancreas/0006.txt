Pathology Studies A preoperative pathological diagnosis is best obtained via endoscopic ultrasound-guided biopsy and remains the diagnostic gold standard. Different regions of this heterogeneous tumor have been found to have varying pathological and immunohistochemical patterns; therefore, biopsies must be taken from several sites to ensure an accurate diagnosis. Treatment / Management Surgical parenchyma-preserving enucleation frequently provides curative treatment and represents the preferred therapeutic approach for solid pseudopapillary epithelial neoplasms. In select cases, tumors are considered unresectable because of invasion or encasement of major surrounding arteries, including the aorta, superior mesenteric artery, or celiac artery. Under such circumstances, alternative therapeutic modalities warrant consideration, including radiofrequency ablation, gamma-knife treatment, arterial embolization, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy. Approximately 75% of these tumors express estrogen and progesterone receptors, supporting a potential role for systemic tamoxifen therapy when surgical resection cannot be pursued.
