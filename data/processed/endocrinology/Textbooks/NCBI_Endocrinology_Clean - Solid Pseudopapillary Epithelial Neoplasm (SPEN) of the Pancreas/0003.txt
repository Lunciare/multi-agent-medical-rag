While this trend may represent a true rise in case frequency, multiple authors attribute the increase to advances in imaging modalities and improved diagnostic techniques. A marked female predominance characterizes these neoplasms, with a reported female-to-male ratio of 10:1. The age at detection ranges from 8 to 67 years, with an average age of 28.5 years at diagnosis. In male patients, diagnosis typically occurs earlier, with a reported mean age of 18.5 years. Histopathology On gross examination, solid pseudopapillary epithelial neoplasms appear large and well encapsulated, with a variegated cut surface that includes foci of necrosis, hemorrhage, and cystic change. Vascular ischemia has been proposed as the primary explanation for this heterogeneous appearance. Microscopic evaluation demonstrates marked regional variation in cellular architecture. The solid components consist of sheets or cords of homogeneous cells interspersed with numerous blood vessels. Widespread areas of cystic degeneration are common, and pseudopapillary formations with pseudorosettes are pathognomonic features of these neoplasms. Detachment of tumor cells from supporting blood vessels leads to the development of the pseudopapillary pattern.
