Medical Oncology There is no indication for chemotherapy for IPMN as it is considered a premalignant condition. Currently, surgery is the mainstay of therapy. If an invasive cancer component is identified after surgery then the patient needs adjuvant chemotherapy. The chemotherapy would be gemcitabine, or gemcitabine plus capecitabine or modified FOLFIRINOX for 6 months Staging Staging needs to be completed with CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast. Prognosis The 5-year survival for IPMNs is about 45 to 50% in comparison to pancreatic adenocarcinoma's 5-year survival of 8% . Complications After surgery, complications such as bleeding, anastomotic leak, abscess, or death can occur. Pancreatic enterocutaneous fistulas are a dreaded complication of pancreatic surgery for which to be aware. Typically, these are treated with total parenteral nutrition (TPN), nothing by mouth, and drainage and possibly somatostatin analogs . Postoperative and Rehabilitation Care Early ambulation is recommended postoperatively. Drain placed at surgery should be removed after the oral diet is advanced and the patient tolerates a regular diet without an increase of drain output.
