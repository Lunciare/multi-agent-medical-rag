In adolescents, both hyperandrogenism and ovulatory dysfunction must be present for a diagnosis of PCOS. However, ultrasound and AMH testing are not recommended in this population due to their low specificity. Additionally, chronic oligo-ovulation or anovulation may be established based on the history of irregular cycles. Notably, ovulatory dysfunction can still occur with regular cycles; therefore, if diagnostic confirmation of anovulation is needed, serum progesterone levels should be measured. Excluding disorders that can mimic the clinical features of PCOS is also essential, including thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia, and atypical congenital adrenal hyperplasia due to 21-hydroxylase deficiency. For this last condition, a measurement of serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone is required, which may need to be followed by an adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) stimulation test.
