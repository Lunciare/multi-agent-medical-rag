Hence, ACSCs continuously repopulate the adrenal cortex upon stimulation by their trophic peptide hormone (ACTH1–39) along with the fact that ACSCs may be ‘invisible’ to the immune response owing to the lack of steroidogenic enzyme expression ( 68 ). This theory has been reinforced by the publication of cases with resolution in patients with autoimmune Addison’s disease without or with treatment with high doses of ACTH1–24 (1 mg tetracosactide) or depletion of B-lymphocytes with iv rituximab (1 g) ( 2 ), ( 3 , 69 ). Allogeneic adrenocortical cell transplantation by the production of adrenal-like steroidogenic cells from either stem cells or lineage conversion of differentiated cells, or by as organoids developed for islet transplants and may be ideal as adrenal transplantation ( 69 ). Gene therapy to replace or repair a defective gene to treat CAH or other defective genes are novel treatments to come. Particularly for CAH a number of novel drugs have been introduced. The corticotrophin-releasing factor type 1 receptor (CRF1) antagonists crinecerfont or tildacerfont, lowered androstenedione and allowed GC dose reduction.
