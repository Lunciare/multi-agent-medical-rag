The mortality rates from LA are high and associated with higher lactic acid levels. Based on data from National Hospital Discharge Surveys 2001–2010, LA accounts for 1.2% of all hospitalizations in diabetic patients (Table 17.4). PREVENTION AND TREATMENT LA is usually associated with unexpected and catastrophic hypoxic events and is therefore less likely to be amenable to preventive measures. Long-term preven­ tion of cardiovascular disease or sepsis among diabetic patients through improved glucose control and alteration of other risk factors could decrease the incidence of LA. The only effective treatment for LA is cessation of lactic acid production by the improvement of tissue oxygenation. Treatment of underlying conditions, such as shock or myocardial infarc­ tion, includes restoration of the fluid volume, improvement of cardiac function, amelioration of sepsis, and correction of hyperglycemia (104). HYPOGLYCEMIA PATHOGENESIS Hypoglycemia is the most common, life-threatening acute complication of diabetes treatment. It is characterized by multiple risk factors and complex patho­ physiology (105).
