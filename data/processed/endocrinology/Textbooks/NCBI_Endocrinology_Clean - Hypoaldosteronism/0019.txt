Most patients with hypoaldosteronism postadrenalectomy for Conn syndrome resolve early, while some at high risk may need longer than 6-month surveillance. Complications In patients treated for hypoaldosteronism, overdosage with fludrocortisone can cause fluid retention, low potassium, significantly suppressed plasma renin concentration, and, ultimately, can even precipitate congestive heart failure. Less severe but clinically significant adverse effects include constipation due to increased colonic mucosal sodium and water reabsorption, weight gain due to fluid retention, and muscle weakness due to hypokalemia. Dosing should be titrated to plasma renin concentration levels in the upper limits of the normal range. Undertreated children will have poor growth. When the diagnosis is delayed in infants with severe disease, mortality is increased.
