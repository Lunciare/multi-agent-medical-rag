Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Umbilical Cord
KEYWORDS: umbilical, cord, umbilical cord, fetal, blood, vessels, placental, arteries, vein, umbilical vein, tunica, flow, artery, placenta, vascular

Cord Length An umbilical cord is considered abnormally short when its length measures less than 40 cm. A markedly short cord may result in premature placental separation, which can interrupt fetal circulation and lead to intrauterine hemorrhage and fetal death. By contrast, an umbilical cord longer than 65 to 70 cm is considered excessively long. A long umbilical cord may encircle the fetal neck, increasing the risk of adverse outcomes. A long umbilical cord may also descend into the cervix during malpresentation, leading to umbilical cord prolapse. Omphalocele Also known as exomphalos, an omphalocele is a congenital abdominal wall defect in which bowel and, at times, other abdominal organs herniate into a membranous sac at the base of the umbilical cord. This anomaly results from the failure of the physiological umbilical herniation to resolve during development. Surgical correction is typically undertaken in the neonatal period to prevent complications, such as intestinal obstruction. Umbilical Cysts Umbilical cord cysts are classified as either true cysts or pseudocysts. These lesions are typically identified near the fetal insertion site of the umbilical cord and are most commonly detected during the 1st trimester. Most resolve spontaneously by the end of the 12th week of gestation.
