Physiologic Variants Umbilical Cord Coiling Patterns One of the most frequent morphological variations of the umbilical cord involves differences in its helical coiling pattern. The 4 principal types include rope (the most common), undulating, segmented, and linked configurations. The degree of coiling is quantified by the umbilical cord index, typically measuring around 0.2 coils/cm. Hypercoiling is defined by an umbilical cord index exceeding 0.3 coils/cm and occurs in approximately 6% to 21% of all pregnancies. Abnormal coiling patterns are strongly associated with fetal vascular obstruction, a condition that may lead to complications such as fetal thrombi, avascular villi, and villous stromal vascular karyorrhexis. False Knots of the Umbilical Cord False knots appear as bulging masses along the surface of the umbilical cord. These formations result from redundant loops or folds of the umbilical vessels within the cord, commonly referred to as "folds of Hoboken."
