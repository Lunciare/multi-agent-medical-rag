ATI is then converted to angiotensin II (ATII) by angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) and other non-ACE pathways. ATII is the principal vasoactive peptide in the RAAS and acts on 2 receptors: angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) and type 2 (AT2). ATII activation of AT1 receptors increases blood pressure by inducing vascular smooth muscle contraction, elevating systemic vascular resistance, and enhancing sympathetic activity. This pathway also promotes sodium and water retention through increased sodium reabsorption in the proximal convoluted tubule. ATII directly mediates sodium reabsorption in this region and is indirectly promoted by increased adrenal cortical aldosterone production, which enhances distal sodium reabsorption. Chronically elevated ATII levels contribute to the growth and proliferation of smooth and cardiac muscle cells, endothelial dysfunction, platelet aggregation, increased inflammatory responses, and apoptosis. Conversely, ATII binding to AT2 receptors leads to vasodilation, primarily through increased nitric oxide and bradykinin production. Additionally, AT2 receptor activation promotes renal sodium excretion and exerts antiproliferative and cardiovascular protective effects. RAAS system blockade can occur at multiple levels.
