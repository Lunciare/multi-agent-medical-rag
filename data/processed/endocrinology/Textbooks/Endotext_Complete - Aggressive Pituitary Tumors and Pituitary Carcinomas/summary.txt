Aggressive Pituitary Tumors and Pituitary Carcinomas
KEYWORDS: pituitary, pubmed, apt, tumor, tumors, patients, endocrinol, aggressive, tmz, treatment, response, adenomas, expression, article pubmed, article

ABSTRACT Aggressive pituitary tumors (APT) refer to pituitary adenomas exhibiting radiological invasiveness and an unusually rapid tumor growth rate, or clinically relevant tumor growth despite optimal standard treatments. Pituitary carcinomas (PC) are defined by non-contiguous craniospinal or distant metastasis. PC is exceedingly rare, comprising only 0.1-0.4% of all pituitary neoplasms, whereas APT accounts for up to 15% of all pituitary neoplasms depending on the definition used. Typically evolving from known pituitary macroadenomas, APT/PC is most frequently diagnosed in the fifth decade of life, with corticotroph and lactotroph neoplasms predominating. Diagnosis relies on MRI, hormonal studies and histological assessment, including Ki67 index and immunohistochemistry for pituitary hormones and now also transcription factors. Structural and molecular mechanisms have been proposed in the pathogenesis of APT/PC, and the genetic basis of APT/PC development is a particularly growing field.
