Portal vein tributaries anastomose with IVC tributaries at different anatomical sites , giving rise to the portocaval anastomosis. These clinically relevant sites are found at the distal esophagus, hepatic bare areas, umbilicus, falciform ligament (accessory portal system of Sappey), ligamentum venosum fissure, behind the peritoneum, lower rectum, and anal canal. The pelvic venous outflow arises from the confluence of adjoining veins in the pelvic area. The various plexuses mainly drain into the internal iliac vein, with some draining into the superior rectal vein before reaching the hepatic portal system. Another minor venous drainage path for women is the ovarian vein. The superior gluteal veins are the largest tributaries of the internal iliac vein. Nerves The thoracoabdominal nerves are the primary innervation of the anterolateral abdominal wall. These nerves originate from the T7-T11 spinal nerves and supply the anterolateral abdominal wall musculature and overlying skin. The lateral cutaneous branches arise from the T7-T9 segments, innervating the skin over the right and left hypochondriac regions.
