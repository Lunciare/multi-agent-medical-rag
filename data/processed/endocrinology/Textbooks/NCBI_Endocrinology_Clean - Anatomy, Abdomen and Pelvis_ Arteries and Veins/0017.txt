Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Arteries and Veins
KEYWORDS: artery, arteries, abdominal, iliac, inferior, veins, branches, internal, superior, aorta, supply, paired, anterior, renal, blood

levator ani supports the abdominopelvic viscera and helps maintain urinary and fecal continence. The abdominal wall is composed of 5 paired muscles. The external and internal obliques and the transversus abdominis are nearly horizontal and horizontal muscles, respectively, while the rectus abdominis and pyramidalis are vertically oriented muscles covered by the rectus sheath. In men, additional paired cremaster muscles originate from the lower abdomen and enter the scrotum. These muscles are responsible for the cremasteric reflex seen when lightly stroking the medial thigh. Physiologic Variants Approximately 20% of the population has an accessory obturator artery arising from the inferior epigastric artery and descending into the pelvis along the route of the pubic branch. A rare variation involves the inferior phrenic artery, superior suprarenal artery, and inferior suprarenal artery originating as a common trunk from the right renal artery. The corona mortis is a vascular variant of the anastomotic branch between the inferior epigastric and obturator vessels in the obturator canal. Another variation includes the accessory obturator artery arising from the external iliac artery and forming a connection with the obturator artery originating from the internal iliac artery. Knowledge of abdominal blood vessel variants is essential during diagnostic and surgical procedures.
