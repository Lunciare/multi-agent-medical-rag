This pathway is a possible metastatic route from primary prostatic or ovarian cancers to vertebral or cranial sites. Liver cirrhosis may lead to portal hypertension and esophageal variceal bleeding. Severe hemorrhage may arise at the portocaval anastomosis at the distal esophagus. Another significant site for portocaval anastomosis occurs at the distal rectum and anal canal, where pathological variations can lead to hemorrhoids. Clinical Significance Renal artery stenosis can reduce renal blood flow and increase renin secretion. This hormone converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, which forms angiotensin II with subsequent cleavage. The resulting vasoconstriction increases renal artery pressure, producing renal hypertension, a condition that must be considered when evaluating hypertension.
