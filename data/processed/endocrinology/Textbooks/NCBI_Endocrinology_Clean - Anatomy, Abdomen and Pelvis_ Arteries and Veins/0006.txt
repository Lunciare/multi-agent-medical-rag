This large artery arises at the T12-L1 level and supplies the foregut, comprising the esophagus, stomach, proximal half of the duodenum, gallbladder, superior pancreas, and liver. The celiac trunk lies posterior to the stomach and lesser omentum. The 3 major branches of the celiac trunk are the common hepatic, left gastric, and splenic arteries. The common hepatic artery further divides into the proper hepatic, right gastric, and gastroduodenal arteries. The paired middle suprarenal arteries are the third abdominal aortic branches, followed by the unpaired SMA. The SMA provides oxygenated blood to the midgut, which includes the distal duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, appendix, ascending colon, right colic flexure, and proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon. The fifth branches are the paired renal arteries, which give rise to the inferior suprarenal arteries. The renal arteries arise at the L1-L2 level and supply the kidneys on either side.
