Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Arteries and Veins
KEYWORDS: artery, arteries, abdominal, iliac, inferior, veins, branches, internal, superior, aorta, supply, paired, anterior, renal, blood

Introduction The abdominopelvic cavity is confined superiorly by the diaphragm and extends inferiorly into the pelvis. This cavity houses most digestive organs and parts of the urogenital system. The abdominopelvic vasculature is complex and supplies various organs and tissue layers across many planes. Physiologic variation among vessels introduces diversity among patient populations. Pathologies affecting the abdominopelvic vasculature, such as atherosclerosis, aneurysms, thrombosis, and vascular malformations, can have profound clinical implications. Surgeons regularly need to manipulate and manage the vasculature during abdominopelvic surgeries. Understanding abdominopelvic anatomy is a critical component of clinical and surgical knowledge. Structure and Function The abdominal aorta supplies the abdomen's circulation (see Image . CT Scan L1-L2 Level). This large blood vessel is the thoracic aorta's furtherance into the abdominal compartment, piercing the diaphragm close to the 12th thoracic vertebra (T12) and bifurcating around the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae (L4 and L5). The abdominal aorta provides a vast network of paired and unpaired branches that supply the abdomen, some of which bifurcate inferiorly to supply the pelvis and lower limbs. Abdominal venous drainage occurs by the inferior vena cava (IVC) and its tributaries. Portal venous blood passes through the liver and finally drains into the IVC.
