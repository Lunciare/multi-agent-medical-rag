Post stroke : Stroke survivors have a higher risk of osteoporosis and fractures related to bone loss from immobilization and neurological disability. Gastrointestinal Diseases Inflammatory bowel disease : Patients suffering from inflammatory bowel disease with either Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis have a significantly higher risk for osteoporosis and fractures than the general population. Celiac disease : Gluten enteropathy results in malabsorption and diarrhea with weight loss, ultimately leading to an increased risk of bone loss and osteoporosis. Chronic liver disease :(+/- hepatitis): There is a higher prevalence of fractures and osteoporosis reported in patients awaiting a liver transplant. Cholestatic liver disease : Cholestatic liver disease has a high risk for fractures and osteoporosis. Non-cholestatic liver disease : Chronic viral hepatitis, including chronic hepatitis B and C, is the most common type of non-cholestatic liver disease, and there is a high prevalence of osteoporosis reported in this population. Nutritional Conditions Anorexia nervosa : Patients with anorexia nervosa have decreased peak bone mass, impaired bone microarchitecture, and a high risk for osteoporosis.
