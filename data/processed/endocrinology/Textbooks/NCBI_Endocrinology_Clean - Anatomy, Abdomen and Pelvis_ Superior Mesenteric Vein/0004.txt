As the vitelline vein continues to regress within this mesentery, a tissue cleft can appear within the mesentery along the superior mesenteric artery. This tissue cleft is observed to be communicating with the regressing left vitelline vein at the superior end of the peritoneal folds. The superior mesenteric vein is considered to develop from this tissue cleft. Rather than the vitelline vein being the primitive basis for the superior mesenteric vein, this study suggests the only contributions of the vitelline vein is the portion of the portal vein at the conflux of the superior mesenteric and splenic veins. Physiologic Variants Often, surgeons request preoperative CT angiography to assess for any existing anatomical variations as well as accurate imagery of the disease process relative to the mesenteric vasculature in patients undergoing intra-abdominal procedures. Imaging studies have shown that there can be a single main trunk of the superior mesenteric vein, which possesses a variable-length (5 to 50 mm) before dividing into the right and left intestinal branches.
