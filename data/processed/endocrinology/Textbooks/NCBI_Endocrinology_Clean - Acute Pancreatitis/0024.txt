Evaluation for chronic pancreatic injury, including signs of malabsorption (eg, steatorrhea) or new-onset diabetes mellitus, is appropriate. Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy may be warranted in select cases. Fluid Resuscitation Early fluid resuscitation is a cornerstone of acute pancreatitis treatment. While aggressive hydration was previously recommended, the 2022 Early Weight-Based Aggressive versus Nonaggressive Goal-Directed Fluid Resuscitation in the Early Phase of Acute Pancreatitis (WATERFALL) trial demonstrated that early aggressive fluid administration, defined as a 20 mL/kg bolus followed by 3 mL/kg/hr, was associated with an increased risk of fluid overload without clinical benefit. This finding prompted a paradigm shift toward moderate, individualized resuscitation guided by frequent reassessment. Lactated Ringer solution is the preferred fluid. Resuscitation goals include maintaining urine output above 0.5 mL/kg per hour and normalizing heart rate and hematocrit. Key parameters such as blood urea nitrogen, hematocrit, lactate, and urine output should be regularly monitored to guide ongoing fluid therapy. Pain Management Hydromorphone and fentanyl are preferred. Meperidine is avoided due to neurotoxicity risk.
