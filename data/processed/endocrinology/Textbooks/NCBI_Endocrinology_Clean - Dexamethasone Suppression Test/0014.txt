Intravenous Dexamethasone Suppression Test Cushing syndrome is diagnosed if the day 2 serum cortisol level is greater than 20% of the baseline value or greater than 4.7 mcg/dL (130 nmol/L) with a sensitivity of 100% and specificity of 96%. In addition, in Cushing disease, unlike other causes of Cushing syndrome, the serum cortisol level at the end of the infusion on day 1 typically shows greater than 70% suppression from the baseline, followed by rebound hypercortisolism in 24 hours. Dexamethasone Suppressed Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone Test Serum cortisol levels greater than 1.4 mcg/dL (39 nmol/L) at 15 minutes suggest Cushing disease with a 90% to 100% sensitivity and 50% to 100% specificity. Raising the cut-off value to greater than 3.8 mcg/dL (87 nmol/L) increases the specificity to 100% at the cost of a slightly reduced sensitivity at 94%. The cumbersomeness of this test limits its extensive application in the ambulatory setting. Clinical Significance Iatrogenic hypercortisolism is the most common cause of Cushing syndrome.
