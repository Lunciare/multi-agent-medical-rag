ENVIRONMENT X ENVIRONMENT INTERACTIONS Environmental exposures may also interact, or act in concert, with other environmental exposures; however, examples of these observations are not prevalent in the scientific literature. DAISY (322) observed that a greater number of gastrointestinal illnesses was associated with an increased risk of islet autoimmu­ nity, but only among children who were exposed to gluten-containing grains (wheat or barley) either age <4 months (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.22–1.55) or age ≥7 months (HR 1.12, 95% CI 1.05–1.19). Power to detect gene-environment or environment-environment interactions has been limited in studies reported so far. Table 11.5 provides a summary of the significant associations with diet described above, including gene-diet and infection-diet interactions. Prospective cohort studies have contrib­ uted enormously to the understanding of the natural history and risk factors for type 1 diabetes. A variety of exposures appear to trigger islet autoimmunity and to promote progression to clinical diabetes in some children; none of the current candidate risk factors seems to explain most of the risk.
