INTRODUCTION Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is an acute life-threatening emergency resulting from severe metabolic decompensation. DKA more commonly affects individuals with type 1 diabetes (T1DM). Approximately 2/3 of adults admitted with DKA have T1DM. DKA is relatively common and in the US; 240,000 patients had a primary diagnosis of DKA in 2020. Early diagnosis and management of DKA is essential to improve outcomes. CLINICAL RECOGNITION Diabetic Ketoacidosis The diagnosis of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is based on the following three criteria: Hyperglycemia ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or a prior history of diabetes irrespective of the presenting glucose reading. Elevated ketone body concentration: venous or capillary β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L or in the early stages of DKA urine ketone 2+ or greater. Metabolic acidosis: pH<7.3 and/or bicarbonate concentration <18 mmol/L. The clinical manifestations of DKA are shown in Table 1 . Table 1.
