Similar effects were found in persons with clinical protein­ uria (Table 22.28) (628,629,630,631). The largest of these studies (632) randomized 82 persons with type 1 diabetes, severe albuminuria, and prerandomization GFR decline of 7.1 mL/min/year to usual-protein diet (1.02 g/kg/day, 95% CI 0.95–1.10) and reduced-protein diet (0.89 g/kg/day, 95% CI 0.83–0.95) (p=0.005) for a period of 4 years. At the end of the study, the CVD-adjusted risk of ESRD or death was 0.23 (95% CI 0.07–0.72, p=0.01) in the group receiving the reduced-protein diet compared with the group receiving the usual diet (Figure 22.53), suggesting that even moderate protein restriction, which moves intakes from excess toward the norm, provides benefits beyond conventional drug therapy in these persons.
