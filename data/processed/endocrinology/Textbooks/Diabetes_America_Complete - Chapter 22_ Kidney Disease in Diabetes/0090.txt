Individuals may remain asymp­ tomatic or develop flank pain and renal colic. Historically, the prevalence of renal papillary necrosis at autopsy is 20–30 times as great in persons with diabetes as in those without (698). Among persons with diabetes, it occurs bilaterally in half of the cases and is 2.5 times as frequent in women as in men (655,656,699). Moreover, persons with diabetes and acute pyelonephritis are at particularly high risk of renal papillary necrosis. In one study, 27% of diabetic subjects with renal papil­ lary necrosis at autopsy also had acute fulminant pyelonephritis (656). A review of the medical records of the 165 patients diagnosed with renal papillary necrosis at the Mayo Clinic between 1976 and 1992 showed that among patient groups with diabetes, analgesic abuse, or urinary tract infections, the diagnosis was more frequent in women than men (700). The 10-year survival rate from the time of diag­ nosis was significantly lower for patients with diabetes than without (44% vs. 77%), and progression to ESRD was more frequent in the presence of diabetes.
