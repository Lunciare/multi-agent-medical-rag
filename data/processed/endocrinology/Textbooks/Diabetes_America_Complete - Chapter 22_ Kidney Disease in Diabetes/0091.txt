Most risk factors for CKD are modifiable; therefore, public health strategies targeting these factors may significantly reduce the disease burden. Diabetes is the leading cause of CKD, and the increasing prevalence of diabetes together with improved availability of dialysis and transplants have sustained a continued rise in the proportion of CKD attributable to diabetes since the 1980s. In persons with type 1 diabetes, the inci­ dence of CKD has declined in parallel with a significant trend for earlier initiation of antihypertensive treatment following the onset of diabetes, expansion of RAAS inhibitor usage, and sustained improve­ ments in glycemic control.
