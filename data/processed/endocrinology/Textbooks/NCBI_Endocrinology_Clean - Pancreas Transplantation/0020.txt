Surveillance biopsies in such cases may be indicated. Biopsy of the graft allows the surgeon to identify rejection accurately, and therefore, it should be used for pancreas transplant monitoring. Clinical parameters that include hyperglycemia, C-peptide level, hemoglobin A1C, serum amylase, lipase, and urinary amylase in case of bladder drainage can not be used alone, and these are not very helpful because they are either too late or nonspecific. The biopsies from the renal graft alone in SPK patients don't determine the pathologic condition of the pancreatic graft. After SPK transplantation, the similarities between pancreas graft biopsies and kidney grafts are not 100%, and grafts can show different types and degrees of rejection. Results of a biopsy taken from a duodenal cuff and pancreas parenchymal biopsy can also differ. The most commonly performed biopsy technique for the pancreas transplant is a percutaneous ultrasound-guided biopsy. This can be done safely and effectively in bladder-drained and enterically drained transplants. Imaging plays a major role in assessing and evaluating thrombosis in the pancreatic graft.
