pancreas plays a key role in the digestion, absorption, utilization, and storage of energy substrates. It consists of 2 parts, the exocrine and endocrine pancreas, which are structurally distinct but functionally integrated glandular systems. Both parts arise from the primitive gut. Neural and hormonal signals control secretion by the exocrine pancreas through gastrointestinal peptides. Islet cells are interspersed in the exocrine pancreas. The exocrine part is important for nutrient digestion, and the endocrine is critical for regulating glucose homeostasis. The endocrine portion consists only of 2% of the pancreas and comprises different types of endocrine cells called islets of Langerhans. Each islet has at least 5 types of cells, which are insulin-producing β-cells (65% to 80%), the glucagon releasing α-cells (15% to 20%), the somatostatin producing δ-cells (3% to 10%), pancreatic polypeptide-containing PP cells (1%), and the ghrelin containing ε-cells (<1%). The exocrine pancreas comprises acinar and ductal cells, organized in lobular branched tissue architecture, which secrete and transport digestive enzymes into the duodenum.
