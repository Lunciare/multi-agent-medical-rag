SCN should be followed up on regularly because long-term data are rare, and recommendations are updated based on evidence. Complications Clinicians must evaluate the risks and benefits of surveillance imaging versus surgical resection. Pancreatic resections are associated with high morbidity (40%–60%) and mortality (2%–5%). Pancreatic surgical morbidity in the short and long term remains high, with pancreatic fistula formation and exocrine or endocrine (diabetes mellitus) pancreatic insufficiency reported in around one-third of cases. When SCNs are not resected, they rarely lead to complications that may warrant surgical intervention, such as pancreatitis, obstructive jaundice due to biliary obstruction, cholangitis, pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, or gastric outlet obstruction.
