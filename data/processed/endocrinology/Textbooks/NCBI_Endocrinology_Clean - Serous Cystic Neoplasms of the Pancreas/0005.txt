Pancreatic SCN are often detected incidentally on abdominal ultrasonography or cross-sectional imaging performed for another condition. This finding leads to a diagnostic evaluation. When patients are symptomatic, the most common presentation includes abdominal pain or a palpable mass in the upper abdomen. Less commonly, patients with large cystic lesions present similar to those with pancreatic ductal carcinoma, with abdominal pain, weight loss, and early satiety secondary to compression of the stomach wall by the adjacent pancreatic mass (gastric outlet obstruction). Rarely seen findings include obstructive jaundice due to obstruction of the biliary tree (may lead to acute cholangitis) and pancreatic duct obstruction causing pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, ductal dilatation, and recurrent pancreatitis. Single cystic lesions may be confused with pseudocysts, which share similar presentations and imaging characteristics. However, pseudocysts usually occur after an episode of acute pancreatitis or insidiously in cases of chronic pancreatitis and are frequently associated with pain.
