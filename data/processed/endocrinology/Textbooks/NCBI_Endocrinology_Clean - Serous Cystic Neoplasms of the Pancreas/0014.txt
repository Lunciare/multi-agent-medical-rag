Alternatively, splenic vessel preservation maintains normal vascular anatomy but requires meticulous dissection of the splenic artery and vein from the pancreatic parenchyma. Splenic preservation is contraindicated when tumor adherence to splenic vessels precludes safe dissection or when malignancy cannot be excluded. Enucleation and Parenchyma-Sparing Techniques Enucleation represents the ultimate parenchyma-sparing approach, removing only the tumor while preserving surrounding pancreatic tissue. This technique is most appropriate for small, well-encapsulated SCNs located peripherally with at least 2 to 3 mm separation from the main pancreatic duct on preoperative imaging. Intraoperative ultrasound confirms duct relationships and guides the dissection plane. The pseudocapsule surrounding SCNs typically facilitates enucleation, though the microcystic architecture may make complete removal challenging. Pancreatic fistula rates following enucleation range from 20% to 40%, reflecting the exposed parenchymal surface, though most fistulas are low-grade and managed conservatively. Enucleation preserves endocrine and exocrine function, avoiding diabetes and exocrine insufficiency that may follow formal resection, making it particularly attractive in younger patients.
