Perioperative mortality at experienced centers is less than 2% for distal pancreatectomy and 2% to 4% for pancreaticoduodenectomy. Major morbidity rates range from 20% to 40%, depending on procedure type and patient factors, with pancreatic fistula representing the predominant complication. Long-term survival following resection of benign SCNs is comparable to that of age-matched population controls. The exceedingly rare serous cystadenocarcinoma carries a more guarded prognosis, though outcomes remain better than pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma given its indolent biology. Recurrence following complete resection of benign SCNs is essentially unreported, and routine surveillance imaging is unnecessary unless histopathology reveals unexpected findings. Staging If a rare malignant SCN is diagnosed, staging is performed in accordance with the National Cancer Center Network pancreatic adenocarcinoma guidelines. Prognosis SCNs are benign with a low malignant potential (<1%). In a long-term follow-up study of patients with SCN, morphologic changes were observed in 17% of patients. Overall, 50% had tumor enlargement, and in the remaining cases, there was no change in size. The median growth rate was 0.29 cm per year. The prognosis for patients with SCNs is excellent.
