Endoscopic Ultrasound This modality readily identifies pancreatic cysts through high-resolution imaging. When endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) is combined with other imaging modalities, diagnostic accuracy is significantly improved, enabling high-quality imaging and the detection of small clusters of microcysts. EUS also allows for accurate diagnosis in cases misdiagnosed by other imaging modalities. EUS-guided fine-needle aspiration usually provides a definitive diagnosis; however, sensitivity may decrease when an adequate sample for analysis is not obtained. On EUS, serous cystadenoma typically presents as multiple small, anechoic cystic areas with thin septations. Because of the vascular nature of serous cystadenoma, aspirates are thin and sometimes bloody or may contain hemosiderin-laden macrophages. Cytologic smears usually show low cellularity, with cells having clear cytoplasm and well-defined borders. Pancreas Cyst Fluid Analysis This includes chemical analysis (amylase), tumor markers (carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen (CA 19-9), mucin), and cytology. Serous cystadenoma typically shows scant serous fluid, low viscosity, low amylase levels, and low CEA levels (<5-20 ng/mL), with variable pancreatic enzyme levels, including amylase isoenzymes and CA-125.
