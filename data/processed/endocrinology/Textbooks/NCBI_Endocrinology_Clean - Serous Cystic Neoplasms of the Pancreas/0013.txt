Serous Cystic Neoplasms of the Pancreas
KEYWORDS: pancreatic, cystic, cysts, lesions, scns, imaging, serous, tumors, neoplasms, patients, pancreatectomy, tumor, scn, cases, pseudocysts

Pancreatic body lesions Central pancreatectomy (middle segment pancreatectomy) offers a parenchyma-sparing option for tumors of the pancreatic body, resecting the involved segment while preserving the pancreatic head and distal tail. This technique maintains endocrine and exocrine function but creates two transection surfaces, resulting in pancreatic fistula rates of 30% to 50%. Patient selection is critical, favoring younger individuals with adequate pancreatic tissue on both sides of the lesion and a low tolerance for perioperative risk in potential fistula management. Distal pancreatectomy extending from the tumor to the tail provides a single anastomosis or staple line and lower fistula rates but sacrifices more functional parenchyma. The choice between these approaches requires individualized assessment of tumor location, patient age, comorbidities, and surgeon experience. Pancreatic tail lesions Distal pancreatectomy is the standard approach for SCNs in the pancreatic tail, with splenectomy or spleen preservation depending on tumor characteristics and vascular anatomy. Spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy avoids the immunologic and infectious complications of splenectomy and is preferred when technically feasible. The Warshaw technique ligates splenic vessels while preserving splenic perfusion through short gastric and left gastroepiploic collaterals. Alternatively, splenic vessel preservation maintains normal vascular anatomy but requires meticulous dissection of the splenic artery and vein
