Enucleation has been reported to be a safe option for well-selected patients with serous cystadenoma, preserving endocrine and exocrine pancreatic function, but it is rarely used. Differential Diagnosis Differential diagnosis of pancreatic cystic lesions is often challenging, particularly when relying solely on radiological studies. Pseudocysts represent the most common type of pancreatic cyst, whereas pancreatic cystic neoplasms are less frequent and are classified as neoplastic. Distinguishing benign serous lesions, MCNs, and malignant mucinous lesions, such as mucinous cystadenocarcinoma, can be complex. The use of radiologic, morphologic, and laboratory tests is critical for differentiating these lesions prior to resection and for evaluating their malignant potential. Accurate differentiation between pseudocysts and serous lesions is also crucial for planning appropriate surgical management. Rare cystic pancreatic lesions include cystic islet cell tumors, papillary cystic lesions, and lymphoepithelial cysts. The differential diagnosis for serous cystadenomas includes the following entities: Non-neoplastic pancreatic cysts include true cysts, retention cysts, mucinous non-neoplastic cysts, lymphoepithelial cysts, and pseudocysts.
