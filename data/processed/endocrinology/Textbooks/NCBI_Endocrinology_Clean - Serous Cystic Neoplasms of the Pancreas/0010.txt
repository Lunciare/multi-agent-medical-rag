Cytology reveals cuboidal cells with glycogen-rich cytoplasm. Combining these parameters helps classify these cysts and differentiate SCN from malignant or mucinous cysts. Cyst fluid DNA analysis may reveal a mutation in VHL among patients with VHL syndrome. Positive staining for epithelial markers (keratins) is seen, while staining for CA 19-9, chromogranin A, S100, and p53 is negative. Treatment / Management Nonsurgical management should be recommended in asymptomatic cases of confirmed pancreatic SCN. Surveillance imaging can be safely recommended given the slow growth rate of these cysts. There is a small risk of malignancy (<2%). Surgical resection should be considered in the management of symptomatic individuals due to rapidly growing lesions (cyst itself vs hemorrhage), large tumors causing mass effect, obstructive jaundice, pancreatic duct obstruction causing pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, or gastric outlet obstruction. Surgical resection should be considered when malignancy cannot be ruled out. The surgical approach depends on the location and may include distal pancreatectomy with or without splenectomy, central pancreatectomy, or pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple procedure).
