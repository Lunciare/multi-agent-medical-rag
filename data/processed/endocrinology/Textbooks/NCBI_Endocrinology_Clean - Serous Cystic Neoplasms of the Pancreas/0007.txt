In lesions with only a few millimeter-sized cysts (microcysts), the tumor may appear solid due to the numerous interfaces. Areas of calcification also appear hyperechoic with posterior acoustic shadowing. CT Scan SCNs are most commonly lobular. They appear hypodense on unenhanced CT because the tumor has a density similar to that of water. Calcifications are seen in up to 30% of serous cystadenomas; a "sunburst" pattern is less common. The central fibrous scar is hyperdense and is generally arranged in a stellate pattern known as a "stellate central scar." After iodinated contrast administration, the fibrous portions will enhance. Serous microcystic adenomas are considered the only hypervascular cystic pancreatic tumors; hence, their enhancement pattern is an important distinguishing feature. Lesions with fewer fibrous septations show fluid density regardless of contrast administration. Lesions composed primarily of microscopic cysts can appear solid and show homogeneous enhancement after contrast administration. In these cases, as in all other cystic pancreatic neoplasms, MRI can be used to further characterize the lesion.
