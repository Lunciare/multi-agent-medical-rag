Currently, there is no evidence that morphologic variants of SCNs confer any clinical or prognostic significance. All variants appear to share a similarly low risk of malignancy. Histopathology Pancreatic SCNs are typically unifocal, round, well-demarcated lesions that frequently exhibit a honeycombed appearance. The cysts are loculated, contain serous fluid devoid of mucin, and are lined by flattened or cuboidal epithelial cells. The absence of mucin is a key distinguishing feature, as mucin is commonly present in mucinous cysts and IPMNs, both of which carry a significant risk of malignancy. SCNs can exceed 20 cm in diameter. These lesions do not communicate with the pancreatic duct, resulting in cyst fluid with low amylase concentrations. A dense fibronodular scar, also referred to as a fibrous stellate scar, is often present at the center of the lesion and consists of acellular hyalinized tissue and small clusters of tiny cysts lined by a single layer of cuboidal epithelial cells.
