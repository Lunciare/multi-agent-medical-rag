In contrast, "true" cysts possess an epithelial lining, making them susceptible to malignant transformation. Additional types of pancreatic cystic masses include MCN, IPMN, cystic neuroendocrine neoplasm, solid-pseudopapillary neoplasm (also referred to as Hamoudi tumor), acinar-cell cystic neoplasm, and ductal adenocarcinoma with cystic degeneration. Etiology The etiology of SCNs remains poorly understood. Genetic mutations are implicated in its development and are the subject of ongoing research. Alterations in the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) pathway have been described, and SCN is observed in most patients with VHL syndrome. Results from recent studies indicate that inhibin, a gonadal hormone, may be expressed by these tumors and could assist in diagnosis. Detection of Inhibin in cyst fluid analysis strongly supports a diagnosis of SCN. Epidemiology SCNs account for approximately 30% of pancreatic cystic neoplasms. These lesions are predominantly benign and are more prevalent in women, with a female-to-male ratio of 2 to 3:1.
