Surgical Oncology Surgical Approaches by Tumor Location Pancreatic head and uncinate process lesions Tumors in the pancreatic head traditionally require pancreaticoduodenectomy (eg, Whipple procedure) or pylorus-preserving pancreaticoduodenectomy when resection is indicated. The classic Whipple procedure involves en bloc removal of the pancreatic head, duodenum, distal common bile duct, gallbladder, and distal stomach with reconstruction via pancreaticojejunostomy, hepaticojejunostomy, and gastrojejunostomy. Pylorus-preserving modifications maintain gastric function and may reduce dumping syndrome and marginal ulceration. Given the benign nature of SCNs, parenchyma-sparing alternatives should be considered in appropriately selected patients. Enucleation may be feasible for small, peripherally located lesions that are sufficiently distant from the main pancreatic duct, thereby preserving functional parenchyma and avoiding the morbidity of formal pancreaticoduodenectomy. Central pancreatectomy is another option for select neck lesions, though it carries a higher fistula rate. Pancreatic body lesions Central pancreatectomy (middle segment pancreatectomy) offers a parenchyma-sparing option for tumors of the pancreatic body, resecting the involved segment while preserving the pancreatic head and distal tail.
