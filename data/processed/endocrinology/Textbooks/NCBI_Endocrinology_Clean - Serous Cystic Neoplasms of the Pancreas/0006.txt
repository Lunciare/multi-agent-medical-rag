Evaluation This century, there has been a 20-fold increase in the detection of pancreatic cystic neoplasms, mainly through cross-sectional imaging such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Evaluation of SCNs relies on laboratory and imaging modalities to differentiate morphological and radiological patterns. The advent of imaging modalities has led to the identification of four morphological patterns of SCN: microcystic, macrocystic, mixed microcystic, and macrocystic with solid components. The microcystic pattern was defined as multiple cysts measuring less than 2 cm, separated by thin fibrous septa, resulting in a honeycomb appearance. Macrocystic type is diagnosed when cysts measuring 2 cm or more are present. Mixed microcystic and macrocystic types combine both microcystic and macrocystic patterns. Solid SCNs are serous tumors without any cystic lesions on cross-sectional imaging. Ultrasound Ultrasound is typically used as a screening examination, upon which SCN usually presents as a well-circumscribed, loculated lesion.
