Approximately 77% of individuals with VHL syndrome develop pancreatic serous cystic lesions. Coexistence with other tumors occurs in 10% to 15% of cases, most commonly with pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. Pathophysiology The pathophysiology of pancreatic SCNs is characterized by benign tumorigenesis, thought to result from the hyperplastic expansion of cuboidal or columnar epithelial cells resembling those of the fallopian tube. Although the precise etiology remains unclear, genetic mutations are also implicated. SCNs lack the specific KRAS and BRAF mutations commonly observed in other tumors, such as carcinomas. These tumors typically develop as cysts and papillae, often presenting as thin-walled, unilocular or multilocular masses filled with clear, watery fluid (see Image. Microscopic View of Cystadenoma). SCNs associated with VHL syndrome may involve the entire pancreas or present in a patchy distribution. The mixed serous neuroendocrine neoplasm, a rare form of serous cystadenoma, is associated with pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors and is also indicative of VHL syndrome. Currently, there is no evidence that morphologic variants of SCNs confer any clinical or prognostic significance.
