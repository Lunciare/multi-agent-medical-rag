Whether the cardiorenal benefits of SGLT2i seen in the general population can be extrapolated to the transplant population awaits further studies. Limited prospective and retrospective studies in the setting of solid organ transplantation showed that SGLT2i has a modest effect on glycemic control and a favorable effect on weight reduction ( 90 - 92 ). In a single-center, prospective, double-blind study consisting of 44 kidney transplant recipients with PTDM randomized to receive either empagliflozin (n=22) or placebo (n=22) for 24 weeks, a significant reduction in A1C was observed among empagliflozin-treated patients compared with their placebo-treated counterparts (-0.2% vs. 0.1%, p=0.025). A significant reduction in body weight was also observed (-2.5 kg vs. +1.0 kg, respectively p=0.014). There were no significant differences in adverse events, immunosuppressive drug levels, or eGFR between the two treatment groups ( 88 ). A small retrospective single-center observational study consisting of 97 heart transplant recipients with PTDM demonstrated that empagliflozin-based treatment (n=20) resulted in a significant reduction in body weight (p=0.05), BMI (p=0.04), mean furosemide dose (p=0.05), and systolic and diastolic blood pressure (p=0.03) compared with control (non-empaglifloxin-based treatment, n=77) at 12-month follow-up. There was a statistically nonsignificant mean reduction in A1C of 0.6%. No serious adverse events were observed ( 90 ). Based on the study findings the investigators suggest that SGLT-2 inhibitors are suitable for use following heart transplantation ( 93 ). In a multicenter propensity-matched cohort study consisting of 1063 pairs of heart transplant recipients with diabetes who received SGLT2i vs. those who did not (non-users), SGLT2i users exhibited a lower risk of dialysis requirement [hazard ratio (HR) (95% confidence interval [CI]): 0.566 (0.385-0.833)], graft rejection and failure [0.873 (0.774-0.985)], hospitalizations [0.822 (0.739-0.916)], and all-cause mortality [0.767 (0.627-0.938)] compared to non-users. The risks of post-transplantation infection or sepsis, ischemic heart disease, and worsening heart failure were comparable between the two treatment groups ( 94 ). Adverse effects associated with SGLT2i use include increased risk for urinary tract infections, genital candidiasis, euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis, and acute kidney injury. Similar to the general population, the use of SGLT2i in the transplant setting can cause an acute transient dip in eGFR, thought to be due to SGLT2i induced afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction.
