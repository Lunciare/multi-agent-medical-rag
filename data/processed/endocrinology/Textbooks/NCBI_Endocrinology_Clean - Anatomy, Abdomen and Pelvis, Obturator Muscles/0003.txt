The muscle inserts as a cylindrical tendon onto the greater trochanter of the femur. A cadaveric study highlighted that the obturator externus forms a musculotendinous junction at the level of the femoral neck and that the fibers at the inferior margin of the acetabulum act like a sling for the inferior portion of the femoral neck. Additionally, when placing the hip in abduction and external rotation, it was noted that the obturator externus was brought closer to the caudal border of the acetabulum. Together, these findings suggest that the obturator externus may provide some reinforcement to the hip joint. The location of the obturator externus has also been shown to protect the medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA). One study's results demonstrated that the obturator externus protected the deep branch of the MCFA from being damaged during hip dislocation in any direction. The obturator internus has 2 points of origin. It originates from both the posterior surface of the obturator membrane and the rami surrounding the obturator foramen and inserts onto the greater trochanter of the femur.
