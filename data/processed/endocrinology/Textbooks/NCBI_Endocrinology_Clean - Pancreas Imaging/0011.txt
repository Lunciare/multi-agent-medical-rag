Additionally, magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) can better visualize the pancreatic and biliary ducts and demonstrate any communication or obstruction related to a lesion (see Image. Chronic Pancreatitis via MRCP). A typical pancreatic MRI protocol includes T1- and T2-weighted images, with multiphase imaging after intravenous gadolinium administration. Pancreatitis evaluation in the acute setting is nearly always performed with computed tomography; however, MRI is comparable to computed tomography and can be useful in some complex cases or in patients who cannot receive iodinated contrast. Fat-suppressed T2-weighted sequences demonstrate more significant differentiation between pancreatic and peripancreatic tissues, making any peripancreatic inflammation much more conspicuous. T2-weighted images can also help distinguish simple-appearing pseudocysts from more complex walled-off necrosis. MRCP can demonstrate pancreatic and bile ducts and gallstone detection with higher sensitivity than computed tomography. Fat-suppressed, T1-weighted images reveal focal or diffuse enlargement of the pancreatic parenchyma with ill-defined borders, similar to findings on computed tomography.
