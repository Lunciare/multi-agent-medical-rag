Necrosis appears hypointense on T2-weighted images and hyperintense when liquified. Hemorrhage can be visualized via fat-suppressed T1 and T2-weighted images, but gradient-recalled echo sequences are also extremely sensitive to the magnetic properties of hemoglobin. Gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted images are best for assessing necrosis and the extent of inflammation. MRCP is increasingly used to diagnose chronic pancreatitis because it can visualize pancreatic duct obstruction and dilation; however, the typical calcifications in chronic pancreatitis are not as well visualized as on computed tomography. Pancreatic atrophy can be seen as irregular pancreas margins and decreased T1-weighted signal with delayed enhancement post-gadolinium injection. Secretin-enhanced MRCP provides better visualization of the main pancreatic duct and its branches. Pancreatic duct flow rates can also be evaluated with secretin-enhanced MRCP. Pseudocysts present as hyperintense lesions on T2-weighted images, while pseudocysts containing blood demonstrate an increased signal on T1-weighted sequences. MRI can help differentiate pancreatic lipomatosis from fibrotic changes in patients with cystic fibrosis.
