Pancreas Imaging
KEYWORDS: pancreatic, pancreatitis, pancreas, cystic, tomography, computed tomography, computed, imaging, weighted, lesions, tumors, mri, duct, cystic lesions, images

The overall reported sensitivity of computed tomography in detecting pancreatic cancer is 89-97%, with a positive predictive value of 89% for predicting tumor resectability. Multiphase, contrast-enhanced computed tomography is also the ideal imaging technique for evaluating pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. NETs are highly vascular tumors that enhance during the arterial or pancreatic phase. Washout should be noted during the portal venous phase sequencing. Smaller tumors are well-circumscribed, homogeneous masses on computed tomography; however, larger NETs can have a variety of appearances due to necrosis, cystic changes, or hemorrhage. Isolated cystic neoplasms are potentially visible via computed tomography; however, characterization is usually more effective with MRI. Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMN) may show pancreatic ductal dilation or cystic lesions. The cystic lesions are typically hypoattenuating and heterogeneous. Mucinous cystic neoplasms also appear hypoattenuating but often display a thick, enhancing wall on delayed imaging. Serous cystadenomas may have a central scar with ‘sunburst calcifications’ extending out from the central focus in up to 30% of cases. Magnetic Resonance Although computed tomography is the initial modality for diagnosing pancreatic abnormalities, MRI is beneficial for characterizing cystic lesions and, to a lesser extent, identifying some solid tumors, particularly smaller ones.
