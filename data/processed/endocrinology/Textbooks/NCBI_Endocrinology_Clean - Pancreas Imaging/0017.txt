Nuclear Medicine In patients with NETs, nuclear medicine studies may be necessary for further staging and lesion characterization. Historically, octreotide scans have been performed in which octreotide (a somatostatin analog) is labeled with indium-111 and injected before imaging. This modality evolved into indium-111 octreotide coupled with single-photon emission computed tomography. More recently, somatostatin receptor (SSTR) positron emission tomography (PET) is increasingly utilized due to improved sensitivity for detecting small lesions, reduced radiation dose, and the ability to quantify uptake. SSTR PET uses Ga-DOTATATE and Ga-DOTATOC tracers, both of which have high affinity for subtype 2 SSTRs. SSTR PET can also be coupled with MRI or computed tomography for greater spatial resolution. Clinical Significance The differential diagnosis of pancreatic disease processes is challenging, but it can demonstrate significant morbidity and mortality in the population. Understanding the various imaging modalities available and when each is appropriate is critically important. Computed tomography and MRI provide excellent spatial resolution of pancreatic anatomy
