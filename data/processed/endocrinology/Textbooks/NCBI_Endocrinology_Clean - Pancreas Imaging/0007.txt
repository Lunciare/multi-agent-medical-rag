Computed Tomography Computed tomography is the backbone of pancreatic imaging. Intravenous contrast is nearly always indicated with multiphase-enhanced imaging and is optimal for identifying and characterizing pancreatic masses or complications from pancreatitis. Multiphase pancreas protocols usually include an unenhanced sequence, a ‘pancreas phase’ (late arterial phase), and a portal venous phase. In acute pancreatitis, intravenous contrast-enhanced computed tomography shows focal or diffuse enlargement of the pancreatic parenchyma due to interstitial edema, with heterogeneous enhancement. The pancreatic margins are ill-defined due to inflammation, and peripancreatic fat stranding may also be present. An obstructing gallstone may or may not be visible if it is the cause of pancreatitis. Complications may be seen acutely or delayed, including necrosis, hemorrhage, vascular complications, pancreatic pseudocysts, and abscess formation. Areas that lack contrast enhancement are diagnostic of pancreatic necrosis. Visualization of necrotic pancreatitis is best between 48 and 72 hours after onset; therefore, early scanning in acute pancreatitis might under-call patients with necrosis.
