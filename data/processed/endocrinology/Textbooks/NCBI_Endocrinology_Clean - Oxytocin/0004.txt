The head of the fetus pushes against the cervix, and the nerve impulses from this action travel to the mother’s brain, which activates the posterior pituitary to secrete oxytocin. This oxytocin is then carried through the blood to the uterus to increase uterine contractions further, and the cycle continues until parturition. Not only does oxytocin stimulate uterine contractions, but it also causes contractions of myoepithelial cells in the alveolar ducts found in female breast tissue. These contractions force milk from these ducts into even larger sinuses, which enable milk expulsion. The physical stimulation of a baby attempting to latch onto the mother’s breast causes oxytocin secretion into the blood in the same manner as vaginal delivery; instead of uterine contractions, milk is ejected from the breast. Like the mechanisms underlying uterine contractions during delivery, positive feedback is also involved in the milk-ejection reflex. Oxytocin is also used off-label to medically terminate pregnancy and has antidiuretic and vasodilatory effects, which increase cerebral, coronary, and renal blood flow.
