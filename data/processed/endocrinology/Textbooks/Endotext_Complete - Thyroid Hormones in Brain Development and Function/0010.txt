These findings suggest the presence of alternative routes by which T4 and T3 could reach the brain parenchyma in the fetus. There is a fundamental difference between rodents and humans in the transport of thyroid hormones through the BBB. The rodent BBB contains MCT8 and OATP1C1 ( Fig. 4 ). MCT8 transports T4 and T3 whereas OATP1C1 transports T4, reverse T3 (rT3), and T4 sulfate. In humans, as in other primates, the BBB contains MCT8 but lacks OATP1C1 ( 100 , 112 ). One explanation why MCT8 mutations cause profound neurological impairment in patients but not in mice is that T4 transport through OATP1C1 compensates for similar mutations in mice by making T4 available as a DIO2 substrate. There is experimental support for this mechanism as follows: i . MCT8-deficient mice lack neurological impairment ( 113 ), with only slight alterations of brain gene expression ( 114 ), contrary to what would be expected; ii . DIO2 activity and T3 generation increase in the brain of MCT8-deficient mice ( 115 , 116 ) and iii . To achieve cerebral hypothyroidism in mice, a compound deficiency of MCT8 and DIO2, or MCT8 and OATP1C1 is needed, i.e. a double knockout of Slc16a2 and Dio2 or Slc16a2 and Slco1c1 ( 72 , 114 , 117 , 118 ). Role of Deiodinases The deiodinases present in the cerebrum are DIO2 and DIO3 ( 119 ). The adult mouse cerebellum also contains significant DIO1 activity ( 120 ). Different cells express DIO2 and DIO3 in the brain. Glial cells and some interneurons express DIO2 , and excitatory neurons express DIO3 ( 121 - 125 ). The glial cells expressing DIO2 include radial glial cells, astrocytes, and tanycytes ( Fig. 5 ). Dio2 expression occurs in most brain areas and increases in hypothyroidism ( Fig. 5 ). Dio3 shows a restricted high expression at P0 in discrete nuclei, related to sexual differentiation of the brain, but the physiological relevance of this expression has not been studied ( Fig. 5 ( 126 ).
