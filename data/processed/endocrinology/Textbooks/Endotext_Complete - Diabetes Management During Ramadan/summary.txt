Diabetes Management During Ramadan
KEYWORDS: ramadan, diabetes, fasting, insulin, risk, dose, hypoglycemia, individuals, fast, fasting ramadan, international, iftar, glucose, patients, dar

ABSTRACT Muslims contribute to 25% of the world population and majority of them reside in the diabetes and obesity endemic Asia-Pacific region. Fasting during Ramadan is one of the five pillars of Islam and an obligatory duty for all healthy adolescents and adult Muslims. However, Islam exempts the ill and pregnant women from fasting. Despite this, many individuals with diabetes who are at high risk from fasting, fast during Ramadan. Individuals fasting during Ramadan are less likely to see their physicians before starting the fast and more likely to fast against medical advice. Hence, these individuals are at increased risk of hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia, and cardiovascular and renal complications.
