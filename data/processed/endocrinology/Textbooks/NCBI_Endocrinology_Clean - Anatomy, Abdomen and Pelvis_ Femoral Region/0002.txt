The acetabular fossa and cartilaginous lunate surface receive the femoral head at the hip joint. The ligaments supporting the hip joint include the ligament of the head of the femur (ligamentum capitis femoris or ligamentum teres) and the joint capsule's iliofemoral, ischiofemoral, and pubofemoral ligaments. The femoral neck extends inferolaterally from the head and connects the femoral head to the femoral shaft (diaphysis or body). The greater and lesser trochanters are bony protuberances situated laterally and posteromedially, respectively, at the femoral neck and body's junction. The femoral shaft is a long, cylindrical bone possessing a raised vertical structure along its posterior aspect, the linea aspera. The medial and lateral condyles are inferior femoral expansions that articulate with the medial and lateral tibial plateaus at the knee joint.
