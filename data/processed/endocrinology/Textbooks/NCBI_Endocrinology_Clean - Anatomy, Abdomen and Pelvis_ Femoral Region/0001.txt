Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Femoral Region
KEYWORDS: femoral, thigh, nerve, artery, muscles, muscle, hip, anterior, posterior, adductor, lateral, limb, femoris, knee, vein

Introduction The femoral or thigh region of the lower limb is bounded proximally by the hip and distally by the knee (see Image . Right Femur Anatomy, Posterior Surface). Connective tissue septae divide this region into 3 muscle compartments: anterior, posterior, and medial. Femoral muscles contribute to the hip and knee's range of motion. The lower limb's major neurovascular structures pass through the femoral compartments before entering or exiting the pelvis. Most thigh injuries arise from trauma or overuse. Surgically necessary femoral procedures include knee replacements and fracture repairs. The femoral compartment is paramount in orthopedic surgery due to its role in knee joint stability and weight-bearing. Understanding this compartment's anatomy allows clinicians to perform interventions effectively and optimize patient outcomes. Structure and Function The Femur and Its Articulations The "femoral region" is appropriately named, as this region surrounds the femur or thigh bone. The area extends between the femur's proximal articulation with the pelvis at the hip joint and distal articulation with the tibia and patella at the knee joint.
