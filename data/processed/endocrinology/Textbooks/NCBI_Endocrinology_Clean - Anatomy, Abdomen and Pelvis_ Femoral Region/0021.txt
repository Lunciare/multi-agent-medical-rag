Clinical Significance The femoral triangle and adductor canal are anatomical landmarks crucial for procedures such as perioperative pain management and femoral venous catheterization. Neurovascular structures supplying the thigh pass through these regions. The femoral triangle is a triangular region in the proximomedial thigh bordered superiorly by the inguinal ligament, medially by the adductor longus, and laterally by the sartorius (see Image . Femoral Triangle Structures). The structures within the femoral triangle from lateral to medial are the femoral nerve, artery, vein, and lymphatics, hence the mnemonic aid "NAVeL" for these structures. The femoral sheath encloses the femoral artery, vein, and lymphatics. The femoral nerve lies lateral to the femoral sheath. The femoral artery and vein and the saphenous nerve pass deep to the sartorius before entering the adductor canal.
