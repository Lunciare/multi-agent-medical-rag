Insulin suppresses the generation of O2 radicals and reactive oxygen species (ROS). Within the macrophage, insulin inhibits NADPH oxidase expression by suppressing 1 of its key components, p47phox. NADPH oxidase aids in generating oxygen radicals, activating the NF-kB kinase beta inhibitor (IKKB). IKKB phosphorylates IkB, leading to its degradation. This degradation releases NF-kB, allowing for its translocation in the macrophage’s nucleus. Once in the nucleus, NF-kB stimulates gene transcription of pro-inflammatory proteins that are released into the circulation, such as inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-8 (IL-8), monocyte chemoattractant protein (MCP-1), and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP). Clinical Significance There is a group of metabolic diseases in which the body experiences chronic hyperglycemia. Type 1, insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus is when the pancreas has low or absent insulin production. Type 2, insulin-independent diabetes mellitus (DM) is a condition in which the body produces insulin but cannot keep up with its glucose metabolic demands effectively.
