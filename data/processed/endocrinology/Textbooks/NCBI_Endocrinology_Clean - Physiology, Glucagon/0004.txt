Among medications, sulfonylureas, meglitinides, and insulin are the most common agents. When hypoglycemia is noted, questions about medications taken, history of alcohol or drug use, history of kidney injury, unintentional weight changes, or history of psychiatric disorders must be asked. Hypoglycemia is treated with IV dextrose or glucose in unconscious patients, while oral glucose (fruit juice preferred) can be attempted in conscious patients. If patients are unresponsive or unable to take oral medications, a 1-mg intramuscular injection of glucagon is an option. The incretin hormone GLP-1 also inhibits glucagon secretion. Gluconeogenic amino acids such as alanine stimulate glucagon secretion. Although it remains unclear whether glucagon feedback to alpha cells acts in an autocrine manner, recent studies indicate that alpha cells do not express the glucagon receptor (GCGR), suggesting that they may regulate their own secretion by activating insulin secretion. Development The proglucagon gene encodes it, and the proglucagon peptide is processed by prohormone convertase-2 (PC2) to produce the 29-amino acid mature peptide.
