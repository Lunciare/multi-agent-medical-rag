Double-Lumen Endobronchial Tubes
KEYWORDS: dlt, lung, bronchus, bronchial, cuff, lumen, placement, ventilation, right, left, airway, tube, main, dlts, used

The bronchial cuff should be just visible without cuff herniation over the carina when inflated. The right upper lobe (RUL) bronchus should be identifiable via the right main bronchus with 3 lobar branches (apical, anterior and posterior). Next, the fiberoptic bronchoscope is advanced through the endobronchial lumen. For a left-sided DLT, identify the origins of the left upper and lower bronchi (confirm that the tip of the endobronchial orifice is not occluding the left upper or lower bronchi). For a right-sided DLT, identify good alignment between the opening slot of the endobronchial lumen relative to the takeoff of the right upper lobe bronchus (to allow ventilation of the right upper lobe bronchus). Identify the bronchus intermedius and the right lower lobe bronchus distally. Fiberoptic bronchoscopy is the gold standard for confirmation of correct placement of a DLT. Auscultation alone is unreliable as an indicator of proper DLT placement. Troubleshooting techniques for DLT insertion include: Using a bronchoscope to cannulate the bronchus and railroading the tube over the scope. Always place the scope in the bronchial (longer) lumen.
