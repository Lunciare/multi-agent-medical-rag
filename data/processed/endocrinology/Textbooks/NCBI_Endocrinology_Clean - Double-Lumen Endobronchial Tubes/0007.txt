Placement of a right-sided DLT is more challenging with a lower safety margin due to the short length of the right mainstem bronchus and the origination of the right-upper lobe bronchus 1.5 to 2 cm from the carina. Right-sided DLT, as a result, incorporates a modified cuff, or slot on the endobronchial side that allows ventilation of the right upper lobe. Placement of a right-sided DLT needs to be positioned to align with the takeoff of the right upper lobe. (Figure 1) Reviewing a chest x-ray, CT scan, or MRI before placing a right-sided DLT may help to locate the position of the right upper lobe take off from the right mainstem bronchus. Because of the higher safety margin, a left-sided DLT is commonly used. However, right-sided DLT may be preferred when it is important to avoid manipulation of left bronchus or when left bronchus is narrowed or has anatomical variations, left pneumectomy, left lung transplant, left mainstem bronchus stent or suspected disruption of the left tracheobronchial tree. Personnel Personnel responsible for the placement of a DLT for lung isolation include an anesthesiologist, certified registered nurse anesthetist (CRNA), or certified anesthesiologist assistant (CAA).
