Complications There are several over-the-counter devices to remove cerumen that patients should be advised not to purchase. Cotton swabs are commonly used but should be avoided, as they may worsen the impaction or cause a tympanic membrane perforation. Another common home remedy is ear candling. This involves a hollow tube coated in beeswax; 1 end is inserted into the ear canal, and the other is ignited. It is falsely claimed to have a "chimney effect," created by the pull of air from the ignited candle. The United States Food and Drug Administration strongly recommends against using this device because it is not effective in scientific studies but does carry the potential for injury. Clinical Significance It is crucial to ensure symptoms of other conditions are not falsely attributed to the cerumen in patients treated for cerumen impaction.
