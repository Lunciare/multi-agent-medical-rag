Imaging Studies Accurate imaging is essential for evaluating the extent of disease and determining resectability. A combination of the following modalities is typically employed: Cross-sectional imaging: On noncontrast CT, somatostatinomas typically appear isodense relative to pancreatic parenchyma. They demonstrate homogeneous arterial phase enhancement with contrast enhancement, often becoming less conspicuous during the portal venous phase (see Image . Somatostatinoma). MRI offers superior sensitivity for detecting hepatic and osseous metastases and is usually complementary to CT. Endoscopic ultrasound(EUS): EUS is highly sensitive and specific for detecting pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms, including lesions smaller than 5 mm. It is particularly advantageous for identifying small or submucosal somatostatinomas that may not be visualized on conventional cross-sectional imaging. Functional imaging (PET): Somatostatin receptor-based positron emission tomography (PET) using radiolabeled ligands such as 68 Ga-DOTATATE, 68 Ga-DOTATOC, 68 Ga-DOTANOC, or 64 Cu-DOTATATE demonstrates excellent sensitivity and specificity for tumor localization and staging.
