In 2016, estimates indicated that approximately 17,000 new cases of SCI occurred nationwide, equating to an annual incidence of 54 cases per million population. Osteoporosis affects 10 million individuals in the United States, while an additional 34 million are considered at risk due to a diagnosis of osteopenia—a condition defined by BMD levels below the normal reference range but not low enough to meet the diagnostic criteria for osteoporosis. These aforementioned epidemiologic trends and patterns merge to result in over 50% of patients with a complete SCI developing osteoporosis by 1 year after the injury. Long-term follow-up increases the prevalence rate to >80%. However, the most devastating complication secondary to osteoporosis developing after an SCI is the fragility fracture. Over half of these patients will sustain at least 1 low-impact fracture at some point after the SCI during long-term follow-up. Pathophysiology Disuse Osteoporosis and Osteopenia Patients sustaining SCIs are predisposed to disuse osteopenia from prolonged immobilization and overall decreased mobility and independent functional capabilities.
