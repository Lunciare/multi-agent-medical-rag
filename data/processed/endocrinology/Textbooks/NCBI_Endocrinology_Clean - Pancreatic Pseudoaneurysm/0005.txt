Angiography defines the character and location of the lesion, as well as provides an opportunity to gain temporary control over the bleeding by transcatheter embolization or possible stenting. There is a classification of pancreatic pseudoaneurysms based on the following features: The type of blood vessel from which the pseudoaneurysm originates Absence or presence of communication with the GI tract Absence of presence of highly concentrated pancreatic fluid at the bleeding site. It is important to note that CT angiography is only suitable for stable patients. Treatment / Management The current standard of therapy is to control the bleeding by endovascular transarterial catheter embolization or by the placement of a covered stent. Patients in whom arterial embolization is unsuccessful, or there is rebleeding after embolization surgery is the only option. The surgical procedure can be either direct ligation of the bleeding vessel or resection of the pancreas along with the pseudoaneurysm. The percutaneous intervention does have a success rate of 70% to 90%. Embolization is often the choice of treatment in very ill unstable patients and in patients in whom the bleeding is diffuse.
