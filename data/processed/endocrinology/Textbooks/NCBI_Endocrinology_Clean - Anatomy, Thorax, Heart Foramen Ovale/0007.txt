Muscles The foramen ovale is a structure derived from muscular tissue. During embryogenesis of the heart, the septum secundum pulls the right horn of the sinus venosus into the right atrium and allows expansion of the right atrium. The right horn of the sinus venosus generates the smooth muscle of the right atrium and also contributes to the smooth muscle of the septum secundum on the wall where the foramen ovale is situated. Whereas in the left atrium, the pulmonary veins produce the smooth muscle in the chamber, which aids in the development of the atrial septum. Physiologic Variants The size of the foramen ovale can vary between individuals. Generally, the foramen ovale is ovular and has been shown to occupy 33% of the whole developing atrial septum. Investigation shows that fetuses between 17.5 and 37 weeks in gestation with variances in the size of the foramen ovale have a risk of congenital defects.
