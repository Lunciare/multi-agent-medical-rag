The lower the diet is in calories, the more likely it is that essential vitamins, minerals and electrolytes such as potassium, copper, magnesium, Vitamin E, Vitamin B6, folic acid, iron, and calcium are likely to be low. As a rule of thumb, diets below 1,200 calories per day are likely to require vitamin and mineral supplements in amounts approximating the Recommended Dietary Allowances (15). Above 1,200 calories per day, women in reproductive age groups may still need iron, calcium, and folic acid supplements, since their needs for these nutrients are high, but most other nutrient needs can be met by a well-balanced diet that follows the Dietary Guidelines for Americans (15). For this reason, foods with high micronutrient density, but low energy density are especially important to include on a reducing diet. They include fruits, vegetables, legumes, and lightly processed whole grains.
