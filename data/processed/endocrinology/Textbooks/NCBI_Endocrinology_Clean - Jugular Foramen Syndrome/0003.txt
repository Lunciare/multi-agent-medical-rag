Jugular Foramen Syndrome
KEYWORDS: jugular, foramen, syndrome, jugular foramen, nerve, foramen syndrome, cranial, nerves, paragangliomas, cranial nerves, common, vascular, cranial nerve, compression, patients

Meningioma Aneurysmal bone cyst Neurofibroma Temporal bone chordoma Malignant Metastasis Squamous cell carcinoma Multiple myeloma Chondrosarcoma Lymphoma Mucoepidermoid carcinoma of the parotid gland Nasopharyngeal adenocarcinoma Metastatic conjunctival malignant melanoma Traumatic Penetrating trauma Fractures involving the posterior skull base Epidemiology The exact incidence of jugular foramen syndrome is challenging to estimate due to its rarity. Metastatic disease is the most common cause of jugular foramen syndrome, but it is often underdiagnosed. Skull base metastasis is typically regarded as a manifestation of systemic malignancy. The most common primary cancers that are likely to metastasize to the jugular foramen are those of the breast, lung, kidney, and prostate. Paragangliomas are the most frequent primary lesions, followed by schwannomas and meningiomas. Recent literature suggests that primary lesions are more common than metastases. This discrepancy arises because metastatic disease is more common in oncology cohorts, whereas primary tumors (especially paragangliomas and schwannomas) predominate in skull-base referral centers. Pathophysiology Cranial nerve IX is primarily sensory, receiving general visceral and special sensory input from the tonsils, larynx, middle ear, posterior one-third of the tongue, and carotid bodies. This nerve has a small general motor efferent to the stylopharyngeus muscle, which aids in elevating the larynx and pharynx.
