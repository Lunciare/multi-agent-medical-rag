Studies have found a higher rate of postoperative atrial fibrillation following PFO closure device placement, the long-term effects of which have yet to be studied. Data exists showing that PFO closure for cryptogenic stroke can be cost-effective. One study demonstrated that the effectiveness of PFO closure depends on the device used. The gold standard for diagnosis of PFO remains a transoesophageal echocardiogram with a bubble test after a sustained Valsalva maneuver (see Video. Transesophageal Echocardiogram). Once identified, the structure can undergo an assessment to determine the need for treatment. There are surgical, transcatheter, and medical therapies available for treatment. The percutaneous transcatheter approach is becoming more favored, as most patients prefer the less invasive procedure. Anatomy and Physiology A patent foramen ovale is a remnant opening of the fetal foramen ovale. The foramen ovale is a conduit for blood to flow from the right atrium to the left atrium in the fetal state.
