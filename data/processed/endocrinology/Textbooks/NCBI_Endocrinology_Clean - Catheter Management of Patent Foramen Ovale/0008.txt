Complications Data show that trans-catheter PFO closure complication rates are approximately 7%. Studies have shown that the incidence of an adverse event is higher in patients >60 years of age. Furthermore, adverse outcomes are higher in patients with a preceding ischemic stroke versus a transient ischemic stroke. Complications include the following in order of most common to least: Arrhythmia (most commonly atrial fibrillation) Vascular complications Hemorrhage/hematoma Cardiac tamponade/perforation Loss of life Pneumothorax/hemothorax Occluder embolus Infection Clinical Significance Patent foramen ovale closure is not necessary for all patients. However, a select number of patients benefit significantly from PFO closure. The sequelae in this population are many times more devastating, especially in those with debilitating strokes. Therefore, primary care providers must recognize the indications for PFO closure and refer patients appropriately. Knowing that PFOs greater than 25 mm are usually not amenable to percutaneous management and more often require surgical closure is critical information to share with the patient.
