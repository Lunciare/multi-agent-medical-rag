Multiple modalities can be implemented in managing craniopharyngioma, including surgery, radiotherapy, and intracystic therapy. The choice of treatment, surgical approach, and extent of resection should be individualized for each patient. The patient's age, underlying medical comorbidities, tumor location, type and invasiveness, and, ultimately, neurosurgeon experience should be considered while determining the optimal treatment strategy. Preoperative Management Hormonal deficiency, specifically secondary adrenal insufficiency, and hypothyroidism, should be treated with glucocorticoid and thyroid hormone replacement before surgery. Glucocorticoid replacement is done with either hydrocortisone 20 to 30 mg in 2 to 3 doses or prednisone 5 to 10 mg in a single or divided dose.
