PCP: CT scan This tumor typically appears as a solid mass with soft tissue densities and rare calcifications. MRI Solid tumors are iso to hypointense on T1-weighted imaging, with variable T2 signal intensity and avid contrast enhancement. Both imaging modalities are essential for identifying associated hydrocephalus, which may develop due to third ventricular obstruction. Visual Examination A complete neuroophthalmologic evaluation is crucial, as 62% to 84% of patients present with visual disturbances. Acuity and visual field testing (perimetry) Identifies classic bitemporal hemianopsia due to optic chiasm compression Fundoscopy May reveal papilledema in patients with increased intracranial pressure due to hydrocephalus Endocrine Evaluation Given the tumor’s proximity to the hypothalamic-pituitary axis, comprehensive hormonal testing is necessary.
