Older patients (65–79 and >80) had a higher prevalence of comorbidities including congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, angina, transient ischemic attack, stroke, COPD, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes (p<0.01). The overall complication rates were 1.3%, 2.2%, and 3.1% in the 45–64, 65–79, and >80 groups, respectively (p<0.01). Notably, 78% of parathyroidectomies were performed by low-volume surgeons ( 95 ). Other studies have concluded that PTX is safe and effective even in patients over 75 years old, with very low rates of postoperative complications, ICU admission, and hospital readmission ( 37 , 39 , 46 , 51 , 52 , 53 , 54 , 65 , 67 , 96 – 102 ). However, one study reported a higher prevalence of respiratory complications, such as pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, and postoperative intubation in patients over 65 years of age ( 95 ). Cardiac complications have also been documented ( 48 , 92 ), as well as prolonged hospital stays ( 53 , 97 , 98 ). Importantly, patients with greater frailty, as indicated by higher scores on the five-item modified frailty index (mFI-5), had over eight times the odds of experiencing complications such as reoperation within 30 days (OR 4.20, 95% CI: 1.64–10.74; p = 0.003) and hospital stays of five days or longer (95% CI: 4.28–5.25; p = 0.001), compared to those with lower frailty scores ( 103 ). SURGICAL BENEFITS In patients with asymptomatic primary hyperparathyroidism, randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that PTX results in significant and sustained improvements in bone mineral density (BMD) at the lumbar spine, femoral neck, and total hip when compared with observation ( 104 - 107 ). In contrast, patients managed conservatively tend to show stable or declining BMD over time. This represents the most consistent clinical benefit demonstrated in randomized studies. Parathyroidectomy consistently achieves long-term normalization of serum calcium and parathyroid hormone levels, effectively correcting the biochemical abnormalities associated with PHPT, as shown across all randomized trials ( 104 - 107 ). Although some trials have explored quality of life, neurocognitive symptoms, and psychological well-being, results have been inconsistent, with modest improvements reported in certain domains but not others ( 62 , 105 , 106 , 108 ).
