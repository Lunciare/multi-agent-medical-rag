Its incidence and prevalence are difficult to determine due to a large number of undiagnosed asymptomatic cases ( 26 , 27 ); however, in recent decades, both the incidence and prevalence have increased, largely due to the incorporation of serum calcium measurements in routine medical checkups ( 28 – 30 ). The prevalence of PHPT varies by sex, age, and population group ( 26 , 27 ), with the most influential factor being the availability of serum calcium and PTH testing ( 30 ). PHPT is more common in women than in men, with a ratio of 3:1; however, among individuals under the age of 45, the sex distribution is equal ( 26 , 27 ). The highest incidence is observed in Black individuals, followed by White, Asian, and Hispanic populations. In 2010, the prevalence in the United States was 233 per 100,000 women and 85 per 100,000 men; however, among women aged 70 to 79, the prevalence rose to 1,409 per 100,000 Black women and 1,110 per 100,000 White women ( 2 , 4 ). No population-based studies are available to determine the prevalence in South America ( 31 ). In Western Europe, incidence has also increased: from 2003 to 2017, Spain reported an incidence of 40.3 per 100,000 women/year and 13.7 per 100,000 men/year ( 32 ). In 2010, the reported incidence in Denmark was 16 per 100,000 people/year ( 33 ). In Scandinavia, the prevalence was found to be 1.07% among individuals over 25 years of age, and 2.6% among women aged 55 to 75 years ( 26 , 27 ). In Eastern Europe, the Czech Republic reported an incidence of 24 cases per 100,000 people/year ( 34 ). In China, the reported prevalence was 0.2% ( 35 ). In Australia, the number of parathyroidectomies has increased, although there are no epidemiological data reporting the prevalence of PHPT. Likewise, there are no data available for the African continent. An autopsy study reported a parathyroid adenoma prevalence of 7% in the elderly population ( 36 ). Etiology PHPT may present as a single adenoma (85%), multiglandular disease, which can manifest as hyperplasia or multiple adenomas (14%), or, in rare cases, as carcinoma (<1%).
