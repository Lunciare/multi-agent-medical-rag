In the kidney, PTH promotes calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion in the urine, increases the production of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25(OH) 2 D), and interferes with sodium and water reabsorption in the proximal tubule. Approximately 10,000 mg of calcium are filtered daily by the glomeruli, with only 100–200 mg excreted in the urine, representing 1–2% of the filtered load. However, in PHPT, the filtered calcium load is increased due to enhanced bone resorption stimulated by PTH and elevated intestinal calcium absorption mediated by 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D ( 5 ). PTH in the Proximal Tubule In the proximal tubule (PT), most solutes and water are reabsorbed. Calcium reabsorption in this segment is closely linked to sodium (Na + ) reabsorption, which is primarily mediated by the sodium/hydrogen exchanger isoform 3 (NHE3) ( 6 ). However, other sodium-dependent cotransporters also contribute, including the sodium-phosphate (NaPi), sodium-glucose (SGLT), and sodium–amino acid cotransporters. In the early segments of the PT, sodium, water, glucose, amino acids, and bicarbonate are reabsorbed, leading to an increase in luminal chloride relative to plasma and the development of negative luminal potential. This environment promotes passive paracellular chloride reabsorption, likely through claudin-10a ( 7 ). The reabsorption of chloride establishes a positive luminal electrochemical gradient that facilitates calcium reabsorption in the more distal segments of the PT via claudin-2 ( 7 , 8 ). In summary, approximately 65% of filtered calcium is reabsorbed in the PT through passive paracellular diffusion, driven by favorable electrochemical and concentration gradients—the latter being the dominant force ( 3 , 9 , 10 ). Additionally, calcium reabsorption in this segment is aided by solute drag, which occurs as water is reabsorbed ( 7 ). Water reabsorption in the PT is triggered by solute reabsorption, particularly sodium and chloride. In the PT, PTH exerts at least three actions, none of which are directly related to calcium reabsorption: 1) PTH stimulates 1α-hydroxylase, which converts 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) into its active form, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25(OH) 2 D); 2) PTH downregulates the NaPi IIa and IIc cotransporters through phosphorylation of the sodium–hydrogen exchanger regulatory factor 1 (NHERF1). NHERF1 acts as a scaffolding protein linking NaPi IIa to the actin cytoskeleton, enabling its retention at the apical membrane.
