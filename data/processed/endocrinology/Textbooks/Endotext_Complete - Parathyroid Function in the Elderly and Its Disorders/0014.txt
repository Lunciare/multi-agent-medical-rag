Typically, there are four parathyroid glands (though 3 to 8 may be found), located posterior to the thyroid gland at the superior and inferior poles of each thyroid lobe. The average weight is 30–70 mg, while adenomas generally range from 500 to 5000 mg in weight ( 72 ). Techniques used for localizing adenomas include: dual-phase technetium-99m sestamibi scintigraphy (99mTc-MIBI), 99mTc-MIBI with specialized imaging acquisition methods (such as pinhole and SPECT), dual-tracer scintigraphy, neck ultrasound, positron emission tomography (PET), four-dimensional computed tomography (4D-CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and hybrid techniques like 99mTc-MIBI SPECT-CT and PET-CT ( 72 ). PET-CT may use tracers such as 11 C-methionine, 11 C-choline, and 18 F-choline. The choice of localization technique depends on the experience and resources of each medical center.
