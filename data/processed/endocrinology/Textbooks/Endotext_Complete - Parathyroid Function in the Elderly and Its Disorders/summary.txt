Parathyroid Function in the Elderly and Its Disorders
KEYWORDS: calcium, pubmed, patients, pth, hyperparathyroidism, primary, primary hyperparathyroidism, hypoparathyroidism, parathyroid, free, free article, article, pmc, pmc free, surgery

ABSTRACT Aging is accompanied by profound alterations in calcium and bone metabolism, many of which arise from changes in parathyroid hormone (PTH) regulation, secretion, age-related modifications of the physiology of PTH, and target-tissue responsiveness. With advancing age, reductions in vitamin D synthesis, impaired renal activation of 1,25(OH) 2 D, and decreased intestinal calcium absorption contribute to a compensatory rise in PTH levels and a negative calcium balance. Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT), whose prevalence increases markedly after the sixth decade of life, may present as asymptomatic hypercalcemia, normocalcemic hyperparathyroidism, or classic symptomatic disease. Older adults frequently exhibit predominant skeletal involvement with a higher fracture risk, a complex interaction with metabolic syndrome, and a lower prevalence of nephrolithiasis, which is approximately 15% among patients older than 75 years. Surgical criteria for asymptomatic PHPT have been defined since 1991 and have undergone successive updates, most recently in 2022 at the Fifth International Workshop.
