In conclusion, randomized evidence suggests that surgical benefits on quality of life are modest and of uncertain clinical relevance, supporting long-term observation as a safe strategy in patients with mild PHPT. Neurocognitive and Psychiatric Symptoms Koman evaluated 110 patients with mild cognitive impairment using a battery of tests, including the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), and the Timed Sit-to-Stand Test (TST) to assess muscle strength. These assessments were conducted at baseline and after four weeks of treatment with calcimimetics, as well as at 6 weeks and 6 months following PTX. Serum calcium normalized after calcimimetic therapy, leading to improvements in MoCA, HADS scores, and TST performance. In patients over 70 years of age, long-term improvement in MoCA scores after PTX was correlated with a reduction in ionized calcium levels (r = −0.536, p = 0.022). Short-term improvements in depression and anxiety following biochemical normalization of hypercalcemia were also associated with sustained cognitive benefits six months after PTX. Nevertheless, it remains uncertain whether more subtle neurocognitive or neuropsychiatric manifestations in patients with PHPT can be directly attributed to the disease itself, particularly in the absence of marked hypercalcemia. In this context, the response to calcimimetic therapy may represent a potentially useful strategy to anticipate postoperative cognitive and functional outcomes, helping to identify patients most likely to benefit from surgical intervention ( 120 ). It should be noted that this was a prospective observational study without a control group. Cardiovascular Outcomes Evidence regarding the impact of PTX on long-term cardiovascular outcomes in PHPT derives from both randomized and observational studies, with differing levels of methodological strength. The randomized controlled trial by Pretorius et al., which enrolled 191 patients with mild PHPT and followed them for up to 10 years, provides the highest level of evidence. In this study, PTX did not result in significant differences compared with observation in mortality or major morbidity outcomes. After a decade of follow-up, mortality rates were nearly identical between groups (8 deaths in the PTX group versus 7 in the observation group), and extended follow-up through 2018 confirmed the absence of divergence in survival.
