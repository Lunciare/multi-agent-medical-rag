Initial approach in patients without prior surgery: 99mTc-MIBI or ultrasound. Sensitivity decreases in older adults. Re-operative approach: confirm 99mTc-MIBI findings with ultrasound for cervical lesions, or with MRI or CT for mediastinal lesions; alternatively, use 99mTc-MIBI SPECT/CT as the first-line modality. Advantages of a selective approach: reduced operative time and surgical risk, improved healing, and lower cost. Surgical Management of Primary Hyperparathyroidism Parathyroidectomy (PTX) is the only curative treatment for patients with PHPT. It cures approximately 97.8% of cases in patients with asymptomatic PHPT ( 79 , 80 ). PTX is considered safe, well-tolerated and, with only a minimal increase in complication risk among older adults ( 81 , 82 ). PTX should be performed by an experienced (high-volume) surgeon, defined as one who performs at least 50 parathyroidectomies annually ( 83 ). Surgery is recommended for all symptomatic patients with PHTP. For patients with an asymptomatic presentation, surgical indications have been established since 1991, with the most recent update presented in the management guidelines of the 5th International Workshop and summarized in Table 3 ( 84 ).
