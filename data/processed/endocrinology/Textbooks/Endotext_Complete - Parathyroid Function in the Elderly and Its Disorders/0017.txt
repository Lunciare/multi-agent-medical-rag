Seib retrospectively analyzed 210,206 patients with PHPT (from 2006 to 2016) to determine which patient characteristics influenced referral for PTX. He found that 62.7% met at least one surgical criterion according to the guidelines; however, only 29.6% underwent PTX within the first year. The mean age was 75.3 years (±6.8). Patients who underwent PTX were younger: 73.5 (±5.7) vs. 76 (±7.1) years ( p < 0.001); they were more likely to be robust according to a frailty index (92.1% vs. 85.7%), had a lower Charlson comorbidity index (score 0–1: 56.4% vs. 44.1%), and were more likely to live in rural areas (18.1% vs. 13.6%). When data were analyzed by surgical indication, a history of urolithiasis increased the likelihood of surgery: OR 1.43 (95% CI, 1.39–1.47); stage 3 chronic kidney disease decreased it: OR 0.71 (95% CI, 0.68–0.74); and osteoporosis showed no significant association: OR 1.01 (95% CI, 0.99–1.03). By age group, the surgical rates were 35.6%, 25.9%, and 11.2% for those aged 66–75, 76–85, and over 86, respectively. The decision to proceed with surgery was more strongly influenced by age, comorbidities, and frailty than by the presence of surgical criteria as defined by clinical guidelines ( 92 ). SURGICAL COMPLICATIONS Calò conducted a retrospective evaluation of 256 patients who underwent PTX for PHPT, dividing them into three groups: Group A: <64 years (60.9%), Group B: 65–74 years (29.7%), and Group C: ≥75 years (9.4%). Transient hypoparathyroidism occurred in 6.4%, 18.4%, and 16.5% of Groups A, B, and C respectively (p=0.013); however, there were no differences among groups in the rate of permanent hypoparathyroidism. Hospital stay duration was also similar across groups: 2.3, 2.5, and 2.5 days respectively (p=0.15) ( 93 ). O’Sullivan retrospectively analyzed 1,900 patients who underwent surgery for PHPT between 1999 and 2018. Of these, 202 patients (10.6%) were over 75 years of age. There were no differences in the prevalence of complications such as recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypoparathyroidism, hematoma, surgical site infections, or persistence/recurrence of PHPT. These findings suggest that PTX is safe in older adults ( 50 ). In Switzerland, PTX was performed in 2,642 patients between 2012 and 2018.
