In patients over 75, the percentage of fractures before and after surgery was 18.4% vs. 17.1%, p = 0.828, while in those under 75, fracture rates decreased significantly from 10.3% to 1.4%, p = 0.020 ( 115 ). Seib found that over a follow-up period of 58.5 months, the unadjusted fracture incidence was 10.2% for any fracture and 2.9% for hip fracture in patients treated with PTX. In contrast, medically managed patients had a fracture incidence of 13.7% for any fracture and 4.2% for hip fracture over 52.5 months. In multivariable analysis, PTX was associated with a lower adjusted rate of any fracture (HR 0.78; 95% CI, 0.76–0.80) and hip fracture (HR 0.76; 95% CI, 0.72–0.79), compared to nonoperative management ( 114 ). Kongsaree et al. conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 35 studies (5 randomized controlled trials and 30 cohort studies) including 238,188 patients with PHPT, comparing PTX with observation. PTX was associated with a lower risk of any fracture (RR 0.80; 95% CI, 0.74–0.86) and hip fracture (RR 0.63; 95% CI, 0.52–0.76), with no significant reduction in vertebral or forearm fractures. Modest increases in bone mineral density were observed at the femoral neck, hip, radius, and spine following PXT ( 113 ). Frailty and Quality of Life Several studies have assessed the impact of PTX on quality of life and frailty in patients with PHPT ( 116 ). Pretorius and colleagues conducted the SIPH study, a 10-year randomized controlled trial in patients with mild PHPT comparing PTX with observation. Surgery produced only modest improvement in the SF-36 vitality domain, while overall quality-of-life scores remained stable in both groups. Neuropsychiatric symptoms assessed by the Comprehensive Psychopathological Rating Scale (CPRS) improved similarly regardless of treatment, suggesting limited long-term impact of surgery on global quality of life in mild disease ( 117 ). Blanchard et al. conducted a prospective multicenter study of 116 patients with mild PHPT and found that quality of life improved modestly one year after PTX, with the mental component score (MCS) increasing from 38.69 to 41.29 (+2.6 points) and the physical component score (PCS) increasing from 39.53 to 42.03 (+2.5 points). The greatest benefits were observed in patients younger than 70 years and those with serum calcium ≥10.4 mg/dL. Men showed no improvement in mental scores, whereas women with low baseline MCS experienced postoperative gains ( 118 ).
