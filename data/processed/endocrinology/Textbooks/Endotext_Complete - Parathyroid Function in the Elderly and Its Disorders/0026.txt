This monitoring aims to detect changes in serum calcium levels, decreases in BMD, the occurrence of fractures, or alterations in renal parameters that would justify surgical intervention at that time ( 136 ). Follow-up of patients who were not surgical candidates showed that serum calcium, PTH, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, urinary calcium excretion, and BMD remained stable over a 10-year follow-up period ( 137 ). However, 37% experienced a decrease in BMD at the radius and hip during longer follow-up periods ( 111 ). Patients under the age of 50 had a higher likelihood of disease progression compared to those over 50 years old (65% vs. 23%). Duskin-Bitan conducted a retrospective evaluation of 141 elderly adults managed conservatively and found that both serum and urinary calcium levels significantly decreased, while vitamin D levels increased at the final visit (10.4 ± 0.5 mg/dL, 161 ± 70 mg/24h, 69 ± 17 nmol/L, p < 0.010, respectively), compared to values at the time of diagnosis (10.6 ± 0.2 mg/dL, 223 ± 95 mg/24h, 53 ± 15 nmol/L, respectively, p = 0.001) ( 138 ). Medical therapy is an option for patients with contraindications to surgery, for those who failed surgical treatment, or for those who decline surgery. In these cases, pharmacologic treatment should be considered when the therapeutic goal is to reduce serum calcium, increase bone mineral density (BMD), or achieve both outcomes. Calcium restriction in the diet is not recommended, and intake should not exceed 1 g/day in patients with elevated 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D levels. All patients with serum vitamin D levels below 20 ng/mL should receive supplementation. Bisphosphonates can be used to improve BMD. Evidence supports that alendronate enhances BMD in the lumbar spine and hip without altering serum calcium levels, while reducing bone turnover markers. However, data on fracture risk reduction is not currently available ( 139 ). Cinacalcet can be used to reduce serum calcium levels. It has been shown to normalize calcium levels in 80–90% of patients with PHPT within the first 6 months of treatment, with levels remaining stable for up to 5.5 years of continuous use.
