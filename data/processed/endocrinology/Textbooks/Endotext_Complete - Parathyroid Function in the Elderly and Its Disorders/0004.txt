This results in a high chloride concentration and a negative luminal charge, which likely promotes chloride transport, possibly via claudin-10a, into the interstitial space. This generates a positive luminal charge that facilitates the paracellular reabsorption of sodium and calcium, likely through claudin-2. The actions of parathyroid hormone (PTH) in this segment include: ( 1 ) stimulation of 1α-hydroxylase to produce 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D; ( 2 ) phosphorylation of NHERF-1, allowing internalization of NaPi-2a and thereby preventing phosphate reabsorption; and ( 3 ) inhibition of the Na + /H + exchanger (NHE3), which decreases sodium, and consequently calcium reabsorption in this segment. Approximately 65% of filtered calcium is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule through passive, paracellular mechanisms driven by an electrochemical gradient, a concentration gradient, and solute drag. PTH in the Loop of Henle In the thick ascending limb (TAL) of the loop of Henle, approximately 25% of filtered calcium and 70% of magnesium are reabsorbed via the paracellular pathway, a process promoted by parathyroid hormone (PTH) and inhibited by the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) ( 11 ). Calcium reabsorption depends on a positive luminal voltage generated by ROMK (a potassium channel that allows K + efflux into the lumen) and claudin-10b, a paracellular tight junction protein that enables bidirectional sodium transport. In the initial segment of the TAL (outer medulla), sodium is reabsorbed from the lumen into the interstitial space through claudin-10b. However, in the more distal segment (cortex), sodium moves from the interstitium into the lumen, also through claudin-10b. This movement contributes to the generation of a highly positive luminal transepithelial voltage (~30 mV), which facilitates paracellular calcium and magnesium reabsorption via claudins 16 and 19 ( 7 ).
