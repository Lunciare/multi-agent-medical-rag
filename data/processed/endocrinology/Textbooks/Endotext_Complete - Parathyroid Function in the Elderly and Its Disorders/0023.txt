Similarly, the incidence of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, fractures, cancer, renal outcomes, and other major morbidities was comparable between management strategies. These findings strongly support current guideline recommendations, which do not advocate PTX in patients with mild, asymptomatic PHPT solely to reduce long-term morbidity or mortality. The absence of excess adverse outcomes in the observation group further indicates that long-term conservative management is a safe approach in this population ( 121 ). In contrast, Seib et al. retrospectively analyzed 210,206 patients with PHPT between 2006 and 2016, examining associations between PTX and cardiovascular outcomes. In unadjusted analyses, the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events was 10.0% among patients who underwent PTX and 11.5% among those managed non-surgically. After multivariable adjustment, PTX was associated with lower rates of major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 0.92; 95% CI 0.90–0.94), cardiovascular-related hospitalizations (HR 0.89; 95% CI 0.87–0.91), and cardiovascular-related in-hospital mortality (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.71–0.81). At 10 years, these associations corresponded to modest adjusted absolute risk reductions of 1.7%, 2.5%, and 1.4%, respectively ( 122 ). However, given its retrospective observational design, this study is subject to residual confounding and selection bias, as patients undergoing PTX may differ from those managed conservatively in baseline cardiovascular risk, medical therapy, frailty, and access to care, factors not fully captured by administrative data. Although this large real-world analysis includes a substantial proportion of older adults, the non-randomized nature of the study precludes causal inference. Consistent with the randomized evidence, current clinical guidelines do not recommend PTX solely for the purpose of cardiovascular risk reduction. Renal Outcomes The evidence regarding renal outcomes following PTX in older adults is controversial. Recent data suggest that PTX has a limited effect on preserving long-term renal function in elderly patients with PHPT. A large target trial emulation study including more than 40,000 patients with PHPT found no significant difference in the development of sustained estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline between surgically and non-operatively managed patients aged 60 years or older, in contrast to younger individuals who appeared to derive greater renal benefit ( 123 ).
