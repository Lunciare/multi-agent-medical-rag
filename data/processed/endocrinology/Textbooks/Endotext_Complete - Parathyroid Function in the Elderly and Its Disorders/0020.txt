Indeed, the guidelines from the 5th International Workshop on the Evaluation and Management of Primary Hyperparathyroidism recommend that: 1) neurocognitive or neuropsychiatric testing should not be routinely performed in patients with PHPT; 2) neurocognitive or neuropsychiatric manifestations, if present, should not be used on their own to recommend PTX; and they conclude that further research is needed, including the development of a disease-specific tool to assess quality of life in this condition ( 84 ). Bone Density and Fractures Successful PTX is associated with sustained increases in bone mineral density (BMD), which are most pronounced and occur most rapidly at the lumbar spine and femoral neck (sites rich in cancellous bone) with later increases observed at the distal one-third radius ( 110 , 111 ). At 1, 5, and 10 years, BMD increased by 9%, 6%, and 12% at the lumbar spine; by 1%, 7%, and 10% at the femoral neck; and by 4%, 8%, and 7% at the distal radius, respectively ( 111 ). These postoperative gains in BMD were maintained over time, with values remaining significantly above baseline throughout the entire 15-year follow-up period at all three skeletal sites ( 111 ). Factors leading to significant BMD increases are severe disease, young age, and good renal function ( 112 ). PTX has shown a consistent association with reduced fracture risk , particularly for hip and overall fractures, in large cohort studies ( 113 , 114 ); however, no randomized controlled trials have evaluated fracture outcomes as a primary endpoint . Hangge compared pre- and post-operative bone densitometry changes in 100 patients under 75 years of age and 100 patients over 75. In the older group, bone mineral density improved by 2.5% at the femoral neck, 5.2% at the lumbar spine, and 1.1% at the distal radius over a 19.8-month follow-up. No significant differences were observed between age groups in terms of post-surgical densitometric changes. Likewise, preoperative bone health was similar between groups. Despite this, patients over 75 had a higher rate of post-surgical fractures: 17.1% vs. 1.4%, p = 0.011.
