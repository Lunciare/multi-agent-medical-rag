PHPT may present as asymptomatic hypercalcemia in 70–80% of patients ( 42 , 43 ), as normocalcemic hyperparathyroidism , or as classic hyperparathyroidism . The clinical presentation of PHPT depends on the duration of the disease at the time of diagnosis and its association with other endocrine syndromes, such as MEN1, MEN4, MEN2A, and hyperparathyroidism-jaw tumor syndrome. ASYMPTOMATIC HYPERCALCEMIA Currently, asymptomatic hypercalcemia is the most common presentation of PHPT across all age groups, including adults over 65 years of age. Patients typically present with stable calcium and PTH levels, and serum calcium rarely exceeds 1 mg/dL above the upper limit of the normal range. Fewer than 5% of patients will experience progressive hypercalcemia over time ( 44 ); however, patients may present with osteoporosis, primarily affecting the forearm and cortical bone ( 45 – 47 ), vertebral fractures, hypercalciuria, and nephrolithiasis ( 48 ). Older adults may report nonspecific constitutional, neuropsychiatric, or cognitive symptoms including weakness, fatigue, joint pain, depression, anxiety, sleep disturbance, and perceived cognitive decline ( 36 , 49 - 55 ). While severe hypercalcemia in symptomatic PHPT is clearly associated with altered mental status ( 56 ), it remains uncertain whether more subtle neurocognitive or neuropsychiatric manifestations are specifically attributable to PHPT in the absence of marked hypercalcemia. Moreover, older adults are particularly susceptible to bone demineralization and fractures ( 57 , 58 ). An Italian study compared the clinical presentation of PHPT in 462 patients, of whom 54.1% were under 65 years and 45.9% were older than 65. No significant differences were found in serum calcium, PTH, or vitamin D levels. Urinary calcium excretion was lower in older adults (205 vs 308 mg/day), and renal involvement was also less frequent (25% vs 49.2%). However, skeletal involvement was more prevalent in older adults (58% vs 44%). Compared to younger patients, older adults exhibited a lower frequency of classic presentation (44.4% vs 57.2%) and a higher frequency of asymptomatic disease without surgical criteria (18.4% vs 5.6%). Skeletal involvement was more pronounced in older adults, with the forearm being the most affected site, whereas the lumbar spine was more commonly involved in younger patients.
