The primary outcome was the occurrence of postoperative complications such as hypocalcemia, vocal cord paralysis and recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, bleeding, neck hematoma, hemorrhagic shock, acute blood loss anemia, tracheal or airway injury, postoperative infection, sepsis, need for reoperation, and other complications. The secondary outcomes were ICU admission, hospital readmission within 30 days, and prolonged hospital stay. A total of 111 patients (4.2%) experienced complications, most commonly reoperation, hypocalcemia, and vocal cord paralysis. There were no statistically significant differences in complication rates among the age groups: 50–64 years: OR 0.51 (95% CI, 0.28–0.92), 65–74 years: OR 0.72 (95% CI, 0.39–1.33), 75 years: OR 1.03 (95% CI, 0.54–1.95). There was also no association between age and ICU admission or hospital readmission. However, older patients had longer hospital stays: OR 2.38 (95% CI, 1.57–3.6). Only 10% of younger patients required hospitalization for more than 3 days, compared to 28% of older patients. Older individuals also had more comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes, heart failure, hypertension, coronary artery disease, renal insufficiency, and osteoporosis ( 46 ). Bartz-Kurycki retrospectively analyzed 1,182 patients who underwent PTX for PHPT. Patients were stratified by age groups: 50–59, 60–69, and >70 years. There were no differences in the prevalence of multiglandular disease: 31%, 26%, and 30%, respectively (p=0.2), nor in the rate of complications such as persistent or recurrent hyperparathyroidism, permanent hypoparathyroidism, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, or hematoma ( 94 ). Thomas conducted a multi-institutional study evaluating 30-day postoperative outcomes in 7,313 patients with PHPT. Patients were grouped by age: 45–64, 65–79, and >80 years. Patients aged 65–79 and >80 were more frequently hospitalized for surgery than those aged 45–64: 42.4%, 46.8% vs. 36% (p<0.01). They also had higher ASA classifications (Class 3 and 4): 42.4%, 59.8% vs. 24.2% (p<0.01). Those >65 years experienced 1–2 complications more frequently: 2.2% vs. 1.3% (p<0.01) and had more respiratory complications: 0.9% vs. 0.3% (p<0.01). Hospital stays longer than one day were also more common in the 65–79 and >80 groups: 7.7%, 12.2% vs. 6.5% (p<0.01). Additionally, 78.1% of patients over 80 years had hypertension compared to 48.1% of those aged 45–64 (p<0.01).
