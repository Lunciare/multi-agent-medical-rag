Diagnostic evaluation should never delay immediate initiation of empiric glucocorticoid therapy when adrenal crisis is suspected, as formal confirmation can be completed after clinical stabilization. In secondary adrenal insufficiency, the cosyntropin stimulation test has limited sensitivity (approximately 64%) and may yield false-negative results, particularly in patients with recent-onset or mild ACTH deficiency. Clinical Significance The test plays a critical role in confirming adrenal cortical dysfunction and guiding appropriate glucocorticoid replacement therapy to prevent adrenal crisis. Diagnostic Utility In primary adrenal insufficiency, the cosyntropin stimulation test serves as the diagnostic gold standard and has been validated against the insulin tolerance test. Elevated endogenous ACTH levels already maximally stimulate the adrenal cortex, preventing an adequate cortisol response even to supraphysiologic doses of cosyntropin. In secondary adrenal insufficiency, the cosyntropin stimulation test has limited diagnostic performance, with a sensitivity of approximately 64% and specificity of 93%. False-negative results may occur in mild or recent-onset ACTH deficiency, as supraphysiologic cosyntropin doses can stimulate partially atrophied adrenal glands.
