Stretching of the optic nerve is yet another postulated mechanism of optic neuropathy in TED. Unlike the peripheral nerves, the optic nerve has a regular and uniform organization of nerve fibers. This anatomical difference presumably makes it more vulnerable to damage through circumferential straining. Impaired venous drainage and hypoperfusion of the apical portion of the optic nerve may also contribute to further optic nerve injury. History and Physical In subclinical forms, the patients may even be asymptomatic. A comprehensive clinical examination is required that may reveal a decrease in best-corrected visual acuity, relative afferent pupillary defect in unilateral and asymmetric cases, optic disc edema and hyperemia (acute form), optic atrophy (chronic form), which may or may not be accompanied by features of activity, that is, a Clinical Activity Score of more than 2 out of 7 (EUGOGO 2016 guidelines), restricted extra-ocular movements, proptosis, strabismus, and even ptosis.
