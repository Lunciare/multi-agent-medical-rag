Additionally, psychiatric patients are at risk for hypokalemia due to disordered eating and drug side effects. This condition is prevalent in hospitalized patients, particularly children and febrile and critically ill individuals. In developing countries, an increased risk of mortality has been observed in children when severe hypokalemia is associated with diarrhea and severe malnutrition. Pathophysiology Hypokalemia's symptoms are primarily attributed to potassium's role in various physiological processes. Potassium maintains cell membrane potential, nerve impulse conduction, muscle function (including skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscles), and acid-base balance. Abnormally low potassium levels can lead to disturbances in these physiological processes, resulting in a wide range of symptoms. Potassium is essential for maintaining normal muscle function. Decreased potassium levels can disrupt neuromuscular transmission and impair muscle contraction. Severe hypokalemia can lead to paralysis, particularly in extreme cases where potassium levels are critically low. This paralysis may affect respiratory muscles, leading to respiratory failure. Severe hypokalemia can also result in muscle cramps, rhabdomyolysis, and myoglobinuria.
