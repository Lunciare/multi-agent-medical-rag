Pancreatic cystic neoplasm: Appears as a multiloculated cyst with thick walls and elevated carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) levels in the cystic fluid. Clinical features include loss of weight and appetite. Other differential diagnoses include extra-pancreatic pathologies such as peptic ulcer disease, gastric cancer, bowel obstruction, ovarian tumor/cysts, acute myocardial infarction, or pneumonia. Prognosis Spontaneous resolution of pseudocyst is common, especially for those that occur after an episode of acute pancreatitis. The maturation of pancreatic pseudocysts takes approximately 2 to 6 weeks, and during this time, 33% of cysts resolve spontaneously. Complications The goal is to avoid complications which include: Infection Rupture into the gastrointestinal tract causing signs and symptoms of bleeding Free rupture into the peritoneal cavity causing abdominal pain, peritonitis, or even death There have been case reports of large pseudocysts causing increased intra-abdominal pressure, which can present with orthopnoea, dyspnea, abdominal pain, distention, and new organ failure. Pancreatic pseudoaneurysm Compression of the surrounding structures causing biliary complications or portal hypertension
