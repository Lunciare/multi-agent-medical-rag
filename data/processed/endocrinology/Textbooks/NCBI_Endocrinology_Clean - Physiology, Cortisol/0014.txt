Hypocortisolism Primary adrenal insufficiency, also known as Addison disease, most commonly results from autoimmune adrenalitis. Other etiologies include malignancy, infection, and adrenal hemorrhage. Autoimmune adrenalitis occurs when immune-mediated destruction targets the adrenal cortex. Secondary adrenal insufficiency arises from inadequate production of ACTH by the anterior pituitary gland. This condition may result from pituitary pathology, although suppression of the HPA axis due to chronic exogenous glucocorticoid therapy represents the most frequent cause. Tertiary adrenal insufficiency occurs when the hypothalamus fails to release sufficient CRH. Aldosterone deficiency contributes to electrolyte disturbances, with laboratory findings commonly showing hyponatremia and hyperkalemia. Glucocorticoid replacement therapy, such as hydrocortisone, is required to manage hypocortisolism. Dosage adjustments are necessary during acute stressors, including illness or surgery, to prevent adrenal crisis.
