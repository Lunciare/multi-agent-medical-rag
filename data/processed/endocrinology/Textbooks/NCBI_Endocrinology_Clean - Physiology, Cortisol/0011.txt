These neuroanatomical changes are associated with an elevated risk of neurodegenerative disorders, such as Alzheimer disease and Parkinson disease. Ophthalmologic Effects Chronic cortisol elevation increases intraocular pressure, predisposing to the development of glaucoma. Prolonged exposure to elevated cortisol is also associated with posterior subcapsular cataract formation. Increased choroidal thickness and macular alterations correlate with heightened cortisol levels, contributing to the risk of central serous chorioretinopathy. Gastrointestinal Effects During acute stress, cortisol exerts gastroprotective effects by maintaining gastric mucosal integrity, mucosal blood flow, and mucus secretion. These mechanisms reduce susceptibility to ulcerogenic injury. In contrast, sustained cortisol elevation, whether endogenous or exogenous, impairs gastrointestinal mucosal defense and barrier function. Cortisol also alters gastrointestinal motility and nutrient absorption. The combined effects promote gut dysbiosis and increased intestinal permeability, leading to heightened mucosal inflammation. Mechanism The HPA axis tightly regulates cortisol secretion. Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), synthesized and secreted by the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus, stimulates the anterior pituitary to release ACTH. ACTH then acts on the adrenal cortex to promote cortisol synthesis and secretion. Magnocellular neurons in the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei produce arginine vasopressin.
