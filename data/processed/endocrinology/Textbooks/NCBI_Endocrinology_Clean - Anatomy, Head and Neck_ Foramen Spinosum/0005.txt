Another developmental variant arises when the middle meningeal artery originates directly from a persistent stapedial artery. In such cases, the artery typically enters the cranial cavity via the foramen ovale rather than the foramen spinosum, resulting in a diminutive or absent foramen. Duplication of the foramen spinosum occurs in approximately 0.4% to 2.5% of skulls, whereas complete absence is observed in fewer than 1% of cases, most commonly associated with a persistent stapedial artery. Hypoplasia is more frequent unilaterally than bilaterally. Surgical Considerations The foramen spinosum serves as a surgical landmark and guide during microsurgery of the middle cranial fossa. Extracranially, the auriculotemporal nerve typically forms a loop around the middle meningeal artery, creating a close anatomical relationship that increases the risk of nerve injury during procedures in the infratemporal fossa. The middle meningeal artery represents a key target in the endovascular management of chronic subdural hematoma.
