CP is an extremely vascular tumor that mostly obtains its blood supply from the coronary circulation (right coronary artery > left circumflex artery > left coronary artery > left anterior descending artery). Etiology Although there is no clear data available, recent reports showed that genetic mutations and familial etiology contribute up to 25% to 50% of pheochromocytomas. In the literature review, one case of cardiac pheochromocytoma associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) was reported . Other genetic syndromes have been found to be associated with pheochromocytomas in general, including Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2 (RET gene), neurofibromatosis 1 (NF1), Von Hippel-Lindau disease (VHL gene), adrenocortical adenoma and/or endocrine tumors as in Carney’s triad and paraganglioma syndromes type 1, 3, and 4 (SDH gene). Thirty percent (30%) of patients with malignant adrenal pheochromocytomas and forty-eight percent (48%) of patients with malignant paragangliomas (extra-adrenal pheochromocytomas) are found to have germline mutations of succinate dehydrogenase subunit B (SDH). The CPs can also be associated with the above syndromes. Epidemiology Cardiac pheochromocytomas (CPs) represent the rarest form of primary cardiac tumors.
