In mild forms of the disease, when treated appropriately with calcium and vitamin D, a normal life expectancy is not unreasonable. For others with a more severe expression of the AHO phenotype, the presence of obesity, sleep apnea, and mental retardation can cause significant morbidity and mortality. Complications Chronic hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia can lead to parkinsonism which may or may not resolve with appropriate calcium and vitamin D supplementation. Children with brachydactyly may suffer from difficulty with fine motor skills. There is an increased prevalence of carpal tunnel syndrome seen in AHO patients. There are reports of higher than normal, as well as an earlier presentation of spinal stenosis, which can lead to lower extremity paraparesis. Finally, there is a more than four-fold increased risk for sleep apnea reported in childhood.
