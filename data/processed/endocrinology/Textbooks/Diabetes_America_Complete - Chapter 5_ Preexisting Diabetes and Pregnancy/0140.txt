The same authors then conducted a retrospective cohort study of term singleton births complicated by shoulder dystocia in 1997–2006 in all of California (383). During the 10-year period, 62,762 deliveries were complicated by shoulder dystocia, with 3,168 reports of BPP (5.0%). Among 5,426 cases of shoulder dystocia in diabetic women (both gesta­ tional diabetes and preexisting diabetes in pregnancy), there were 497 cases of BPP (9.2%) versus 4.7% of births in women without diabetes. The frequency of BPP according to birth weight inter­ vals was graphed for maternal diabetes: ~7% of shoulder dystocia cases at 3,500–3,999 g, ~9.5% at 4,000–4,599 g, ~14.5% at 4,500–4,999 g, and ~19.5% at birth weight >5,000 g (383). Among all cases of shoulder dystocia, the effect size of diabetes on BPP was adjusted odds ratio 1.77 (95% CI 1.58–2.00).
