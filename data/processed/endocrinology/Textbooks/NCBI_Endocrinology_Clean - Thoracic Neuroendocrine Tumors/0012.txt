These tumors exhibit aggressive behavior, with a tendency to invade locally. Hence, 50% of patients present with invasion to local organs at the time of diagnosis, and 20% to 30% have distant metastatic disease, which occurs via lymphatic or hematogenous spread. Most patients present with symptoms of mass compression to local structures, while only a third of cases are asymptomatic. Symptoms can vary depending on the extent of invasion. Common complaints include cough, dyspnea, chest pain, and hoarseness from recurrent laryngeal nerve invasion. Superior vena cava syndrome has been observed in up to 20% of patients at presentation.
