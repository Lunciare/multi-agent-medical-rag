Adjuvant chemotherapy and RT are reasonable options following total resection for control of the residual disease. According to the NCCN, in patients who have had an incomplete resection, RT, preferably with chemotherapy based on cisplatin or carboplatin plus etoposide, is recommended for patients with moderately or poorly differentiated disease. However, the evidence supporting the use of adjuvant RT or chemotherapy is limited. Metastatic or Recurrent Disease Even in the instance of recurrent disease, if the tumor is deemed to be resectable, surgical resection should be attempted. In cases of unresectable or metastatic disease, systemic therapy is favored. For tumors with evidence of somatostatin receptor expression, somatostatin analogs are chosen, although data regarding the benefits of this therapy for thymic NETs are scant. In such circumstances, octreotide and lanreotide are considered reasonable options, especially in the rare instance of patients presenting with carcinoid syndrome.
