ABSTRACT Pediatric primary or monogenic dyslipidemias are a heterogeneous group of disorders, characterized by severe elevation of cholesterol, triglycerides, or rarely a combination of the two. Monogenic hypercholesterolemias have elevated low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C) levels and very high risk of premature atherosclerotic disease. They are caused by mutations in genes involved in the receptor-mediated uptake of LDL by the LDL receptor (LDLR) in hepatocytes. Autosomal dominant familial hypercholesterolemia results from mutations in LDLR, apolipoprotein B-100 (APOB), or proprotein convertase subtilisin-like kexin type 9 ( PCSK9) . Autosomal recessive hypercholesterolemia is caused by mutations in the LDLR adaptor protein 1 ( LDLRAP1 ) gene. Type 1 hyperlipoproteinemia (Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome) have severe fasting hypertriglyceridemia secondary to accumulation of triglyceride (TG)-rich lipoproteins, especially chylomicrons.
