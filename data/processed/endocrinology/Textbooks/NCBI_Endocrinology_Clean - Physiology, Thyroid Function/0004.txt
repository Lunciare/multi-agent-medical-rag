Function T3 is responsible for affecting many organs and tissues throughout the body, which can, in summary, be the effect of increasing metabolic rate and protein synthesis. Parafollicular cells, or C cells, are responsible for the production and secretion of calcitonin. Calcitonin opposes the parathyroid hormone in decreasing blood calcium levels and maintaining calcium homeostasis. Mechanism The thyroid gland produces iodothyronines, of which there are 3. The primary secretory product is inactive thyroxine, or T4, a prohormone of triiodothyronine, or T3. T4 is converted to T3 peripherally by type 1 deiodinase in tissues with high blood flow, such as the liver and kidneys. In the brain, T4 is converted to active T3 by type 2 deiodinase produced by glial cells. The third iodothyronine is called reverse T3, or rT3. rT3 is inactive and forms by type 3 deiodinase activity on T4. These iodothyronines are composed of thyroglobulin and iodine.
