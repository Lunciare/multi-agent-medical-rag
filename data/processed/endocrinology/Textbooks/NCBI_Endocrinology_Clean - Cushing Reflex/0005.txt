Eventually, agonal breathing may develop as herniation of the brain begins, progressing to respiratory and cardiac arrest. Overall, the Cushing reflex seems to be the last hemodynamic response to a systemic sympathetic activation that follows the acute rise in ICP. Clinical Significance As a result of the Cushing reflex, the Cushing triad is typically observed in the later stages of acute head injury. Although the reflex is a homeostatic response by the body to rescue under-perfused brain tissues, the Cushing triad is, unfortunately, a late sign of increasing ICP and indicates that brainstem herniation is imminent. Patients who present to the emergency department with concerns for increased ICP and 2 of 3 signs of the Cushing reflex have been found to have almost 2-fold higher mortality than patients with normal and stable vital signs. It is, therefore, important to recognize early signs of elevated ICP (eg, a headache, nausea, vomiting, altered level of consciousness) and intervene as early as possible.
