Many clinicians use the presence of bradycardia and hypertension as an indication of increased ICP; however, this signals a late-stage Cushing reflex that carries a poor prognosis for the patient. Moving into the future, it may be wise to look for the presence of tachycardia and hypertension in patients with suspected intracranial pathology so that interventions can start promptly. Cushing reflex is most usually an irreversible condition with a terminal prognosis for the patient. Initial emergency treatments aim to lower the ICP rapidly and include elevation of the patient’s head 30° to 45°, mannitol and/or furosemide, which act as an osmotic diuretic, induced hyperventilation, steroids, or cerebrospinal fluid drainage. The nurses caring for the neurosurgical patient in the ward or intensive care unit should be aware of the Cushing reflex and bring any abnormal findings to the attending physician's and other clinicians' immediate attention.
