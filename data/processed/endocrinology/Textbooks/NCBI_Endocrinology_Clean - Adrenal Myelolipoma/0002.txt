Etiology The pathogenesis of adrenal myelolipomas is not definitively known, but many studies show a relation to ACTH stimulation. One hypothesis suggests that stimuli such as necrosis or inflammation could lead to the metaplasia of the reticuloendothelial cells, which could lead to the development of adrenal myelolipomas. The increased lesion incidence in the advanced years of life supports this hypothesis. Another theory is that embryological origins may contribute to myelolipoma development. In fetal circulation, until the bone marrow is fully mature, adrenal tissue is capable of hematopoiesis, and this is also present in adults with chronic anemia or hematologic disorders, such as thalassemias, hereditary spherocytosis, or sickle-cell anemia. This finding is also supported by reports of large doses of erythropoietin-stimulating agents causing bilateral adrenal myelolipomas. Lastly, in an important historical study, Seyle and Stone noted that injection of crude anterior pituitary extract in rats leads to the transformation of the adrenal cortex into bone marrow-like tissue.
