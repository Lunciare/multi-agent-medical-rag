In 1991, Bergren et al. did a large study involving 12205 patients, out of which 29% of the 101 AH patients had a history of DM in comparison to 10% of 101 controls. The difference was statistically significant (P=0.00007). However, Fawzi AA et al., in their study involving 10801 autopsy eyes, did not find any significant association with DM. The unilateral occurrence of AH in the setting of systemic disease like DM questions its significant association. Similarly, an association of AH with other diseases like hypertension, atherosclerosis, and hyperlipidemia failed to be reproducible in large population-based studies. Hypercholesterolemia, elevated serum calcium levels, and gout are described to be associated with AH in small studies. Evaluation Patients with asteroid hyalosis are usually asymptomatic, and this condition is mostly diagnosed during the routine ophthalmic examination. In eyes with AH, only a small proportion of light gets scattered, and the smooth surface of the ABs results in the minimal scattering of light. ABs may be visually symptomatic when they are sufficiently closer to the macula or when a complete posterior vitreous detachment causes the particles to be concentrated behind the lens.
