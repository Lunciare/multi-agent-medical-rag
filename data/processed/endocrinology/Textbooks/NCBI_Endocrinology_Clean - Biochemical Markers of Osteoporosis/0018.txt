A study reported that participants with BTM levels in the highest quartile were twice as likely to experience a bone fragility fracture within the next 5 years compared to those in the first, second, and third quartiles. However, studies examining BTM levels have yet to provide conclusive, universal recommendations for reference ranges to identify abnormalities in postmenopausal patients. Literature has also shown a notable lack of data in examining BTM levels in men. When evaluating CTX-1 and P1NP values to monitor adherence and effective response to anti-osteoporotic regimens, conclusions on absolute values indicating adequate treatment are also lacking. Most studies have focused on establishing reference intervals for oral bisphosphonates, which are first-line medications in osteoporosis treatment and have a high noncompliance rate. Several studies recommend that the goal of bisphosphonate therapy in postmenopausal women should be to achieve BTM levels within the lower half of the reference interval established for healthy premenopausal women. However, this approach depends on the proper establishment of universal reference values.
