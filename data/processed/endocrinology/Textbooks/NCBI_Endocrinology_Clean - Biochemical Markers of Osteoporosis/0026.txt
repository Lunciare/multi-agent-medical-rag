The potential of BTMs and miRNAs as additional tools to assess medication efficacy, predict fracture risk, and investigate the causes of osteoporosis is highly promising. These tools could help healthcare professionals improve patient outcomes and reduce the overall cost of the disease. However, effectively using BTMs to manage osteoporosis in older men remains a challenge and is still some time away. Bone Turnover Markers in Monitoring Pharmacotherapy Response Persistent derangements in BTMs in the setting of pharmacotherapy treatment for osteoporosis can help identify patients who may require alternative treatments and may indicate an independent underlying condition affecting bone turnover rates. Significantly elevated BTM levels (>3 SD above the normal reference range) can assist clinicians in identifying patients who require further evaluation for secondary causes of increased bone turnover. However, a universal reference range must still be established to implement this approach as standard practice in clinical settings.
