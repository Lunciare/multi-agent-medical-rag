They secrete two hormones that influence the rate of metabolism : T3 (tri-iodothyronine) and T4 (tetra-iodothyronine or thyroxine) T3 is more active than T4, even though both affect the target cells. EM features: Electron microscopy of follicular cells shows apical microvilli, abundant granular endoplasmic reticulum, supranuclear Golgi complex, lysosomes, microtubules, and microfilaments. The activity of these cells is influenced by thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) secreted by hypophysis cerebri. Parafollicular Cells (C–cells) These are also known as clear cells or light cells. The C stands for calcitonin or clear. These are large, polyhedral, pale-staining cells with oval and eccentric nuclei. They are widely distributed between follicular cells and their basement membrane. They also lie between adjoining follicular cells but do not reach the lumen. In some species, they are also seen between the follicles in connective tissue in groups. They secrete a hormone known as calcitonin.
