There is no definitive data to support either method; further study is required. Patients should be carefully and repeatedly instructed that placement of a Foley catheter should only be attempted when the AUS is deactivated and the compression cuff is completely open. If a patient seeks care in an emergency department or healthcare facility, all personnel they encounter must be informed of this constraint. Any attempt to pass a Foley catheter without cuff deflation and deactivation could result in significant urethral injury or sphincter damage. Patients should be informed that most healthcare personnel will be unfamiliar with the AUS device and that they must know how to deactivate the device. The manufacturer provides a card for patients to carry with them at all times to identify them as having an artificial urinary sphincter that may require special attention.
