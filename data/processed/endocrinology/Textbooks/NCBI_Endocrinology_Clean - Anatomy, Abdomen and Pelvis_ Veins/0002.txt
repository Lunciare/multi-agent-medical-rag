Superficial veins are located between the skin and the muscular fascia, while deep veins accompany arteries and drain muscles. The intima, media, and adventitia comprise the vascular wall. The intima is anti-thrombogenic due to the in situ production of prostaglandin I2, cofactors of antithrombin, and tissue-type plasminogen activator. Three smooth muscle layers, together with collagen and elastin, constitute the media. The adventitia is the thickest and outermost layer of the vein wall. The primary function of the pelvic veins and gastric veins is to drain deoxygenated blood and return it to the heart. Forming in the abdomen at the level of the fifth lumbar vertebrae, the common iliac vein forms through the unification of the external iliac vein and the internal iliac vein. Each set of external and internal iliac veins on the right and left sides of the body forms an associated left or right common iliac vein.
