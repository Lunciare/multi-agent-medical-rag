Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Veins
KEYWORDS: vein, iliac, veins, iliac vein, internal, pelvic, common, external, left, internal iliac, common iliac, venous, inferior, external iliac, gastric

The left gastric vein originates from the portal vein or splenic vein, and these veins run along the lesser curvature of the stomach from the esophagogastric junction. In the pelvis, the left and right common iliac veins have distinct pathways. The right common iliac vein moves in a straight course to form the IVC, while the left common iliac vein must initially join the right common iliac vein at the confluence with the IVC. Therefore, the left common iliac vein’s pathway is not as linear. The left common iliac vein is also longer than the right and receives drainage from the median sacral vein. The iliolumbar and lateral sacral veins drain into both common iliac veins. Frequently containing 1-2 valves, the external iliac vein receives from the following: the inferior epigastric vein, the deep circumflex iliac vein, and other pelvic veins. The inferior epigastric vein joins the external iliac vein superior to the inguinal ligament. The common femoral vein transitions to form the external iliac vein, which occurs at the level of the inguinal ligament. The external iliac vein receives the deep circumflex iliac vein and the inferior epigastric vein via the same venous tributaries as the external iliac artery.
