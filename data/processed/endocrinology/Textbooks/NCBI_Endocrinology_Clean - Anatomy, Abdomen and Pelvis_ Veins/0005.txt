The internal iliac veins drain the parietal and visceral plexuses (2 independent networks that form the intrapelvic venous system) via valveless, interconnected pathways. It also functions as the main venous drainage system of the small pelvic veins. The internal iliac vein also receives venous drainage from the middle rectal veins, pudendal veins, obturator, and inferior and superior vesical veins. Gonadal veins are 1 of the visceral veins that branch from the internal iliac vein. The left gonadal veins drain into the left renal vein, while the right gonadal vein joins the IVC. These veins can help prevent reflux. Embryology Cardinal veins provide the majority of venous drainage into the sinus venosus during embryogenesis. The anterior cardinal veins drain the cranial portions of the embryo, while the posterior cardinal veins drain the caudal portion. These posterior cardinal veins regress, except for the iliac anastomosis, supracardinal, and subcardinal sections.
