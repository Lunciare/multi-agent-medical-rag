Introduction The perineal body, also referred to as the "central tendon of the perineum," is a fibromuscular structure located in the midline of the perineum (see Image . The exact location of this structure is the midline at the junction of the anus and urogenital triangle in both female and male individuals. In the male body, the central tendon of the perineum is located between the anus and the bulb of the penis. In contrast, the female body has this structure lying between the anus and the posterior limit of the vulvar orifice. The perineal body helps strengthen the pelvic floor and provides attachments to the following muscles: Bulbospongiosus: A striated muscle that adheres anterolaterally to the perineal body. Deep transverse perineal muscle: In the male body, this muscle adheres laterally to the perineal body.
