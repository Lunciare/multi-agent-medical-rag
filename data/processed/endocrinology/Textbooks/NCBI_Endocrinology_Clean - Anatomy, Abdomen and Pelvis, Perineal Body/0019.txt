A perineal body injury may result in a rectocele, characterized by the perineal body's separation into 2 segments connected by a stretched and weakened central portion. Clinical Significance The perineal body is a structure that must be assessed when evaluating pelvic floor function. The perineal body moves inferiorly during inhalation as the pelvic floor muscles relax and superiorly during exhalation as these muscles contract. During a voluntary squeeze, the perineal body lifts anterosuperiorly. Manual vaginal palpation is used to evaluate pelvic floor muscle strength. Movement of the perineal body against the examiner's fingers, inserted into the vagina, is restricted during active muscle contraction. Muscle strength is graded using the Modified Oxford Grading System, with severity scores as follows: 0 = No contraction 1 = Flicker 2 = Weak 3 = Moderate 4 = Good (with lift) 5 = Strong An alternative method involves external palpation of the perineal body using a light touch to detect the balance of forces from tissues converging on the perineal body.
