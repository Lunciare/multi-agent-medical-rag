Neurosurgical techniques may be indicated for rhinorrhea and some symptomatic secondary causes of empty sella syndrome with increased intracranial pressure and acute visual disturbances. If hypopituitarism is present it should be treated following the current recommendations ( 23 ) and hyperprolactinemia treated with dopamine agonists. CONCLUSION Primary empty sella can be heterogeneous in origin and presentations range from an asymptomatic incidental radiological finding to endocrine and neuro-ophthalmological manifestations. Female sex, multiple pregnancies, obesity, and arterial hypertension are associated risk factors as well as the syndrome of benign intracranial hypertension. Secondary empty sella is caused by various pathological processes resulting in shrinkage of the pituitary gland.
