(Source: Vanner et al , 2023) Unintentional tracheostomy occurs when the incision is made too low or in an incorrect location, causing tracheal damage. Careful identification of the CTM and precise technique are essential to avoid this complication. If significant tracheal damage occurs or if a stable airway cannot be maintained due to incorrect placement, conversion to a formal tracheostomy may be necessary. A false tract forms when an instrument deviates from the tracheal lumen, often due to poor visualization, incorrect technique, or difficult anatomy. False tracts may cause failed ventilation, subcutaneous emphysema, hemorrhage, infection, and esophageal perforation. This complication may be prevented by ensuring proper identification of the CTM before incision and using a midline, caudally directed approach when inserting a scalpel or needle.
