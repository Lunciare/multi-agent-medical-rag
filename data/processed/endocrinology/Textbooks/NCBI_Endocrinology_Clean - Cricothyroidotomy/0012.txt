Monitoring After proper placement is confirmed, ventilation and oxygenation should be continuously assessed using capnography and pulse oximetry. Standard monitoring should also include noninvasive blood pressure and heart rate assessment. Electrocardiography should be considered when available. Complications Early Complications Early complications of cricothyroidotomy include bleeding, endobronchial intubation, laceration or fracture of local structures, posterior tracheal injury, unintentional tracheostomy, false tract formation, and hypoxia. Bleeding is the most commonly reported complication, though some hemorrhage is expected during CTM incision and dissection due to the presence of several overlying or adjacent blood vessels. Significant hemorrhage occurs in up to 50% of cases and, in rare instances, may lead to asphyxiation or aspiration. Bleeding in the cricothyroid area can also reduce visibility during airway management. If severe bleeding occurs, direct pressure or packing at the site can help control the sources.
