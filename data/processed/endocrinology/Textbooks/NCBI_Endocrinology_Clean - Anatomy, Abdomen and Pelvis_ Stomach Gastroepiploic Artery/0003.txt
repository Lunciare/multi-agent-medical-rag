Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Stomach Gastroepiploic Artery
KEYWORDS: artery, rgea, stomach, lgea, branches, blood, greater, vessels, supply, gastric, branch, shown, posterior, omentum, gastroepiploic

The most voluminous are the right and left primitive aorta, from which the fetal arterial tree originates, which develops parallel to the cardiac tube and its subsequent evolution. The formation of anastomoses between the intra- and extra-embryonic vessels completes the circulatory system. The vasculature that supplies the gastrointestinal system originates from the anterior ventral segmental arteries of the primitive aorta between 2 layers of dorsal mesogastrium. The tenth segmental artery is the source of the foregut artery (celiac artery). This artery supplies blood to all foregut structures, migrates causally, and rotates in concert with the stomach during embryonic development. The celiac artery gives rise to both the hepatic and splenic arteries. The hepatic artery further branches into the gastroduodenal artery, giving rise to the RGEA. The splenic artery then gives rise to the LGEA. The RGEA and LGEA, along with other local structures, elongate with the development of the greater curvature of the stomach and lesser sac. Blood Supply and Lymphatics The origin of the RGEA is usually posterior to the first part of the duodenum, arising from the gastroduodenal artery.
