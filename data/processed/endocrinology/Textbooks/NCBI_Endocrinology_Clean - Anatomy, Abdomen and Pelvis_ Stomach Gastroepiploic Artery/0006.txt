Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Stomach Gastroepiploic Artery
KEYWORDS: artery, rgea, stomach, lgea, branches, blood, greater, vessels, supply, gastric, branch, shown, posterior, omentum, gastroepiploic

Variants Evidence of anastomosis between the LGEA and the RGEA ranges from about 50 to 94%. One anatomical study of 17 cadavers has shown that 94% of vessels are anastomose, with 70% being continuous (without a branching system connecting the two). Various other angiogram studies have shown a 43 to 65% anastomosis rate, with 23 to 37% continuous. The RGEA has been shown to have many different origins. The RGEA most commonly branches from the gastroduodenal artery but may branch off the superior mesenteric artery when no gastroduodenal artery develops . Anatomical studies have also shown that the RGEA receives equal-sized branches from both the celiac and superior mesenteric arteries. The LGEA often arises as a common branch with the inferior splenic artery and then branches into 2 separate arteries distally in its course. The omental branches of the LGEA have been shown to create anastomoses with branches of the middle colic artery. Surgical Considerations The stomach depends largely on the RGEA and right gastric arteries for its blood supply; therefore, surgeons need to minimize iatrogenic damage that could compromise these vessels during surgery.
