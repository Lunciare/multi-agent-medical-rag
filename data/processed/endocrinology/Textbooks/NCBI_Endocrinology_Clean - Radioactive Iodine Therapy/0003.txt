Radioactive Iodine Therapy
KEYWORDS: thyroid, iodine, therapy, radioactive, radioactive iodine, patients, rai, iodine therapy, effects, carcinoma, uptake, cancer, treatment, tissue, disease

iodine is a natural precursor for thyroid hormones. All patients should be on an iodine-free diet for seven days and longer for patients receiving therapy for thyroid carcinoma. Although contraindicated, treatment with antithyroid agent methimazole (MTZ) may be initiated before radioactive iodine therapy in select patients with severe hyperfunctioning thyroid disease or cardiac disease. Transient thyrotoxicosis may transpire shortly following radioactive iodine therapy due to the leakage of stored triiodothyronine (T3) and thyroxine (T4). Thionomide therapy may decrease any storage of thyroid hormones and ensure a euthyroid state to prevent possible complications of thyrotoxicosis. Methimazole is preferred over propylthiouracil(PTU) as it has a significant failure rate. If a patient receives ethionamide, the recommendation is to discontinue it three to five days before radioactive iodine therapy. Adverse Effects Adverse effects range from mild to severe, depending on the dose administered.
