Transcription factors SF1 ( 127 , 128 , 451 , 453 - 460 ), GATA4 ( 128 , 456 , 461 - 467 ), WT1 ( 454 , 468 ) increase, whereas DAX1 ( 454 ) and β-catenin ( 468 ) reduce, SOX9-activated AMH transcription either by binding to specific response elements or by protein-protein interaction ( 469 , 470 ). In vivo , genes can affect AMH levels indirectly through their impact on testicular differentiation instead of acting on gene transcription. Although initially gonadotropin-independent, AMH production falls under FSH control later in fetal life and after birth ( 128 , 216 , 471 - 473 ). FSH regulates AMH transcription through the FSH receptor-Gsα protein-adenylate cyclase-cyclic AMP pathway, resulting in a stimulation of protein kinase A (PKA) activity. PKA mediates phosphorylation of the transcriptional regulators SOX9, SF1 and AP2, as well as of IκB which releases NFκB. In the nucleus these factors activate AMH transcription by binding to their specific response elements on the AMH promoter ( Fig. 21 ). FSH may also indirectly upregulate AMH expression by stimulating estrogen synthesis ( 474 ); estradiol activates AMH promoter activity signaling through estrogen receptor alpha (ERα) binding to a specific estrogen response element (ERE) sequence. A modest action is also mediated through the membrane estrogen receptor GPER.
