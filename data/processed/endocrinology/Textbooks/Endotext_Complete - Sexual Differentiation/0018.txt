SOX9 also affects the differentiation of the reproductive tract by upregulating the expression of anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) ( 127 , 128 ), a Sertoli cell factor involved in male differentiation of the internal genitalia (see below). SOX8 and SOX10 are two other members of the SOX family expressed in the gonads and in several other tissues. During mouse embryo development, the expression of SOX8 and SOX10 is triggered shortly after that of SOX9, but at lower level ( 129 - 132 ). SOX8 is regulated by SOX9 ( 117 ). Like SOX9 itself, SOX8 and SOX10 can synergize with SF1 and upregulate SOX9 expression ( Fig. 4 ) upon binding to TESCO ( 28 ). SOX8 can bind the canonical target DNA sequences and activate Amh transcription acting synergistically with SF1, but with less efficiency than SOX9 ( 129 , 133 ). Later during fetal development, an interaction between SOX9 and SOX8 is required for basal lamina integrity of testis cords and for suppression of FOXL2, two events essential to the normal development of testis cords ( 132 ). An X-linked member of the SOX family, SOX3, although not involved in the normal pathway of fetal gonadal differentiation, is capable of inducing SOX9 expression and testis differentiation when ectopically expressed in the XX gonad ( 134 ). It is also possible that indirect mechanisms mediate Sox9 activation, in line with the hypothesis indicating that SRY might act as a repressor of a negative regulator of the male cascade ( 135 ). For instance, targeted disruption of Foxl2 leads to SOX9 upregulation in the XX gonad ( 136 ), and prostaglandin D2 (PGD2) has been shown to upregulate SOX9 in the absence of SRY ( 137 ). SOX9 expression is maintained at high levels in the male gonad despite down-regulation of SRY soon after testicular differentiation, at least in the mouse ( 110 , 111 ). As mentioned, SOX9 is capable of autoregulating its expression ( 108 ), and other members of the SOX family like SOX3, SOX8 and SOX10 are also able to interact with SF1 to maintain SOX9 expression in the male gonad ( 28 , 132 ).
