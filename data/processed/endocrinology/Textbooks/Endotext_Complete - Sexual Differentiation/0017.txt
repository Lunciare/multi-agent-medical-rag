Another enhancer, mXYSRa/Enh13, located far upstream of Sox9 transcription start site, also plays a critical role in GATA4-, SRY- and SOX9-mediated Sox9 transcription ( 113 , 114 ). SOX9 mimics SRY effects independently of SRY expression. In fact, overexpression of SOX9 during early embryogenesis induces testicular differentiation in two different models of transgenic XX mice ( 115 , 116 ). Functional analysis of SOX9 during sex determination, by conditional gene targeting in mice, has shown that homozygous deletion of Sox9 in XY gonads interferes with sex cord development and with activation of testis specific markers ( 117 ). Further evidence for the role of SOX9 in testicular development comes from observations in humans, in whom a double dose of SOX9 expression is required. Heterozygous mutations result in haploinsufficiency resulting in campomelic dysplasia, a polymalformative syndrome that includes sex-reversal due to gonadal dysgenesis in XY individuals ( 106 , 118 ), whereas gain-of-function of SOX9 in XX individuals leads to sex reversal ( 119 ). In humans more distant regulatory regions of SOX9 have been identified ( 120 ), and confirmed by observations in patients with XY gonadal dysgenesis. No mutation has been found in the TES sequence ( 121 ), instead a 1.9 kb SRY-responsive subfragment of a 32.5 kb interval lying 607.1–639.6 kb upstream of SOX9 —termed XY SR for XY Sex Reversal— seems to be the core of the Sertoli-cell enhancer of human SOX9 . Heterozygous deletions encompassing these sequences were identified in four families with SRY -positive 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis without campomelic dysplasia ( 122 ) and a deletion of a 557–base pair element named enhancer 13 (Enh13), reproduced in mice, led to XY sex reversal ( 123 ). This region is included in a 1.2-Mb deletion previously described in a case of 46,XY DSD with gonadal dysgenesis and no skeletal phenotype ( 124 ). Finally, in line with these observations, overexpression of SOX9 is supposed to underlie testicular development in familial 46,XX SRY -negative males with a 178-kb duplication or a 96-kb triplication in sequences lying 500–600 kb upstream of SOX9 ( 125 , 126 ).
