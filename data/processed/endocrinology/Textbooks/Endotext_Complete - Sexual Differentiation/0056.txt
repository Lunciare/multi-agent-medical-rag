In women, AMH levels are a reliable marker of follicular reserve ( 483 , 484 ) and may be used with relative accuracy to predict the onset of menopause ( 509 ) or to follow the evolution of granulosa cell tumors ( 510 , 511 ). Some AMH mutations with reduced in vitro bioactivity are associated with premature ovarian insufficiency ( 512 ). In contrast, the clinical usefulness of AMH in seminal fluid in men with non-obstructive azoospermia is debatable ( 513 ). Further discussion of the diagnostic and potentially therapeutic value of AMH in the adult ovary and testis is beyond the scope of this review. AMH Transduction: Type I and II AMH Receptors Like other members of the TGFβ family, AMH signals through two distinct membrane-bound receptors, both serine/threonine kinases. Unlike other members of the TGFβ family truncated forms of the AMH primary receptor AMHR2 are not secreted, unless the signal sequence is replaced by the TGFβ one, suggesting that the AMHR2 signal sequence is defective ( 514 ). A three-dimensional model of extra- and intracellular domains built by analogy with crystallized receptors of the TGFβ family ( Fig. 23 ) has served to analyze structure/activity relationship of the receptor molecule ( 514 , 515 ). The AMHR2 gene, located on chromosome 12q13.13, spans 8 kb pairs and is divided into 11 exons. Exons 1-3 code for the signal sequence and extracellular domain, exon 4 for most of the transmembrane domain, and exons 5-11 for the intracellular serine/threonine kinase domains ( 516 ). AMHR2 is expressed in the mesenchymal cells which surround the Müllerian duct, and also in Sertoli, granulosa ( 517 , 518 ), Leydig ( 448 ) and germ cells ( 417 ), endometrium ( 418 ), neurons ( 419 , 421 ) and hypothalamus ( 420 ). Expression of the receptor in the peri-Müllerian mesenchyme requires the presence of the signaling molecule WNT7A ( 387 ).
