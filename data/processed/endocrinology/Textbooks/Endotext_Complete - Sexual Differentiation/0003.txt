Several general transcription factors belonging to the large homeobox gene family play an important role in the stabilization of the intermediate mesoderm and the formation of the urogenital ridges ( Table 2 ). Mice in which Lhx1 ( 7 ), Emx2 ( 8 , 9 ) or Pax2 ( 10 ) have been inactivated fail to develop urogenital derivatives ( 11 ). Most of these ubiquitous factors are essential for the development of other vital embryonic structures. In the early stages of gonadal ridge development, EMX2 is co-expressed with PBX1 and CBX2 and regulates the assembly of tight junctions in the coelomic epithelium ( 12 , 13 ). PBX1, CBX2 and EMX2 are part of transcription complexes that regulate gene expression ( 14 ). CBX2 has two isoforms derived from alternative splicing: the shorter one, CBX2.2, seems to be involved in the formation of the gonadal ridge ( 15 ). Another LIM homeobox factor, LHX9, seems to be essential only for the proliferation of somatic cells of the gonadal ridge ( 16 ) by interacting with WT1 to regulate Sf1 ( 17 ).
