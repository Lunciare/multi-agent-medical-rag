It is clinically and surgically significant due to its connection to major arterial vessels, and as such, transection of the vessel can cause life-threatening hemorrhagic shock. The course of epigastric arteries on the abdominal wall slightly varies among different individuals. The SEA is found 3 to 8.5 cm lateral to the midline of abdomen bilaterally. The IEA can be 4 to 8 cm from midline regardless of body fat habitus or levels. Findings by Rao et al. suggested, however, the arterial strips were not always equidistant from midline on the left and right sides. They found that the right IEA could be 3.2 to 6 cm from the midline, and the left IEA had a range of 1.2 to 5cm from the midline. The IEA takes a medial to lateral path as it ascends from the inferior abdominal wall towards the umbilicus. Surgical Considerations Surgically, it is vital to note the course of epigastric arteries along the abdominal wall for many procedures such as trocar placement in laparoscopic surgeries, percutaneous endoscopic gastronomy (PEG) tube placement, inguinal hernia repair, paracentesis, and drain placement during cardiothoracic surgery.
