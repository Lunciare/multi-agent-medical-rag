know which anatomical zones are safe or dangerous to pierce with their instruments. “Safety” and “danger” zones have been defined by various studies in cadavers in different countries. Bhatti et al. defined the “danger zone” of the superior epigastric artery to be 3 to 8.5 cm lateral to midline and “safety zone” to be from midline to 3cm immediately adjacent to midline bilaterally on the abdomen. Saber et al. found that IEA vessels are found 4-8 cm from midline regardless of body fat habitus. Rao et al. in India as mentioned earlier suggested the arterial strip was not equidistant from midline on the left and right side of the abdomen. They suggested the safety zone of the IEA to be 0 to 3.2cm from midline and 0 to 1.2cm on the right. The IEA as they had found was located more laterally on the abdominal wall on the right then left, especially as it courses upwards toward the umbilicus.
