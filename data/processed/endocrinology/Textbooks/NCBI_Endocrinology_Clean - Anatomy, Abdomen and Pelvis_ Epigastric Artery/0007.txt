Among these numerous anastomoses of the intersegmental vessels are the internal thoracic, superior and inferior epigastric arteries and veins. Physiologic Variants The internal thoracic artery, superior epigastric artery, inferior epigastric artery, left subclavian artery, right subclavian, and right brachiocephalic artery anastomose to form collateral vessels to the thoracic and abdominal aorta. It is mportant to note that over time, in severe distal artery or aortic sclerosis, or coarctation of the aorta, significant enlargement of these vessels occurs to provide blood supply to lower limbs. A transection of the epigastric arteries in these cases will, therefore, result in more severe bleeding and potential critical limb ischemia.Many other anatomical variations of branches exist within different individuals; however, one of the most significant variations to note is the corona mortis branch variation of the inferior epigastric artery, found in 77.27% of patients studied. The corona mortis is a branch of the deep inferior epigastric artery that connects to the external iliac and obturator artery directly posterior and superior to superior pubic rami.
