Secondary Hyperparathyroidism
KEYWORDS: pth, calcium, bone, vitamin, increased, patients, phosphorus, levels, parathyroidectomy, parathyroid, hyperparathyroidism, phosphate, shpt, calcium phosphorus, hyperplasia

Parathyroidectomy is a surgical modality available if medical therapy is unsuccessful or refractory. Other indications include calciphylaxis, refractory pruritus, severe hypercalcemia (serum calcium greater than 10.2 mg/dL) or hyperphosphatemia (serum phosphorus greater than 5.5 mg/dL), anemia hyporesponsive to erythropoietin, PTH levels more than 800 pg/mL (for more than 6 months despite medical therapy) and extraskeletal calcification. The parathyroid glands in secondary hyperparathyroidism are characterized by asymmetric enlargement and nodular hyperplasia. Assessment of parathyroid mass is an important factor in predicting the response to medical management. Glands larger than 1 cm in size or greater than 500 mm^3 ) on ultrasound represent glandular hyperplasia and are usually refractory to medical treatment. It is estimated that surgery will be required in about 15% of patients in 10 years and 38% of patients in 20 years after the initiation of dialysis. Following the introduction of calcimimetics, there appears to have been a reduction in parathyroidectomy rates. Surgical techniques include subtotal parathyroidectomy and total parathyroidectomy with or without autotransplantation. Subtotal parathyroidectomy involves leaving a remnant part of the gland while total parathyroidectomy removes all the glandular tissue.
