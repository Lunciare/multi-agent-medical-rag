Osteitis fibrosa cystica is revealed on standard radiographs by subperiosteal resorption, especially at the distal phalanges, clavicles, distal ulna, and skull. Cortical thinning in long bones, bone cysts, and densification of the trabecular bone are also seen. Periarticular, vascular, and more rarely, visceral metastatic calcifications can also be found. There are no clear guidelines regarding lab or diagnostic testing for vascular calcifications in SHPT. Treatment / Management Medical Treatment Management of SHPT targets abnormal phosphocalcic metabolism. Maintaining the serum calcium and phosphorus levels within the normal range along with control of PTH and vitamin D levels is the key in management of secondary hyperparathyroidism. The U.S. National Kidney Foundation (NKF) proposed the Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) guidelines and established targets for biomarkers (calcium, phosphorus, and PTH levels) to lower SHPT related mortality. However, most of these targets are difficult to achieve in the long term. Phosphate binders, vitamin D, and calcimimetics have been reported in the management of calcium and phosphate levels in patients with CKD.
