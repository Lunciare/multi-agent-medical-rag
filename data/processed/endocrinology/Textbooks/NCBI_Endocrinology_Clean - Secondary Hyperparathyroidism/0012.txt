Sometimes, in total parathyroidectomy, small amounts of the parathyroid gland could be autografted post-surgery. There are no major differences between subtotal parathyroidectomy and total parathyroidectomy in outcomes such as complications, readmissions, 30-day mortality. Subtotal parathyroidectomy is preferred in renal transplant patients with tertiary hyperparathyroidism due to a lower risk of recurrence. Total parathyroidectomy without autotransplantation is associated with a lower rate of recurrence of refractory SHPT and is preferred in patients with longer life expectancy or less likelihood of renal transplantation. Total parathyroidectomy with autotransplantation is preferred for patients who cannot undergo repeat neck surgeries due to conditions such as co-existing thyroid disorders requiring surgery, history of repeated neck surgeries, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury or patients with significant perioperative comorbidities. An interesting postoperative complication of surgery is the hungry bone syndrome where the lack of osteoclastic activity causes decreased PTH, leading to hypocalcemia. It can be prevented by administering high doses of calcium and using a high calcium dialysate post-surgery. Differential Diagnosis Primary and tertiary hyperparathyroidism are always in the differential of secondary hyperparathyroidism.
