Also, vitamin D suppresses PTH secretion from parathyroid glands and regulates the calcium and phosphorus levels. Fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF-23) is secreted by osteocytes and plays an important role in phosphorus homeostasis by increasing phosphorus clearance in the renal tubules. FGF-23 also inhibits 1, alpha-hydroxylase activity, and thus the active form of vitamin D. FGF-23 is not known to modulate PTH secretion directly but may do so indirectly through regulating phosphate and vitamin D metabolism. In chronic kidney disease, decreasing glomerular filtration rate (GFR) leads to increased secretion of PTH. Decreasing GFR leads to decreased phosphate clearance and hyperphosphatemia, which then stimulates the parathyroid glands to secrete PTH. Also, hyperphosphatemia causes hypocalcemia (phosphorus forms complexes with calcium) and stimulates FGF-23 and increased PTH secretion.
