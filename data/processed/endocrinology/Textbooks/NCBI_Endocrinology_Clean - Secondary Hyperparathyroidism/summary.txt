Secondary Hyperparathyroidism
KEYWORDS: pth, calcium, bone, vitamin, increased, patients, phosphorus, levels, parathyroidectomy, parathyroid, hyperparathyroidism, phosphate, shpt, calcium phosphorus, hyperplasia

Introduction Parathyroid hormone (PTH) is secreted by parathyroid glands and plays a role in calcium and skeletal metabolism. Important triggers for PTH secretion are hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia. On the other hand, the secretion of PTH is reduced by 1,25 (OH)(2) vitamin D(3). Secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) is an increased secretion of PTH due to parathyroid hyperplasia caused by triggers such as hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, or decreased active vitamin D. The increased PTH secretion, in turn, causes increased calcium in the blood by acting on bones, intestines, and kidneys. Prolonged SHPT is often associated with disturbances of bone turnover, as well as visceral and vascular calcifications, which are responsible for cardiovascular morbidity and mortality.
