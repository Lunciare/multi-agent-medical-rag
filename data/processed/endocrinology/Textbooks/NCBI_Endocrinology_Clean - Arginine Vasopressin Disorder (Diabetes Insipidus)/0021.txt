An algorithmic approach can help diagnose and classify suspected cases of AVP-D. It involves the following steps: 1. Confirmation of hypotonic polyuria 2. Diagnosis of the type of polyuria-polydipsia syndrome 3.Identification of the underlying etiology. Performing diagnostic testing in this order can potentially aid with establishing the appropriate diagnosis and choosing the most relevant biochemical and imaging tests. 1. Confirmation of hypotonic polyuria The primary objective in this step involves differentiating between conditions that give rise to polyuria resulting from osmotic diuresis (such as in hyperglycemia) and primary polydipsia, in which polyuria predominantly involves water diuresis. The first step is to confirm if the patient indeed has polyuria. Calculate the total 24-hour urine volume to confirm polyuria. Obtain baseline values of plasma electrolytes, random serum, and urine osmolality. Ruling out other causes of polyuria is essential in this step. A 24-hour urine output of less than 2.5 L is reassuring and suggests causes besides DI.
