The incidence of AVP-D in such instances varies with the extent of injury (10-20% for surgical removal of adenomas limited to the sella to 60%-80% after removal of large tumors). Minimally invasive endoscopic pituitary surgery has seen a lower incidence of AVP-D than the traditional approach. Craniopharyngioma is associated with AVP-D both before and particularly after surgery. Despite the relatively high frequency of AVP-D in patients undergoing neurosurgery, most cases of polyuria in this setting are not due to AVP-D. More common causes are the excretion of excess fluid administered during surgery and an osmotic diuresis induced by mannitol or glucocorticoids (which cause hyperglycemia and glucosuria) to reduce cerebral edema.
