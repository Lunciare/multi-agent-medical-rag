Gestational AVP-D typically presents in the third trimester and spontaneously resolves about 2 or 3 weeks postpartum.[ 43 ] It is often underdiagnosed, since polyuria during pregnancy is considered normal and does not generally cause complications.[ 44 ] History and Physical The primary symptoms common to both AVP-D and AVP-R include polydipsia, polyuria, and nocturia. Polyuria is defined as a urine output of more than 3 L/day in adults or 2 L/m² in children. Urine is usually most concentrated in the morning due to a lack of fluid ingestion overnight and increased AVP secretion during the late sleep period. As a result, the first manifestation of a mild to moderate loss of concentrating ability is often nocturia. However, nocturia is often nonspecific and can be secondary to other factors. In children, symptoms can be nonspecific, and they may present with severe dehydration, constipation, vomiting, fevers, irritability, failure to thrive, and growth retardation. In patients with central nervous system (CNS) tumors, headaches and visual defects may present in addition to the classic symptoms.
