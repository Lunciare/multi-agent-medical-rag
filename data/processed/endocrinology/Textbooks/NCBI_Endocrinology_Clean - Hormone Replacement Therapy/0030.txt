Hormone Replacement Therapy and Diabetes In 2024, a retrospective cohort study, which included 6,566 participants (both HRT users and non-users) with matched cohorts, found that HRT was associated with a lower incidence of diabetes mellitus over a 20-year follow-up, with a hazard ratio of 0.693. The protective effect of HRT was more pronounced in individuals aged 46 to 50 and 55 to 60, as well as in White individuals, but not in Black or Asian populations. Additionally, HRT significantly reduced diabetes risk in those with a BMI of 24.9 or lower and 25 to 29.9 kg/m², but not in those with a BMI of 30 kg/m² or higher. The study concluded that HRT reduces long-term diabetes risk in premenopausal individuals with prediabetes.
