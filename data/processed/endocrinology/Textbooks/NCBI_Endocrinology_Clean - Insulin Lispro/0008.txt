Renal impairment: In renal impairment, the pharmacokinetics of regular insulin and insulin lispro do not exhibit discernible differences across varying degrees of dysfunction. However, there is an observed change in insulin sensitivity, with an increased response to insulin as renal function declines. Studies focusing on human insulin have documented elevated circulating insulin levels in the presence of renal impairment. Consequently, careful glucose monitoring and appropriate insulin lispro dose adjustments may be necessary for renal dysfunction. Pregnancy considerations: Insulin lispro is a former FDA pregnancy category B drug, meaning animal reproduction studies have not shown risk to the fetus. Per the FDA, this drug should only be used during pregnancy when indicated. Gestational diabetes increases the risk of spontaneous abortion, preeclampsia, fetal demise, macrosomia, fetal anomalies, neonatal hypoglycemia, neonatal respiratory distress syndrome, and hyperbilirubinemia. Gestational diabetes also increases the risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and obesity later in life.
