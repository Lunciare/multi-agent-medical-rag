Adapted from Umpierrez GE, et al. Diabetes Care, 2024, 47:1257-75. FOLLOW-UP Meticulous clinical and laboratory follow up is critical in patients with HHS. Capillary blood glucose levels should be monitored every 1-2 hours to allow adjustment of the insulin infusion. Electrolytes, BUN, creatinine, phosphate, glucose, and osmolarity should be checked every 2-4 hours until the patient is stable. When plasma osmolarity is <300 mOsm/L, there is good urine output, glucose is <250 mg/dl and the patient is alert and able to eat, a multidose insulin regimen consisting of long-acting insulin and short/rapid acting insulin before meals may be initiated. Intravenous insulin infusion should be continued 1-2 hours after the subcutaneous insulin is given to ensure adequate plasma insulin levels are maintained ( 1 , 6 ). It is also important to monitor for possible treatment-related complications, the most common of which are hypoglycemia and hypokalemia ( table 4 ). These are both usually due to overzealous treatment with insulin and can be minimized with frequent monitoring. One may consider reduction of the insulin dose used in patients with reduced renal function, frailty, or older age. Hypokalemia can be avoided by close follow up of electrolytes and ensuring fluid resuscitation includes potassium supplementation. Cerebral edema is extremely rare in patients with HHS and usually occurs in younger adults. To reduce the risk of cerebral edema in high-risk patients, sodium, glucose, and water deficit may be more gradually corrected to avoid the rapid decline in plasma osmolarity. Table 4.
