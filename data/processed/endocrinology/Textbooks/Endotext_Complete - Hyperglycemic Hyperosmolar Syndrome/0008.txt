Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar Syndrome
KEYWORDS: insulin, hhs, glucose, dka, patients, diabetes, meq, fluid, potassium, treatment, dehydration, hyperglycemia, serum, severe, care


Complications of HSS View in own window • Hypoglycemia: A common complication of treatment. • Hypokalemia: Due to intracellular shift of potassium with insulin treatment, hypokalemia is often seen during treatment of hyperglycemic emergencies. • Cerebral Edema: This complication is more common in younger patients with T1D. A declining level of consciousness, lethargy, and headache should raise suspicion. • Hypoxemia and rarely non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema may be associated with a decrease in the colloid osmotic pressure leading to pulmonary edema. • Thrombosis: HSS is a prothrombotic state. An individual risk assessment for venous thromboembolism should be performed for all patients presenting with HSS to decide if prophylactic or therapeutic dose of anticoagulation should be prescribed. • Acute kidney failure: A common complication usually resulting from severe dehydration due to osmotic diuresis. Recurrence of HHS can be prevented by improved patient as well as caregiver education and enhanced access to medical care. For elderly nursing home residents, nursing home staff should be educated in recognition of signs and symptoms of HHS and the importance of adequate fluid intake. REFERENCES 1.
