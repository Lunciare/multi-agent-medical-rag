CLINICAL RECOGNITION The hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) is a life-threatening metabolic decompensation of diabetes which presents with severe hyperglycemia, marked hyperosmolality, and profound dehydration, typically accompanied by alteration in consciousness ranging from lethargy and focal deficits to coma; the mental status changes are thought to be a direct consequence of the severe hypertonicity and resultant neuronal dehydration. Clinical Manifestations of the Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State View in own window Fatigue, lethargy, and weakness Polyuria and polydipsia Unintentional weight loss preceding the acute presentation Confusion, focal neurological deficits, seizures, and coma Severe dehydration and volume depletion causing tachycardia and severe hypotension Blurry vision In contrast to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in which acidemia and ketonemia are key features, these abnormalities are limited or absent in HHS.
