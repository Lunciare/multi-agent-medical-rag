Patients should be educated that this drug slows gastric emptying, and there may be a feeling of early satiety. Nausea can occur if attempting to eat when already full. A 34-trial meta-analysis demonstrated that once-weekly exenatide had the lowest risk of vomiting when comparing the adverse effects. Although classified as low risk, GLP-1RA can be associated with hypoglycemia with no clinically significant difference in incidence among the agents. The concomitant use of insulin or insulin secretagogues, such as sulfonylureas, potentiates this risk. Injection site reactions and erythema have been reported to be more frequent with exenatide administered once weekly than twice daily. Injection site reactions are also more common, with exenatide taken once weekly compared to liraglutide taken once daily. Exenatide also has the adverse effect of increasing the INR in patients taking warfarin. For semaglutide, rapid improvement of glycemic control has been associated with a temporary worsening of preexisting diabetic retinopathy.
