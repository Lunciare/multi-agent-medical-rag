Sepsis itself may contribute to hyperglycemia by enhancing stress response, reducing insulin secretion, and impairing peripheral glucose utilization. Poor wound healing and long-term effects, including insulin resistance, glucose intolerance, and adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes, have also been reported. Management of neonatal hyperglycemia carries additional risks. Insulin infusion may induce hypoglycemia and hypokalemia, emphasizing the importance of addressing underlying causes alongside glucose control. While hyperglycemia may directly have adverse effects, the evidence is unclear as to whether the underlying condition (eg, infection) or the higher glucose levels themselves are the primary drivers of many of the adverse outcomes identified above. For example, several studies have found an association of hyperglycemia with various adverse outcomes in preterm neonates, such as intraventricular hemorrhage, Necrotizing enterocolitis, retinopathy of prematurity, fungal infections, prolonged hospitalization, and mortality. Titchiner et al, in a large multicenter cohort study, observed that premature infants with hyperglycemia who received insulin treatment for more than 14 days had a significantly increased risk of retinopathy of prematurity and chronic lung disease.
