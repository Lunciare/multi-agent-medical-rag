Other laboratory studies include: Urine glucose : A level of 2+ or higher suggests osmotic diuresis. Complete blood count and C-reactive protein : These studies help in ruling out sepsis. Comprehensive metabolic panel : Ongoing monitoring of serum electrolytes is critical, as osmotic diuresis can cause significant electrolyte losses. Infant weight : Tracking the infant’s weight helps assess hydration status. Insulin and C-peptide : If hyperglycemia persists beyond 2 weeks, serum insulin levels and serum and urine C-peptide levels are used to rule out monogenic diabetes and Type 1 diabetes. Genetic testing : Genetic testing should be considered if hyperglycemia continues despite prolonged insulin therapy, particularly in infants with syndromic features, a family history of diabetes, or low C-peptide levels. Treatment / Management In preterm and very-low-birth-weight infants (defined as those with a birth weight below 1500 g), persistent blood glucose levels exceeding 200 mg/dL require careful evaluation. Persistent hyperglycemia with readings above 200 mg/dL at 4-hour intervals, accompanied by glucosuria of +2 or higher, may indicate the need for treatment. Current evidence does not support treating hyperglycemia
