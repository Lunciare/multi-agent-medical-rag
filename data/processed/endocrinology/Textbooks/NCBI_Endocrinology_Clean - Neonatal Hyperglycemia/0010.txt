Hyperglycemia has also been linked to necrotizing enterocolitis, although study findings vary, and its association with retinopathy of prematurity remains inconsistent, despite experimental models suggesting impaired retinal angiogenesis. For instance, Soghier et al and Kao et al demonstrated a significant association between hyperglycemia and necrotizing enterocolitis, whereas Akmal et al reported no significant association. Sustained hyperglycemia in the neonatal mouse model has been associated with impaired retinal angiogenesis, similar to that observed in retinopathy of prematurity. However, clinical evidence in humans is inconsistent. While some studies suggest an association between hyperglycemia and retinopathy of prematurity, a meta-analysis of 9 studies involving 1,939 infants found no significant association between mean blood glucose levels and the development of ROP. Dysregulated glucose metabolism may contribute to bronchopulmonary dysplasia by increasing oxidative stress and impairing alveolar growth, while hyperglycemia can compromise immunity and elevate the risk of late-onset sepsis. Hyperglycemia was significantly associated with an increased incidence of late-onset sepsis, consistent with findings by Ramel et al, and Van der Lugt et al.
