Neonatal medications, eg, dopamine, dobutamine, epinephrine, caffeine, theophylline, phenytoin, and corticosteroids, frequently impair glucose regulation and promote hyperglycemia. Epidemiology Hyperglycemia is more common in preterm infants than in term infants and is generally noted during the first week of life. Acute hyperglycemia usually resolves over 2 to 3 days in most neonates, but it can persist for up to 10 days. Hyperglycemia is less common than hypoglycemia among neonates, but this condition is associated with increased mortality and morbidity. Neonatal hyperglycemia is inversely related to birth weight and gestational age. Hyperglycemia occurs in one-third of preterm and small for gestational age infants. Pathophysiology Glucose is an essential energy source for the fetus and the neonate. The brain growth of the fetus exclusively depends upon glucose. In the early postnatal period, glucose homeostasis is maintained through glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis. The critical regulatory mechanisms of glucose homeostasis are sluggish in the initial days, particularly among preterm neonates. The mechanisms of neonatal hyperglycemia are multifactorial. The risk of hyperglycemia increases with the severity of the accompanying illness.
