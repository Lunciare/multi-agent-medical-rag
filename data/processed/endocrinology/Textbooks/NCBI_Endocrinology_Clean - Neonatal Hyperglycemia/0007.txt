Continuous insulin infusion can be administered either by adding insulin to maintenance fluids or running it independently, allowing adjustment of the insulin rate without altering total IV fluid delivery. Initial infusion rates typically range from 0.01 to 0.05 U/kg/hour and are titrated in 0.01 U/kg/hour increments, with a maximum of 0.1 U/kg/hour. The target blood glucose range is 100 to 150 mg/dL, with gradual reductions emphasized to avoid rapid fluctuations and hypoglycemia. Insulin infusion should be halved if glucose decreases to 180 mg/dL and discontinued if levels drop below 150 mg/dL. Rapid decreases exceeding 50 mg/dL per hour require immediate insulin adjustment or GIR increase to prevent complications (eg, cerebral edema). Hourly glucose monitoring during insulin therapy is essential, with rechecks 30 minutes after any infusion change. Hypoglycemia requires discontinuation of insulin and administration of 2 mL/kg D10 IV bolus, followed by monitoring for rebound hyperglycemia. Recent case reports suggest subcutaneous insulin glargine may provide a safer and more stable alternative to intravenous infusion in selected neonates, although standardized protocols remain limited.
