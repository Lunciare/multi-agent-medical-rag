Neonatal Hyperglycemia
KEYWORDS: hyperglycemia, glucose, insulin, neonatal, infusion, infants, preterm, levels, neonates, increased, sepsis, neonatal hyperglycemia, risk, diabetes, osmotic

Persistent hyperglycemia with readings above 200 mg/dL at 4-hour intervals, accompanied by glucosuria of +2 or higher, may indicate the need for treatment. Current evidence does not support treating hyperglycemia in the absence of increased osmolarity or osmotic diuresis. Glucose Infusion The initial step in managing neonatal hyperglycemia involves assessing the glucose infusion rate (GIR), which is calculated as the IV infusion rate (mL/kg/day) × dextrose concentration (%)/144. GIR can be reduced by decreasing either the IV dextrose concentration or the infusion rate, typically by 1 to 2 mg/kg/min every 2 hours, with frequent glucose monitoring until reaching 4 mg/kg/min. Persistent hyperglycemia warrants investigation and management of underlying causes, including sepsis, physiologic stress, and medication effects. Insulin Therapy Insulin therapy may be indicated when hyperglycemia persists at low GIR (4 mg/kg/min), suggesting relative insulin deficiency or resistance. Insulin should be considered if blood glucose exceeds 250 mg/dL and urine glucose is +2 or higher in 2 samples taken 4 hours apart, despite GIR reduction. Bolus insulin therapy carries a high risk of hypoglycemia and is generally avoided. Continuous insulin infusion can be administered either by adding insulin to maintenance fluids or running it independently, allowing adjustment of the insulin rate
