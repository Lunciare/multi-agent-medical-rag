Preventative Measures Prevention of hyperglycemia is essential in all neonates, especially preterm infants, due to increased risks of infection, impaired immunity, poor wound healing, and higher morbidity and mortality. Preventive strategies include early initiation of enteral feeding, timely amino acid supplementation in TPN to stimulate insulin secretion, targeting physiologic GIR based on glucose monitoring, and limiting IV lipid infusions during hyperglycemia. Direct interventions, such as reducing or discontinuing catecholamine infusions and glucocorticoid therapy as soon as clinically feasible, further support glucose stabilization and overall metabolic health. Differential Diagnosis Neonatal hyperglycemia typically reflects an underlying clinical condition rather than a primary disorder of glucose metabolism. It may signal serious illnesses such as sepsis, necrotizing enterocolitis, or seizures. A rare cause of neonatal hyperglycemia is neonatal diabetes mellitus, which occurs in approximately 1 in 400,000 births. Neonatal diabetes presents in the following 3 distinct forms: The transient form accounts for more than 50% of cases and is often caused by mutations in sulfonylurea receptors.
