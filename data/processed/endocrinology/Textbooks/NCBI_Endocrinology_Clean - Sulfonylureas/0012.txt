The patient should receive 15 grams of glucose, recheck blood glucose levels 15 minutes later, and repeat the process as needed until hypoglycemia resolves. Sources of oral glucose include, but are not limited to, glucose tablets, juice, or soft drink. Depending on the duration of action of the sulfonylurea responsible for the overdose, hypoglycemia may reoccur. Educate patients to eat a meal or a snack once their serum glucose is normal or trending up to prevent a recurrent hypoglycemic episode. In hospitalized patients, intravenous (IV) dextrose is the first-line therapy for severe hypoglycemia from sulfonylureas. Patients may require a continuous IV infusion of dextrose to prevent recurrent hypoglycemia, especially with long-acting sulfonylureas. Check blood glucose levels every 15 minutes. Glucagon stimulates glucose production in the liver and is useful for patients who refuse or cannot take oral glucose. Glucagon is available in different formulations for various routes of administration. The powder for injection requires reconstitution before intramuscular or subcutaneous administration. Newer formulations of glucagon that patients may favor do not require reconstitution.
