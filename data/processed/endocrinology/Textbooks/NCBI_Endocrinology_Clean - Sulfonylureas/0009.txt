The 2012 American Geriatric Society Beers Criteria highly recommends and with strong evidence to avoid chlorpropamide and glyburide in older adults. There is a debate whether hypersensitivity to sulfonamides is a contraindication to prescribing sulfonylureas. A literature review suggested that patients with allergies to sulfonamides may not need to avoid sulfonylureas. However, given the small number of patients reviewed, there is limited data to confirm or refute the need to avoid sulfonylureas in patients with sulfa allergy. The medication package insert provides the FDA-approved product information, including contraindications. Providers should review this information carefully. The court may determine a provider to be negligent if they prescribe a contraindicated medication that causes harm to a patient. Oral antidiabetic agents, including sulfonylureas, are not FDA-approved for use in pregnancy and lactation. The Endocrine Society supports the NICE guidance in recommending glyburide for gestational diabetes in selected patients. The ADA guideline recommends avoiding glyburide in patients with CKD.
