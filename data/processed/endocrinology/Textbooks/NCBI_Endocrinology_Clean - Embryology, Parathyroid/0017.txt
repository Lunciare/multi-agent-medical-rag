Per the Miami criteria, a decrease of PTH over 50% from preoperative levels at 10 minutes is considered adequate resection. ioPTH increases the detection of multiglandular disease, minimizing the need for recurrent operations. However, localization studies and ioPTH are no replacement for strong comprehension of the anatomy/embryology of the parathyroid glands. Complications - Complications of parathyroid surgery are infrequent, and even more so in minimally-invasive surgery (3.1% for open, 1.2% for minimally invasive). Transient Hypoparathyroidism (50%) - Seen commonly, this occurs because the resected/abnormal parathyroid tissue has suppressed the remaining/normal parathyroid tissue. Eventually, the normal glands achieve calcium homeostasis, and hypoparathyroidism/hypocalcemia resolves. Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Injury (10 to 15%) - Unilateral injury can lead to voice hoarseness, while bilateral injury can lead to airway occlusion. Damage can be transient in cases of nerve compression or stretching, or permanent if the nerve is transected or suffers sufficient thermal injury. In a 2017 study, 10.6% of thyroid/parathyroid surgeries led to transient recurrent laryngeal nerve injuries, while 1.1% of surgical patients suffered permanent injury. Persistent Hypercalcemia (1 to 5%) - Generally, this is due to a failure to identify all adenomas or resect all of the hyperplastic tissue.
