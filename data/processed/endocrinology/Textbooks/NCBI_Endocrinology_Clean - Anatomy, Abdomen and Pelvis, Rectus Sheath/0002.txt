The arcuate line can be a sharp demarcation, or it can be a gradual transition zone where the fibers of the posterior sheath gradually disappear. Superior to the arcuate line, the fascia of the external oblique and half of the internal oblique pass anterior to the rectus abdominis muscle and make up the anterior rectus sheath. The other half of the internal oblique fascia, the transversus abdominis fascia, and the transversalis fascia make up the posterior rectus sheath superior to the arcuate line. Extending inferiorly from the arcuate line, the anterior rectus sheath is comprised of all three fascial layers of the lateral abdominal wall musculature, leaving only transversalis fascia covering the posterior aspect of the rectus abdominis muscle and pyramidalis muscle. Embryology The muscles that comprise the abdominal wall derive from mesoderm, specifically in thoracic myotomes within the somites. The thoracic myotomes divide into dorsal epaxial and ventral hypaxial divisions during the sixth week of gestation. The abdominal wall forms from the ventral hypaxial divisions of the myotomes.
