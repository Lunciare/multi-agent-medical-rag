Vagina The vagina is an elastic, muscular tube that connects to the cervix at its proximal end and extends to the external surface through the vulvar vestibule (see Image . Uterine Tubal Anatomy and Ligaments). The distal opening of the vagina is typically partially covered by a membrane called the "hymen." This opening is located posterior to the urethral opening. The primary functions of the vagina are sexual intercourse and childbirth. During intercourse, the vagina serves as a reservoir for semen, allowing it to collect before sperm cells ascend into the cervix and travel toward the uterus and fallopian tubes. The vagina also functions as an outflow tract for menstrual blood. Embryology During the embryonic stage, the fetus begins with undifferentiated gonads, which later develop into either testes or ovaries. The presence of the SRY gene influences the gonads to develop into testes. In the absence of this gene, the gonads default to becoming ovaries.
