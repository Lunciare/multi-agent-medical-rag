One example is androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS), where androgen receptors are unresponsive to androgens. Consequently, the external genitalia default to developing as female despite the presence of male chromosomes. Meanwhile, female individuals may experience virilization if they have excessive blood androgen levels, as seen in congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH). In congenital adrenal hyperplasia, a defect in the adrenal production of aldosterone and cortisol causes the precursors of these hormones to be diverted toward androgen production. The excess androgens can masculinize the female external genitalia, resulting in a larger clitoris (clitoromegaly) and fusion of the labia majora, making them appear more scrotal-like.
