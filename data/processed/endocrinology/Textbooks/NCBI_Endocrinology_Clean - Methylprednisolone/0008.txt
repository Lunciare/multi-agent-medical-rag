Ulcerative colitis: The American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) recommends methylprednisolone 60 mg daily for remission in patients with severe ulcerative colitis. Lupus nephritis: According to the KDGIO 2024 guidelines, methylprednisolone pulses may be followed by a regimen of glucocorticoids during the initial treatment of active lupus nephritis upon satisfactory improvement in renal and extrarenal disease manifestations. Initial treatment typically involves IV methylprednisolone at a dose of 0.25 to 0.5 g/day for up to 3 days. However, intra-articular glucocorticoids such as triamcinolone are recommended as part of the initial therapy for active oligoarthritis. Steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome: According to the International Pediatric Nephrology Association, pulse therapy with intravenous methylprednisolone (500 mg/m 2 or 15 mg/kg) may be considered for patients with steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome. Specific Patient Populations Hepatic impairment: No dosage adjustments are provided in the product labeling. Caution is advised. Renal impairment: No dose adjustment is required; caution is advised as high corticosteroid doses are associated with scleroderma renal crisis.
