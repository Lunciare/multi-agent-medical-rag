In patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, a BMI> 30, and type 2 diabetes the effect of weekly semaglutide (2.4 mg) (n= 310) vs placebo (n= 306) for 52 weeks was determined ( 318 ). The Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire clinical summary score, a measure of symptoms and physical limitations, was greatly improved in the semaglutide group and the 6-minute walk distance increased by 12.7 meters in the semaglutide group and decreased by 1.6 meters in the placebo group. Additionally, the NT-proBNP level decreased by 23% in the semaglutide vs. 4.6% the placebo group. Hospitalization or urgent visit for heart failure was also decreased in the semaglutide group (2.3% vs 5.9%). In the STEP-HFpEF study similar beneficial effects of semaglutide on heart failure with preserved ejection fraction have been observed in patients without diabetes ( 319 ). In a meta-analysis of 4 studies (STEP-HFpEF, STEP-HFpEF DM, SELECT, and FLOW) 3743 patients had heart failure with preserved ejection fraction. Semaglutide reduced the risk of worsening heart failure by 41% (HR 0·59; CI 0·41-0·82; p=0.0019). The mechanisms accounting for this improvement is uncertain but could be related to reductions in glycated hemoglobin, body weight, systolic blood pressure, postprandial triglyceride levels, inflammation, or the direct effect of activation of GLP-1 receptors on the myocardium. The results of these studies provide evidence that GLP-1 RAs have favorable effects on heart failure and additional studies are in progress to confirm and extend these findings.
