Low-dose semaglutide (0.5mg) reduced the amount of alcohol consumed during a posttreatment laboratory self-administration task but did not affect average drinks per calendar day or number of drinking days. However, semaglutide significantly reduced drinks per drinking day and weekly alcohol craving predicting greater reductions in heavy drinking overtime relative to placebo. In a secondary analysis of the study described above in the nicotine section on the effect of dulaglutide on nicotine (Lengsfeld et al) Probst et al determined the effect of dulaglutide 1.5mg on alcohol consumption after 12 weeks of treatment ( 350 ). In the placebo group there were 75 alcohol drinkers and in the dulaglutide group there were 76 alcohol drinkers. At week 12, participants receiving dulaglutide drank 29% less than participants receiving placebo. EFFECT OF GLP1 RECEPTOR AGONISTS IN PATIENTS ON SGLT2 INHIBITOR THERAPY As discussed earlier, the effect of a GLP-1 RA (efpeglenatide- not available) in patients on an SGLT-2 inhibitor was determined in the AMPLITUDE-O trial ( 312 ). The effect of efpeglenatide vs. placebo on cardiovascular and renal outcomes (macroalbuminuria) was similar in the absence and presence of baseline SGLT-2 inhibitors. In the HARMONY trial the effect of albiglutide in patients on an SGLT-2 inhibitor on cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke was similar. In a meta-analysis of the AMPLITUDE-O trial, HARMONY trial, and FLOW trial the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events with GLP-1 RA therapy was similar in those receiving and not receiving an SGLT2 inhibitor at baseline (HR 0.77; 95% CI 0.54–1.09 and HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.71–0.87; P-heterogeneity=0.78) ( 351 ). Additionally, the risk of hospitalization for heart failure with GLP-1 RA therapy in participants receiving and not receiving SGLT2 inhibitors at baseline was similar (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.36–0.93 and HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.63–0.85; P-heterogeneity=0.26).
