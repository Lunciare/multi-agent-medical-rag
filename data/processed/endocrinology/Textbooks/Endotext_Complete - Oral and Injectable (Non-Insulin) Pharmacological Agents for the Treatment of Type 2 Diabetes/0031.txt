In the ADOPT trial weight gain was greater with TZD therapy than with glyburide therapy (2.5 kg over 5 years) ( 31 ). The weight gain induced by TZDs is dose related and can be minimized by using low doses ( 113 ). The TZD induced increase in body weight is due to an expansion of the subcutaneous fat depot whereas the mass of visceral fat remains unchanged or even decreases ( 77 ). While weight increases, waist circumference typically remains stable. Stimulation of PPAR gamma in subcutaneous adipocytes stimulates lipid accumulation ( 78 ). Fluid retention, as discussed below, may also contribute to the increase in weight. FLUID RETENTION Edema has been reported in 3.0 to 7.5% of patients treated with the TZDs compared with 1.0 to 2.5% in patients on placebo or treated with other oral antidiabetic therapy ( 114 ). The increase in fluid retention is dose related. The risk of developing edema is greatest when a TZD is used in combination with insulin ( 114 ). The occurrence of edema is reduced when a TZD is used in combination with an SGLT2 inhibitor ( 111 ). TZD induced edema responds poorly to treatment with thiazide and loop diuretics but responds to diuretics that effect the distal tubules such as spironolactone, triamterene, and amiloride ( 113 ). Additionally, edema improves when TZD treatment is discontinued ( 114 ). The increased fluid retention can lead to an increase in plasma volume resulting in a modest decrease in hemoglobin levels (2-4%) ( 113 ). The increase in fluid retention is likely due to TZDs activating PPAR gamma in the renal tubules leading to the increased expression of the epithelial Na(+) channel resulting in the increased resorption of sodium ( 115 ). TZDs have been shown to decrease urine sodium excretion and to increase plasma renin and aldosterone levels ( 116 ).
