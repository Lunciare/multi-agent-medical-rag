A trial of semaglutide subcutaneously given daily (0.1, 0.2, and 0.4 mg) demonstrated an improvement in MASH without a beneficial effect on fibrosis ( 337 ). A recent publication presented the interim results of a phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 1197 patients with MASH and fibrosis stage 2 or 3 comparing once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide at a dose of 2.4 mg or placebo for 240 weeks ( 338 ). As expected, the mean change in body weight was -10.5% in the semaglutide group and -2.0% in the placebo group. Resolution of steatohepatitis without worsening of fibrosis occurred in 62.9% of the patients in the semaglutide group and in 34.3% of the patients in the placebo group. A reduction in liver fibrosis without worsening of steatohepatitis was reported in 36.8% of the patients in the semaglutide group and in 22.4% of those in the placebo group. Combined resolution of steatohepatitis and reduction in liver fibrosis was reported in 32.7% of the patients in the semaglutide group and 16.1% of those in the placebo group. Based on the results of this study semaglutide was approved by the FDA for the treatment of MASH. OSTEOARTHRITIS A trial in individuals with obesity (BMI ≥30) without diabetes and a clinical and radiologic diagnosis of moderate knee osteoarthritis with at least moderate pain randomly assigned patients to semaglutide (2.4 mg) (n= 271) or placebo (n= 136) for 68 weeks ( 339 ). The primary end points were the percentage change in body weight and the change in the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) pain score. As expected, weight loss was greater in the semaglutide group (-13.7% with semaglutide vs. -3.2% with placebo; P<0.001). Additionally, the WOMAC pain score improved to a greater degree with semaglutide (-41.7 points vs. -27.5 points; P<0.001) and improved physical function. This study indicates an improvement in osteoarthritis with semaglutide therapy. It is likely that weight loss played a major role in the improvement, but the anti-inflammatory effects of GLP-1 agonists might also play a role. Additionally, it is possible that GLP-1 directly affects joints to improve osteoarthritis ( 340 ).
