PERIPHERAL VASCULAR DISEASE The STRIDE trial was a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial in patients with T2D and peripheral artery disease with intermittent claudication and an ankle-brachial index of less than or equal to 0.90 or toe-brachial index of less than or equal to 0.70 ( 313 ). Patients were treated with subcutaneous semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly (n=396) or placebo (n=396) for 52 weeks. The primary endpoint was maximum walking distance at week 52 measured on a constant load treadmill. At 52 weeks maximum walking distance was significantly greater in the semaglutide group than in the placebo group. Semaglutide improved walking distance regardless of diabetes duration, BMI, HbA1c, or medication use ( 314 ). Additionally, the improvement in walking distance was not linked to improvements in glycemic control or weight loss ( 314 ). HEART FAILURE Several of the large cardiovascular outcome trials described above have analyzed the effect of administration of GLP-1 RAs in the subgroup of patients with a history of heart failure. In the EXSCEL trial patients with heart failure at baseline had no decrease in all-cause mortality whereas mortality was reduced in the subgroup without heart failure (HR 0.79; CI 0.68–0.92) ( 315 ). Similarly, in the combined data from the SUSTAIN-6 and PIONEER-6, patients with prior heart failure were the only subgroup that did not have a decrease in cardiovascular events ( 316 ). In contrast, in the LEADER trial the decrease in cardiovascular events was similar in patients with and without a history of heart failure ( 304 ). The large cardiovascular outcome studies have determined the effect of GLP-1 RAs on the occurrence of heart failure events. In a meta-analysis of the seven large cardiovascular outcome trials with a combined total of 56,004 participants, hospital admission for heart failure was decreased by 9% (HR 0.91, 0.83-0.99; p=0.028) ( 307 ) ( Table 24 ). In the SELECT trial that determined the effect of semaglutide 2.4mg weekly in patients with obesity who were not diabetic a decrease in heart failure events was also observed (HR 0.82; 95%CI 0.71- 0.96) ( 309 ). Finally, in the FLOW trial (described in detail in the section on kidney disease), which focused on patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic renal failure, semaglutide reduced the risk of heart failure by 27% (HR 0.73; 95% CI 0.58-0.92; P = 0.0068) ( 317 ).
