A recent meta-analysis of 10 randomized studies with 70,361 participants examined the effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on CKD progression, defined as kidney failure, at least 50% reduction in eGFR, or death due to kidney failure ( 198 ). They reported that beneficial effects were observed regardless of baseline eGFR (eGFR > 60, HR 0.61; eGFR 45 to <60, HR 0.57; eGFR of 30 to <45, HR 0.64; eGFR < 30, HR 0.71) or albuminuria (albuminuria ≤30, HR 0.58; albuminuria 30-300, HR 0.74; albuminuria > 300, HR 0.57). This supports the use of SGLT2 inhibitors to inhbit the progression of kidney disease across a wide range of kidney function. The mechanism accounting for this effect is unknown but a leading hypothesis is that an increase of sodium chloride in the macula densa due to SGLT2 inhibition triggers a cascade that reduces GFR through constriction of the afferent glomerular arterioles (tubuloglomerular feedback) ( 139 , 195 ). This would reduce glomerular hydrostatic pressure and initially decrease GFR, an effect that is observed with SGLT2 treatment, but in the long run this decrease in GFR protects the kidney from damage resulting in improved kidney function long-term ( 139 ).
