Monogenic Disorders Altering HDL Levels
KEYWORDS: levels, deficiency, hdl-c, apo, pubmed, cholesterol, hdl, disease, a-i, hdl-c levels, apo a-i, patients, low, ascvd, lcat

ABSTRACT Very low HDL-C levels (<20mg/dL) may be due to severe elevations in triglycerides, very poorly controlled diabetes, inflammation, infections, malignancy, liver disease, and certain medications such as anabolic steroids. Additionally, variants in multiple genes that each have a small effect but cumulatively lead to a decrease in HDL-C can result in very low HDL-C levels. Finally, rare monogenic disorders such as familial hypoalphalipoproteinemia, Tangier disease, and lecithin acyltransferase (LCAT) deficiency can lead to very low HDL-C levels. In this chapter we discuss the lipid abnormalities and clinical features of these monogenic disorders causing very low HDL-C levels. An elevated concentration of apo A-I and apo A-II is called hyperalphalipoproteinemia (HALP). HALP is classified as moderate (HDL-C levels between 80 and 100 mg/dL) or severe (HDL-C levels > 100 mg/dL).
