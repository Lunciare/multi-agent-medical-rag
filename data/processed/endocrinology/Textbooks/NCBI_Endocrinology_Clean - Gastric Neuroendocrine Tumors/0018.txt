In cases characterized by a high proliferation index, rapid progression, or bulky disease, systemic chemotherapy—eg, capecitabine and temozolomide (CAPTEM) or an infusional regimen of fluorouracil, leucovorin, and oxaliplatin—is more appropriate. For patients with resectable disease, neoadjuvant chemotherapy using these agents may be considered following a comprehensive discussion among the interprofessional care team. Additionally, palliative radiation can be employed for symptomatic bone metastases. In tumors that are microsatellite instability-high (MSI-high) or demonstrate a high tumor mutational burden, immune checkpoint inhibition with pembrolizumab may be an option. Overall, a tailored, interprofessional approach is essential to optimize outcomes for patients with grade 3 well-differentiated GNETs. Management of Neuroendocrine Carcinomas NECs (poorly differentiated, high-grade) are treated aggressively due to their rapid progression and poor prognosis. The following management approaches are recommended based on the extent of the disease: Systemic chemotherapy : Platinum-based regimens (cisplatin/carboplatin + etoposide) are standard first-line treatments. Surgical resection : Considered in highly selected cases with limited disease. Radiation therapy : May be used in cases of unresectable or metastatic disease with symptomatic local involvement.
