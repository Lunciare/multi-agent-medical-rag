Lymphomas of the stomach, including mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma, may present as gastric nodularity, thickened mucosal folds, or mass-like lesions and require histopathologic confirmation with lymphoid markers, eg, CD20 or CD3. Peptic ulcer disease and ZES should be considered, especially in patients with hypergastrinemia. However, ZES-associated lesions are typically multiple and occur in the duodenum rather than the stomach. Autoimmune gastritis with ECL cell hyperplasia can mimic type 1 GNETs but lacks the clonal proliferation characteristic of actual neuroendocrine tumors. Additionally, metastatic neuroendocrine tumors from the pancreas or small intestine should be ruled out in cases of multiple gastric lesions or liver metastases, as they may mimic primary GNETs. Distinguishing GNETs from these entities requires endoscopic assessment, histopathology with immunohistochemical markers (eg, chromogranin A, synaptophysin, and Ki-67 index), and biochemical evaluation for hypergastrinemia and hormone secretion. Prognosis The prognosis of GNETs varies significantly depending on the tumor type, grade, and stage at diagnosis. Type 1 GNETs have an excellent prognosis, with a low risk of metastasis (<5%) and indolent behavior.
