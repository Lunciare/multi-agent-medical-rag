For liver-dominant disease, hepatic arterial embolization, cytoreductive surgery or radiation therapy are viable options in many cases SSAs can be discontinued in nonfunctional tumors when the disease progresses. Targeted therapy: Everolimus (an mTOR inhibitor) and sunitinib (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor) may be considered for progressive, unresectable disease, particularly in patients with nonfunctioning tumors or SSTR-negative NETs. Chemotherapy Cabozantinib is recommended if prior treatment with 177 Lu-dotatate therapy or everolimus. 5-fluorouracil-based chemotherapy Capecitabine and temozolomide (CAPTEM) can be attempted in patients with well-differentiated NETs. There may be an increased risk of MDS or acute leukemia in cases with prior 177 Lu-dotatate therapy Management of Well-Differentiated Grade 3 Gastric Neuroendocrine Tumors The management of well-differentiated grade 3 GNETs is complex, given their intermediate prognosis—worse than grade 1 and grade 2 well-differentiated tumors but more favorable than neuroendocrine carcinomas. In patients with SSTR-positive, low-volume, indolent tumors and minimal symptom burden, somatostatin analogs (SSAs) may be sufficient as monotherapy.
