However, leadership in male contraceptive development has come almost exclusively from university-based investigators working with public sector organizations ( 9 ), notably WHO ( 262 ), CONRAD ( 263 ), and the Population Council ( 264 ). Steroidal Methods ANDROGEN ALONE Testosterone provides both gonadotropin suppression and androgen replacement making it an obvious first choice as a single agent for a reversible hormonal male contraceptive. Although androgen-induced, reversible suppression of human spermatogenesis has long been known ( 267 - 270 ), systematic studies of androgens for male contraception began in the 1970’s ( 271 , 272 ). Feasibility and dose-finding studies ( 273 ), mostly using testosterone enanthate (TE) in an oil vehicle as a prototype, showed that weekly im injections of 100-200 mg TE induce azoospermia in most Caucasian men ( 274 ) but less frequent or lower doses fail to sustain suppression ( 275 - 278 ). The largest experience with an androgen alone regimen arises from the two WHO studies in which over 670 men from 16 centers in 10 countries received weekly injections of 200 mg TE. In these studies ~60% of non-Chinese and >90% of Chinese men became azoospermic and the remainder were severely oligozoospermic ( 15 , 16 ) ( Figure 1 ). The high efficacy among Chinese men has also been replicated using monthly TU injections ( 17 , 18 ). Effective gonadotropin suppression is a prerequisite for effective testosterone-induced spermatogenic suppression in human ( 19 , 279 - 283 ) and non-human primates ( 284 , 285 ). However, the reasons for within and between population differences in susceptibility to hormonally-induced azoospermia remain largely unexplained ( 286 ). Possible factors include population differences in reproductive physiology of environmental ( 287 , 288 ), genetic ( 289 - 291 ), or uncertain ( 292 , 293 ) origin that may lead to differences in rate and extent of suppression of circulating gonadotropins and/or depletion of intratesticular androgens.
