Neuropsychiatric Function Despite normal intelligence, girls with Turner syndrome frequently experience learning disabilities that may require specialized education and assessment. Interventions should be tailored to the diagnosed disability. Although earlier reports suggested a higher prevalence of psychiatric illnesses, including depression, a recent nationwide retrospective study from Sweden found a lower prevalence of psychiatric conditions in individuals with Turner syndrome than in the general population. This finding urges caution against overdiagnosing severe psychiatric disorders in this group. Renal Conditions Renal abnormalities are common in Turner syndrome, including collecting system malformations, positional or horseshoe kidneys, and malrotation. Obstruction caused by ureteropelvic junction anomalies may lead to hydronephrosis and increase the risk of pyelonephritis. Patients with detected abnormalities should be promptly referred to nephrology and urology specialists, as appropriate.
