Ovarian Dysfunction Ovarian dysfunction is one of the most common causes of primary amenorrhea. Patients with primary ovarian dysfunction typically have normal function in the hypothalamus and pituitary but nonresponsive ovaries. This results in low estrogen production leading to a lack of endometrial growth and, due to a lack of negative feedback at the pituitary, elevated FSH levels. This state is known as hypergonadotropic hypogonadism. Primary amenorrhea due to ovarian dysfunction is most frequently a result of gonadal dysgenesis, which can be seen with Turner syndrome 45,X karyotype, or in patients with mosaicism. Less commonly, gonadal dysfunction can also be seen in patients with pure gonadal dysgenesis with either 46,XX or 46,XY karyotypes, the latter known as Swyer syndrome. Patients with Swyer syndrome have nonfunctional testicular tissue; therefore, they do not secrete antimüllerian hormone (AMH) or testosterone in utero.
