Other factors : Trauma can be a stimulus for activating an autoimmune cascade in the orbit. High serum cholesterol may also be a risk factor for TED. Pathophysiology TED has a self-limited course owing to the absence of orbital lymphoid tissue. This absence prevents sustained immune activity, resulting in an active phase followed by a stable, quiescent phase. Wilson Rundle categorized the progression of TED into 3 phases, often referred to as the "Rundle Curve." Active or inflammatory phase: A steep rise in disease severity, lasting 6 months to 5 years (mean 2 years). Smoking can extend this stage. Static or plateau phase: Disease activity persists but does not progress. Inactive or burnout phase: TED usually improves after 18 months of disease activity. However, the disease rarely completely resolves, and residual fibrotic changes persist in the orbit. Aggressive immunosuppressive management during the initial active phase can limit the destructive and fibrotic consequences of the immune cascade.
