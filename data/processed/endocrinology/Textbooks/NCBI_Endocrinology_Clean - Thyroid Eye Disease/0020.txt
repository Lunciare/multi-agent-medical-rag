Immunobiologic agents Rituximab, a humanized chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20 on B cells, has shown a 100% response rate and minimal reactivation in patients with moderate-to-severe TED. Tocilizumab (IL-6 receptor antibody), adalimumab (tumor necrosis factor[TNF]-α antibody), and infliximab (TNF-α antibody) also show promising results. Teprotumumab, a specific type 1 insulin-like growth factor receptor blocker, is administered as 8 infusions over 24 weeks. The improvement in CAS, proptosis, diplopia, and quality of life, with a reduction in proptosis, is comparable to that achieved with surgical orbital decompression. Inactive cases For cases with lagophthalmos, dry eye disease symptoms, and exposure keratopathy, adequate lubrication and corneal protection are advised. Appropriate surgical management is effective for residual proptosis, strabismus, eyelid retraction, and significant lagophthalmos. Surgical decompression in patients with euthyroid status and inactive disease provides sustained and predictable cosmetic results, with improvement in orbital perfusion. Sight-threatening disease High-dose systemic corticosteroid, 1 g IVMP daily for 3 days, is the most effective therapy for DON cases. Orbital radiotherapy is the second-line treatment that reduces the need for surgical orbital decompression.
