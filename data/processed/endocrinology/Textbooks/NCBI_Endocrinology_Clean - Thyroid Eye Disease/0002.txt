The thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor is the primary target of the autoimmune cascade within the orbit, and serum levels of thyrotropin receptor antibodies correlate with severity. Another possible target molecule is the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ, which is present on Thy-1(-) fibroblasts and regulates adipogenesis. The type 1 insulin-like growth factor receptor is another critical autoantigen on Thy-1 (+) fibroblasts. The stimulation of this receptor enhances the synthesis of IL-16 and the RANTES (regulated upon activation, normal T cell expressed and secreted) complex. This chemokine recruits CD4+ T cells in the orbital inflammatory cascade. Thy-1 (-) fibroblasts activated by thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor activation differentiate into adipocytes, secrete IL-6, and recruit B cells and plasma cells. Thy-1 (+) fibroblasts, activated by the type 1 insulin-like growth factor, secrete IL-16 and RANTES to recruit T cells. T cells engage in CD40-CD154 interactions that induce IL-1 secretion from fibroblasts. T cells also produce interferon-γ and tumor necrosis factor-alpha to stimulate myofibroblast activity and hyaluronate production.
