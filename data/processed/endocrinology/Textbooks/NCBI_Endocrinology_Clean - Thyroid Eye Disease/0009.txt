Infiltration of the EOM occurs through the accumulation of hyaluronate and the development of late-onset fibrosis. The most commonly involved muscles are the inferior rectus, the medial rectus, the superior rectus, the lateral rectus, and the oblique muscles. Restrictive strabismus and loss of binocular single vision. Diplopia presents initially in 6% to 18% of cases. Diplopia during the primary and inferior gaze is the most debilitating complication. Fluctuation of diplopia is common, with worsening in the morning and during active disease. Optic Nerve Changes Dysthyroid Optic Neuropathy (DON) is a potentially reversible optic nerve dysfunction in 4% to 10% of patients with TED . Early symptoms include diminished vision, altered color vision, decreased contrast sensitivity, and constricted visual fields. See Table 1 . The possible mechanisms are: Compressive optic neuropathy: The most common cause is compression by the enlarged EOM at the orbital apex. Optic nerve stretching: This occurs in cases of severe fat hypertrophy with proptosis.
