Lagophthalmos: Incomplete eyelid closure is a classic sign of TED. Lower eyelid retraction is present in up to 49% of TED cases. Upper eyelid retraction is present in around 90% to 98% of cases. The mechanisms proposed are as follows: Enlargement and fibrosis of the levator muscle are the most commonly affected muscles. Scarring between the levator aponeurosis and the surrounding soft tissues. Overextension of the levator muscle to counteract the ipsilateral tight inferior rectus muscle. Increased sympathetic stimulation of the Müller muscle. Ocular Surface Changes (Active TED Phase) Following inflammatory damage to the conjunctival mucous glands, the interface between the eyelid and the cornea is destabilized, resulting in tear film alterations. Lacrimal gland inflammation alters the tear film's protein profile and reduces the secretion rate. Persistent lagophthalmos disturbs the ocular surface, increases the reflex tear production rate, and alters the tear osmolarity. Dry eye symptoms and watering occur in 13% to 20% of patients.
