The cone model is divided into 3 phases and posits that disease progression is due to expansion of the muscle cone against a rigid bony orbit: Circumferential cone expansion displaces the extraconal fat outward, visible as fat prolapse into the upper and lower eyelids. Axial elongation of the cone displaces the globe forward, causing proptosis and muscle strain. Cone hypertension and muscle stiffness impede orbital venous drainage, manifesting as orbital venous congestion. History and Physical Ocular discomfort is the most common presentation in patients with thyroid eye disease. Other common symptoms are dryness, tearing, erythema, photophobia, and retro-orbital pain. Visual disturbances, such as blurring and diplopia, require prompt intervention. Furthermore, the onset of TED can precede the diagnosis of GD in 19.9% of cases. Eyelid Changes Lateral flare: The altered contour of the lateral upper eyelid is a pathognomonic sign caused by scarring between the lacrimal gland fascia and the levator aponeurosis.
