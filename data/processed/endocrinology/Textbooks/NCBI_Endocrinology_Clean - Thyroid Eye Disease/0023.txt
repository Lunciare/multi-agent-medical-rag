Common cosmetic concerns are proptosis, strabismus, upper and lower eyelid retraction, lateral flare, and lagophthalmos. Asymmetrical proptosis is common during inactive disease. A graded unilateral or bilateral surgical orbital decompression helps achieve a cosmetically symmetrical look. The surgical plan is customized based on the degree of proptosis correction, the pattern of strabismus, and the orbital anatomy. Surgical approaches are individualized: Lateral wall decompression via Kronlein, modified Kronlein, Stallard-Wright, Berke, and lateral eyelid crease incisions The preferred technique is ab interno bone removal with sparing of the lateral orbital rim. Complications include intraoperative cerebrospinal leak, infraorbital anesthesia, and new-onset diplopia. Medial wall decompression using transcutaneous, transconjunctival, or transnasal approaches The transnasal endoscopic approach is ideal for surgeries on the medial wall.
