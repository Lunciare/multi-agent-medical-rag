Treatment / Management Prevention Clinical assessment and accurate grading of thyroid eye disease are essential for appropriate management. Primary prevention includes smoking cessation. Secondary prevention includes early detection of any dysthyroid state and prompt management to prevent disease progression; tertiary prevention includes using artificial tears to help prevent exposure keratopathy. Cosmetic and visual rehabilitation are essential. Medical management includes the prompt restoration of euthyroid status, which is essential. Mild TED Patients with corneal exposure and ocular surface symptoms require extensive lubrication with artificial tears, gels, and ointments. Overnight eyelid taping, occlusive eye pads, cold compresses, or sleeping with the head elevated are of questionable benefit. However, selenium supplements can improve the quality of life, reduce inflammation, and slow the progression of TED. Eyelid retraction <2 mm is managed by: Transconjunctival injection: 5 units of botulinum toxin type A suspension Transconjunctival injection: 10 mg of triamcinolone suspension Both techniques demonstrate excellent results for the temporary resolution of eyelid retraction.
