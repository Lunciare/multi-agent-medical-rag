DON cases. Orbital radiotherapy is the second-line treatment that reduces the need for surgical orbital decompression. Urgent orbital decompression is reserved for active TED with DON, not responding to high-dose IVMP. Surgical options Elective surgical options are reserved for stable inactive thyroid eye disease cases not responding to conservative or maximal medical management. During cosmetic rehabilitation, orbital decompression surgery is the primary procedure to correct proptosis. After 6 months, strabismus surgery is performed to achieve adequate ocular alignment. After an additional 6 months, a definitive eyelid retraction surgery helps restore the cosmetic and functional status. Indications for orbital decompression are corneal exposure, DON, disfiguring proptosis, chronic pain or discomfort, and congestion. Differential Diagnosis The differential diagnosis may include the following: Nonspecific orbital inflammatory disease: Bilateral proptosis with lacrimal gland enlargement EOM enlargement involves the muscle belly and the tendinous origin. The lacrimal gland enlarges and prolapses out of the lacrimal fossa. This disease can be associated with systemic autoimmune diseases, such as systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and IgG4-related disease. Serology and soft-tissue biopsy are diagnostic. Lymphoma: Bilateral proptosis with associated lymphadenopathy Results of complete blood counts are usually normal. Orbital CT scan shows bilateral diffuse soft-tissue enlargement
