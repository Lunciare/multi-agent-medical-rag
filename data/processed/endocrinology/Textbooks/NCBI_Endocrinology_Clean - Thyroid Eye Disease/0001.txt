Thyroid Eye Disease
KEYWORDS: orbital, disease, ted, eyelid, cases, proptosis, decompression, orbit, optic, wall, inflammation, muscle, eye, patients, active

Introduction Thyroid eye disease (TED) is a chronic, immune-mediated inflammation of the orbit and is the most common cause of unilateral and bilateral proptosis in adults. About 25% to 50% of Graves disease cases present with thyroid eye disease. Goiter-associated ophthalmopathy was first described in AD 1000 by Avicenna and Al-Jurjani. In 1835, Robert Graves, an Irish physician, described 4 young women with thyrotoxicosis, palpitations, thyroid gland enlargement, and exophthalmos. Graves ophthalmopathy is an orbital inflammation or infiltration involving the periocular soft tissues, resulting in proptosis and ophthalmoplegia. Etiology The etiology of TED is incompletely understood; this condition involves a complex, immune-mediated cycle involving the orbital fibroblasts, adipocytes, and lymphocytes. Marked orbital inflammation produces high levels of proinflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin (IL)-1, and prostaglandins that target the orbital fat deposits. Orbital fibroblasts are the primary drivers of the pathogenesis of TED because they have increased expression of thyroid-stimulating hormone receptors and can differentiate into mature adipocytes. A subpopulation of orbital fibroblasts overexpresses the Thy-1 (CD90) marker. Transforming growth factor-beta stimulates the Thy-1 fibroblasts to differentiate into adipocytes, while the Thy-1 (+) fibroblasts are stimulated to differentiate into myofibroblasts.
