The posterior division innervates the quadriceps femoris muscles and gives rise to the saphenous nerve. The genitofemoral nerve originates from L1 and L2. It provides innervation to the skin, overlying the femoral triangle, cremaster muscle, and scrotal skin in males and mons pubis and labia majora in females. The femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve follows a course within the femoral sheath. It lies lateral and superficial to the femoral artery. The femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve pierces the anterior layer of the sheath and fascia lata at the saphenous opening to innervate the skin and subcutaneous tissue above. Muscles The femoral triangle includes the inguinal ligament and the following muscles: Adductor Longus - forms the medial border and a portion of the medial floor of the femoral triangle. Its origin is the superior ramus of the pubis, and its insertion is on the linea aspera of the femur. Sartorius - is the longest muscle in the body, making the lateral border of the femoral triangle.
