It originates at the anterior superior iliac spine and inserts into the pes anserinus. Pectineus - forms a portion of the medial floor of the femoral triangle. It is a quadrangular muscle that originates from the pectinate line of the pubis and inserts on the pectinate line of the femur. Iliopsoas - forms the lateral floor of the triangle. Iliopsoas refers to the 2 muscles, iliacus, and psoas, that originate as separate muscles in the abdomen and join into 1 in the hip to serve as the most powerful flexor of the thigh at the hip. Physiologic Variants The basic structure of the femoral triangle is highly conserved and could theoretically undergo alteration by the congenital absence of a muscle that composes 1 of its borders or rare anatomical malformation of its contents. The overall size of the femoral triangle can vary based on the size of the individual.
