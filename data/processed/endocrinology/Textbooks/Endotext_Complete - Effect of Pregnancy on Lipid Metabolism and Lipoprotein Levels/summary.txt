Effect of Pregnancy on Lipid Metabolism and Lipoprotein Levels
KEYWORDS: pregnancy, levels, pubmed, women, lipid, risk, cholesterol, maternal, increase, free, fetal, pmc, article pubmed, free article, article

ABSTRACT Lipoprotein and lipid physiology in pregnancy has important implications for the developing mother and her fetus/newborn.. Adequate cholesterol and essential fatty acids are needed for normal fetal development. In pregnancy, multiple physiological changes contribute to changes in lipid profiles of healthy, gestating women. Initially, an anabolic phase is characterized by increased lipid synthesis and fat storage in preparation for the increasing fetal energy needs in late pregnancy. In mid to later pregnancy, lipid physiology transitions to a net catabolic phase with increasing maternal insulin resistance. In a normal pregnancy, total cholesterol levels increase by approximately 50%, LDL-C by 30-40%, HDL-C by 25%, and TGs by 2- to 3-fold.
