The dermatomes begin posteriorly and travel anteriorly, wrapping around the sides of the abdomen and meeting in the midline along the linea alba. Each dermatome has a slightly inferior slope, almost following the slant of the rib cage. Important dermatomes to remember are the T10 and L1 dermatomes, including the umbilicus and the inguinal fold. It is important to remember that if a nerve block is needed for an umbilical or an inguinal hernia before an operation, it is necessary to decrease the postoperative pain the patient may experience. Major nerves to the anterolateral abdominal wall include the thoracoabdominal, lateral cutaneous, subcostal, iliohypogastric, and ilioinguinal nerves. The thoracoabdominal nerves are derived from T7-T11 and form the inferior intercostal nerves. These nerves run along the internal obliques and the transversalis muscles. They then enter subcutaneous tissue to become the anterior cutaneous branches of the skin in the anterior abdominal wall. Most of these nerves innervate the muscles of the anterolateral wall and the skin overlaying. The lateral cutaneous nerves innervate the skin, overlying the hypogastric regions bilaterally.
