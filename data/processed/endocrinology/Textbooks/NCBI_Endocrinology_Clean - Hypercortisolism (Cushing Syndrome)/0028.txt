Hypercortisolism (Cushing Syndrome)

Prothrombotic state with increased risk for deep venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism Secondary or primary adrenal insufficiency and glucocorticoid withdrawal syndrome following successful treatment Nelson syndrome after bilateral adrenalectomy Sleep disorders, including sleep deprivation, chronic insomnia, obstructive sleep apnea, and obesity hypoventilation syndrome In pregnancy, impaired glucose tolerance or gestational diabetes, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, preeclampsia, congestive heart failure, psychiatric disorders, and increased fetal loss. While the fetus tends to be somewhat protected from the maternal hypercortisolism because of the effects of placental 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, which converts approximately 85% of the maternal cortisol reaching the placenta to relatively inert cortisone, this protective system can be overwhelmed, resulting in fetal loss, intrauterine growth retardation, premature delivery, and neonatal adrenal insufficiency.
