Femoral Triangle Structures): Proximal border - inguinal ligament Lateral border - medial border of sartorius Medial border - medial border of adductor longus Floor - iliopsoas, pectineus, adductor longus, and adductor brevis muscles Apex - the intersection between the medial borders of the sartorius and adductor longus muscles Published literature regarding the location of the AC's roof and medial border is inconsistent. Some sources name the AC's medial border as the sartorius muscle, with the canal located deep to this muscle and the muscle's span determining the AC's limits. However, the VAM lies deep to the sartorius muscle. The true AC is roofed and bordered medially by the VAM. The space between the VAM and sartorius muscle is a plane called the "subsartorial space" or "subsartorial compartment." This designation can cause confusion, as the AC or Hunter canal is sometimes called the "subsartorial canal." The distinction is important because the true AC and the subsartorial space, which is superficial to the VAM, contain different nerve groups.
