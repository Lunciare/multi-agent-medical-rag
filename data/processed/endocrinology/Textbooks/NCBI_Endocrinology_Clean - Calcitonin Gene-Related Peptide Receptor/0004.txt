Different combinations of CLR/CTR and RAMPs (1, 2, and 3) create different glycosylation products, thus producing different receptors such as the ones for amylin and adrenomedullin. CGRP may also signal via receptors other than the CLR/RAMP1 complex, such as the CLR/RAMP3 combination, which is an adrenomedullin receptor. RAMP1 may also serve as an antagonist binding site, but its role as a ligand for the binding of CGRP or its antagonists remains unclear. RCP (receptor component protein) is important in intracellular G protein signaling. Erenumab is a human immunoglobulin G2 (IgG2) monoclonal antibody produced from Chinese hamster ovary cells. Erenumab acts by potently and competitively inhibiting the binding of CGRP to its receptor. Eptinezumab is a humanized immunoglobulin G1 produced in Pichia pastoris yeast cells by recombinant DNA technology. Eptinezumab potently and selectively binds to the α and β-forms of the human CGRP ligand and prevents the activation of the CGRP receptor.
