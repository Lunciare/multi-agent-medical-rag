Mechanism of Action To understand the mechanism of action, some information on the anatomy of the CGRP receptors may prove useful. The CGRP receptor is a heteromeric receptor complex composed of three subunits: CLR (calcitonin receptor-like receptor) is a type B, G protein-coupled receptor. CLR has a 55% overall identity with the calcitonin receptor for the thyroid hormone. The CLR receptor (class B GPCR) contains a structured N-terminal extracellular domain (ECD) or ectodomain, a seven helix transmembrane (7-TM) domain, and an intracellular carboxyl terminus. The ECD functions as a receptor for the CGRP molecule. RAMP1 (receptor activity modifying protein 1): This is a member of the single transmembrane RAMP family and is necessary for the proper functioning of CLR. RAMP presents CLR to the plasma membrane as a mature glycoprotein. Amino termini of RAMPs determine the glycosylation of CLR, which in turn correlates with the receptor phenotype.
