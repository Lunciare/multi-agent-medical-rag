The portal vein enters the liver within the hepatoduodenal ligament, traveling posterior to the proper hepatic artery and the common bile duct. When the portal vein reaches the hilum of the liver, it divides into right and left branches and feeds into the liver sinusoids. This vasculature comprises the majority of the blood flow to the liver, as well as draining back toxins and nutrients from the drained gastrointestinal tract. Blood then empties into the inferior vena cava (IVC). Figure 1 demonstrates normal portal venous anatomy and branching patterns, as shown by contrast-enhanced CT. The remaining minority 25% of blood flow to the liver is supplied by the proper hepatic artery. Hepatic parenchymal cells are some of the most richly-perfused cells in the entire human body due to this blood flow. They receive well-oxygenated blood from the hepatic artery and nutrient-rich blood from the splanchnic vessels via the portal venous system. This blood mixes within the hepatic sinusoids.
