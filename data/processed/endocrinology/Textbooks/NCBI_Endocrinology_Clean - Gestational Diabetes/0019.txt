Additionally, glyburide is associated with an increased risk of neonatal intensive care admission, respiratory distress syndrome, hypoglycemia, and birth injury, as well as possible fetal insulin stimulation. Therefore, ACOG has recommended that glyburide should not be considered a first-line therapy. The initial dosage of metformin is typically 500 mg per day for 1 week, followed by twice-daily administration thereafter, with a maximum dose of 2,500 to 3,000 mg. Metformin is known to cross the placenta and have adverse effects, including preterm birth, maternal abdominal pain, and diarrhea. Furthermore, when compared to insulin, outcomes with metformin use, such as macrosomia, neonatal hypoglycemia, or cesarean delivery, were similar. Significant treatment failure was also noted, with 50% of patients requiring insulin therapy. Metformin may be considered as an alternative to insulin in patients who decline or are unable to afford insulin.
