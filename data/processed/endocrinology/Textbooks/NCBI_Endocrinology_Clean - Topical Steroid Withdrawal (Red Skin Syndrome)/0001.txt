Topical Steroid Withdrawal (Red Skin Syndrome)
KEYWORDS: topical, skin, withdrawal, corticosteroid, topical corticosteroid, corticosteroid withdrawal, corticosteroids, dermatitis, topical corticosteroids, patients, use, atopic, atopic dermatitis, red, syndrome

Introduction Topical corticosteroid withdrawal, also known as red skin syndrome or topical steroid addiction, is a drug-related dermatosis characterized by burning, itching, and erythema following the sudden termination of medium- to high-potency topical corticosteroids after prolonged use (typically at least after 3 months of use). The syndrome is a form of tachyphylaxis and rebound vasodilation with skin barrier dysfunction. Topical corticosteroid withdrawal most frequently affects the face and scrotum. However, every part of the body can be involved. Etiology The etiology of topical corticosteroid withdrawal remains inadequately defined, but the condition typically manifests following the application of medium-to-high-potency fluorinated topical corticosteroids belonging to class I, II, and III for more than 12 weeks. The withdrawal less frequently occurs with short-term use of topical corticosteroids or those classified as low-potency topical corticosteroids. As a type of drug addiction emerging from the inappropriate, excessive use of topical corticosteroids, the reaction occurs when the topical drug is used without supervision for an extended time, without a prescription, or when applied for unrecognized indications (eg, acne vulgaris). For example, use in high potency in age groups (like children and older adults) or on unsuitable body areas like the face and scrotum is not indicated.
