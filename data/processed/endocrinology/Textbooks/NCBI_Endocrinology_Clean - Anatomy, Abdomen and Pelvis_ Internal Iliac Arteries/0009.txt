These lymph nodes are nestled on and around branches of the internal iliac artery and receive lymphatic fluid from the pelvic portions of the digestive tract, urinary organs, and reproductive organs. Afferent lymph vessels convey lymphatic fluid towards the internal iliac nodes, and efferent lymph vessels convey it to collectors located superior to the nodes, primarily the common iliac lymph nodes. Physiologic Variants The internal iliac artery and its branches display significant variation in branching patterns, location, and areas supplied. The internal iliac artery itself varies with regard to the level of its origin and where it branches into anterior and posterior divisions. Some of the more common arterial variants arising from the anterior and posterior divisions of the IIA are summarized in the following paragraphs. Typically, 1 of the first branches of the anterior division of the internal iliac artery, the obturator artery, can arise from the posterior division or other branches of the anterior division, such as the inferior epigastric artery or inferior vesical artery.
