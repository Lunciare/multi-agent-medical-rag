gluteal artery. Embryology In adults, the internal and external iliac arteries bifurcate from the common iliac arteries at the fifth lumbar to the first sacral (L5-S1) vertebral level. Given the differential growth of the aorta and its branches relative to the vertebral column, the vertebral level for the origin of the internal iliac artery shifts through development. The bifurcation level of the common iliac artery and origin of the inferior iliac artery are at the first sacral (S1) vertebral level during the first trimester. It is at the fifth lumbar (L5) vertebral level at birth. During fetal development, the internal iliac artery conveys oxygen-poor, nutrient-poor fetal blood to the placenta via the umbilical artery. The umbilical artery is within the umbilical cord and is a critical component of fetoplacental circulation. At birth, the portion of the umbilical artery that is extracorporeal contracts and is ultimately removed from neonatal circulation. Within the neonate, the proximal portion of the umbilical artery persists as the patent portion of the umbilical artery, but the distal portion that communicated with the placenta contracts and becomes occluded.
