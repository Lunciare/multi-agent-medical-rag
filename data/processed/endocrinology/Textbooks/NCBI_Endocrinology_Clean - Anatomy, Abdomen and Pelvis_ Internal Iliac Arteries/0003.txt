Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Internal Iliac Arteries
KEYWORDS: artery, iliac, internal, iliac artery, internal iliac, branches, arteries, pelvic, gluteal, common, inferior, division, obturator, anterior, umbilical

Post-birth, the umbilical artery is continuous with a non-patent fibrous cord called the medial umbilical ligament that continues to the deep surface of the lower anterior abdominal wall. In both sexes, the umbilical artery gives rise to the superior vesical artery, which supplies the superior surface of the bladder. The umbilical artery also gives rise to the uterine artery, which serves the uterus in females. Subsequently, the uterine artery gives rise to the vaginal artery, which provides a major blood supply to the vagina. In males, the umbilical artery supplies the prostate and ductus deferens indirectly via the artery to the ductus deferens and prostatic branches that arise from the superior vesical artery. The obturator artery is most often an early branch of the anterior division of the IIA. Still, it arises from the external iliac artery either directly or via the inferior epigastric artery in approximately 19% of individuals. The obturator artery distributes branches to the iliacus and ilium within the pelvis. The obturator artery travels across the pelvis and through the obturator foramen to serve the femoral head and ultimately reach its main territory in the medial thigh.
