Anatomy, Abdomen and Pelvis: Internal Iliac Arteries
KEYWORDS: artery, iliac, internal, iliac artery, internal iliac, branches, arteries, pelvic, gluteal, common, inferior, division, obturator, anterior, umbilical

Introduction The internal iliac artery (IIA), or hypogastric artery, is the primary artery supplying the pelvic viscera and an important contributor to structures of the pelvic wall, perineum, gluteal region, and thigh. The internal iliac artery arises where the common iliac artery bifurcates into internal and external iliac arteries; it then crosses the pelvic brim to give off numerous branches within the pelvis. The pelvic arteries arising from the internal iliac artery exhibit highly variable branching patterns and numbers, an important feature to note during pelvic surgery. In addition, there are notable differences in the branches of the internal iliac artery in males and females, given that the reproductive organs are within their supply territory. Structure and Function The internal iliac artery is 1 of 2 major arteries arising from the common iliac artery. The right and left common iliac arteries begin at approximately the level of the 4th or 5th lumbar vertebra (L4-L5), marking the terminal end of the abdominal aorta. Each common iliac artery bifurcates into an internal and external iliac artery, usually just anterior to the sacroiliac joint.
