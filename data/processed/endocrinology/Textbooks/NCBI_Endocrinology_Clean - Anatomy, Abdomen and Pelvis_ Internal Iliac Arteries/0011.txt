In addition, the artery to the ductus deferens may arise from either the superior vesical artery or the inferior vesical artery. In both sexes, the internal pudendal artery and the middle rectal artery sometimes arise from a common trunk. An accessory internal pudendal artery is variably present, arising from another pelvic artery such as the inferior vesical artery, obturator artery, or external iliac artery. The posterior division's iliolumbar artery may arise directly from the trunk of the internal iliac artery proximal to its bifurcation into anterior and posterior divisions. Finally, the branches of the internal iliac artery participate in highly variable, rich collateral circulatory routes in the pelvis, thigh, and gluteal region. Surgical Considerations Knowledge of the internal iliac artery and its branches is critical for a variety of pelvic procedures, given the IIA's primary role in supplying blood to the pelvic viscera and its potential for ligation or compression during massive pelvic hemorrhage.
