Function Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) ADH, also known as vasopressin, acts as a water-preserving hormone. ADH is released into the blood circulation to vasoconstrict and reabsorbs water from the collecting duct in the kidney to maintain equilibrium intracellularly and extracellularly. There are two regulating receptors in the hypothalamus that sense water depletion and hyperosmolar concentration, the subfornical organ and organum vasculosum. This particular area of the nervous system does not have an intact blood-brain barrier. Therefore, blood products can come into contact with the structures with ease and eventually signal the hypothalamus for ADH secretion. A small concentration is enough to generate water conservation in the renal tubules. The renal tubules are broken down into segments known as the proximal, descending, ascending, distal, and collecting duct. The collecting duct is a highly diluted, ADH-dependent structure in the kidney. The cells in the collecting duct have a 7 pass receptor on the basolateral side where ADH can bind itself.
