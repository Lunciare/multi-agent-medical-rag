Inhibin B is typically undetectable, and anti-Müllerian hormone levels are typically low in adults with Klinefelter syndrome, reflecting abnormal Sertoli cell function. Both biomarkers typically remain within normal ranges during childhood but become abnormal after puberty. Noninvasive prenatal testing for cell-free fetal DNA can detect sex chromosome abnormalities. Published positive predictive values for identifying Klinefelter syndrome through noninvasive prenatal testing range from 67% to 78%. Noninvasive prenatal testing analyzes small fragments of fetal DNA circulating in maternal serum. Any suspected case requires additional prenatal or postnatal testing for confirmation. A definitive diagnosis of Klinefelter syndrome typically involves prenatal or postnatal karyotype analysis or chromosomal microarray testing. Referral to a reference laboratory with expertise in these examinations is recommended. Confirmation by karyotyping requires analysis of at least 20 cultured metaphase lymphocytes. Using fluorescence in situ hybridization can improve diagnostic accuracy. A baseline dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry scan is recommended due to the higher prevalence of reduced bone mineral density and increased risk of osteoporosis in affected individuals. Contributing factors include hypogonadism, vitamin D deficiency, and altered bone remodeling. Treatment / Management Earlier diagnosis, often prenatally, allows timely developmental evaluation and early intervention
