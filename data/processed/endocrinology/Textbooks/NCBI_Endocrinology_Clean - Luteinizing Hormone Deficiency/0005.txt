Women also give a history of primary amenorrhea. Men can have gynecomastia. Stress-related hypogonadotropic hypogonadism commonly presents in women of reproductive age who have recently had significant stress or excessive weight loss, such as anorexia nervosa. Factors that cause weight loss and the resulting hypogonadotropic hypogonadism include excessive exercise, psychiatric disorders like anorexia nervosa, and malnutrition. Idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (IHH) in children presents identical to Kallmann syndrome, except for anosmia. Adult-onset IHH presents with sexual dysfunction (loss of libido, erectile dysfunction) in men and amenorrhea in women . Patients with pituitary tumors have varying presentations, but visual disturbance and headache are common. Hyperprolactinemia presents with amenorrhea and galactorrhea in women and impotence, infertility and hypogonadism in men . There can be associated galactorrhea in men; however, it is uncommon.
