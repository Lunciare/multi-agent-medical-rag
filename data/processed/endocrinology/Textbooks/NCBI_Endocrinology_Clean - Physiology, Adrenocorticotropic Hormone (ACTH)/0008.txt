Definitive management involves surgical excision of the tumor when it is identifiable on imaging and metastasis has been excluded. Radiotherapy may be employed in cases of metastatic or unresectable tumors. Medical therapies and, in selected cases, bilateral adrenalectomy can serve as adjunctive or alternative interventions. Primary Adrenal Insufficiency Primary adrenal insufficiency is a rare disorder resulting from inadequate production of glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids, or both. Multiple etiologies exist, with autoimmune adrenalitis, known as Addison disease, representing the most common cause in Western populations. The corticotropin stimulation test, also known as the short Synacthen test, serves as the standard diagnostic tool for adrenal insufficiency. Administration of 250 mcg of intravenous corticotropin is followed by measurement of plasma ACTH and cortisol levels at baseline, 30 minutes, and 60 minutes. Historically, cortisol levels below 18 mcg/dL (500 nmol/L) were considered diagnostic, although thresholds varied with assay methodology. More recent studies support the use of lower cutoffs of 14 to 15 mcg/dL (approximately 400 nmol/L) for diagnosis.
