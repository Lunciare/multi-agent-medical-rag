Electrolyte abnormalities, including hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and hypoglycemia, are frequently observed in affected patients. Screening for other autoimmune disorders is recommended, as patients with Addison disease often present with concomitant autoimmune conditions. Acute presentations of primary adrenal insufficiency require immediate intervention, even before biochemical confirmation. Management includes aggressive resuscitation with intravenous fluids to address severe volume depletion and intravenous glucocorticoid replacement, typically with hydrocortisone. Long-term management of primary adrenal insufficiency necessitates lifelong glucocorticoid replacement, with fludrocortisone supplementation for patients with aldosterone deficiency. Hydrocortisone is preferred for glucocorticoid therapy, usually administered in 3 divided doses per day, with the highest dose in the morning to replicate physiological circadian variation. Mineralocorticoid replacement is commonly achieved with fludrocortisone. Patients should be provided with an emergency steroid card to inform healthcare personnel of chronic steroid therapy and receive counselling regarding "sick day rules," including doubling the hydrocortisone dose during periods of acute illness.
