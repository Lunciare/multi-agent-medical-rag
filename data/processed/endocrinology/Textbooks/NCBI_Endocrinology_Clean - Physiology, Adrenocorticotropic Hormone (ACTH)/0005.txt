Initial evaluation of Cushing syndrome involves 1 of several tests: urine-free cortisol measurement, the 1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test, or late-night salivary cortisol assessment. Late-night salivary cortisol requires the collection of 2 samples on consecutive nights between 11 pm and 1 am, with precise labeling of collection times. Cortisol and ACTH normally follow a diurnal rhythm, resulting in low salivary cortisol levels at night. This rhythm is disrupted in Cushing syndrome, and nighttime cortisol levels remain elevated. The 1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test involves taking 1 mg of dexamethasone orally, typically between 11 pm and midnight, followed by measuring serum cortisol the next morning between 8 and 9 am. Dexamethasone suppresses ACTH secretion in individuals with normal glucocorticoid regulation, producing low serum cortisol levels. In contrast, patients with autonomous glucocorticoid overproduction demonstrate absent or inadequate suppression of ACTH and cortisol. Positive results from initial testing, or negative results in patients with high clinical suspicion of hypercortisolism, warrant referral to a specialist endocrinology service for comprehensive evaluation.
