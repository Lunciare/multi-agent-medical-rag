Concurrent assessment should exclude other physiologic causes of elevated cortisol, including pregnancy, depression, poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, and significant obesity. Following confirmation of Cushing syndrome, differentiation between ACTH-dependent and ACTH-independent forms requires measurement of serum ACTH. Elevated or high-normal ACTH indicates ACTH-dependent Cushing syndrome, whereas suppressed ACTH identifies ACTH-independent disease. ACTH-dependent Cushing syndrome arises from either Cushing disease, caused by a pituitary adenoma secreting excess ACTH, or ectopic ACTH production. Differentiation between these etiologies relies on diagnostic procedures such as the high-dose (8 mg) dexamethasone suppression test, desmopressin stimulation test, and inferior petrosal sinus sampling (IPSS). Magnetic resonance imaging is frequently employed to identify pituitary adenomas responsible for Cushing disease. For ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome, obtaining a computed tomography (CT) scan of the adrenal glands after excluding exogenous glucocorticoid exposure can confirm the presence of adrenal tumors. First-line management of Cushing syndrome involves surgical resection of the tumor when it is visible on imaging and considered resectable. Transsphenoidal surgery is the preferred approach in Cushing disease.
