Function The principal function of ACTH is the stimulation of the steroidogenic pathway in the adrenal cortex. ACTH regulates the zona fasciculata and zona reticularis, where cortisol and adrenal androgens are produced, respectively. Cortisol exerts wide-ranging physiologic effects, including enhancement of metabolic activity leading to elevated blood glucose and free fatty acid levels, suppression of the inflammatory and immune responses, and increased cardiac output and vasoconstriction. Cortisol production is particularly critical during stress. Following activation of the sympathetic nervous system, the HPA axis augments cortisol synthesis, sustaining the stress response through these mechanisms. Persistent HPA axis activation in the setting of chronic stress may contribute to adverse health outcomes. ACTH also regulates adrenal androgen synthesis. Dihydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) and androstenedione, precursors of sex steroids, are primarily controlled by ACTH. Although ACTH can influence mineralocorticoid secretion, the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system serves as the primary regulator of this pathway. Mechanism The hypothalamus, located in the ventral brain, secretes corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), which stimulates ACTH secretion from the anterior pituitary.
