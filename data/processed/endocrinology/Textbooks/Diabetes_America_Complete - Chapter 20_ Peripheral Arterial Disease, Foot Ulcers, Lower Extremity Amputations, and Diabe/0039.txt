PREVENTION STRATEGIES Guidelines from 2004 advocate that education should be provided to all diabetic patients on foot care and that they should have their risk status assessed at least annually (76,148). This recommendation is frequently neglected (104,125,142). In a U.K. study, <20% of diabetic patients had their feet examined by a health care professional, and the foot exam annual rate ranged from 30% to 50% in the physician’s office (125). A complete assessment was performed in only about 10% of the diabetic population in outpatient clinics and in 14% of those admitted to hospital due to DFU (80). This low assessment rate may be partly explained by the lack of knowledge of the most important items to assess during the screening evaluation (104). In addition, evidence regarding the impact of podiatric care (91,135,149) and diabetic foot educa­ tional programs on DFU risk is insufficient (88,150,151,152).
