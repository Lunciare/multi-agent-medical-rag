As they reach the endometrium, the radial arteries further divide into basal and spiral arteries. These branches ensure adequate perfusion throughout the uterine wall and play a critical role in sustaining endometrial function during menstruation and supporting placental circulation during pregnancy. Lymphatic drainage of the uterus follows a segmental pattern. The fundus primarily drains into para-aortic lymph nodes, mirroring the drainage pathways of the ovaries and fallopian tubes. Additional lymphatic channels follow the round ligament to reach the superficial inguinal lymph nodes. The lower uterine segments drain along the uterine vasculature toward external and internal iliac lymph nodes. Nerves The internal pelvic organs receive autonomic innervation from both sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions. Sympathetic input to the uterus arises from spinal levels T11 and T12 via the hypogastric plexus, while parasympathetic fibers originate from S2 to S4. The uterus and cervix are sensitive to distension, and cervical dilation during labor contributes significantly to labor pain.
