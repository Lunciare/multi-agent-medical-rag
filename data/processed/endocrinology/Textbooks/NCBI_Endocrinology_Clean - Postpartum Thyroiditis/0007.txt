Postpartum Thyroiditis
KEYWORDS: thyroid, ppt, postpartum, tsh, disease, thyroiditis, individuals, levels, state, months, patients, phase, graves, hypothyroidism, symptoms

The thyroid gland is composed of follicles, containing colloid, surrounded by follicular cells. The pathophysiology of PPT is characterized by immunomediated cytolysis. Histopathologic findings include prominent lymphocytic infiltration, including associated germinal centers, and the collapse of thyroid follicles. Fine-needle aspiration of the thyroid gland demonstrates follicular cells, lymphocytes, and an accumulation of colloid. During the recovery phase, follicles return to normal, but some degree of fibrosis and lymphocytic infiltration persists. History and Physical Most patients with PPT have symptoms that are easily misattributed to routine postpartum symptoms from hormonal changes, sleep deprivation, and caring for a newborn. Additionally, symptoms may not begin until after the 6-week postpartum visit, prompting the patient to seek care outside of the usual schedule. The thyrotoxic state often demonstrates mild symptoms, including irritability, palpitations, fatigue, and heat intolerance. The hypothyroid phase may be symptomatic, with constipation, dry skin, fatigue, impaired concentration, cold intolerance, and paresthesia. Moreover, patients with PPT and TPO antibodies may be more symptomatic than those without. Evaluation The diagnosis of PPT is based on clinical presentation and thyroid function tests (levels of TSH and free T4).
