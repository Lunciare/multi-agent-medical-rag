Impact of Sleep and Circadian Disturbances on Glucose Metabolism and Type 2 Diabetes 25–15 To date, nine prospective cohort studies, with approximately 65,000 participants, have been conducted to explore whether the presence of OSA, using objective sleep assessments, at baseline predicted incident diabetes during a follow-up, after adjusting for BMI or other measures of adiposity and other confounders (Table 25.10) (28,124,125,126,127,128,129,130, 131). The studies varied in the methods and criteria used to diagnose OSA (pulse oximetry vs. full or limited PSG, and cutoff for AHI/oxygen desaturation index), verification of diabetes diagnosis, and duration of follow-up period (2.7–16 years).
