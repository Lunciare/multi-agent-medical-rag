In a 2010 Korean study, Joon et al measured the longitudinal axis of the kidneys and found a mean coronal angle of 16.8° (range, 15° to 20°) relative to the spine. This angle may be reduced in the presence of accessory renal arteries. At the medial margin of each kidney lies the renal hilum, where the renal artery enters the kidney, and the renal vein and pelvis exit the renal sinus. The renal vein is positioned anterior to the renal artery, with the renal pelvis located posterior to both. The renal pelvis represents the superior end of the ureter and typically receives 2 or 3 major calyces, with each major calyx drained by 2 or 3 minor calyces, although considerable variation exists. Minor calyces are indented by the renal papillae, which form the apices of the renal pyramids. A renal pyramid, together with its overlying cortical tissue, constitutes a renal lobe. Each kidney is enclosed by a 2-layered capsule and surrounded by perinephric fat, the Gerota fascia anteriorly, and the Zuckerkandl fascia posteriorly.
