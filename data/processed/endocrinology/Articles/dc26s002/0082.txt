The GDM diagnostic criteria for the 75-g OGTT from the International Asso­ ciation of the Diabetes and Pregnancy Study Groups (IADPSG) and the GDM screening and diagnostic criteria with the two-step approach were not derived from data in the first half of pregnancy and should not be used for early screening (198). Both the FPG and A1C are low-cost tests. An advantage of the A1C test is its convenience, as it can be added to the pre­ natal laboratory tests and does not require an early-morning fasting appointment. Disadvantages include inaccuracies in the presence of conditions such as increased or decreased red blood cell turnover (usually decreases and increases A1C, respectively), hemoglobinopathies, and iron deficiency anemia (increases A1C) (199,200). GDM is often indicative of underlying β-cell dysfunction (201), which confers markedly increased risk for later devel­ opment of glucose intolerance and diabe­ tes in the mother after delivery (202–204).
