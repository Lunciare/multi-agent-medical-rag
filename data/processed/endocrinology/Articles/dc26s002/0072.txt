MONOGENIC DIABETES SYNDROMES Monogenic defects that cause β-cell dys­ function (e.g., neonatal diabetes and MODY) or insulin resistance syndromes (e.g., mono­ genic lipodystrophies) are present in a small fraction of people with diabetes (<5%) (170). Table 2.7 describes the most com­ mon causes of monogenic diabetes. For a comprehensive list of causes, see Ge­ netic Diagnosis of Endocrine Disorders (171) and ISPAD 2022 clinical practice consensus guidelines (170). Recommendations 2.29a Regardless of current age, all people diagnosed with diabetes in the first 6 months of life should have ge­ netic testing for neonatal diabetes. A 2.29b Children and young adults who do not have typical characteris­ tics of type 1 or type 2 diabetes and have a family history of diabetes in successive generations (suggestive of an autosomal-dominant pattern of inheritance) should have genetic test­ ing for maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY).
