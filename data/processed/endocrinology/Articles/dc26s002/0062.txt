In a large meta-analysis that included 3,900 participants treated with everolimus across nine RCTs, the inci­ dence of all-grade hyperglycemia was as high as 27%, whereas grades 3 and 4 were reported in 2.5% of participants (134). In a single-center retrospective co­ hort study in people with cancer treated with everolimus, the estimated median time to onset of hyperglycemia was 5–6 months, but the incidence distribution extended from a few weeks to several years after the initiation of treatment (135). In the absence of a clear-cut temporal pat­ tern for the onset of hyperglycemia, fasting or random plasma glucose should be checked before treatment initiation and at each visit during treatment with mTOR inhibitors, or sooner if symptoms of hyperglycemia develop in the in­ terim, while monitoring A1C every 3 months can also be considered. Finally, hyperglycemia is also a rela­ tively frequent complication of Akt in­ hibitors (e.g., capivasertib) and antibody drug conjugates (e.g., enfortumab vedotin), which will not be reviewed.
