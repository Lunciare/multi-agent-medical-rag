Introduction Eating disorders are mental illnesses that, much more clearly than other mental illnesses, are characterised in their course, treatment, and prognosis by those changes that are marked by the physical consequences of the eating disorder. In this context, the more signiﬁcant part of the ultimate consequences is due to the food restriction, the lack of energy and, ultimately, the excessively low body weight. In this respect, the ﬁndings are particularly valid for anorexia nervosa and somewhat less so for other eating disorders, such as bulimia nervosa or binge eating disorder. The endocrine consequences that will be reviewed in this review affect the pituitary gland, hypothalamus, adrenal glands, gonads, and brain–gut axis.
