Growth Hormone—IGF-1 Axis The endocrine adaptation to chronic fasting or starving also includes a state of GH resistance, in which elevated levels of GH due to elevated stimulation by Ghrelin can be observed alongside reduced levels of IGF-1. Again, this resistance takes place at the level of the liver and might be an adaptation to the energy deﬁciency in order to decrease energy consumption by growth . As GH also has a role in gluconeogenesis, partly through the mobilization of fatty and amino acids and, thus, is similar to the effect of glucocorticoids, the hypothesis is that the primary adaptive process is the up-regulation of GH in order to preserve euglycaemia. Secondary to this, the liver regulates GH effects down in order to save energy, which would otherwise outdo this adaptation by using the provided energy for growth. Attempts to explain this in endocrine terms have included the regulatory effect of ﬁbroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) and insulin, whilst low IGF-1 levels and elevated ghrelin levels stimulate GH secretion.
