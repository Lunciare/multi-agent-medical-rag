Interestingly, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) of the AMH gene affect male cognitive function (119). An increased AMH level is connected to worse episodic memory, reaction time and reduced reasoning scores, but has no inﬂuence on executive skills and complex processing speed (119). There is a slight negative correlation between the level of AMH and speciﬁc characteristics of boys’ autism, such as social interaction and communication. However, total concentrations of AMH in healthy boys compared with boys having autism spectrum disorders are similar although the SMAD pathway may be engaged in autism spectrum disorders (120). On the other hand, AMH levels reﬂect different drawing skills of boys and girls at the age of 5-6 years, in favor of girls in an inversely dependent manner in comparison to the concentration of AMH (121). This suggests that autistic boys may have greater artistic abilities (122).
