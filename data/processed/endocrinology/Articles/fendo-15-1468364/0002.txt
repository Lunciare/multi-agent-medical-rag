KEYWORDS AMH, AMHR2, neuroendocrinology, oncology, signal transduction 1 Introduction AMH, also known as a Müllerian inhibiting factor or Müllerian inhibiting substance, has a mani-fold and complex effect on the development and the function of a variety of human tissues. AMH is a glycoprotein belonging to the transforming growth factor beta (TGFb) superfamily (1). This family of signaling proteins includes 32 other peptides, such as activins, inhibin A and B, bone morphogenic factors (BMPs) and growth differentiation factors (GDFs) like myostatin (2, 3). The AMH protein composed of a N-terminal prodomain and a C- terminal growth factor (GF) domain (Figure 1), which is encoded by a gene consisting of 5 exons and 4 introns, located on the short arm of human chromosome 19 (4).
