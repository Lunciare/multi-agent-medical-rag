Type 2 Diabetes People with type 2 diabetes who may have had undiagnosed diabetes for years and have a signiﬁcant risk of prevalent diabetic retinopathy at the time of diag- nosis should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination at the time of diagnosis. Pregnancy Individuals who develop gestational diabe- tes mellitus do not require eye examinations to be at increased riskofdeveloping diabetic retinopathy during pregnancy (27). How- ever, individuals of childbearing potential with preexisting type 1 or type 2 diabetes who are planning pregnancy or who have become pregnant should be counseled on the baseline prevalence and risk of devel- opment and/or progression of diabetic retinopathy. In a systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 observational studies of pregnant individuals with preexisting type 1 or type 2 diabetes, the prevalence of any diabetic retinopathy and proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) in early preg- nancy was 52.3% and 6.1%, respectively.
