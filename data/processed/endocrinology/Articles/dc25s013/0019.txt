The care of older adults with diabetes is and functional heterogeneity and their varied prior experience with disease management. Some older individuals may have developed diabetes years earlier and have signiﬁcant complications, others are newly diagnosed and may have had years of undiagnosed diabetes with resul- tant complications, and still, other older adults may have truly recent-onset disease with few or no complications (51). Some older adults with diabetes have other un- derlying chronic conditions, substantial dia- betes-related comorbidity, limited cognitive or physical functioning, or frailty (52,53). Other older individuals with diabetes have little comorbidity and are active. Life expectancy is affected by the age of the individual, disease burden, and degree of disability. Multiple prognostic tools for life expectancy for older adults are available (54,55).
