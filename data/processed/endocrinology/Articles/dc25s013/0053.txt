Nev- ertheless, a feasibility study in LTC facili- ties showed that CGM can be useful in older adults with diabetes, although it re- quires substantial staff training (140). Fur- thermore, an observational study of older adults with diabetes living in LTC facilities using CGM revealed a high prevalence of hypoglycemia both in people using insulin and in those using sulfonylureas, thus showing that this population of older adults in LTC facilities are at increased risk for hypoglycemia (141). Therefore, using CGM can provide useful and more prompt information on hypoglycemia in this vulner- able population. Of note, a recent random- ized controlled trial in LTC facilities showed that real-time CGM use for up to 60 days was safe and effective in guiding insulin doses compared with BGM by point of care. There were no differences in TIR, time below range, or mean glucose levels (142). Some staff may be less knowledge- able about the differences between type 1 and type 2 diabetes.
