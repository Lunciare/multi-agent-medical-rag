KEYWORDS: weight, loss, weight loss, surgery, diabetes, management, obesity, metabolic, health, treatment, individuals, type, metabolic surgery, risk, people

real-world data anal- ysis through the National Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Network (PCORnet) in the U.S. compared surgical outcomes be- tween 6,233 individuals with type 2 diabe- tes who underwent RYGB and 3,477 who underwent VSG. At 1 year after surgery, those who had RYGB lost on average 29.1% of their total body weight, while those who had VSG lost on average 22.8% of their total body weight. At 5 years after surgery, the total body weight loss was 24.1% for those who had RYGB and 16.1% for those who had VSG, with 86.1% of indi- viduals experiencing type 2 diabetes remis- sion after RYGB and 83.5% of individuals experiencing type 2 diabetes remission af- ter VSG. Among the 6,141 individuals who experienced type 2 diabetes remission, the subsequent type 2 diabetes relapse rate was lower for those who had RYGB than for those who had VSG (hazard ratio 0.75 [95% CI 0.67–0.84]). Estimated relapse rates for those who had RYGB and VSG were 33.1% and 41.6%, respectively, at 5 years after surgery.
