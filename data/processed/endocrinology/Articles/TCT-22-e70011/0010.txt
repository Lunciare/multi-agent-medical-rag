The remaining two studies that did not use mannequins included one by Bansal et al. , which aimed to improve pediatric res- idents' knowledge and perspectives on insulin pump therapy. Investigators provided a lecture on insulin pump technology and then rotated residents through three insulin pump stations, where company representatives demonstrated their own pumps and allowed hands-­on practice. The other study by Pinnaro et al. aimed to develop a pediatric diabetes simulation, incorporating both blood sugar and urine ketones as outputs or use in teaching pediatric diabetes management. This interactive virtual simulation required trainees to manage a patient with new-­onset T1D, with the goal of clearing the patient's ketones. Written feedback was then provided on inaccurate insulin dos- ing and timing. It ended if ketones continued to rise or if the pa- tient became hypoglycaemic.
