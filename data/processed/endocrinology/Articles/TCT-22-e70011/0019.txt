Moreover, incorporating in- novative assessment strategies that encompass a wider range of competencies, from communication to technical skills, will better align education with the multifaceted demands of PE practice. National and international organizations like ESPE, ISPAD and the Pediatric Endocrine Society (PES) play an important role in supporting the global PE community. ESPE's e-­learning courses, accredited by the European Accreditation Council for Continuing Medical Education (EACCME), offer interna- tional certification and uphold professional standards . ISPAD's Science Schools for Physicians (ISSP) and Healthcare Professionals (ISSHP) enhance research skills, scientific pro- duction, and international collaborations [78–80]. Curriculum developers should address potential barriers to implementing educational initiatives. For example, in a study done by Tania Dumont on pediatric and adolescent gynaecology curricu- lum, which is a field that overlaps with PE, authors found that despite recognizing its importance, lack of resources and other obstacles hinder curriculum implementation. Regarding limitations, PE education is an emerging field with lim- itations in existing studies, which often focus primarily on diabetes education and lack inclusion of interdisciplinary health profes- sionals involved in patient care.
