Upon retirement, he reflected on how medical education had changed: focused on vocational training. For example, one may wonder whether the countless master’s degrees that are being established in the context of one or another college at our university actually belong within the university. Univer- sity education must provide students with the basic knowledge and the nec- essary critical sense to question this knowledge; students must be taught to acquire and critically evaluate knowledge. Acquiring technical skills is largely part of postgraduate education. The previous generation, of which I would like to mention my mentor Prof. Regniers as an example, possessed a much more limited technical knowledge, but a much broader humanistic view of medicine.” (. . .)
