In a U.S.-based study conducted between 2006 and 2012 in Chicago, Illinois, 21.6% of people hospitalized for DKA had more than one episode over 6 years, with 5.8% of individuals accounting for 26.3% of DKA hospitalizations (23). Similarly, analysis of inpatient data from the U.K. in 2014 revealed that 33.7% of people admitted with DKA had at least one episode of DKA in the prior year (24). In general, the all- cause readmission rate after episodes of DKA or hyperglycemic crises in general ranges between 10% and 20%, with 40–65% of these readmissions being for recurrent hyperglycemic crises (the remain- der are for other causes, including occa- sionally for severe hypoglycemia), mostly occurring within 2 weeks of discharge from the prior DKA episode (25–27). Morbidity and Mortality Hyperglycemic crises are associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, and costs (28–31). In the U.S., the mean length of stay for patients hospitalized with DKA is 3.0 days among people with T1D and 3.7 days among people with T2D (32) and has been shortening over time (29).
