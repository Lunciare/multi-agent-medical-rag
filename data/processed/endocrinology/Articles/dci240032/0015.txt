Rates of DKA during the COVID-19 pandemic in- creased primarily among individuals with newly diagnosed diabetes and preexisting T2D (79,80). While rates of DKA decreased among people with preexisting T1D in the U.K., they increased among people with T1D in the U.S. (79,81). Older adults from racial and ethnic minority back- grounds experienced the greatest rise in DKA events (79,81). Some drug classes can affect carbohy- drate metabolism and precipitate the development of DKA and HHS (82). Glu- cocorticoids may precipitate acute and sustained hyperglycemia by countering insulin action (83,84). Antipsychotic med- ications may also raise DKA risk, although the precise mechanism is uncertain (85). Approximately 1–2% of patients receiving checkpoint inhibitors develop new-onset autoimmune diabetes (86), characterized by rapid onset of hyperglycemia, swift progression of endogenous insulin deﬁ- ciency, and a high riskof DKA or severe hy- perglycemia if not detected and treated promptly with insulin therapy (87,88).
