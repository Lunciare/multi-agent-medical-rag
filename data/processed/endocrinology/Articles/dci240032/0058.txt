Partici- pation in a structured diabetes educa- tion program leads to a substantial risk reduction for DKA and HHS (156). In pa- tients with recurrent DKA, up to 75% of the admissions have been attributed to insufﬁcient use of insulin therapy (i.e., missed insulin doses) as the immediate contributing factor (48). Omission or insuf- ﬁcient use of insulin therapy is a major cause of DKA admissions and readmissions (159). Thus, education on insulin admin- istration and “sick day advice” must be provided or reinforced. Upon discharge, patients should receive an adequate sup- ply of insulin and diabetes-durable medi- cal equipment (i.e., glucose monitoring and insulin administration devices) as well as contact information for health care professionals who can assist in managing future episodes of high blood glucose and ketone concentrations. For individuals with poor access to insulin, the social service de- partment should be consulted to address these barriers to optimal self-management.
