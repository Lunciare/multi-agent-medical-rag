The resulting changes in the insulin/glucagon ratio lead to increased gluconeogenesis, accelerated glycogenolysis, and impaired glucose utilization by peripheral tissues.The combination of insulin deﬁciency and increased counterregulatory hormones results in the release of free fatty acids from adipose tissues (lipolysis), leading to unre- strained hepatic fatty acid oxidation and the production of excess ketone bodies with re- sultingketonemiaandmetabolicacidosis(3). In HHS, compared with DKA, there is less severe insulin deﬁciency and, there- fore, sufﬁcient insulin to prevent keto- genesis but not enough to prevent hy- perglycemia, due to increased hepatic glu- cose production and decreased glucose utilization by peripheral tissues. Hypergly- cemia leads to an osmotic diuresis, leading to volume depletion and hemoconcentration.
