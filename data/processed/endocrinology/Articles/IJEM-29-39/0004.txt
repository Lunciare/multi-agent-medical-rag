Superstition might have a declining trend with the advancement of medical science, and yet these beliefs continue to influence evidence-based medical care. According to a study from Japan, superstitious beliefs influenced the decision to discharge from the hospital contributing to increased medical care expenses. Patients preferred staying in the hospital beyond recommended days by consulting the doctor and getting a discharge on an auspicious day. According to a study from Chandigarh, about two-thirds of people attributed their symptoms of mental illness to sorcery/ witchcraft (Jaadu Tona), planetary/celestial influences (Grah Nakchatra), bad deeds in a previous life (Karma), spirit intrusion (Opari Kasar), evil spirits (Buri Atma), ghosts (Bhoot- Pret) and divine wrath (Devi Devta Prakop) and believed that offering prayers or performing magico-religious rituals only is sufficient for treatment. Thus, these superstitious beliefs delayed the proper treatment of mental illness leading to complications.
