Cardiovascular outcomes trials of dipep- tidyl peptidase 4 (DPP-4) inhibitors have all, so far, not shown cardiovascular bene- ﬁts relative to placebo. In addition, the CAROLINA (Cardiovascular Outcome Study of Linagliptin Versus Glimepiride in Type 2 Diabetes) study demonstrated noninferior- ity between a DPP-4 inhibitor, linagliptin, and a sulfonylurea, glimepiride, on cardio- vascular outcomes despite lower rates of hypoglycemia in the linagliptin treatment group (254). The BI 10773 (Empagliﬂozin) Cardiovascular Outcome Event Trial in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients (EMPA- REG OUTCOME) showed that treatment with empagliﬂozin reduced the composite outcome of MI, stroke, and cardiovascular death by 14% (absolute rate 10.5% vs. 12.1% in the placebo group, HR in the em- pagliﬂozin group 0.86 [95% CI 0.74–0.99]; P = 0.04 for superiority) and cardiovascular death by 38% (absolute rate 3.7% vs. 5.9%, HR 0.62 [95% CI 0.49–0.77]; P < 0.001) (255). Similarly, canagliﬂozin signiﬁ- cantly reduced the composite outcome of cardiovascular death, MI, or stroke versus placebo (occurring in 26.9 vs. 31.5 partici- pants per 1,000 person-years; HR 0.86 [95% CI 0.75–0.97]).
