Risk Management: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2025 increase and only a minority of people with type 2 diabetes achieve recom- mended risk factor goals and are treated with guideline-recommended therapy (7–9). Therefore, continued focus on deliv- ering high-quality comprehensive cardio- vascular care and on addressing barriers to risk factor management are required to im- plement the treatment recommendations (1,10) outlined in this section. Diabetes is also an important risk fac- tor for incident heart failure, which is at least twofold more prevalent in people with diabetes compared with those with- out diabetes and is a major cause of mor- bidity and mortality (11). People with diabetes may present with a wide spec- trum of heart failure, including heart fail- ure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmEF), or heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) (12). Comorbid conditions including excess body weight and hypertension often pre- cede the development of HFpEF and have been implicated in the pathophysiology of HFpEF (13).
