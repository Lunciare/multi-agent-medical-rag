The primary composite outcome of nonfatal MI, non- fatal stroke, or death from cardiovascu- lar causes was not signiﬁcantly reduced in the intensive treatment group. The prespeciﬁed secondary outcome of stroke was signiﬁcantly reduced by 41% in the intensive treatment group. Adverse events attributed to blood pressure treatment, including hypotension, syncope, bradycar- dia, hyperkalemia, and elevations in se- rum creatinine, occurred more frequently in the intensive treatment arm than in the standard therapy arm. Of note, the ACCORD BP and SPRINT trials aimed for a similar systolic blood pressure <120 mmHg, but in contrast to SPRINT, the primary composite cardio- vascular end point was nonsigniﬁcantly reduced in ACCORD BP. The results have been interpreted to be generally consis- tent between the two trials, but ACCORD BP was viewed as underpowered due to the composite primary end point being less sensitive to blood pressure regula- tion (38,40).
