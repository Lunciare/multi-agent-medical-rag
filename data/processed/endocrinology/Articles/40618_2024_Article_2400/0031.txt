Individuals with known malignancies are more likely to have adrenal metas- tases. ­[18F]FDG PET/CT are effective tools for distin- guishing between benign and malignant adrenal lesions, pri- marily because metastatic lesions typically exhibit higher ­[18F]FDG uptake when compared to the liver. Nonetheless, in certain cases, benign lesions may display similar features, potentially leading to false-positive results . Adrenal metastases typically present as irregular, round lesions with a varied internal texture . Alternatively, they can manifest as an enlarged gland with indistinct borders. On unenhanced CT scans, these lesions are often depicted with an attenuation value exceeding 10 Hounsfield Units (HU) . In patients with established malignancies, adrenal lesions larger than 3 cm in size, or masses lacking hemor- rhage or calcification with an attenuation of approximately 43 HU, strongly suggest metastatic involvement. Some highly vascular metastatic lesions may resemble pheochro- mocytomas in CT images . Pheochromocytomas are rare tumors originating from neural crest cells, with reported incidence rates ranging from 0.04 to 0.21 per 100,000 person-years . They can be bilateral, occur in children, and occasionally exhibit malignancy.
