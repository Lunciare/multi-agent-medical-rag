Focal thyroid incidentalomas Incidentalomas are unex- pected lesions discovered incidentally, usually not related to can be either focal or diffuse. In patients without known thy- roid pathology, the prevalence of diffuse uptake is 0.1–4.5% and is usually related to benign processes such as chronic thyroiditis, Graves’ disease, while focal thyroid uptake ranges from 0.1 to 4.8% with an average malignancy rate of about 34% . However, almost two thirds of focal ­[18F] FDG uptakes within the thyroid gland are related to benign diseases . While focal thyroid uptake on ­[18F]FDG PET/ CT should always be considered in the context of the possi- bility of an aggressive tumor, the majority of such instances are not linked to malignant conditions. Many studies evalu- ated the usefulness of different parameters to differentiate malignant from benign thyroid lesions, with controversial results . Higher SUVmax are more likely to be malig- nant but to date no statistically significant data are available and a safe threshold has not been identified.
