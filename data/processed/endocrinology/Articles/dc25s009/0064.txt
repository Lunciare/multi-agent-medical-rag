Often doses can be converted unit for unit and subsequently adjusted based on glucose monitoring; however, an initial dose re- duction of 10–20% can be used for indi- viduals in very tight management or at high risk for hypoglycemia and is typically needed when switching from insulin de- temir or U-300 glargine to another insulin (169). Clinicians should also be aware of the potential for overbasalization with in- sulin therapy (i.e., use of higher than clini- cally necessary and appropriate dose of basal insulin, typically masking insufﬁcient mealtime insulin). Clinical signals that should prompt evaluation for overbasali- zation include high bedtime-to-morning or preprandial-to-postprandial glucose differential (e.g., bedtime-to-morning glucose differential $50 mg/dL [$2.8 mmol/L]), hypoglycemia (aware or unaware), and high glucose variability. Evidence of over- basalization should prompt reevaluation of the glucose-lowering treatment plan to better address postprandial hyperglyce- mia (170).
