Importantly, metformin should not be started in those whose eGFR is <45 mL/min/1.73 m2. For those already treated with metformin, the dose of metformin should be reduced once eGFR is <45 mL/min/1.73 m2 and should be stopped once eGFR is <30 mL/ min/1.73 m2 (98). A secondary analysis of the GRADE trial found that insulin glar- gine, liraglutide, sitagliptin, and glimepiride did not prevent the development of CKD when added to metformin monotherapy in individuals without underlying CKD (145). Importantly, an SGLT2 inhibitor was not included in the GRADE trial. Individuals with CKD, particularly ad- vanced CKD and kidney failure, are at high risk for hypoglycemia (132). If treated with insulin and/or sulfonylur- eas, treatment needs to be closely moni- tored and adjusted as eGFR declines and individuals need to be educated about and closely monitored for hypoglycemia occurrence (136).
