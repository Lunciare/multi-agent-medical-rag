Vitiligo serves as a good comparator; it has a similar heritability and prevalence to HT. Approximately 60 common genetic variants account for over two-thirds of total vitiligo heritability, and these have effect sizes greater than other complex diseases, meaning that genome-wide screening has been a greater success in vitiligo than in HT and other autoimmune disorders . It has also been possible to impute that there is no ‘missing heritability’ (for instance due to epistasis or epigenetic effects) in viti- ligo, whereas this remains an assumption for most other autoimmune diseases. Nonetheless identifying the myriad rare and private genetic variants that account for 29% of the genetic risk in vitiligo will take huge populations or a fresh approach; this challenge will be far greater still in HT where rare variants seem to predominate.
