Reduced hospitalization rates for acute diabetes events and cardiovascular com­ plications have been observed in adults with type 1 diabetes using CGM in Swe­ den, and there is similar evidence from Canadian private payer system (66), Medic­ system (67), and from Swedish and Austral­ asian registries (68–71). Despite the evidence-based benefits of diabetes technology, coverage for devices is variable depending on the country and even the district of residence. For example, recent policy changes in Australia have per­ mitted examination of how coverage of de­ vices can impact care at a population level. Government funding for CGM was ap­ proved in April 2017 for people with type 1 diabetes under 21 years of age. Before this change, ∼5% of this population were using CGM, but this increased to 79% 2 years af­ ter the subsidy became effective. Concomi­ tant with the adoption of CGM was an increase in the odds of achieving an HbA1c <53 mmol/mol (7%), which persisted dur­ ing the 2-year follow-up period (70).
