DEFINING OPTIMAL OUTCOMES As traditionally defined, the optimal out­ comes in diabetes care involve multiple tar­ gets: glucose levels, blood pressure, lipid levels, cardiovascular risk, and diabetes- related complications. Many factors must be considered for an individual trying to reach such evidence-based goals and tar­ gets, including the extent of diabetes dis­ tress and burden, access to care, social erences. Therefore, to achieve optimal outcomes, the focus should shift toward personalized care (e.g., “How can the avail­ able diabetes technology best be matched to each individual with diabetes?” and “Which factors determine whether an indi­ vidual can use diabetes technology, and which devices should be considered?”) rather than crude metrics (“X% of my pa­ tients are on pumps” or “Y% of our pa­ tients are on sensors”).
