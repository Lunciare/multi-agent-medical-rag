Some solutions have been proposed for these issues but have not yet been widely imple­ mented (131). Remote monitoring can be performed in a synchronous or an asynchronous manner. Much of what is discussed above relates to the synchronous (or real-time) use of remote monitoring. However, asyn­ chronous monitoring can allow for clinic- based protocols that use cloud-based programs to continuously monitor pa­ concerning glucose levels or use of devi­ ces. Wearables are becoming increas­ ingly common in the general population for a variety of uses (132), and there are reports of successful use of this technol­ ogy in cardiology (133), for monitoring high-risk diabetic feet (134), and in pediat­ ric diabetes programs (135), with potential for expansion into other areas. People living in rural areas are the ideal target for telemedicine, but they may ex­ perience specific barriers, including lim­ ited internet access, fewer technological services, and socioeconomic barriers.
