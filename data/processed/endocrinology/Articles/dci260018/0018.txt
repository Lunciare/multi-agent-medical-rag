Special Populations People at High Risk of Hypoglycemia Diabetes technology can benefit individu­ als with long-standing type 1 diabetes and impaired awareness of hypoglycemia (IAH), a condition that affects 20–40% of people with type 1 diabetes and increases the risk of severe hypoglycemia. Both CGM and AID reduce the frequency and magni­ tude of hypoglycemic events, although an elevated risk of severe hypoglycemia in IAH persists (44), underscoring an ongoing need for educational initiatives and improved treatment strategies. At present, it is un­ clear whether CGM and AID restore aware­ ness of hypoglycemia or simply decrease the frequency of hypoglycemic episodes via alarms (45). Older People Older adults with type 1 diabetes, who are frequently affected by comorbidities, are at greater risk of severe hypoglycemia and its consequences, including changes in cognition, seizures, falls, and fractures (46). Use of diabetes technology by older people is growing, with a remarkable im­ pact on diabetes-related outcomes and quality of life. In particular, CGM has been shown to increase TIR, reduce time below range (TBR), and decrease the risks of hypoglycemia and hospitalization (46,47).
