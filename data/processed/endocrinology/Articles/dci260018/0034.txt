Access and Disparities As mentioned above, acquisition costs and prices of consumables for diabetes technology can be high, and there are costs associated with initiating and training people with diabetes on using diabetes technology and interpreting data. Unfortu­ nately, in many places, this has created a digital divide in knowledge and access to technology as well as in the availability of diabetes educators and training. This can in­ advertently worsen health care disparities and lead to a lower rate of use of diabetes technology in the people who need it most (e.g., in ethnic minority and other under­ served populations) (75–79).
