Reorganization of health care delivery may be required to ensure that people with diabetes re­ ceive proper education on new technolo­ gies and integrate them correctly into professional roles and use of remote and/or group education). For example, in Scotland, additional administrative staff have been secured from the National Health Service (NHS) so that health care professionals can use their time more efficiently in the roll­ out of the next stage of technology, and all users of conventional CSII have the chance to upgrade to AID systems (17). This is a good example of helping health care pro­ fessionals onboard individuals with diabe­ tes to diabetes technology. A growing body of evidence indicates that diabetes technology improves glycemic outcomes in type 1 diabetes, increasing the time in range (TIR) and the likelihood of meeting other clinical targets across all age groups and socioeconomic backgrounds (18,19).
