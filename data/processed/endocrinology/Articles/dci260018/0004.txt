Identifying the right device for an individual is likely to increase adherence, which in turn may translate into better glycemic manage­ ment and/or improved quality of life. In this way, the costs associated with device acquisition and use are more likely to be offset by the benefits associated with the prevention of acute and long-term diabe­ tes complications and/or the decreased burden of living with the condition. The choice of a (new) device normally requires face-to-face or telemedicine con­ tact. Interactions with health care profes­ sionals can be augmented with reputable online educational resources provided by diabetes charities, patient organizations, scientific bodies, and device manufacturers. as proficiency in the language in which the materials are provided, will affect the ability of people with diabetes to benefit from these resources. Increas­ ingly, materials can also be provided in the form of infographics and video con­ tent that has been codeveloped by peo­ ple with diabetes.
