Some health care professionals may perceive that people with diabetes dislike wearing devices, that diabetes technology may be too complicated for them or their families, or that technology will increase the time spent on diabetes management (116). There may also be implicit biases that re­ strain health care professionals from pro­ posing diabetes technology to people with diabetes who they may consider not to be “good” candidates, perhaps because of their age, low education level, or low in­ come. It is critical to understand the conse­ quences of such biases, whether implicit or explicit (117). The perception of an individual’s de­ vice readiness should be openly discussed with the individual to limit the risk of de­ nying access to technology (104,118). SAFETY Even with modern technology systems, errors can occur, for example, insulin pump failure, insulin precipitation and infusion set obstruction, problems at the infusion site, user error or a combination of these issues.
