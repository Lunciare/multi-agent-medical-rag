The automation behind AID systems lowers the level of understanding re­ quired to manage diabetes and optimize treatment; however, this only holds true if the technology always works perfectly. As this is not the case in daily life, people with diabetes need to have access to back-up strategies and be able to solve problems when specialist support is less available. For example, if there is inade­ quate understanding of a concept such as ketone testing, a reaction to a technical issue might not be sufficient and might result in further harm. However, previously, telehealth for minority or other populations was not uniformly re­ imbursed, frequently used, or easily ac­ cessed by individuals (123).
