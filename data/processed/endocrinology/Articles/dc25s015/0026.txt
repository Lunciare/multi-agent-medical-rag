Medical Nutrition Therapy In people with preexisting diabetes, gly- cemic goals are usually achieved through a combination of insulin administration and medical nutrition therapy. Because glycemic goals in pregnant individuals are stricter than in nonpregnant individuals, it is important that pregnant people with diabetes eat consistent amounts of car- bohydrates to match their insulin dosage and to avoid hyperglycemia or hypoglyce- mia. Referral to an RDN is important to establish a food plan and insulin-to- carbohydrate ratio and determine weight gain goals. The quality of the carbohy- drates should be evaluated. A subgroup analysis of the CONCEPTT study demon- strated that the diets of individuals plan- ning pregnancy and currently pregnant assessed during the run-in phase prior to randomization were characterized by high-fat, low-ﬁber, and poor-quality car- bohydrate intakes. Fruit and vegetable consumption was inadequate, with one in four participants at risk for micronutri- ent deﬁciencies, highlighting the impor- tance of medical nutrition therapy (61).
