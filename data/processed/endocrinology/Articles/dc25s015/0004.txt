Although observational studies are confounded by the association between elevated peri- conceptional A1C and other engagement in self-care behaviors, the quantity and consistency of data are convincing and support the recommendation to opti- mize glycemia prior to conception with an A1C <6.5% (<48 mmol/mol), as this is associated with the lowest risk of con- genital anomalies (given that organogenesis occurs primarily at 5–8 weeks of gestation), preeclampsia, and preterm birth (12–16). In a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, preconception care for pregnant individuals with preexist- ing diabetes was associated with lower A1C and reduced risks of birth defects, preterm delivery, perinatal mortality, small- for-gestational-age births, and neonatal in- tensive care unit admissions (17). To minimize the occurrence of compli- cations, beginning at the onset of puberty or at diagnosis, all adults and adolescents with diabetes of childbearing potential should receive education about 1) the risks of malformations associated with unplanned pregnancies, even with mild hyperglycemia, and 2) the use of effective contraception at all times when trying to prevent a pregnancy.
