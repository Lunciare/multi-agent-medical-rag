Nowadays most pheochromocytomas are found in the work up of adrenal incidentalomas (36, 39), and the proportion of pheochromocytomas that are found during yearly surveillance of a genetic syndrome increase. Phenoxybenzamine, a non-selective irreversible alpha-adrenoceptor antagonist, has been the standard preoperative treatment but its use has decreased in favor of selective reversible alpha-adrenoceptor antagonists such a doxazosin which are more readily available (40). Some patients use a calcium channel blocker instead or no medication at all preoperatively which is controversial, however, large RCTs are needed to tease out the best preoperative treatment. More and more genetic variants resulting in PPGLs are found increasing our understanding and changing our follow-up of these conditions (37). Metastatic PPGLs can appear many years after the initial surgery and curative therapy can then be difﬁcult to achieve (41). Primary aldosteronism Primary aldosteronism (PA) is severely underdiagnosed (42). It has been estimated that 4-14% of all patients with hypertension in primary care have it secondary to primary aldosteronism if properly investigated (43–45).
