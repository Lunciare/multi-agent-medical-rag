Congenital adrenal hyperplasia CAH is considered a rare group of disorders affecting the steroid synthesis of which 21-hydroxylase deﬁciency is by far the most frequent (11, 12). CAH is one of many genetic disorders of the adrenal glands causing PAI and the most common. The incidence of classic CAH is around 1/15000 according to neonatal screening programs (11). Non-classic CAH does not have apparent cortisol insufﬁciency but is usually diagnosed due to adrenal androgen excess or due to family screening. The prevalence of non-classic CAH in the general US Caucasian population has been claimed to be 1/200 (13), while in a country such as Sweden with around 10 million inhabitants only 90 cases of non-classic CAH have been diagnosed (14). However, it can be assumed that most patients with non-classic CAH probably never are diagnosed (15). The prevalence needs to be studied in larger cohorts which have been screened for non-classic CAH to be able to have more well-founded data.
