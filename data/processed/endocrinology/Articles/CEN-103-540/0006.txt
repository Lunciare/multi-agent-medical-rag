Small quantities of the active androgen tes- tosterone (T) can also be produced from A4 in the adrenal glands through the activity of aldoketoredutase type 1 C3 (AKR1C3) , but the bulk of circulating T in women is of ovarian origin (see below). DHEA and DHEAS are therefore considered biomarkers of adrenal androgen secretion, while A4 can originate from both ovaries and adrenals. Androgen synthesis in the theca cells of the ovarian follicular unit is under tonic stimulation by pituitary luteinising hormone (LH). Ovarian thecal cells can generate DHEA de novo from cholesterol and pregnenolone through the actions of cholesterol side‐chain cleavage enzyme and 17α‐hydroxylase, and thereafter it is converted to A4 and T . Venous sampling studies have confirmed significant gradients between ovarian vein and peripheral samples for both T and A4, even after the menopause, with a significantly greater relative production of T from the ovary .
