The prevalence of VOTs in post‐menopausal (PM) women with hyperandrogenism (HA) is around 2.7% . Approximately 5%–8% of ovarian tumours are hormonally active and 1% secrete androgens [59–61]. Androgen‐secreting tumours derive from sex‐cord and stromal cells of the ovary (Figure 2) . Sertoli‐Leydig Cell Tumours (SLCT) are the most common type in premenopausal women, whereas Leydig cell tumours (LCT) are the most common pathology after the menopause . Granulosa cell tumours (GCT) account for 70% of pure sex cord tumours and are the commonest cause of hyperoestrogenism . 3.4.1 | Pure Stromal Tumours 3.4.1.1 | Thecoma (Theca Cell Tumours). Only 10% of thecomas secrete androgens. They usually secrete oestrogens and, therefore, may present with abnormal menstrual bleeding. They are usually benign and occur in postmenopausal women . 3.4.1.2 | Sclerosing Stromal Tumours. These are rare (< 5%) and usually present in younger patients (< 30 years).
