In turn, cortisol deficiency leads to increased pituitary ACTH secretion and hyperplasia of the adrenal glands. Steroidogenic precursors proximal to the enzymatic defect accumulate and are shunted down the pathway of chemical androgen excess, often with overt virilisation. New- born screening data report the incidence of classical CAH at 1:10,000 to 1:20,000 live births depending on the population studied. The non‐classic form is milder due to relative preservation of cortisol and aldosterone production (25%–50% residual enzyme activity) , with varying degrees of postnatal androgen excess. The true prevalence of non‐ classic CAH is unclear; estimated at 1:200 to 1:1000 in the general Caucasian population , but up to 1:50 to 1:100 among populations with high rates of consanguinity .
