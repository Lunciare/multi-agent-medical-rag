They are mostly unilateral and benign, but 20% are associated with peritoneal metastases. They typically present in postmenopausal women; 75% are hormo- nally active with rapid virilisation in 30%. Reinke crystals are seen on histopathology. The non‐hilar subtype has distinct resemblance to a lipid cell luteoma of pregnancy . 3.4.1.4 | Steroid Cell Tumours (SCTs). These are seen in premenopausal women, and 50% are androgen‐producing. However, they can also produce other hormones. Reinke crys- tals are not seen on histopathology. 3.4.2 | Pure Sex Cord Tumours 3.4.2.1 | Granulosa Cell Tumours (GCT). These malig- nant tumours represent 5% of all malignant ovarian tumours. Adult GCTs (95% of GCT) occur mainly in postmenopausal women. Juvenile GCTs occur in children and women under 30 years of age. They are mainly oestrogen‐producing, but 10% secrete androgens; they frequently co‐secrete inhibin B and AMH. 3.4.2.2 | Sertoli Cell Tumours.
