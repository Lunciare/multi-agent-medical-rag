The imaging approach can vary depending on the level of suspicion for PCOS versus non‐PCOS pathology. When there is a high index of suspicion for PCOS, particularly in premenopausal women with irregular menstrual cycles and insidious onset of clinical features of hyperandrogenism, transvaginal ultrasound, if acceptable to the patient, is the most accurate as the first‐line radiological investigation. The transvaginal ultrasound should particularly be undertaken by a professional with expertise in ovarian ultrasonography. The purpose of this is to screen for polycystic ovarian morphology to support the suspected diagnosis, rather than to identify a suspected neoplasma. However, from a diagnostic perspective this may be unnecessary in those patients with typical features of androgen excess and oligomenorrhoea .
