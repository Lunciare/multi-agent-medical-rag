Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is the most prevalent endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age and is the main cause of anovulatory subfertility. In PCOS, the hypothalamic release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) rises, prompting the pituitary gland to preferentially secrete luteinizing hormone (LH), resulting in ovarian hyperandrogenism and decreased ovulation. As a negative feedback, hyperandrogenemia reduces the response of estradiol to the hypothalamus, thereby maintaining a persistent rise in gonadotropin-releasing hormone (3, 4).
