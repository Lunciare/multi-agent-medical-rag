Conse- quently, even if a wide separation in A1C could be safely obtained, it would take a long time for the CVD beneﬁt to accrue. However, meta-analysis of individual participant data from UKPDS, ACCORD, ADVANCE, and VADT demonstrated a signiﬁcant reduction in myocardial in- farctions and major CVD events but no difference in stroke, heart failure, or mortality between intensive and less in- tensive glycemic control (54). Longer-term epidemiological follow-up has been performed in these studies, and a clear pattern of CVD beneﬁt has emerged (55–57). In the post-DCCT follow-up of the EDIC cohort, participants previ- ously randomized to the intensive arm had a signiﬁcant 57% reduction in the risk of nonfatal myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular death com- pared with those previously random- ized to the standard arm (55). The beneﬁt of intensive glycemic control in this cohort with type 1 diabetes has been shown to persist for several deca- des (56) and to be associated with a modest reduction in all-cause mortal- ity (58).
