Time below range (<70 and <54 mg/dL [<3.9 and <3.0 mmol/L]) and time above range (>180 mg/dL [>10.0 mmol/L]) are useful parameters for insulin dose adjustments and reevaluation of the treatment plan. The international consensus on CGM provides guidance on CGM metrics (Table 6.2) and their clinical interpretation (31). To make these metrics actionable, stan- dardized reports with visual summaries, such as the ambulatory glucose proﬁle (Fig. 6.1), are recommended (31) and can help individuals with diabetes and health care professionals interpret the data to guide treatment decisions (26,29). BGM and CGM can be useful to guide medical nutrition therapy and physical activity, de- tect and prevent hypoglycemia, and aid medication management. CGM metrics, including TIR (with time below range and time above range), can provide helpful in- sights to inform a personalized diabetes management plan. Remote access to glu- cose data is growing and may help im- prove diabetes management (32–34). CGM systems have evolved rapidly in both accuracy and affordability.
