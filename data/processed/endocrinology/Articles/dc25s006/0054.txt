The diagnostic cri- teria for DKA and HHS are summarized in Table 6.8; all criteria must be met to establish these diagnoses. Importantly, approximately 10% of people experiencing DKA present with euglycemic DKA (plasma glucose <200 mg/dL [11.1 mmol/L]); therefore, DKA diagnosis requires either the presence of hyperglycemia or prior history of diabetes (151). Euglycemic DKA requires insulin deﬁciency and can be associated with a variety of factors including reduced food intake, pregnancy, alcohol use, liver failure, and/or SGLT2 inhibitor therapy (153). Additionally, DKA and HHS often pre- sent concurrently (154), though few studies have examined mixed DKA-HHS events. There has been a concerning rise in the rate of hyperglycemic crises in peo- ple with both type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes over the past decade (91,155– 161). Recent data suggest hyperglycemic crisis rates of up to 44.5–82.6 per 1,000 person-years among people with type 1 di- abetes (91,159) and up to 3.2 per 1,000 person-years among people with type 2 di- abetes (91).
