Person- centered integrated care approaches have been shown to improve both de- pression and glycemic outcomes (443). The behavioral health professional provid- ing treatment for depression should be in- corporated into or collaborate with the diabetes treatment team (443). Depres- sive symptoms may also be a manifesta- tion of reduced quality of life secondary to diabetes burden (also see DIABETES DIS- TRESS, above) (411). When depressive symptoms are identiﬁed, it is important to query origins and exacerbating factors, both diabetes-speciﬁc ones and those due to other life circumstances (408,444). Trials have shown consistent evidence of improvements in depressive symp- toms and variable beneﬁts for A1C when depression is treated simultaneously with diabetes (445), whether through phar- macologic treatment, group therapy, psychotherapy, parenting intervention, mindfulness-based approaches, or col- laborative care (6,390,446–449).
