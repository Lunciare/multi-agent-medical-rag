A study in adults with type 1 diabetes found a dose-response inverse relationship be- tween self-reported bouts of physical activ- ity per week and A1C, BMI, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes-related compli- cations such as hypoglycemia, DKA, retinop- athy, and microalbuminuria (281), and higher physical activity reduces mortality risk in people with type 1 diabetes (260). Over time, activities should progress in intensity, frequency, and/or duration to at least 150 min/week of moderate- intensity exercise. Adults able to run at 6 mph (9.7 km/h) for at least 25 min can beneﬁt sufﬁciently from shorter dura- tions of vigorous-intensity activity or inter- val training (75 min/week) (267). Many adults, including most with type 2 diabetes, may be unable or unwilling to participate in such intense exercise and should engage in moderate exercise for the recommended duration. Adults with diabetes are encouraged to engage in 2–3 sessions/week of resis- tance exercise on nonconsecutive days (282).
