Evidence of spe- ciﬁc increased risk of diabetic keto- acidosis associated with cannabis use has been reported in adults with type 1 diabetes (337–339). Diabetic ketoacido- sis in individuals with type 1 diabetes using cannabis is associated with canna- bis hyperemesis syndrome, which is marked by severe nausea, abdominal pain, and vomiting (337–339). Recom- mended diagnostic criteria for cannabis hyperemesis syndrome include a blood glucose of $250 mg/dL, an anion gap of >10, a serum b-hydroxybutyrate level of >0.6 mmol/L, a pH level of $7.4, and a bicarbonate level of $15 mmol/L (339). Health care professionals should con- sider cannabis hyperemesis syndrome in people with type 1 diabetes with pH $7.4 and bicarbonate >15 mmol/L in the presence of ketosis (339). Diabetes education programs offer po- tential to systematically reach and engage individuals with diabetes in smoking ces- sation efforts. A cluster randomized trial found statistically signiﬁcant increases in quit rates and long-term abstinence rates (>6 months) when smoking cessation in- terventions were offered through dia- betes education clinics, regardless of motivation to quit at baseline (340).
