About 4–7 mCi of I-131 were usually administered for hyperthyroidism, and 10–36 mCi were administered for thyroid cancer . After that, high-dose radioiodine was widely used to treat thyroid cancers. There were conflicts with the regulatory agency related to medical radioisotope use, but starting then, shielded isolation rooms for radioiodine therapy were installed . As Professor Mazzaferri demonstrated the beneficial effects of radioiodine therapy in differentiated thyroid can- cer in 1994 , radioiodine therapy was considered one of the main therapeutic tools for differentiated thyroid cancer. After the cloning of sodium/iodide symporter (NIS) in 1996 , the relationship between thyroid cancer and radioiodine was studied more in depth. Since the first use of radioiodine therapy for thyroid cancer in Korea , radioiodine therapy has been widely applied for managing patients with thyroid cancer.
