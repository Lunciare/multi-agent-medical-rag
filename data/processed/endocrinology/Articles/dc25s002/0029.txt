The rate of b-cell destruction is quite variable, being rapid in some individuals (particularly but not exclusively in infants and children) and slow in others (mainly but not exclusively adults) (44,58). Chil- dren and adolescents often present with DKA as the ﬁrst manifestation of the dis- ease, and rates in the U.S. have in- creased over the past 20 years (30–32). Others have modest fasting hyperglyce- mia that can rapidly change to severe hyperglycemia and/or DKA with infection or other stress. Adults may retain sufﬁ- cient b-cell function to prevent DKA for many years; such individuals may have remission characterized by decreased in- sulin needs for months or years, eventu- ally become dependent on insulin for survival, and are at risk for DKA (33–35, 59,60).
