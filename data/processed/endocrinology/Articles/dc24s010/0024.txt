The Control of Hyper- tension in Pregnancy Study (CHIPS) (46) enrolled mostly women with chronic hyper- tension. In CHIPS, targeting a diastolic blood pressure of 85 mmHg during pregnancy was associated with reduced likelihood of devel- oping accelerated maternal hypertension and no demonstrable adverse outcome for infants compared with targeting a higher di- astolic blood pressure. The mean systolic blood pressure achieved in the more inten- sively treated group was 133.1 ± 0.5 mmHg, and the mean diastolic blood pressure achieved in that group was 85.3 ± 0.3 mmHg. A similar approach is supported by the International Society for the Study of Hypertension in Pregnancy, which speciﬁ- cally recommends use of antihypertensive therapy to maintain systolic blood pres- sure between 110 and 140 mmHg and dia- stolic blood pressure between 80 and 85 mmHg (47).
