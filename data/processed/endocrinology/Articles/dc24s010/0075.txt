The primary safety outcome was major bleeding (i.e., intracranial hemor- rhage, sight-threatening bleeding in the eye, gastrointestinal bleeding, or other serious bleeding). During a mean follow- up of 7.4 years, there was a signiﬁcant 12% reduction in the primary efﬁcacy end point (8.5% vs. 9.6%; P = 0.01). In contrast, major bleeding was signiﬁcantly increased from 3.2 to 4.1% in the aspirin group (rate ratio 1.29; P = 0.003), with most of the excess being gastrointestinal bleeding and other extracranial bleeding. There were no signiﬁcant differences by sex, weight, or duration of diabetes or other baseline factors, including ASCVD risk score. Two other large, randomized trials of aspirin for primary prevention, in peo- ple without diabetes (ARRIVE [Aspirin to Reduce Risk of Initial Vascular Events]) (163) and in the elderly (ASPREE [Aspi- rin in Reducing Events in the Elderly]) (164), which included 11% with diabe- tes, found no beneﬁt of aspirin on the primary efﬁcacy end point and an in- creased risk of bleeding.
