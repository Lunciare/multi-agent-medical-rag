This study involved 14 patients, showing an odds ratio (OR) of 14% (95% CI, 2%-43%) at 27 weeks, based on the RECIST v1.1 criteria . Another phase 2 study, which included 39 patients with advanced ACC who had re­ ceived any prior line of treatment, showed an OR of 23% (95% CI, 11% to metastatic ACC) and an OS of 24.9 months . In these phase 2 studies, microsatellite instability high and TMB were not significantly associated with response to pem­ brolizumab (Table 3) . In the cohort of Raj et al , 7 of 9 patients who exhibited a significant response to pembrolizumab had MSS tumors. The median TMB was low and not associated with response to pembrolizumab, consistent with the present patient. These findings indicate that, in selected patients, the efficacy of pem­ brolizumab as a treatment option for ACC may be independ­ ent of microsatellite status and TMB.
