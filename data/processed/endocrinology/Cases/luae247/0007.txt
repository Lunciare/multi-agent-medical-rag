As glucose is being reduced in this pathway, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate hydrogen is also being oxidized to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate, and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide is reduced to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide hydrogen . Hyperglycemia in diabetes can lead to increased aldose reductase activation, creating high levels of sorbitol and thus an osmotic gradient . The gradient causes increased fluid accumulation and os­ motic damage in the lens of the eye. Separately, hyperglycemia leads to decreased nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phos­ phate hydrogen concentration, depleting the ability to create antioxidants and therefore leading to increased oxidative stress in the lens. This combination of osmotic damage and oxidative stress then leads to the collapse of fibers in the lens, creating a cataract (Fig. 2). It is possible that these diabetic cataracts could be revers­ ible. The pathophysiology is unclear, but it might be due to the dehydrated state in hyperglycemia that is then improved when euglycemia is achieved, or the lens could regain the abil­ ity to metabolize the accumulated sorbitol .
