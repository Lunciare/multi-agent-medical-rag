Discussion Cataracts in pediatric patients with diabetes are exceedingly rare. The prevalence of early diabetic cataracts (within first 12 months of diagnosis) in children and adolescents is be­ tween 0.7% and 3.4% and is more common in female patients . In these patients, there are snowflake deposits found in the cataract, and the cataracts are typically in bilateral eyes. In 1 comprehensive study, pediatric patients with early diabetic cataracts were found to have a higher hemoglobin A1c at diag­ nosis than those with late diabetic cataracts (after 12 months of diagnosis) . The formation of diabetic cataracts has been previously studied and hypothesized to be via the polyol pathway. The polyol pathway consists of the metabolism of glucose to fruc­ tose via sorbitol: glucose is converted to sorbitol via aldose re­ ductase and sorbitol is then converted to fructose via sorbitol dehydrogenase.
