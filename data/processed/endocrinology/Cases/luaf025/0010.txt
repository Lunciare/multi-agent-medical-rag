This anatomy, de­ scribed as a “vascular dam,” places the adrenal glands at heightened susceptibility to hemodynamic disturbances lead­ ing to platelet aggregation, microvascular thrombosis, poor perfusion, and bleeding with abrupt flow changes in the arter­ ial plexus. During situations of serious physiological stress, a marked ACTH surge leads to increased adrenal vascularity and blood flow while the catecholamine release induces venous vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation, thrombosis, and ische­ mia. In extreme cases, the venous stasis and increased adrenal venous pressure can result in rupture of the thin-walled adrenal venules . In our patient, septic shock with presumed ACTH and catecholamine surges, cytokine release, and the prothrombotic state of severe acute pancreatitis all likely con­ tributed to the development of ischemic adrenal injury. Characteristic imaging findings of acute adrenal ischemia observed in contrast-enhanced CT include adrenal hyper­ trophy, hypoenhancing areas, and surrounding inflammatory changes .
