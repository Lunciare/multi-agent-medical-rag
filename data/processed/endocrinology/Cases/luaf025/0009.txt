At 20 months after initial AI diagnosis, she re­ mains on daily replacement doses of hydrocortisone and fludro­ cortisone and has a plan for stress-dose glucocorticoids. Discussion We describe an 8-year-old girl who developed AI following an ischemic insult to the adrenal glands in the setting of acute se­ vere pancreatitis with septic shock, ARDS, acute renal failure, and coagulopathy. There have been few reports of adrenal hemorrhage in adult patients following acute pancreatitis [2-4], but none to our knowledge linking adrenal ischemia to AI. Adrenal ischemia has been reported in patients with anti­ phospholipid antibody syndrome, Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome, and Leiden V factor mutation . Adrenal ischemia and adrenal hemorrhage are known to occur together; how­ ever, despite serial images, our patient did not have any mass- like findings to suggest large adrenal hemorrhages . The adrenal glands have a distinctive vascular structure with a rich blood supply via 50 to 60 small arteries originating from the superior, middle, and inferior adrenal arteries, which form a plexus near the zona reticularis of the adrenal cortex, while venous drainage occurs through comparatively fewer venous channels draining into a single central vein.
