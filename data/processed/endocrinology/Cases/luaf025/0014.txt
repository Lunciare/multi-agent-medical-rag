Our patient had normal serum electrolytes including serum sodium, potas­ sium, and glucose after the development of biochemical evi­ dence of AI. We hypothesize that hyperpigmentation was absent given the abrupt development of AI. Early initiation of replacement therapy with hydrocortisone and fludrocorti­ sone prevented our patient from presenting with metabolic abnormalities and classic clinical features of AI. This is the first report of a pediatric patient with AI due to an ischemic adrenal injury in the setting of severe acute pan­ creatitis. Our findings highlight the importance of early recog­ nition of adrenal changes on CT images as an early sign of possible AI, the importance of prompt endocrine evaluation, and benefits of early initiation of corticosteroid and mineralo­ corticoid replacement therapy to promote illness recovery and avoid metabolic decompensation. Learning Points • Ischemic insult to the adrenal glands is an extremely rare cause of acquired primary AI. • Primary AI due to ischemic adrenal injury can develop in the setting of severe, acute pancreatitis with septic shock. • Timely clinical diagnosis corroborated by laboratory and image studies and early initiation of corticosteroid
