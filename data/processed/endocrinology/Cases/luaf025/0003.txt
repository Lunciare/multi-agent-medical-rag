Case Presentation An 8-year-old girl with acute pancreatitis was incidentally found to have enlarged adrenal glands on a computed tomog­ raphy (CT) scan of the abdomen (Fig. 1A). The patient pre­ She did not have fever, weight loss, or skin changes. Her past medical history included cerebral palsy following hypoxic is­ chemic encephalopathy at birth, seizure disorder, global development delay, and asthma. Endocrine history was not­ able for premature adrenarche that began with development of apocrine body odor at 4 years, pubic hair at 5 years, and ax­ illary hair at 8 years. The pubic and axillary hair growth had been progressive and was present at the time of hospitalization. Neither clinical nor biochemical evaluation of the premature adrenarche had been performed prior to this hospitalization. Six months prior to her current presentation with pancreatitis, she underwent general anesthesia for a right acetabular osteot­ omy and had an uneventful postoperative period. Medications prior to hospitalization included levetiracetam, baclofen, montelukast, and albuterol.
