Initial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) find­ ings (Fig. 1A) were consistent with hypophysitis; however, the presence of a suprasellar extension prompted further diagnostic evaluation. Digital perimetry revealed no visual field defects. Additional diagnoses were considered based on the initial MRI findings, particularly pituitary neuroendocrine tumor, lymphoma, and germinoma. However, following the partial response to oral glucocorticoid therapy and the persistence of the suprasellar extension, the focus shifted to infiltrative dif­ ferential diagnoses. Specifically, autoimmune and granuloma­ tous diseases, such as small-vessel vasculitis and other granulomatous disorders associated with hypopituitarism, were investigated. Some of these differential diagnoses are summarized in Table 2. In the absence of a definitive etiological diagnosis and given the progression of the suprasellar lesion with hypothalamic in­ volvement, there was a high suspicion of an infiltrative process. A stereotactically guided transcranial biopsy of the lesion was performed, revealing a chronic granulomatous inflammatory lesion. Histopathological findings included noncaseating granulomas positive for CD34, CD8, CD68, and glial fibrillary acidic protein, confirming a definitive diagnosis of NS.
