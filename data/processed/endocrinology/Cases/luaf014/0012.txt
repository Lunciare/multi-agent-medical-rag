Imaging studies such as Gallium-67 scintigraphy and fluo­ rodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (18FDG-PET/ CT) have been reported in some studies as potential diagnostic tools that can complement MRI findings . According to the consensus group of the NS Consortium, diagnosing NS is challenging, as histopathological confirm­ ation is not always feasible. Diagnoses are categorized as pos­ sible, probable, or definitive. A “possible” diagnosis is made when histopathological confirmation is unavailable; a “prob­ able” diagnosis is based on confirmation from a lesion consist­ ent with systemic sarcoidosis elsewhere; and a “definitive” diagnosis requires histopathological confirmation of NS. Definitive diagnoses are further classified as type “a” when there is evidence of extraneural sarcoidosis or type “b” for iso­ lated NS . In our patient, the diagnosis was particularly complex due to the absence of biochemical or systemic find­ ings suggestive of systemic sarcoidosis. A biopsy was ultimate­ ly necessary to establish an etiologic diagnosis, which confirmed NS as type “b” (isolated NS), with no evidence of extraneural involvement or systemic disease activity. Treatment strategies for NS are challenging. Glucocorticoids are typically used as the first-line therapy, as highlighted by Fritz and colleagues in a 2016 systematic review.
