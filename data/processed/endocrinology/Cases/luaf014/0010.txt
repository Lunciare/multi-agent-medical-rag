Granulomatous and inflammatory diseases of the pituitary should be considered in the differential diagnosis of sellar le­ sions. Inflammatory lesions typically do not present with hor­ mone hypersecretion (except for hyperprolactinemia due to pituitary stalk compression) but rather manifest with neuro­ logical symptoms and hypopituitarism resulting from the mass effect of the lesion. Hypopituitarism, in particular, can be highly suggestive, as the presence of arginine-vasopressin deficiency is often indicative of a nonadenomatous sellar lesion . The diagnosis of NS is often challenging due to the lack of specific biomarkers.
