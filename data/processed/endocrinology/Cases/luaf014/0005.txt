As the patient exhibited no obvious cutaneous manifesta­ tions or symptoms indicative of other organ involvement, fur­ ther imaging was performed to rule out systemic sarcoidosis. This included a chest computed tomography (CT) scan, cervical, mediastinal, and abdominopelvic CT scans with con­ trast, as well as ultrasound evaluations of the liver, spleen, and kidneys. We do not have gallium-67 scintigraphy availability. Treatment Treatment addressed both the hormonal deficiencies caused by hypopituitarism and the underlying NS. Hormonal re­ placement therapy included the following: • Arginine-vasopressin deficiency: desmopressin 120 mcg sublingually, 3 times daily. • Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism: ethinyl estradiol/ norelgestromin (6/0.6 mg) transdermally, applied weekly in a cyclic manner. • Central hypothyroidism: levothyroxine therapy was ad­ justed based on free thyroxine targets, reaching a final dose of 200 mcg daily. • Secondary adrenal insufficiency: following completion of high-dose glucocorticoid regimens, maintenance therapy with prednisone 5 mg daily was instituted. Initial treatment for NS involved high-dose glucocorticoids. The patient was started on oral prednisone 50 mg daily for 3 months, which resulted in improvement within the pituitary gland but not in the suprasellar lesion (Fig. 1B).
