The most frequent outcomes include incomplete remission (31%) or stable disease (24%) . These findings support the consideration of rituximab as a second-line or third-line therapy in patients with refractory NS, particularly when standard treatments fail to achieve remission or when con­ siderable treatment-related complications arise. Few studies have explored complications and mortality in this patient population. Leonhard et al , in a 2016 study of 52 patients, reported that 58% experienced complications attributed to either the disease or its treatment. The most com­ mon treatment-related complications were weight gain (50%) and diabetes (38%). Additional complications potentially linked to increased mortality included cardiovascular events (1 case), pulmonary embolism (4 cases), immunosuppression related to sarcoidosis (1 case), and infectious events (4 cases, including pneumonia, meningitis, and osteomyelitis). Similarly, few studies have explored the recovery of hormo­ nal deficiencies following treatment response.
