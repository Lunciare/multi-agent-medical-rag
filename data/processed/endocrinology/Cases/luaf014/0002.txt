Introduction Sarcoidosis is a systemic disease characterized by the forma­ tion of noncaseating granulomas in various organs, most com­ monly presenting between ages 20 and 40 years. The incidence varies worldwide but is estimated to range between 10 and 20 cases per 100 000 population. Sex distribution is variable, al­ though it appears to be slightly higher in women [1-5]. Neurosarcoidosis (NS) is a rare condition, affecting approxi­ mately 5% to 15% of patients with sarcoidosis. Involvement of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis can lead to neuroendocrine manifestations, including panhypopituitarism and arginine- vasopressin deficiency, which occur in approximately 2% of af­ fected individuals . Although its etiology remains unclear, several hypotheses sug­ gest a multifactorial origin involving immune-mediated mecha­ nisms, as well as environmental, genetic, and ethnicity-related factors. These elements may significantly influence both the pathogenesis and prognosis of the disease . We report the case of a patient with NS who exhibited mul­ tiple endocrine abnormalities from diagnosis through post treatment.
