The patient demonstrated a partial response to glucocorticoids and a successful response to rituximab. The most frequent manifestations of NS are neurological symptoms such as sensory disturbances, chronic aseptic men­ ingitis, cranial nerve palsy (most commonly involving the fa­ cial nerve), and spinal cord involvement. Neuroendocrine dysfunction is rare, with panhypopituitarism reported in only 6% of cases and arginine-vasopressin deficiency in ap­ proximately 2% . Ramos-Casals et al report only 3 pa­ tients with polyuria/polydipsia among 85 patients with NS. Unlike the typical presentation, our patient exhibited only neuroendocrine manifestations without neurosensory symptoms. A comprehensive work-up ruled out systemic sar­ coidosis and autoimmune diseases, supporting the diagnosis of isolated NS. Symptoms of hypothalamic involvement, such hyperphagia, diaphoresis, and thermal dysregulation, are rarely reported in NS. Blazin and colleagues , in a narrative review focused on endocrinological alterations, report only 3 cases that re­ ported hyperphagia during a period from 2002 to 2014. In our case, hyperphagia contributed to considerable weight gain and metabolic alterations, likely exacerbated by high-dose glucocorticoid therapy.
