Subsequently, bone repair processes are activated, with differentiation of mesenchymal cells into osteoblasts to build new bone and hematopoietic stem cells into osteoclasts to re­ move necrotic tissue. However, because of impaired bone re­ sorption and formation, subchondral fractures eventually occur . Exogenous glucocorticoid treatment is 1 of the most common causes of AVN and may account for up to 38% of atraumatic AVN and is dose dependent . Glucocorticoid treatment is theorized to cause AVN through increased systemic lipids, leading to compromised perfusion to the femoral head re­ sulting from fat emboli or external lipocyte compression, as well as alterations in the inflammatory cytokines resulting in osteo­ clast activation and osteoblast apoptosis . Compared to ex­ ogenous glucocorticoid treatment, AVN caused by endogenous hypercortisolism is not frequently reported nor is it screened for on diagnosis of CS. We describe a patient who presented with bilateral hip AVN in the context of florid CD.
