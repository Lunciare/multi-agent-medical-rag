As the patient developed nausea, lack of appetite, and mal­ aise with decreasing cortisol levels, osilodrostat was reduced to 1 mg daily, and he was started on hydrocortisone replacement therapy on week 11 postoperatively (Table 3). Ultimately, both osilodrostat and hydrocortisone were discontinued following normalization of cortisol levels. Regarding the rest of the hormonal deficiencies, his total testosterone and IGF-1 levels improved to levels of 483 ng/dL (16.76 nmol/L) and 99 (12.97 nmol/L), respectively, and he did not require hormone replacement therapy. Clinically, the patient improved with resolution of his hypertension and diabetes and achieved a 38-lb weight loss. Additionally, his diplopia improved and his hip pain resolved without any restriction in mobility. However, 1 year postoperatively, the patient developed bilat­ eral shoulder pain.
