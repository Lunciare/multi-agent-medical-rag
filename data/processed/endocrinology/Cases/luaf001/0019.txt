As the disease progresses, structural changes, including cystic and sclerotic changes (stage 2), subchondral collapse (stage 3), flattening of the femoral head (stage 4), joint narrowing and acetabular changes (stage 5), and, finally, advanced degenerative changes (stage 6) can be detected on most imaging modalities. Management of early stages of AVN includes observation or conservative weight-bearing management, medical therapy with bisphosphonates, anticoagulation therapy, statins, and vasodilators. Invasive procedures such as mesenchymal stem cells implantation, osteotomy, surgical joint decompression, and total hip replacement are reserved for more advanced stages . Indeed, AVN accounts for approximately 10% of total hip replacements in the United States . Staging has prognostic implications for treatment options and disease outcomes. Early-stage disease, when diagnosed and treated, can often regress, and be cured. Conservative measures, med­ ical treatment, biophysical stimulation, extracorporeal shock­ wave therapy, or core decompression, can prevent femoral head collapse and further hip arthroplasty. On the other hand, late-stage disease, characterized by joint collapse, is ir­ reversible and often requires joint replacement .
