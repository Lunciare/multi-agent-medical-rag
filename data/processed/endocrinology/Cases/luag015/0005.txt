Further imaging with a Gallium-68 DOTA-(Tyr³)-octreotate positron emission tomography (68Ga DOTATATE PET) scan revealed one small focus of tracer uptake that appeared to involve the prox­ imal tail of the pancreas, along with another small focus of trac­ er uptake, potentially secondary to a small peripancreatic lymph node, with no abnormal uptake in the liver to suggest liv­ er metastases (Fig. 2). Diagnostic assessment The patient has a biochemical diagnosis of pHPT, with a baseline PTH of 74.8 pg/mL (7.9 pmol/L) and serum calcium of 10.5 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L). A 24-hour urinary calcium of 219.2 mg/24 hours (5.5 mmol/24 hours) effectively excludes familial hypocalciuric hy­ percalcemia, while a vitamin D level of 44 ng/mL (110 nmol/L) and preserved renal function (creatinine 0.7 mg/dL [62 µmol/L]) exclude secondary hyperparathyroidism. Imaging identified 2 parathyroid adenomas. Additionally, abdominal MRI revealed a pancreatic le­ sion, confirmed by fine-needle aspiration (FNA) as a well- differentiated pNET. The MEN panel identified a heterozygous VUS: MEN1 (NM_130799.2): c.652C>T (p.Arg218Trp). These findings collectively support the diagnosis of MEN1-related multigland endo­ crine involvement.
