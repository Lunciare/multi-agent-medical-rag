Neck ultra­ sonography (US) revealed 2 hypoechoic lesions, measuring 16 mm on the right and 12 mm in the left, compatible with parathyroid ad­ enomas (Fig. 1). Given her young age, family history, and bilateral adenomas on imaging, germline genetic testing through a commer­ cial laboratory was pursued. The multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) panel identified her to have a heterozygous variant in the MEN1 gene (NM_130799.2): c.652C>T (p.Arg218Trp). This variant had conflicting interpretations among commercial genetic testing laboratories in ClinVar, classified as VUS by 3 other laboratories at the time and as pathogenic by 1 laboratory. Given the high clinical suspicion for this variant to be indeed pathogenic, further evalu­ ation was pursued. Pituitary magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) re­ vealed a 3-mm cyst, and anterior pituitary hormonal evaluation was within normal limits (Table 1). An abdominal MRI was notable for a 1-cm low gradient echo, T2 hyperintense, progressively peripherally enhancing lesion within the anterior superior pancreatic body.
