Current guidelines recommend genetic evalu­ ation in patients with pHPT diagnosed before age 30, multigland disease, or a suggestive family history . Menin is a tumor suppressor regulating transcription, genome stability, and cell proliferation. Tumorigenesis typically follows a “2-hit” model, with a germline pathogenic variant and somatic loss of the wild-type allele. De novo MEN1 variants account for ∼10% of cases, and pathogenic variants are not identified in 5-25% of clinically diagnosed patients . Such cases may re­ present phenocopies, partial deletions, or noncoding mutations . Population-level studies suggest evolutionary constraint within the MEN1 coding region . American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines classify genet­ ic variants as benign, likely benign, pathogenic, or likely patho­ genic, but some variants—especially rare missense changes— remain as “VUS” due to limited or conflicting evidence. Incorporating patient phenotype can improve interpretation, and MEN1-specific modifications to ACMG criteria have been pro­ posed to enhance accuracy [11-14]. and genetic criteria.
