In conclusion, this case highlights the importance of integrat­ ing clinical phenotype when interpreting VUS results in MEN1. Clinical evidence can support reclassification to likely pathogen­ ic, reducing uncertainty in genetic counseling and guiding man­ agement. The case also emphasizes the value of genetic testing in young patients with multiple endocrine tumors and relevant family history. Further studies on genotype–phenotype correla­ tions and the functional impact of MEN1 variants in MEN1-associated endocrine tumors will be essential to improve risk stratification and management strategies. Learning points • Young patients with pHPT and a family history of pHPT should be offered genetic testing to evaluate for hereditary syndromes such as MEN1. • pHPT is the most common and often the earliest manifest­ ation of MEN1 syndrome.
