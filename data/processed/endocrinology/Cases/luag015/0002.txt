type 1, c.652C>T (p.Arg218Trp), pathogenic variant, primary hyperparathyroidism Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) is a rare endocrine tumor predisposition syndrome caused by pathogenic variants in the MEN1 gene on chromosome 11q13 . The MEN1 gene enc­ odes menin, a tumor suppressor protein that plays a crucial role in gene transcription regulation. With penetrance exceeding 95%, nearly all carriers develop tumors by age 50. In patients with primary hyperparathyroidism (pHPT), the most prevalent hereditary syndrome is MEN1 . A clinical diagnosis of MEN1 syndrome can be established in patients who exhibit at least 2 out of the 3 characteristic tumor types—parathyroid, pituitary, or gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors—or in individ­ uals with one of these tumors and a first-degree relative diag­ nosed with MEN1. Alternatively, diagnosis may be confirmed by identifying a germline MEN1 pathogenic variant . More than 1500 MEN1 pathogenic variants have been reported, most result­ ing in protein truncation, though no clear genotype–phenotype correlation has been identified .
