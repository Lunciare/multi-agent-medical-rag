Advances in imaging have in­ creased detection of nonfunctional pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (NF-PanNETs), which now comprise most cases; gastrino­ mas account for ∼30% and insulinomas for 10-15% . Surgery is recommended for NF-PanNETs larger than 2 cm or those demonstrating progression . In our patient, a 1-cm well- differentiated PanNET was identified and confirmed by FNA. Given its size, lack of metastases, and absence of hormonal activity, con­ servative management with surveillance was appropriate. MEN1-related pituitary adenomas (PAs) exhibit a slight pre­ dominance among females, and the average patient’s age at diagnosis falls within the fourth decade. The lifetime incidence of PAs in patients with MEN1 syndrome is ∼50%. The most com­ mon type of PAs among patients with MEN1 syndrome is prolac­ tinomas, accounting for 28-45% of cases, followed closely by nonfunctioning PAs . In this case, pituitary MRI revealed a small cyst with normal hormone levels. The significance of pi­ tuitary cysts in MEN1 is unclear, and given the absence of func­ tional abnormalities, surveillance was chosen.
