Discussion In this case report, we describe MEN1 syndrome diagnosed clin­ ically and supported by genetic testing in the patient and her mother, demonstrating familial segregation of the identified vari­ ant in 2 affected individuals. Our findings provide additional evi­ dence to support reclassification of this variant from a VUS to likely pathogenic, as conflicting interpretations of its pathogen­ icity currently exist. Although strong genotype–phenotype corre­ lations are generally limited in MEN1 syndrome, characterizing the type of MEN1 variant may provide insight into its functional impact and clinical consequences. The patient’s variant (c.652C>T, p.Arg218Trp) is a missense change in exon 3 of the MEN1 gene, reported to result in aberrant splicing and an absent or altered protein. Primary hyperparathyroidism is often earliest manifestation of MEN1, though symptomatic disease typically emerges in the third decade of life. MEN1-associated pHPT is usually multigland­ ular, with asymmetric and asynchronous gland involvement . Serum calcium and PTH elevations are often milder than in spor­ adic pHPT .
