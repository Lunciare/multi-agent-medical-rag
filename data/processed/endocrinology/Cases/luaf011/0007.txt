There have been similar case reports in the literature illus­ trating hypercalcemia with inappropriately normal 1,25(OH)2D in the setting of granuloma-forming disorders [10-12]. Proposed contributing factors for normal 1,25(OH)2D concentrations may include increased oral cal­ cium intake, dehydration, and decreased calcium excretion, particularly in renal insufficiency , as seen in our patient with a baseline serum creatinine of 247 μmol/L (2.79 mg/dL). Our patient’s low 24-hour urinary calcium excretion should be interpreted in the context of her impaired renal function and reduced glomerular filtration . A normal urinary calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio of 0.23 (>0.02), along hypocalciuric hypercalcemia. The clinical utility of serum ACE activity in sarcoidosis is also limited because of poor sensitivity and specificity, with high ACE activity occurring in up to 75% of cases . In addition, in our case, the patient was on an ACE inhibitor, which invalidated the ACE activity measurement. In such scenarios, measurement of the mass concentration of ACE is considered more useful.
