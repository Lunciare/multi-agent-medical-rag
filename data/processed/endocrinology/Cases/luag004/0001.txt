Unilateral Graves disease with Takayasu arteritis Key Words vasculitis, thyrotoxicosis, scintigraphy A 33-year-old woman with Takayasu arteritis on 5 mg of pred­ nisolone daily presented with a 1-year history of losing 6 kg and experiencing palpitations. She had a diffuse goiter, tremors, and no ophthalmic findings. Thyroid profile showed thyrotropin <0.001 µIU/mL (SI: <0.001 mIU/L) (reference range, 0.4-4.5 µIU/mL [SI: 0.4-4.5 mIU/L]), total T4 18.3 µg/dL (SI: 235.5 nmol/L) (reference range, 4.2-11.5 µg/dL [SI: 54-148 nmol/L]), total T3 288 ng/dL (SI: 4.4 nmol/L) (reference range, 75-210 ng/dL [SI: 1.1-3.2 nmol/L]), and thyrotropin re­ ceptor antibody 5.6 IU/L (reference range, <1.75 IU/L). Pertechnate scan showed increased uptake in the left gland, disproportionate to the right (Fig. 1; panel A), indicating uni­ lateral Graves disease. A previous computed tomography aor­ togram revealed near-total occlusion of the right subclavian artery with narrowing of the left subclavian artery (panel B). She was treated with 30 mg of carbimazole daily, and her symptoms improved after 3 months, with a weight gain of 3 kg. Unilateral Graves disease could result from hemiagene­ sis, differential expression of sodium-iodide symporters, or a history of unilateral thyroiditis .
