Outcome and Follow-up Five months after prednisone treatment initiation, repeat MRI demonstrated that the size of the pituitary gland had regressed back to its normal size (Fig. 3) with the patient noting signifi­ cant improvement on the frequency and severity of her head­ aches and joint pains 1 month after prednisone treatment initiation. The sequence of clinical events is displayed in Fig. 4. Discussion Pituitary involvement in MPA is an exceedingly rare manifest­ ation of this autoimmune vasculitis . Although its preva­ lence in MPA is undocumented, studies on GPA—a closely related condition—report pituitary dysfunction in approxi­ mately 1% of cases In patients with GPA and pituitary in­ volvement, common presentations include symptoms from mass effect and polyuria and polydipsia due to arginine vaso­ pressin deficiency . Despite treatment with glucocorticoids and immunosuppressive agents such as cyclophosphamide, rituximab, and methotrexate, pituitary dysfunction often persists, necessitating long-term hormone replacement therapy . Conversely, the diagnosis of pituitary enlargement in our pa­ tient was made incidentally during MRI imaging for headache workup, which initially presented as sinus/facial discomfort.
