Isolated Adrenocorticotropin Deficiency With Depressive
KEYWORDS: iad, symptoms, cortisol, acth, hormone, patients, case, nmol, patient, mmol, adrenal, reference, diagnosis, insufficiency, hospitalization

Symptoms such as anorexia, nausea, fatigue, and depression are easily misinterpreted as primary psychiatric or gastrointes­ tinal disorders . In a systematic review, apathy (38%) was the most frequently reported symptom, followed by weight loss (25%), anorexia (22%), nausea or vomiting (8%), and cog­ nitive impairment (8%) . The present case followed a similar course, beginning with such nonspecific symptoms that eventu­ ally progressed to life-threatening adrenal insufficiency. This case had several notable features. First, the patient ini­ tially developed depressive symptoms, which progressed to gastrointestinal complaints and, within a few weeks, culmi­ nated in severe adrenal insufficiency. Second, despite the ab­ sence of overt metabolic derangements such as hyponatremia or hypoglycemia and no evidence of structural brain lesions, the patient developed a marked disturbance of consciousness (unresponsiveness). Most previously reported cases have de­ scribed either psychiatric or somatic symptoms alone, and only a small number of reports have documented severe multi­ systemic manifestations. Third, this case represented idiopathic IAD, without typical risk factors such as chronic glucocorticoid use, pituitary irradiation, or immune checkpoint inhibitor ther­ apy. These findings highlight the importance of considering IAD in the differential diagnosis of rapidly progressive, multi­ system symptoms, particularly in older patients .
