They are usually discovered at a more advanced stage with distant metastases, leading to poor treat­ ment outcomes and a shorter life expectancy [4-6]. ACC, a sporadic and highly aggressive malignant tumor, is relatively rare in MEN1, with a reported incidence of 0.5 to 2 cases per million populations annually. Patients typically have a poor prognosis, with a 5-year overall survival rate of only 16% to 44% . ACC can occur at all ages, but the mean age of diagnosis in MEN1 is younger than that of spor­ adic ACC. Overall, approximately 10% of ACC cases occur in patients with a genetic predisposition, so it is recommended that all ACC patients undergo clinical genetic testing.
