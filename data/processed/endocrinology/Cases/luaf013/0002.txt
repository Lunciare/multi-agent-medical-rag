The simultaneous presence of a viable fetus and molar tissue, which is characterized by abnormal trophoblastic proliferation, can lead to severe complications, including hyperemesis gravidarum, preeclampsia, hyperthyroidism, and the potential for malignant transformation . Fetal complications include abortion, growth restriction, and pre­ term labor . Monitoring the fetus and deciding about pregnancy continuation, particularly in cases with metasta­ sis, are critical. Hyperthyroidism can be a life-threatening complication as­ sociated with molar pregnancies as it can progress to severe thyrotoxicosis and even thyroid storm . It arises due to the excessive production of β human chorionic gonadotropin (β- hCG) by the molar tissue, which can mimic TSH and over­ stimulate the thyroid gland . This leads to elevated thyroid dia, weight loss, and persistent nausea . Effective management of hyperthyroidism in a twin molar pregnancy involves a multidisciplinary approach, including obstetricians, endocrinologists, and oncologists. We present a 34-year-old pregnant woman with a hydatidiform mole and a coexisting live fetus with comorbid hyperthyroidism.
