Regular ultra­ sounds are essential to monitor the growth of the normal fe­ tus, the size of the molar pregnancy, and any complications such as theca lutein cysts or excessive amniotic fluid . Decision-making about continuing the pregnancy depends on fetal viability, the absence of severe complications like pre­ eclampsia or hyperthyroidism, and the ability to provide com­ prehensive monitoring . If there is evidence of metastasis, of the pregnancy is often recommended . Postevacuation management includes chemotherapy for GTN, along with regular β-hCG surveillance to ensure no recurrence . In the case of our patient, hyperthyroidism was managed conservatively due to the patient’s mild symptoms. Her lack of significant medical history, including the absence of cardiac disease, supported the decision for conservative management. Healthy patients without significant cardiac disease can with­ stand conservative management of mild hyperthyroidism. Additionally, the decision for conservative management was supported by the improving levels of free T4 and free T3, along with a corresponding decline in the serum β-hCG levels.
