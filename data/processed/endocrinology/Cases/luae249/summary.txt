Postprandial Hypoglycemia in a Patient With Clinical Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome Abstract A male neonate exhibited hallmark features of Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS) including large for gestational age, macroglossia, multiple ear pits, and umbilical hernia. He had neonatal hypoglycemia, requiring a glucose infusion rate of 9.7 mg/kg/min. Over time, he demonstrated persistent hypoglycemia with point-of-care glucose <60 mg/dL (<3.3 mmol/L) (70-140 mg/dL, 3.9-7.8 mmol/L) prompting a critical sample. A diagnostic fast of 13 hours revealed no hypoglycemia <50 mg/dL. However, he was found to have postprandial hypoglycemia after 2 hours to 58 mg/dL (3.2 mmol/L) (70-140 mg/dL, 3.9-7.8 mmol/L) with low β-hydroxybutyrate of <1.8 mg/dL (<0.17 mmol/L) (>3.6 mg/dL, >1.8 mmol/L) and increased insulin 3.9 μIU/mL (27 pmol/L) (2-13 μIU/mL; 14-90 pmol/L). Low-dose diazoxide (6 mg/kg/day) and chlorothiazide (10 mg/kg/day) were initiated.
