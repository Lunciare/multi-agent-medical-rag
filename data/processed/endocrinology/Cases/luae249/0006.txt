After receiving clearance from cardiology, diazoxide 6 mg/kg/day divided every 8 hours was initiated along with chlorothiazide 10 mg/kg/day divided every 12 hours. The pa­ tient remained admitted for further glucose monitoring and diazoxide dose adjustment for postprandial hypoglycemia. After 1 day of diazoxide treatment, postprandial glucose lev­ els, (POC and whole blood i-STAT) remained in the normal range for 24 hours (Table 3) on both nonfortified breast milk and formula feeds. Given glucose stability for 48 hours on diazoxide, a 19-hour safety fast while on diazoxide was performed, during which all POC blood glucose values re­ mained greater than 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L). The patient also demonstrated the ability to produce β-hydroxybutyrate at 13 and 19 hours (4.9 mg/dL and 15.9 mg/dL, 0.83 mmol/L and 0.91 mmol/L) (Tables 4 and 5). Outcome and Follow-up The parents continued to monitor glucose levels 2 to 3 hours after each feed at home. One week after discharge, POC blood glucose levels remained stable between 80 to 120 mg/dL (4.4-6.7 mmol/L), without further episodes of hypoglycemia.
