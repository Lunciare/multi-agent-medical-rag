Key Words: hyperinsulinism, postprandial hypoglycemia, diazoxide, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome Introduction Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS) is a congenital over­ growth syndrome, mostly diagnosed in the neonatal period with a current estimated prevalence of 1:10 000 births . The incidence of hypoglycemia in BWS is about 50%. Most in­ fants with hypoglycemia will be asymptomatic and have reso­ lution of hypoglycemia within the first 3 days of life. Less than 5% will have hypoglycemia beyond the neonatal period re­ quiring diazoxide or octreotide treatment, continuous feeding, or even a partial pancreatectomy . Although hypoglycemia is a cardinal feature of BWS, diagnosis and management of hypoglycemia remain unclear in this patient population. Here we present a 2-month-old male with clinical features of BWS, born to a mother with clinically confirmed BWS, who presented with postprandial hypoglycemia managed with diazoxide. Our case highlights the broad spectrum of BWS, the heterogeneity of hypoglycemia presenting as either fasting or postprandial, and the challenges in diagnosing mild hyperinsulinism.
