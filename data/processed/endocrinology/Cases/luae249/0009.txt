Despite recent advances in knowledge, diag­ nosis can be delayed and elusive due to a lack of diagnostic criteria and variability in presentation. Hyperinsulinism, a cardinal feature of BWS, is defined as hypoglycemia requiring an infusion rate of >8 mg/kg/min, a detectable level of insulin and/or C-peptide, an undetectable level of ketones and low free fatty acids, and preservation of a large glycemic response to glucagon . This patient developed hypoglycemia immediately after birth, which required an increased GIR of 9.7 mg/kg/min. Preprandial glucose checks at home demonstrated glucose lev­ els in the 60 seconds mg/dL (3.3 mmol/L), raising concern for persistent hypoglycemia. The patient was admitted to our fa­ cility for a fasting hypoglycemia challenge. He had 1 postpran­ dial episode of hypoglycemia to 58 mg/dL (3.2 mmol/L) on a POC glucometer and whole blood i-STAT at 3.5 hours after the fast was started. The remainder of glucose levels during the diagnostic fast were >60 mg/dL (>3.3 mmol/L).
