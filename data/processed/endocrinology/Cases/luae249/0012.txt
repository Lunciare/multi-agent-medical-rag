The entry of glucose into the beta cell leads to a rise in the ATP/ADP ratio, closing of the KATP channel, and depolarization of the membrane, resulting in insulin release. Variants in either SUR1 or KIR6.2 genes lead to uncontrolled persistent insulin secretion by pancreatic beta cells and subsequent fasting hyperinsulinemic hypogly­ cemia. Oral diazoxide remains the first-line pharmacologic treatment in persistent hyperinsulinism. Diazoxide inhibits in­ sulin secretion by activating (opening) SUR1. However, neo­ natal hyperinsulinism can present as diazoxide-responsive or unresponsive . In this patient, hypoglycemia occurred only in the postpran­ dial setting, suggesting that the mechanism of insulin secretion is not always sustained. The rapid response to diazoxide, rep­ resented by immediate normoglycemia less than 24 hours after initiation of treatment, suggests that his genetic mutation may not be related to KATP channel. Currently, this patient is treated with diazoxide and chlorothiazide, and blood glucose levels have remained in the normal range.
