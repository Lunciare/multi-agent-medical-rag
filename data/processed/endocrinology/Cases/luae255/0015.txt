Case Report
KEYWORDS: cortisol, osilodrostat, severe, pmol, nmol, acth, hour, mmol, adrenal, cushing, syndrome, levels, eas, treatment, cushing syndrome

Hypocalcemia is very rare in severe hypercortisolism, which can be due to accelerated renal calcium loss, and the most effective treat­ ment appears to be effective reduction of cortisol levels. Glucocorticoid treatment was initiated when glucocorticoid withdrawal symptoms developed but continued throughout her treatment course as part of a block-and-replace regimen given the incurable nature of the underlying disease that causes Cushing syndrome. Whether and when to initiate PCP prophylaxis is still controversial. Bilateral adrenal arter­ ial embolization was attempted in our case and its utility in treating severe hypercortisolism remains unclear. Learning Points • Severe EAS can be of sudden onset and rapid progression and in rare cases can present with hypocalcemia. • The adrenal steroidogenesis inhibitor osilodrostat can be successfully utilized to reduce cortisol levels in severe EAS as part of the block-and-replace approach. • Hypocalcemia in EAS can be best managed with treat­ ment of hypercortisolism. • Adrenal embolization may be a feasible alternative thera­ peutic option for definitive management in the context of unfeasible BAL for EAS.
