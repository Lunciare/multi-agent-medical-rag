Etomidate inhibits cortisol production and can be used intra­ venously in the intensive care setting to gain rapid control of hypercortisolism . Metyrapone and osilodrostat have rapid onset of action and have the potential to quickly reduce cortisol production . Osilodrostat inhibits 11-beta hydroxylase, and its efficacy and safety has been demonstrated in the treatment of Cushing disease . Efficacy and safety of osilodrostat has been described in the treatment of non-Cushing disease [1, 6, 9, 10, 20]. In our case, high-dose osilodrostat resulted in rapid im­ provement in hypercortisolism and associated complications. Attention needs to be paid to avoid potential side effects of osi­ lodrostat, such as adrenal insufficiency and long QTc syndrome. Before initiation of osilodrostat, correction of potassium and magnesium deficiency and completion of baseline EKG to en­ sure normal QTc interval are necessary. During treatment, ac­ tive monitoring of electrolytes and EKG are critical to ensure continued safety of the medication. We utilized a block-and-replace approach with close bio­ chemical and clinical monitoring in the inpatient setting to aim for more prompt control of the life-threatening hypercor­ tisolism .
