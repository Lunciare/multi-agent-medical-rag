Her 24-hour urine calcium was inappropriately elevated, consistent with accel­ erated renal calcium loss . Our patient’s severe hypocal­ cemia was initially refractory to aggressive calcium repletion but only responded well with reduction of cortisol, highlight­ ing importance of controlling hypercortisolemia in managing hypocalcemia in EAS. Hypertension and hypokalemia can be seen in severe Cushing syndrome due to inadequate deactivation of cortisol by 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase 2 and resultant ac­ tivation of mineralocorticoid receptor by cortisol .
