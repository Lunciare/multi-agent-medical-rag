Unfortunately, despite fluctuation of her ACTH levels, her disease has been progress­ ing and has involved many organ systems, including liver, lung, peritoneum, lymph nodes, bones, breast, and body walls. Discussion Severe EAS from metastatic NET is rare. In this case report, our patient had rapidly progressive hypercortisolism complicated by new onset hypocalcemia, hyperglycemia, and hypokalemia. In our case, the abrupt onset of her hypercortisolism was postulated to be due to sudden release of large amount of ACTH from chemotherapy-induced tumor lysis, which was evidenced by her extremely high level of ACTH at presenta­ tion but decreased levels over time . Severe hypocalcemia is rare in patients with hypercortiso­ lism. Cortisol inhibits 25-hydroxy vitamin D activation, increases calcium release from the bone, and reduces intes­ tinal absorption and renal reabsorption of calcium [7, 11, 12].
