The initial high dose of osilodrostat resulted in rapid decline of the cortisol levels along with improvement of symptoms and comorbidities. Hydrocortisone therapy was initiated at the onset of glucocorticoid withdrawal symp­ toms. Interestingly, over time, her requirement for osilodro­ stat gradually decreased, which may be due to dwindling ACTH production from her metastatic NET after initial tu­ mor lysis, and reduced cortisol producing capacity due to uni­ lateral adrenal embolization . Despite recent reports that adrenal steroidogenesis may be suppressed long after discon­ tinuation of osilodrostat , we chose to continue the block-and-replace approach due to her variable tumor burden as a result of uncontrolled NET and fluctuating ACTH levels. For patients with severe hypercortisolism, there is risk for opportunistic infections including Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP). In a case series of 53 patients with Cushing syndrome in which standard PCP chemoprophylaxis was not prescribed, 5 patients developed PCP after initiation of cortisol-lowering therapy .
