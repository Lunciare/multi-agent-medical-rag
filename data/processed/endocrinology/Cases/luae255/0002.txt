Introduction Cushing syndrome due to ectopic adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) secretion (EAS) is rare, accounting for 5% to 10% of endogenous Cushing [1-3]. It can be indolent, but in severe manifestations may include new onset or rapid worsening of hyperglycemia, hypokalemia, and hypertension, and catabolic symptoms like muscle loss, but may not include typical symp­ toms or signs of Cushing syndrome such as dysregulated fat de­ position . Hypocalcemia is extremely rare in patients with EAS but has been seen in severe Cushing syndrome mainly due to impaired renal reabsorption of calcium . Severe EAS can progress rapidly and be life-threatening. Therefore, prompt con­ trol of hypercortisolism is the key in management of these cases. Osilodrostat, an 11-beta hydroxylase inhibitor, is approved to treat adult patients with Cushing disease who are not eligible for surgery or not cured by surgery and has been reported in treatment of EAS .This case report describes the use of osilodrostat in a block-and-replace approach and explor­ ation of adrenal artery embolization in the management of severe EAS due to a metastatic neuroendocrine tumor (NET).
