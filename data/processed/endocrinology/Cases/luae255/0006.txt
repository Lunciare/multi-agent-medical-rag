During hospitalization, white blood counts and symptoms of potential infection were actively monitored. Her profound hypocalcemia was initially refractory to aggressive oral and intravenous calcium repletion. Only after improvement of her severe hypercortisolemia did her hypo­ calcemia eventually improve (Fig. 2). She also developed severe hyperglycemia with fasting blood glucose levels of 200-300 mg/dL (11.1-16.7 mmol/L) (normal reference range, 65-100 mg/dL; 3.6-5.6 mmol/L), requiring basal and bolus insulin treatment. Mechanical thrombosis prophylaxis was used due to her severe thrombocytopenia (platelet count < 20 000 per microliter of blood), precluding pharmaco­ logical prophylaxis. Because of her severe hypercortisolism, bilateral adrenalec­ tomy (BAL) was considered. Unfortunately, she was deemed un­ feasible for BAL given high risk of morbidity and mortality from her extensive history of abdominal surgeries, wide peritoneal metastases, and severe thrombocytopenia. Bilateral adrenal em­ bolization was attempted but was ultimately converted to right- only adrenal embolization due to difficulty with catheterization and hypertensive urgency, the latter of which may be due to lack of alpha-adrenergic blockade prior to embolization.
