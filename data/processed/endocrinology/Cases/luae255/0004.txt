Diagnostic Assessment signs including blood pressure, low body mass index (BMI) (19.6 kg/m2), truncal obesity, ecchymoses, facial acnes, and proximal muscle weakness. Laboratory findings (Table 1) showed extremely elevated morning cortisol of 173.4 µg/dL (4783.8 nmol/L) (normal refer­ ence range, 5.0-25.0 µg/dL; 137.9-689.7 nmol/L); ACTH of 1425.0 pg/mL (313.5 pmol/L) (normal reference range, 7.2-63.6 pg/mL; 1.58-13.9 pmol/L); and 24-hour urine cortisol of 29 498.4 mcg/24 hours (81 386 nmol/24 h) (normal reference range, 4.0-50.0 mcg/24 h; 11.0-137.9 nmol/24 h). Her metastat­ ic NET, severe hypercortisolism, and markedly elevated ACTH confirmed the diagnosis of EAS. She was also found to have severe hypocalcemia with calcium level of 6.3 mg/dL (1.57 mmol/L) (normal reference range, 8.3-10.5 mg/dL; 2.1-2.6 mmol/L) and hypokalemia with potassium of 2.7 mmol/L (normal reference range, 3.5-5.1 mmol) (Table 1). Six months prior to presentation, computed tomography (CT) showed normal adrenal gland in size and shape. At pres­ entation, CT revealed new diffuse thickening of bilateral ad­ renal glands (Fig. 1).
