At pres­ entation, CT revealed new diffuse thickening of bilateral ad­ renal glands (Fig. 1). Immunohistochemistry of the tumor was positive for ACTH. Treatment The patient was admitted to the hospital given the life- threatening nature of her rapidly progressing hypercortiso­ lism. She received potassium and phosphorus repletion. After her electrolyte abnormalities were corrected, she was in­ itiated on high-dose osilodrostat at 20 mg twice daily aimed at quickly controlling her hypercortisolism. Her electrolytes, morning cortisol, electrocardiogram (EKG), and glucocortic­ oid withdrawal symptoms were monitored at least daily dur­ ing the hospitalization. In response to high-dose osilodrostat, there was a rapid im­ provement in morning cortisol levels (Table 2; Fig. 2). By day 5 of treatment, morning cortisol dropped 46% from 173.4 µg/dL (4784.1 nmol/L) to 93.4 µg/dL (2577.0 nmol/L). During treatment, she developed glucocorticoid withdrawal symptoms, including significant fatigue and poor appetite, for which hydrocortisone at 20 mg at 8 am and 10 mg at 2 PM was initiated. When her morning cortisol level reached the upper normal range, around 20 µg/dL (551.8 nmol/L), sulfa­ methoxazole/trimethoprim at 800/160 mg every other day was started for Pneumocystis pneumonia prophylaxis.
