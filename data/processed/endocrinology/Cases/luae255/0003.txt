Case Presentation A 27-year-old woman presented to endocrinology outpatient clinic for evaluation of Cushing syndrome. Her medical history was notable for metastatic gastro-entero-pancreatic NET. Her metastatic NET was diagnosed 3 years prior to presenta­ tion during an attempted resection of a biopsy-proven pelvic NET. During surgery, her disease was found to be unresectable. Histopathology showed metastatic well-differentiated neuro­ endocrine tumors with Ki67 of 13%, consistent with stage 4 tu­ mor. She received multiple lines of chemotherapy including cisplatin, etoposide, and durvalumab shortly before presenta­ tion. One month prior to presentation, she developed sudden onset fatigue, proximal muscle weakness, easy bruising, and profound depression with impaired cognitive function. She had worsening hypokalemia which had been present for 2 months and required increased potassium supplementation and initiation of spironolactone. Chemotherapy was held given concern for tumor-related hormonal abnormalities and persist­ ent thrombocytopenia. She identified as a nonsmoker and social drinker, and she denied illicit drug use.
