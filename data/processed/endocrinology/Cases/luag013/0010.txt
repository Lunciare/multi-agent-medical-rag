His total cal­ cium intake from formula and medication was 3021 mg/day, which was more than 3 times higher than 1000 mg/day recom­ mended at his age . The first episode of hypercalcemia was worse than the second one because he received more calcium from enteral formula and calcium carbonate than from enteral formula alone. To our knowledge, this is the first report of excessive dietary calcium intake from enteral formula during treatment of hypo­ parathyroidism that resulted in severe hypercalcemia and hyper­ calciuria. The European Society of Endocrinology clinical guidelines for treatment of hypoparathyroidism in adults stated that intake of calcium from dietary source (mainly dairy prod­ ucts) could be equivalent to intake from calcium supplements . This case shows that dietary calcium intake from enteral for­ mula may even become excessive in children when the volume of formula is markedly increased in pursuit of more calories. It is also the first reported case of successful use of natural food blenderized tube feeding (BTF) instead of synthetic commercial enteral formula in a child with hypoparathyroidism.
