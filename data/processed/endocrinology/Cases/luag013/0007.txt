He resumed taking calcitriol 0.25 mcg twice daily when hypercalcemia resolved. Outcome and follow-up The patient’s blood calcium and urine calcium/creatinine ratio remained normal with the same dose of calcitriol without cal­ cium carbonate during the following 2 years (Fig. 2). He tolerated his BTF well, and his dietary calcium intake met the recom­ mended daily allowance established by the Institute of Medicine . At age 8 years, he had blood calcium of 9.1 mg/dL (SI: 3.75 mmol/L), phosphate of 4.6 mg/dL (SI: 1.48 mmol/L), PTH of 8.0 pg/mL (SI: 0.85 pmol/L), 25-dihydroxyvitamin D of 38 ng/mL (SI: 94.8 nmol/L), 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D of 33 pg/ mL (SI: 85.8 pmol/L), and urine calcium/creatinine ratio of 0.14 mg/mg (SI: 0.39 mmol/mmol). Discussion The amount of calcium absorbed from the intestine depends on the interplay between calcium intake and vitamin D level .
