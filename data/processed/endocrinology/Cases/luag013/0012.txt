Changes in blood calcium during treatment of hypoparathyroidism. JCEM Case Reports, 2026, Volume 4, Issue 3 described 3 adult women with postsurgical hypoparathyroidism who developed symptomatic hypercalcemia with anorexia, muscle weakness, and blood calcium as high as 13.6 to 15.2 mg/dL (SI: 3.4-3.8 mmol/L) within 1 to 2 months of treat­ ment with large doses of 1α-hydroxyvitamin D converted to 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D in the liver. In a retrospective study of 29 children with hypoparathyroidism, Levy et al observed that higher doses of calcitriol were associated with higher blood calcium and increased urine calcium excretion. Schroth et al reported a 14-year-old boy with hypopara­ thyroidism treated with calcium carbonate and calcitriol who de­ veloped severe hypercalcemia with calcium of 14.03 mg/dL (SI: 3.5 mmol/L) and nephrocalcinosis within 2 months of immobil­ ization for tibial fracture, which required temporarily suspension of substitution therapy. Prolonged immobilization results in im­ balance of bone remodeling where the rate of bone resorption exceeds the rate of bone formation, which may lead to hypercal­ cemia and hypercalciuria particularly in young patients .
