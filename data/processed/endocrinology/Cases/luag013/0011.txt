reported case of successful use of natural food blenderized tube feeding (BTF) instead of synthetic commercial enteral formula in a child with hypoparathyroidism. Our patient used a combination of homemade BTF, which incorporated dairy products such as milk, yogurt, and cottage cheese, and commer­ cial BTF. Milk and yogurt were simply added to commercial BTF, and cottage cheese was mixed with water and blenderized. He re­ ceived calcium about 300 mg/day from dairy products and 600 mg/day from commercial BTF, according to his dietitian. Studies of BTF in children with feeding difficulties reported im­ provement in gastrointestinal symptoms, including reductions in gagging, retching, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea compared with commercial enteral formula [14-16]. The use of BTF in our patient made it possible to markedly increase his cal­ orie intake without causing dietary calcium excess. Besides calcium carbonate overdose and excessive dietary cal­ cium intake from enteral formula, the 2 other causes of hypercal­ cemia during treatment of hypoparathyroidism are vitamin D intoxication and prolonged immobilization.
