Hypocalcemia was corrected with calcitriol and calcium car­ bonate (each 1 g of salt contains 400 mg of elemental calcium). Transthoracic echocardiography showed ventricular septal de­ fect. Testicular ultrasound found testicles situated within the pelvis. He underwent repair of choanal atresia, closure of ven­ tricular septal defect with a patch, and orchiopexy for undes­ cended testicles. He had feeding difficulties that prompted insertion of gastrostomy tube, and he exhibited developmental delay. At age 5 years, his blood calcium and urine calcium/creatinine ratio were normal when he was treated with calcitriol 0.25 mcg twice daily and calcium carbonate at a dose of 18 mg/kg/day of elemental calcium, which provided him with extra calcium 300 mg/day. His blood calcium “dropped” to 6.6 mg/dL (SI: 1.65 mmol/L) when he did not receive his medications, which re­ quired admission to the Children’s Hospital. Hypocalcemia was corrected, and he was discharged to home with calcitriol 0.25 mcg twice daily and calcium carbonate at the same dose of 75 mg/kg/day of elemental calcium that was used to correct hypocalcemia.
