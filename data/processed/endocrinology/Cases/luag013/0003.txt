Calcium carbonate also played a role in the first episode of hypercalcemia; the second time hypercalcemia resulted entirely from dietary calcium excess. Based on this case and review of literature, we highlight 4 pos­ sible reasons for hypercalcemia during treatment of hypopara­ thyroidism. Given the severity of hypercalcemia in our patient, this case underscores the importance of assessment of dietary calcium intake in children with hypoparathyroidism, especially those receiving enteral formula. Case presentation A 2-week-old infant presented with irritability, blood calcium of 6.2 mg/dL (SI: 1.55 mmol/L) (reference range, 8.6-10.3 mg/dL [SI: 2.1-2.6 mmol/L]), phosphate of 8.6 mg/dL (SI: 2.77 mmol/L) (reference range, 2.5-4.5 mg/dL [SI: 0.81-1.45 mmol/L]), and in­ appropriately normal PTH of 18 pg/mL (SI: 1.90 pmol/L) (refer­ ence range, 15-65 pg/mL [SI: 1.6-6.9 pmol/L]) consistent with hypoparathyroidism. Physical examination was significant for choanal atresia, hypertelorism, micrognathia, heart murmur, and undescended testicles. Auditory brainstem response demonstrated bilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Renal ultrasound showed poor corticomedullary differentiation. Chromosomal microarray analysis revealed de novo unbalanced 4;10 translocation with 26.9-Mb 4q32.2-4q35.2 duplication and 9.5-Mb 10p15.3-10p14 terminal deletion that included the GATA3 gene responsible for HDR syndrome [4-7].
