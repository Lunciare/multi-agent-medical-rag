PTH of <4 pg/mL (SI: <0.42 pmol/L), 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 46 ng/mL (SI: 114.8 nmol/L) (reference range, 30-100 ng/dL [SI: 75-249 nmol/ L]), 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D of 35 pg/mL (SI: 84 pmol/L) (refer­ ence range, 24-86 pg/mL [SI: 58-206 pmol/L]), and parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP) of 2.5 pmol/L (reference range, <4.2 pmol/L). There was no evidence of nephrocalcinosis on re­ nal ultrasound. It turned out that because of feeding difficulties and poor weight gain his dietitian switched his diet to enteral formula for toddlers and young children via gastrostomy tube to give him more calories. His formula contained 330 mg of calcium per 237 mL bottle, and he received 5 bottles of formula per day that provided him with calcium intake of 1650 mg/day, which was significantly higher than recommended for children aged 4 through 8 years . Treatment switched from synthetic commercial enteral formula to natural food blenderized tube feeding (BTF), and calcitriol was held to fa­ cilitate negative calcium balance. His calcium intake from his diet was adjusted to be appropriate for his age.
