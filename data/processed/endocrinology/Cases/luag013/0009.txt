His calcium intake from enteral for­ mula was increased to 1650 mg/day, which was more than one-and-a-half times higher than 1000 mg/day recommended at his age, but less than the upper limit of 2500 mg/day above which there was known risk of adverse events . We speculate that a lesser dietary calcium excess caused hypercalcemia in our patient because he was taking calcitriol to enhance intestinal cal­ cium absorption. Normalization of blood calcium and urine cal­ cium/creatinine ratio after diet change which reduced his dietary calcium intake to the age-appropriate amount confirmed that the source of calcium overload was his diet. When we discovered that the second calcium overload was caused by excessive dietary calcium intake from enteral formula, we analyzed his dietary calcium intake leading up to the first epi­ sode of hypercalcemia and found that it was a major contributor to it. He was given the same formula containing 330 mg of cal­ cium per 237 mL bottle in the amount of 1152 mL/day which pro­ vided him with calcium intake of 1604 mg/day in addition to extra calcium 1417 mg/day from calcium carbonate.
