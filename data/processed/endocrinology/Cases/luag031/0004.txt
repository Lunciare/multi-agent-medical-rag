The cisterna chyli is cannulated with a microwire, and a microcath­ eter is advanced into the thoracic duct. Via the microcatheter, standard nonionic iodinated contrast is usually used to opacify the thoracic duct to identify the site of leakage. TDE is then per­ formed with embolization coils and a mixture of ethiodized oil and n-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate (n-BCA) glue . Little is known about the pharmacokinetics of ethiodized oil when administered in the central lymphatics for TDE and how this may affect RAI therapy evaluation. To date, we are unaware of other published case reports of pa­ tients with DTC who developed a post-op CL, which required treatment with TDE, and were subsequently evaluated for RAI therapy. Herein, we present 2 such cases and their challenges.
