Key Words radioiodine, thyroid cancer, ethiodized oil, lymphangiography, thoracic duct embolization Introduction Radioiodine (RAI) therapy with iodine-131 (I-131) sodium iodide (NaI) is considered in differentiated thyroid cancers (DTC) following surgery . Patient preparation for RAI therapy re­ quires either endogenous hypothyroidism via thyroid hormone withdrawal with a target TSH level >30 µIU/mL (>30 mIU/L; nor­ mal reference range: 0.30-5.50 µIU/mL; 0.30-5.50 mIU/L) or ad­ ministration of recombinant human TSH. Furthermore, a low-iodine diet (LID) for 1 to 2 weeks and avoiding iodinated con­ trast media 4 to 6 weeks prior to RAI therapy minimizes nonra­ dioactive iodine interference of RAI uptake, which can reduce therapy efficacy . A recommended optimal urinary iodine concentration (UIC) at the time of RAI therapy is <50 µg/L (ad­ equate: <100 µg/L; normal reference range: 100-300 µg/L), whereas UIC ≥300 µg/L is excessive . Locoregional spread of DTC via adjacent draining lymphatic channels is common; therefore, central/lateral neck dissection may be appropriate with total thyroidectomy prior to RAI therapy .
