An uncommon complication of neck dissection is a chyle leak (CL), which occurs in 0.9% of all patients undergoing thyroi­ dectomy with or without neck dissection . However, in cases requiring lateral neck dissection, reported incidences of CLs range from 3.9% to 8.3% . Postoperative (post-op) CL treatment is guided by drain output, patient condition, and institution/surgeon preference and includes conservative measures, topical agents, repeat surgical exploration, thoracic duct ligation (TDL), and thoracic duct embolization (TDE) . Surgical management, TDL, and TDE are reserved for patients who are refractory to conservative measures and/or have high chylous outputs. If TDL or TDE are needed, the latter is generally preferred because TDL is associated with a 38.8% morbidity and a 2.1% mortality rate . TDE has a lower morbidity (14.3%) and can be repeated if initially unsuccessful . TDE is performed following intranodal lymphangiography, where ethiodized oil (Lipiodol®) is injected into targeted inguinal lymph nodes to visualize the central lymphatic chains and opa­ cify the cisterna chyli .
