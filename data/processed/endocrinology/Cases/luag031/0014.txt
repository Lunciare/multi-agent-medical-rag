Following intralymphatic adminis­ tration, it is transported as droplets in the blood to the liver, lungs, spleen, and adipose tissue, which undergo slow metabol­ ism for several weeks or months during which free iodine is con­ tinuously released . Thyroid dysfunction, RAI uptake interference, reduced RAI therapy effectiveness, and falsified thy­ roid diagnostic tests can result from ethiodized oil retention [10, 11]. In these cases, a mixture of ethiodized oil and n-BCA glue was used with coils to embolize the thoracic duct. The glue effectively traps ethiodized oil in the intrathoracic lymphatics, preventing dispersal and elimination of oil droplets via normal physiologic pathways (see Fig. 5).
