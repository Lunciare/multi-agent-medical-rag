Retrospectively, we identified that these hyperglycemic episodes occurred close to scheduled tubing changes and were likely related to line malfunctions secondary to very low-volume delivery. In these instances (and others) we augmented IV insulin with subcutaneous hyperglycemic correction dosing, mimicking a basal-bolus like system. Glycemic control was monitored using a CGM placed on the infant on the seventh day of life, coincident with initiation of insulin therapy. A Dexcom G7 (DexCom Inc) was placed on the outer thigh of the infant. CGM measurements were compared to serum glucose measurements for 24 hours, with CGM readings within the manufacturer’s reported oper­ ating parameters and correlated with serum values. CGM val­ ues are reported in this study unless otherwise noted. Off-label use of HCLS was discussed with the parents, in­ cluding the anticipated benefits of improved glycemic control and quality of life balanced against the lack of broad efficacy in this population. The parents elected to proceed with the HCLS trial while the infant was hospitalized. HCLS with insulin lispro U100 was initiated on the 29th day of life, using the Dexcom G7 CGM and a Tandem Mobi Insulin Delivery System (Tandem Diabetes Care).
