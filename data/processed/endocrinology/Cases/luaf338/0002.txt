Introduction Neonatal diabetes mellitus (NDM) is a rare form (1:90 000-160 000 births) of diabetes with onset before age 6 months and nearly always is attributed to a genetic/epigen­ etic variation . Approximately 60% of NDM cases require insulin treatment . In general, insulin treatment is uniquely challenging in NDM due to balancing the indispensable na­ ture of insulin for growth and weight gain against the risk of hypoglycemia and technical limitations of administering low doses. Additionally, the estimation of carbohydrate content in breast milk is challenging, and the requirement for precise carbohydrate estimation alters typical infant feeding behavior with the potential for neurodevelopmental outcomes. For other forms of diabetes, hybrid closed-loop systems (HCLS) are the gold standard of treatment and importantly al­ low for modification of insulin dosing based on data from con­ tinuous glucose monitors (CGMs) . In patients with type 1 diabetes, HCLS have been shown to improve every aspect of glycemic control over conventional insulin pumps or injec­ tions and improve patient and family quality of life .
