PI is defined as a radiologic finding of gas within the bowel wall [1-3]. The causes of PI range from benign conditions requiring no intervention to life-threatening bowel ischemia necessitating im­ mediate surgery . Bowel obstruction, chronic pulmonary diseases, autoimmune disorders, medications such as corticoste­ roids and glucosidase inhibitors, infectious diseases such as COVID-19, and gastrointestinal instrumentation are all risk fac­ tors for PI . Urgent investigation—particularly in symptom­ atic patients—is essential in determining the clinical significance of PI and ensuring timely treatment, especially in the case of this elderly patient with multiple comorbidities. Case presentation The patient was an 87-year-old male nursing home resident with a complex medical history including paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, prostate cancer, type 2 dia­ betes mellitus, chronic prosthetic joint infection, chronic bilateral lymphedema, and limited mobility due to severe lumbar stenosis. He was prescribed semaglutide approximately 10 months prior to symptom onset, starting at 0.25 mg weekly and titrating gradually to a dose of 2.4 mg weekly, primarily for weight loss.
