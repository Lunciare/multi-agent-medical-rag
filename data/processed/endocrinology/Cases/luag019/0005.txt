Abdominal computed tomography (CT) scan revealed portal and superior mesenteric venous gas with thickened loops of distal je­ junum, mesenteric edema, and a few bubbles of extraluminal air (Figs. 1-3). The CT scan report stated that there was no evidence or suggestion of bowel obstruction or pseudo-obstruction. Treatment Due to the symptomatic presentation and the suggestive CT scan findings of intestinal ischemia and possible early perforation, the patient underwent emergent exploratory laparotomy, where he was found to have viable-appearing bowel with adequate perfu­ sion on indocyanine green injection. All other intraperitoneal or­ gans appeared normal with no evidence of infection, perforation, or ischemia. The exploratory laparotomy also revealed yellowish- clear, nonpurulent ascites. No bowel was resected. Blood and ascitic fluid cultures, stool polymerase chain reaction (PCR), hepatitis B and C panel, and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) tests were all negative. The patient was placed on broad- spectrum antibiotics (cefepime and metronidazole) after con­ sultation with infectious disease.
