Emerging case reports also suggest a potential link between GLP-1RAs and pneumatosis. For instance, one case report documents a 30-year-old woman developing PI from tirzepatide, a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP-1RA, with symptoms resolving with discontinuation of the drug, antibiotics, and bowel rest . Another case report docu­ ments a 61-year-old man developing gastric pneumatosis after 9 months of semaglutide use, with symptoms resolving upon dis­ continuation of the drug . We hypothesize that GLP-1RAs’ mechanism of action may con­ tribute to PI development. This is likely because GLP-1RAs modu­ late the enteric nervous system to switch from propulsive, coordinated motility toward nonpropulsive, segmental contrac­ tions within the GI tract . This pattern slows transit and may increase intraluminal pressure, particularly in the context of de­ layed food clearance. In our case, the patient denied any recent changes in bowel movements and was recorded as having a large bowel move­ ment earlier in the day prior to developing his symptoms.
