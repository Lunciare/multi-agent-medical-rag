His dosing schedule included 0.25 mg weekly for 4 weeks, fol­ lowed by stepwise increases to 0.5, 1, and 1.7 mg, ultimately reaching 2.4 mg weekly, which he remained on for over 6 months. Before semaglutide initiation, his diabetes was controlled on sliding-scale aspart insulin and glipizide, with a baseline hemoglo­ bin A1c of 5.3% [reference range, 4.2-6.2%]. Following semaglutide titration, his glycemic control remained stable, with subsequent A1c values of 5.2% and 4.9%. Simultaneously, he began low-dose prednisone 5 mg twice daily in combination with abiraterone 1000 mg daily for prostate cancer. The patient’s medications were closely monitored by the phar­ macy service for adverse effects. The last pharmacy monitoring note occurred around 2 weeks prior to this patient’s presenta­ tion. At that time, the patient had normal bowel function and no evidence of constipation. On the morning of symptom onset, the patient suddenly devel­ oped nausea, bilious vomiting, and stabbing, poorly localized ab­ dominal pain while having lunch.
