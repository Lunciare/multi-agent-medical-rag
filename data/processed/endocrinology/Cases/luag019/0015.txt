As this is only the second reported case of PI associated with GLP-1RA use, further studies are needed to clarify the potential link between GLP-1RAs, corticosteroids, and PI. Additionally, further research on nonischemic causes of PI with seemingly high-risk features—as seen in our patient—is critical to prevent unnecessary exploratory laparotomies in patients with PI with­ out necrosis, ischemia, or other emergent surgical conditions. These cases remain under-recognized, and there are currently no reliable tools to distinguish between patients who require ur­ gent surgical intervention and those who may be safely managed with medical therapy or observation. Learning points • Pneumatosis intestinalis (PI) can be benign and is not always a sign of bowel ischemia. • Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are associated with a range of side effects; emerging case reports suggest they may contribute to PI. • In elderly patients with multiple comorbidities, the decision to pursue laparotomy can be challenging; medication- induced PI should be considered before surgical intervention.
