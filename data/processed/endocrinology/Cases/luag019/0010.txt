Importantly, for our patient’s case, several radiographic fea­ tures traditionally interpreted as ischemic—such as mesenteric edema, bowel wall thickening, and ascites—may reflect obstruc­ tion of venous return from increased intraluminal pressure rather than true ischemia . This interpretation aligns with the ab­ sence of ischemic changes, or perforation, at the time of surgical exploration, and underscores the diagnostic complexity of PI in symptomatic, medically complex patient. The Naranjo scale is a causality assessment method for assess­ ing all forms of drug-induced adverse events . Using the ori­ ginal Naranjo scale, prednisone and semaglutide receive a score of 4 and 3, respectively, indicating “possible” causality (Table 2). Goh et al proposed a modified version that replaces items rarely clinically tested; although more clinically relevant, this adaptation has not yet been formally validated.
