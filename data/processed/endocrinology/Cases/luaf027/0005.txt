Given the presence of an adrenal lesion with high HU, coexistence of presumably catecholamine excess–related symp­ toms (hypertension, excessive sweating, stress/anxiety), as well as what appeared to be ACTH-dependent hypercortisolism (neither ACTH nor dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate [DHEA- S] were suppressed), there remained a suspicion for an ACTH-secreting pheochromocytoma. However, the findings of baseline normal plasma metanephrines and normal plasma normetanephrines seemed to contradict this hypothesis. Given the presence of ACTH-dependent hypercortisolism (and despite there being evidence of an adrenal lesion), a mag­ netic resonance imaging pituitary study was pursued to rule out other potential causes of the patient’s hypercortisolism. This was negative for pituitary lesion and overall unremarkable. Ultimately, the patient was referred to surgery for right adrenalectomy. Treatment The patient underwent a right adrenalectomy with the subse­ quent pathology report detailing findings of a pure AGN (4.7 cm). Given the patient’s history of ACTH-dependent CS, immunostaining for ACTH was requested and revealed positive expression (Fig. 1).
