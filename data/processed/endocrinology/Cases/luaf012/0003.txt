Patients with TIO are often undiagnosed for a long time, ow­ ing to nonspecific symptoms, such as bone pain and muscle weakness . Also, FGF23-producing tumors are often too small to localize . Somatostatin receptor (SSTR) scintigraphy is an imaging method for visualizing SSTR expressed in neuroendocrine tu­ mors . Among various forms of SSTR scintigraphy, only indium 111 (111In)-pentetreotide scintigraphy is currently cov­ ered by National Health Insurance in Japan for neuroendocrine tumors. However, the sensitivity is relatively low, and there should be careful consideration of physiological accumulation in normal organs . Systemic venous sampling (SVS) of FGF23 is also reportedly useful for localizing FGF23-producing tumors, but it is an invasive method . A more accurate imaging method for detecting TIO is therefore desired. Gallium 68 (68Ga)-DOTATOC positron emission tomography (PET)/com­ puted tomography (CT) has reported high sensitivity and specifi­ city in the localization of TIO .
