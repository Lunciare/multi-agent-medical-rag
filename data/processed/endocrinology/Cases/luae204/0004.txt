Due to the complex regulatory pattern of the GNAS1 locus, there is little understanding of the molecular pathways that link pathogenic variants to POH and other GNAS1-related osteopathy disorders. Enriching the database of GNAS1 var­ iants and associated phenotypes will provide valuable insights into the inheritance and, potentially, unknown molecular patterns of GNAS1-related osteopathy disorders. POH often presents in infancy or early childhood (average age of diagnosis younger than 1 year) as cutaneous ossification throughout one or multiple sections of the body and pro­ gresses slowly into deeper connective and skeletal muscle tissues . Nonetheless, a small subgroup of late-onset, mildly affected POH patients has been identified. These patients re­ ceived their POH diagnoses later than 4 years of age and expe­ rienced sparse and slow ossification, as well as bone formation that sometimes began in subdermal tissues [6-12]. There is little information in the literature on the subgroup of late-onset POH patients. Some have been misdiagnosed as tumoral calcinosis or asymptomatic, due to the lack of other symptoms apart from ectopic ossification
