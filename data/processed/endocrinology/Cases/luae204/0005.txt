Some have been misdiagnosed as tumoral calcinosis or asymptomatic, due to the lack of other symptoms apart from ectopic ossification and the difficulties of locating small, discrete ossification plates in skeletal muscles and deep connective tissues. Misdiagnosis of mild, late-onset POH patients may prompt unnecessary surgeries to remove the ossification plates that will quickly return. To raise awareness of mild POH phenotypes and emphasize the importance of consulting genetics in diagnosis of ectopic ossification, we present the natural histories, physical presen­ tations, diagnostic odysseys, and follow-ups of 2 patients with late-onset, mild POH. Furthermore, in one of our reported patients, we assessed the utility of the sodium 18F-fluorine positron emission tomography (PET) scan in detecting and visualizing heterotopic ossification sites . Sodium 18F-fluorine is effective in this PET scan since the compound preferentially binds to hydroxyapatite matrix in normal and abnormal bone and provides high quality images of bone and calcified tissues, including bone changes in inflammation, degeneration, bone tumors, and bone metastases.
