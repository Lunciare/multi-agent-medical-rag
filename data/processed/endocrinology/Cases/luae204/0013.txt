In most cases of POH, the ossification plates reappear after surgery, and the patients are at risk of increased ossification rates trig­ gered by the trauma from surgery. Whole body PET scanning employs a radioactive tracer to discern sites of normal and abnormal metabolic activity, in­ cluding inflammation, malignancy, and ectopic calcification . As illustrated herein, sodium 18F-fluorine PET scanning provides a sensitive technology for identifying the distribution of ectopic calcifications in POH and should be useful for tracking disease progression and provide utility in initial diagnosis. The current number of medical facilities performing sodium 18F-fluorine PET scanning is suboptimal, although we antici­ pate broader availability in the near future as its advantages become more evident. Learning Points • Progressive osseous heteroplasia (POH) is caused by loss of function variants in GNAS1. • There is a wide range of variability in clinical and radio­ logical manifestations of POH. • Sodium 18F-fluorine PET scanning provides a sensitive tool for identifying and tracking the ectopic calcifications that characterize POH. • Surgical removal of ectopic calcifications in POH is not advised since exacerbation
