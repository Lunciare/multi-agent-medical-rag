Introduction Progressive osseous heteroplasia (POH) is a rare autosomal- dominantly inherited disorder associated with heterozygous deactivating pathogenic variants of the GNAS1 paternal allele . POH is characterized by cutaneous ossification and heterotopic ossification in skeletal muscles and subdermal connective tissues. Heterotopic ossification in POH often ap­ pears in random patterns, clusters in some areas of the body but not others, and presents asymmetrical distributions across the midline. The ectopic ossification plates may or may not cause pain or physical discomfort. Clinical manifestations of POH range from osseous lesions to unequal bone growth in extremities, ankylosis, and scoliosis. The rate of disease progression is variable, even between family members with the same pathogenic variant.
