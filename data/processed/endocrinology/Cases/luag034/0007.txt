Since its first description, there have been more than 160 different pathogenic variants linked to RTH . The clinical presentation varies based on the specific varia­ tions within the TH receptor . Newborns with homozygous pathogenic variations in THRB are often identified by neonatal screening with abnormal levels of FT4, TSH, or both . However, RTH has gone undetected at childbirth in patients with heterozygous pathogenic variations due to minimal altera­ tions in levels of FT4 and TSH with no prominent clinical symp­ toms . Patients with untreated RTH often present later in life with variable symptomatology based on their TH levels and de­ gree of resistance. according to the location of TH resistance. GRTH is character­ ized by resistance to TH in the pituitary and peripheral tissues; however, compensatory increases in both TH and TSH typically result in an asymptomatic presentation . Patients with hypothyroidism present with goiter, fatigue, somnolence, de­ pression, and bradycardia, while patients with hyperthyroid­ ism present with tachycardia, hyperactivity, short stature, and altered bone age .
