Assay interference presents with similar discordant thyroid levels sec­ ondary to autoantibodies, excess biotin, or Macro-TSH molecules . Assay interference is excluded by autoantibody screenings, sample dilution, or different immunoassays . The other principal condition to consider is a TSHoma. Initial testing to differentiate a TSHoma from RTH includes a thyrotropin- releasing hormone stimulation test where TSHomas do not exhibit TSH excitability, and a L-triiodothyronine inhibition test where supra-physiologic levels of T3 fail to suppress TSH production . For inconclusive results, a somatostatin inhibition test with a significant decrease in TSH levels can support a TSHoma diagno­ sis . Pituitary MRI may identify larger TSHomas, although reli­ ance on imaging alone is unreliable as case reports of concurrent RTH and TSHoma have been documented .
