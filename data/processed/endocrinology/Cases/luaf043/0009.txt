Although medical therapy is the first line of management, surgery offers a definitive solution by rapidly restoring euthyroidism. This intervention has been shown to improve cardiac function within 2 months . Thyroidectomy is indicated in patients with AIT and under­ lying heart disease who exhibit deterioration in cardiac func­ tion, resistance to medical treatment, or an inability to discontinue amiodarone . However, thyroidectomy for treatment-refractory AIT is associated with higher periopera­ tive morbidity and mortality compared to thyroidectomy for other causes of hyperthyroidism. A study conducted at the Mayo Clinic revealed a mortality rate of 5.9% for thyroidec­ tomy in cases of treatment-refractory AIT, compared to 0.4% for thyroidectomy for toxic multinodular goiter and 0.2% for Graves disease . These cases underscore the intricate rela­ sizing the need for a multidisciplinary approach in managing AIT, particularly in patients with existing cardiovascular disease. In severe cases where conventional treatments are ineffective or contraindicated, plasmapheresis serves as an option to achieve clinical and hemodynamic stability .
