In our patients, plasmapheresis served as a bridge to total thyroidectomy, re­ ducing the surgical risks associated with high thyroid hor­ mone levels in patients with cardiac conditions. One case demonstrated an urgent surgical indication, where plasma­ pheresis enabled sufficient stabilization to proceed with sur­ gery. Although the initial plan was to delay thyroidectomy until the fT4 level dropped below 30 pmol/L (23.3 pg/mL) (reference range, 8.6-25 pmol/L, 9-20 pg/mL), this target was not achieved, and surgery was performed as plasmapher­ esis effects waned during the last sessions. The other case high­ lighted a surgical indication due to treatment resistance and the emergence of side effects from therapy. Although plasma­ pheresis is not a first-line treatment for thyrotoxicosis, it should be considered as part of a management strategy to pre­ vent severe cardiac complications.
