Case 2 A total thyroidectomy was performed, and the postoperative course was uneventful. The patient was placed on hydrocorti­ sone due to the risk of tertiary adrenal insufficiency associated with the discontinuation of corticosteroids. At 1 month post­ operatively, the patient was euthyroid with substitution ther­ apy and no longer exhibited tremors. His cardiac function remained stable. Discussion These 2 clinical cases illustrate 2 different presentations where plasmapheresis was utilized to reduce circulating thyroid hor­ mone levels and thereby decrease perioperative risks. In both patients, the specific type of amiodarone-induced thyrotoxi­ cosis (AIT) was not definitively established, and they received mixed medical treatment. AIT poses a significant management challenge, particularly because patients receiving amiodarone typically have preexist­ ing cardiac conditions. Uncontrolled thyrotoxicosis in individ­ uals with reduced ejection fraction is associated with a significantly increased risk of mortality, primarily due to severe left ventricular dysfunction.
