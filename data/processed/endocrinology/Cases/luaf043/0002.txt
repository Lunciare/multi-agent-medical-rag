While amiodarone- induced hypothyroidism is typically manageable with substi­ tution therapy, managing amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis (AIT) is far more complex due to its potential to precipitate or worsen cardiac arrhythmias, particularly in patients with pre­ existing left ventricular dysfunction . AIT is divided into 2 distinct types. Type I AIT occurs in a thyroid with underlying pathology and is triggered by iodine overload, which exacerbates the thyroid’s autonomous func­ tion. Type II AIT occurs in a healthy thyroid and results from a destructive thyroiditis caused by the toxic effects of amiodar­ one . Differentiating between Type I and Type II AIT can be challenging and requires comprehensive evaluation, espe­ cially since mixed forms exist. However, radioactive iodine up­ take (RAIU) testing can aid diagnosis. Type I AIT typically shows detectable values (high, normal, or low), while Type II AIT presents with very low or undetectable values . Medical therapy is the first-line treatment for AIT. In Type I AIT, thiamazole remains the cornerstone of pharmacological management, while corticosteroids are critical for managing Type II AIT.
