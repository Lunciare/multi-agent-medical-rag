However, after methylprednisolone doses were gradually re­ duced to 16 mg, the patient experienced a recurrence of hyper­ thyroidism symptoms. Laboratory tests revealed an increase in thyroid hormone levels, with free T4 at 47.8 pmol/L (37.1 pg/mL) (reference range, 8.6-25 pmol/L, 9-20 pg/mL). Methylprednisolone doses were subsequently increased back to 64 mg. The patient developed clinical Cushing syndrome as a side ef­ fect of corticosteroid therapy. Despite treatment adjustments, thyroid hormone control remained inadequate, with fT4 at 43.5 pmol/L (33.7 pg/mL) (reference range, 8.6-25 pmol/L, 9-20 pg/mL) (Table 2). Given the patient’s cardiac history and treatment resistance, a total thyroidectomy was decided upon to restore euthyroidism. To reduce surgical risk associated with elevated thyroid hormone levels, 3 sessions of preoperative plasmapheresis were performed. Unfortunately, thyroid hor­ mone levels were not measured during the plasmapheresis period.
