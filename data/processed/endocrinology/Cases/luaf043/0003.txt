However, a multimodal pharmacological ap­ proach may be necessary when mixed or undefined forms of AIT are present . Surgery represents a definitive treatment that can quickly restore euthyroidism, thereby reducing the risk of cardiac events and mortality. Plasmapheresis, although transient in effect, is an effective tool to reduce excess circulat­ ing thyroid hormones. It can stabilize patients clinically and hemodynamically, making them suitable candidates for surgi­ cal intervention . Case Presentation Case 1 A 65-year-old man with heart failure due to nonischemic dilated cardiomyopathy (left ventricular ejection fraction estimated at 40% during the last echocardiogram) was equipped with an implanted cardiac resynchronization therapy– defibrillator for primary prevention. Following an ischemic stroke caused by atrial fibrillation, he was treated with amio­ darone for 3 years. This treatment had to be discontinued due to altered thyroid function tests with negative antibodies (anti- thyroglobulin, anti-thyroid peroxidase, anti-thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor). A synthetic antithyroid medication (thiama­ zole 20 mg) was then introduced.
