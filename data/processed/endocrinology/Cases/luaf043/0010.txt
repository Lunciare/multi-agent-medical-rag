In severe cases where conventional treatments are ineffective or contraindicated, plasmapheresis serves as an option to achieve clinical and hemodynamic stability . Despite its transient effect, plasmapheresis effectively reduces circulating thyroid hormone levels in patients who are unresponsive to medical treatments. The American Society of Apheresis classi­ fies plasmapheresis for thyrotoxicosis as a category II indica­ tion, recommending its use as a second-line treatment in refractory cases. Sessions are advised every 24 hours to every 3 days until clinical improvement is achieved . Plasmapheresis is preferred over other filtration methods due to its ability to remove high-molecular-weight substances, in­ cluding free thyroid hormones and hormone-protein com­ plexes. It is a relatively safe and well-tolerated procedure, though complications such as catheter-related issues (pneumo­ thorax, infection, local bleeding) and, more rarely, severe com­ plications (eg, anaphylactic reactions, severe hypotension) have been reported in 1% to 2% of sessions in critically ill pa­ tients . Interpretation of laboratory tests during plasma­ pheresis can also be challenging. This report highlights the benefits of preoperative plasma­ pheresis in cases of medically resistant AIT.
