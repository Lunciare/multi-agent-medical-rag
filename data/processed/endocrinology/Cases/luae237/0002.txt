See the journal About page for additional terms. Introduction Male hypogonadism is a common presentation in endocrin­ ology practice. Congenital or acquired disturbances at any level of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis can lead to an impairment of reproduction function and the clinical syn­ drome of hypogonadism. Hypogonadism can be caused by a primary testicular pathology (hypergonadotropic hypogonad­ ism) or hypothalamic and/or pituitary failures leading to sec­ ondary hypogonadism (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). The signs and symptoms of hypogonadism vary, depending on age of onset, severity of testosterone deficiency, and andro­ gen sensitivity.
