Discussion The diagnosis of male hypogonadism is based on assessment of signs and symptoms and on low serum testosterone concen­ trations on a reliable assay. Klinefelter syndrome is the most common genetic cause of hypogonadism, and hence the most likely initial differential diagnosis. In contrast to 46, XX testicular DSD, Klinefelter syndrome is often character­ ized by tall stature, speech delay, learning disorders, and be­ havioral problems. DSD is an umbrella term for rare conditions characterized by an incongruence of chromosomal, gonadal, and genital sex development . Discrete genetic conditions that under­ lie DSD are rarely identified .
