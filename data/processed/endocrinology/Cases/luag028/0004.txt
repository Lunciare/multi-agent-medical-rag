His 24-hour urine calcium was elevated at 719 mg (SI: 17.9 mmol/24 hours) (reference range: 100-300 mg/ 24 hours; 2.5-7.5 mmol/24 hours). During outpatient follow-up with endocrinology, his 1,25(OH)2D levels were found to be elevated at 84 pg/mL (201 pmol/L). Computed tomography (CT) scan did not show pulmonary atypia but revealed retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy (Fig. 1). He under­ went a core needle biopsy of the retroperitoneal lymph node, which showed benign lymphoid tissue without malignancy. Subsequently, he had excisional biopsies of the liver, peritoneum, and retroperitoneal lymph node, which revealed nonnecrotizing granulomatous inflammation (Fig. 2), consistent with sarcoidosis. Treatment During the first hospitalization, he was treated with fluids, received 4 doses of 400 units of calcitonin and a single 4-mg dose of zole­ dronic acid, resulting in an improvement of serum calcium to 11.6 mg/dL before discharge. During the second hospitalization, he was again treated with fluids and 4 doses of 400 units of calci­ tonin, which reduced his serum calcium level to 10.8 mg/dL.
