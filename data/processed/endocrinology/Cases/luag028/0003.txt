Bone survey showed no lytic or destructive osseous lesions, and chest X-ray revealed no signs of hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy. He denied using thiazide diuretics or vitamin A supplements. Treatment led to a partial improvement in calcium levels; how­ ever, the etiology of hypercalcemia remained unclear at the time of discharge, as further laboratory tests and imaging studies were still planned. He was advised to follow up with nephrology and endocrinology for additional diagnostic evaluation. Three months later, he returned with similar symptoms and an elevated calcium level of 14.3 mg/dL, again with suppressed PTH.
