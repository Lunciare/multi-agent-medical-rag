Isolated nonparathyroid hormone-mediated hypercalcemia: a rare presentation of retroperitoneal sarcoidosis Abstract Non-parathyroid hormone (PTH)-mediated hypercalcemia has diverse etiologies, including granulomatous disorders such as sarcoidosis, in which extrarenal 1-α-hydroxylase activity leads to excess production of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25(OH)₂D). We report a rare case of sarcoidosis presenting as isolated hypercalcemia without pulmonary involvement, complicated by initially normal 1,25(OH)₂D levels and a false-negative core biopsy. A 44-year-old man with hypertension and type 2 diabetes presented with malaise, polyuria, and polydipsia. Laboratory evaluation showed severe hypercalcemia with suppressed PTH. PTH-related peptide, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, TSH, and albumin were normal. Imaging revealed no osseous lesions or lymphadenopathy.
