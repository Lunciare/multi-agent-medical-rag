Researchers have begun to explore the HbA1c values of patients with hemoglobinopathies to create data banks of Hb variants that interfere with HbA1c measurements, thereby aiding clinicians in monitoring these patients . The United States is a heterogeneous population of ethnic­ ities and cultural backgrounds. With this diversity comes the need to acknowledge their differences, especially when consid­ ering the implications on their health. The Northern California Comprehensive Thalassemia Center has informative resources on the birth prevalence of varying Hb disorders by race and eth­ nicity that may be useful when considering hemoglobinopa­ thies . The astute clinician should acknowledge HbA1c’s limitations, varying methodologies, and the next steps for val­ ues that do not align with patient presentation. If an abnormal value is detected, the method by which the HbA1c was calcu­ lated should be verified and compared to prior samples to assess for incongruence. If conflicting data persist, clinicians should consider hemoglobin DNA sequencing to determine the exact hemoglobinopathy present and use past scientific literature to determine the HbA1c methodology best suited for that exact variant.
