For this rea­ son, such techniques are recommended for patients with a known history or those undergoing a first-time analysis. Compared to the other modalities, HPLC is affected by several rare hemoglobinopathies and can lead to erroneously high val­ ues. An inconclusive HPLC test demands further workup and requires a subsequent change in the methodology of HbA1c analysis. Beyond HbA1c, clinicians must incorporate alternative glucose monitoring methods for patients in whom HbA1c may be inconclusive. Such methodologies include fructosamine, glycated albumin, and 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) . Fructosamine measures the mean blood glucose concentration over weeks by analyzing the amount of fructose bound to se­ rum protein through glycosylation . The glycated albumin method measures the proportion of serum glycated albumin to the total albumin within the blood. The last alternative method uses 1,5-AG, which remains at a constant concentration during euglycemic states. However, as glucose levels rise, 1,5-AG levels decrease .
