These cases expand the phenotypic spectrum of X-linked AHC to include selective tubular dysfunc­ tion, evidenced by azoospermia, low inhibin B, and reduced tes­ ticular volume despite normal androgenization . Reported variants are heterogeneous, with no consistent geno­ type–phenotype correlation . The lack of genotype–phenotype correlation may reflect tissue-specific differences in NR0B1 transcriptional activity [6, 8]. NR0B1 functions as a context-dependent transcriptional repressor, with regulatory effects that vary across adrenal, hypothalamic–pituitary, Sertoli, and Leydig cells . These tissue-specific actions provide a biological basis for the heteroge­ neous presentations of NR0B1-related disease, including pheno­ types in which Sertoli cell dysfunction predominates despite preserved gonadotropin secretion, as reported in human and ex­ perimental studies . The patient described here had preserved pituitary function, indicating intact central NR0B1 transcriptional regulatory activity despite this pathogenic variant. Comparable findings have been reported in the literature. Raffin-Sanson et al described a 32-year-old man with a frame­ shift NR0B1 variant (c.116delG; p.Gly39Aspfs*12), who had nor­ mal puberty and testicular volume but developed severe oligospermia over time.
