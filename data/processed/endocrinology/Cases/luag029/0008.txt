Discussion The clinical manifestations of X-linked AHC follow a well-defined phenotypic pattern, with early-onset adrenal crises and progres­ sive impairment of the gonadal axis. Recurrent metabolic de­ compensations—often associated with hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, and dehydration—are typically triggered by inter­ current illness or physiological stress. Hypogonadotropic hypo­ gonadism usually emerges in adolescence with delayed or arrested puberty, reduced testicular volume, and infertility [7, 12]. These features, consistently observed across multiple cohorts, establish HH as a hallmark of the classical phenotype . However, our patient underwent normal pubertal devel­ opment with adequate testosterone levels and preserved sexual JCEM Case Reports, 2026, Volume 4, Issue 3 function, despite carrying a novel pathogenic nonsense NR0B1 variant. The patient presented low testicular volume and azoospermia, indicating Sertoli cell dysfunction. This was supported by borderline-low inhibin B and low AMH levels—both markers of Sertoli cell activity —favoring a tubular defect rather than gonadotropin deficiency. Although HH is typical in X-linked AHC, rare cases with spon­ taneous puberty, normal testosterone, and subsequent infertility have been described .
