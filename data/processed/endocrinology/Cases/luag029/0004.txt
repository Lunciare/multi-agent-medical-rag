According to pediatric records, pubertal onset occurred at the ex­ pected age, with Tanner stage II at approximately 11-12 years and stages III–IV between 13 and 15 years. By late adolescence, he exhibited Tanner stage V pubic hair and normal male viriliza­ tion; testicular volume was not documented in available pediat­ ric records during pubertal development. At 21 years, he remained clinically stable after switching gluco­ corticoid therapy from hydrocortisone to prednisone. Libido and erectile function were preserved. On examination, Tanner stage V for pubic hair was observed along with normal male virilization, normal penile length but reduced testicular volumes measuring 10 mL bilaterally. At age 24, he remained asymptomatic, with normal vital signs, body mass index of 20.8 kg/m2, sparse facial and chest hair, and no gynecomastia. Testicular volume re­ mained 10 mL bilaterally, below the adult reference range. Family history was negative for known X-linked AHC, although 3 maternal uncles had died in infancy of unknown causes, sug­ gesting possible undiagnosed cases in the family (Fig. 1).
