Diagnostic assessment Initial biochemical evaluation at age 7 demonstrated severe PAI, with markedly elevated plasma ACTH of 12 360 pg/mL (SI: 2719.2 pmol/L) (reference range, <46 pg/mL [SI: <10.1 pmol/L]) and low serum cortisol of 1.1 µg/dL (SI: 30.35 nmol/L) (reference range, 5-25 µg/dL [SI: 138-690 nmol/L]). Precise sodium and potas­ sium values were not documented at that time, although hypona­ tremia and hyperkalemia were reported. Further investigation ruled out autoimmune adrenalitis and adrenoleukodystrophy. Hormonal evaluation (Table 1) in adulthood confirmed persist­ ent adrenal insufficiency, with suppressed serum cortisol and markedly elevated ACTH levels despite replacement. Androgen evaluation showed normal total testosterone (355-731 ng/dL [SI: 12.32-25.39 nmol/L]; male reference range, 250-964 ng/dL [SI: 8.7-33.4 nmol/L]), normal calculated free testosterone (6.6-11.4 ng/dL [SI: 0.23-0.40 nmol/L]; reference range, >5 ng/ dL [SI: >0.17 nmol/L]) and normal gonadotropins (FSH 8.2 IU/L [reference range, 1.4-18.1 IU/L]; luteinizing hormone 1.82 IU/L [reference range, 1.5-9.3 IU/L]). Laboratory markers of tubular gonadal function were altered: inhibin B was at the lower limit of normal, and anti-Müllerian hor­ mone (AMH) levels were below the reference range for men aged 20 to 39 years (Table 1).
