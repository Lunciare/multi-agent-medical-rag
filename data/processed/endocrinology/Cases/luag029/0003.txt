. NR0B1 functions as a transcriptional repres­ sor of steroidogenic factor-1, a key regulator of adrenal and go­ nadal development . NR0B1 pathogenic variants include frameshift, nonsense, splice-site, and missense variants, and no consistent genotype–phenotype correlation has been demon­ strated, as identical variants may result in variable severity, age of onset, and patterns of gonadal involvement . This article highlights a novel NR0B1 pathogenic variant in a patient with X-linked AHC, azoospermia, and normal testoster­ one levels, emphasizing the broader phenotypic spectrum of NR0B1-related disease. This case illustrates a reproductive phenotype that deviates from the classic presentation associ­ ated with NR0B1 pathogenic variants. Case presentation A 23-year-old male with PAI and normal pubertal progression was transitioned to adult care after 16 years of follow-up in the pediatric endocrinology clinic. He experienced recurrent hospi­ talizations in early infancy due to dehydration and poor feeding, and at 7 years of age was diagnosed with PAI, presenting with ab­ dominal pain, hyperpigmentation, and biochemical evidence of combined glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid deficiency. Symptoms improved after initiation of hormone replacement therapy. Pubertal development was spontaneous and unremarkable.
