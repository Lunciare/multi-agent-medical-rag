Although combined gonadotropin therapy with recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone and human chorionic gonadotropin has been reported to induce spermatogenesis in individuals with NR0B1 variants , this approach was not pursued in this pa­ tient due to limited access and lack of routine coverage in the Brazilian Public Healthcare System. Nevertheless, it remains a consideration for assessing residual spermatogenic potential, particularly if he pursues biological paternity in the future. This case broadens the clinical spectrum of NR0B1-related disease by demonstrating adrenal insufficiency with preserved pubertal androgenization but impaired spermatogenesis, highlighting dissociation between Leydig and Sertoli cell func­ tion. Clinicians should therefore consider testing for NR0B1 variants not only in patients with adrenal insufficiency and HH, but also in those presenting with infertility despite normal testosterone levels. Early molecular diagnosis facilitates appro­ priate hormonal management, fertility evaluation, and family counseling.
