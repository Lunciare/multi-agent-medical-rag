adrenal insufficiency (PAI) and hypogonado­ tropic hypogonadism (HH). In NR0B1-related disease, the PAI phenotype typically reflects combined glucocorticoid and min­ eralocorticoid deficiency , whereas gonadal steroidogenesis may be variably affected . Adrenal insufficiency presents in the first weeks of life in most patients, although some develop symptoms later in childhood, often triggered by illness or stress . Rare late-onset forms may initially manifest with biochemical abnormalities such as elevated adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) levels . Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, resulting from hypothalam­ ic–pituitary dysfunction, usually presents in adolescence with de­ layed or arrested puberty and infertility; most individuals do not complete secondary sexual development without testosterone replacement . Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism occurs in ap­ proximately 75% to 80% of affected adolescents and is nearly universal in adulthood . The NR0B1 gene, also known as DAX1, is located on Xp21.2, contains 2 exons and encodes a 470–amino acid protein of the nuclear receptor superfamily . Pathogenic variants are rare, with an estimated prevalence of 1 in 140 000 to 1 in 1 200 000 individuals . NR0B1 functions as a transcriptional repres­ sor of steroidogenic factor-1, a key regulator of adrenal and go­ nadal development .
