There was no cervical lymphadenopathy on US. A computed tomography (CT) scan of the neck demon­ strated an intrathyroidal mass causing tracheal deviation (Fig. 1C). A fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy of the nodule was performed, which revealed atypical spindle cells with hy­ perchromatic elongated nuclei with inconspicuous nucleoli, and scant to moderate cytoplasm with indistinct cell borders, nuclear crowding, and rare colloid, suspicious for a spindle cell neoplasm or malignancy (Bethesda V) (Fig. 2A and 2B). Due to the concern for an aggressive form of thyroid cancer given the presence of spindle cells, a whole-body CT scan was obtained, which did not reveal any lymphadenopathy or distant metastasis. Treatment The patient underwent total thyroidectomy, and a right central neck lymph node dissection. On gross examination, the tumor was attached to the right thyroid lobe, surrounded by the thyroid capsule.
