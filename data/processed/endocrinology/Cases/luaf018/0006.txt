Histopathological examin­ ation revealed spindle cells with predominantly cell-rich areas (Antoni type A pattern) (Fig. 4A) with nuclear palisading (Verocay bodies) (Fig. 4B), which was consistent with thyroid schwannoma. The tumor was characterized by a low Ki-67 pro­ liferation index. There was also evidence of Hashimoto thyroi­ ditis in the background. Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining of the tumor cells revealed focal positivity for thyroid transcrip­ tion factor-1 (TTF-1), diffuse positivity for S-100 and vimentin (Fig. 5A), and negative staining for thyroglobulin (Fig. 5B) and calcitonin (Fig. 5C), further supporting the diagnosis of a thy­ roid schwannoma. Next-generation sequencing of the DNA and RNA derived from tumor tissue using Oncomine Comprehensive Assay v3 detected an NF2 p.Leu505fs frame­ shift pathogenic variant. The examination of genetic testing of the peripheral leukocytes did not reveal a germline NF2 variant. Outcome and Follow-up There were no postoperative complications, and the patient had an uneventful recovery.
