Thyroid schwannomas are exceptionally rare, with only a few cases documented in the literature [7–9]. Due to their rarity and nonspecific clinical and imaging features, they are often misdiagnosed as more common thyroid conditions, such as adenomas or carcin­ omas. Clinically, thyroid schwannomas usually present as a painless, slow-growing nodule, often discovered incidentally during imaging or physical examination. Given the scarcity of these tumors, there are limited data on their natural history and optimal management. This case report seeks to contribute to the limited knowledge on thyroid schwannomas by present­ ing a case from our center and reviewing the relevant literature management strategies associated with this entity. Case Presentation A 40-year-old woman was referred to our center for evalu­ ation of a thyroid nodule. The patient’s primary care phys­ ician noticed the thyroid nodule 2 years prior to her presentation to our clinic. This nodule had been rapidly enlar­ ging over the past several months. The patient denied symp­ toms of hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism, shortness of breath or hoarseness of voice, but mentioned occasional dys­ phagia.
