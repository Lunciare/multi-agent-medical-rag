About 60% present as asymptomatic neck swelling [7-9, 12-15], while others, including our patient, present as a progressively enlarging neck mass with compressive symp­ toms [16-18]. Although mostly occurring as solitary thyroid nodules, the occurrence of two schwannomas in a single thyroid has also been described . On ultrasound, these tumors are most often hypoechoic (∼80%) , but other presentations, such as cystic changes , heterogeneous masses , or hyperechoic nodules , have also been described. CT scans usually show well- circumscribed, homogeneous soft-tissue densities, though het­ erogeneity can occur due to cystic degeneration in paucicellu­ lar areas . Thyroid schwannomas can be challenging to diagnose sole­ ly based on FNA sampling. Spindle cells are seen in 15% to 20% of samples, as in our patient , but 65% of FNAs yield inconclusive or nondiagnostic results due to pau­ cicellular or inflammatory aspirates . Conditions such as medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) may mimic schwan­ nomas by presenting spindle cells on FNA, necessitating IHC and molecular testing for accurate diagnosis.
