Incidental nodules are detected by ultrasonography (US) in 20% to 76% of the gen­ eral population, and 20% to 48% of individuals with a palp­ able thyroid nodule show additional nodules on US . While most of these nodules are of epithelial origin, a small subset of thyroid tumors arises from mesenchymal tissue, referred to as primary thyroid mesenchymal tumors (TMTs). Mesenchymal tissues, composed of cells primarily derived from embryonic mesoderm (and to a lesser extent from ecto­ derm), interact with epithelial tissues to form nearly every or­ gan in the body . TMTs, also known as thyroid stromal tumors, arise from the stromal and vascular components of the thyroid gland . Numerous types of TMTs have been reported in the literature, including peripheral nerve sheath tumors (PNSTs) such as schwannomas and neurofibro­ mas, paragangliomas, leiomyomas, hemangiomas, and sev­ eral forms of sarcomas . Schwannomas, or neurilemmomas, are benign tumors aris­ ing from Schwann cells, which form the myelin sheath sur­ rounding peripheral nerves.
