In our cases, the blood markers were unrevealing; however, bone lesions involving the calvaria were detected synchron­ ously or metachronously with the HP lesion. This presenta­ tion should raise suspicion for LCH, even though the onset is rare in adults. LCH predominantly affects the bones (eg, skull, pelvis, femur), but other organs can be affected, includ­ ing the lungs, skin, and gastrointestinal tract . The diagno­ sis requires pathology confirmation, with demonstration of granuloma formation, neoplastic histiocyte-like dendritic cells proliferation and immunohistochemistry with CD1A, CD207, and S100 expression . Our cases of adult-onset multisystem LCH (HP and bone) had long-term follow-up, enhancing our understanding re­ garding natural course of LCH and treatment outcomes.
