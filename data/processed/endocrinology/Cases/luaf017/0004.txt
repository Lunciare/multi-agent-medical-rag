Diagnostic Assessment Patient 1 Initial pituitary magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed thickening of the pituitary infundibulum without mass effect on the chiasm (images not shown). Laboratory evaluation indicated hyperprolactinemia (76.8 ng/mL; normal: 3-27 ng/mL or μg/L) and central hypogonadism (Table 1). AVP-D was con­ firmed with water deprivation test. Normal results included chemistry, hemogram with white cell differential, thyroid function tests, α-fetoprotein, C-reactive protein, angiotensin- converting enzyme, and human chorionic gonadotropin levels (Table 1). The patient was referred to our pituitary center and was evaluated by a video visit. She was taking desmopressin 0.1-mg tablets twice per day and an oral contraceptive pill. Self-reported weight and height were 60.9 kg and 157.5 cm, respectively (body mass index, 24.6 kg/m2). After the visit, se­ rum cortisol and IGF-1 levels were normal (Table 1). After consultation with neurosurgery, the recommendation was to undergo serial imaging. After 4 months, the infundibu­ lar thickening was stable. However, after another 6 months, MRI showed slight enlargement of the lesion to 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.6 cm that abutted the optic chiasm (Figure 1A–C).
