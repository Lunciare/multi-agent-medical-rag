Cerebrospinal fluid analysis was negative for malig­ nant cells, with very few natural killer cells and B cells noted. The patient underwent transsphenoidal biopsy. Histopathology showed epithelioid histiocytes, chronic inflammation, and gliosis. The diagnosis was not clear, with differential including hypophysitis, xanthogranuloma of the sella, or histiocytosis. Postoperatively, the patient’s headaches improved, secondary hypothyroidism developed, and mild hy­ perprolactinemia and AVP-D persisted. Three-month postoperative MRI showed near-complete re­ section of the previously seen infundibular mass (Fig. 1D–F) and interval development of an enhancing T1 hypointense and diffusion restricting osseous lesion within the left parietal calvaria (Fig. 2A and B). The patient was referred to hematol­ ogy. Positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) scan was denied by insurance. A skeletal bone sur­ vey confirmed the solitary calvarial lytic lesion. After 4 months, the bone lesion increased from 1.1 to 2.5 cm with lo­ cal mass effect (Fig. 2C). Craniotomy was performed with re­ section of the calvarial lesion, and pathology confirmed LCH with V600E BRAF mutation. Fludeoxyglucose (FDG) PET-CT did not identify other involvement.
