The latest consensus statement classified LCH as unifocal (soli­ tary lesion), single system pulmonary (isolated lung in­ volvement), single system multifocal (more than 1 lesion involving any organ), and multisystem (2 or more organs or systems involved) . Cases of multisystem adult-onset LCH involving the hypothalamus and/or pituitary (HP) are rare. Here we present 2 such cases, both presenting with vaso­ pressin deficiency (AVP-D), and favorable course consisting of remission after 1 year of cytarabine therapy. Case Presentation Patient 1 A 31-year-old woman with a medical history of thyroid nod­ ules presented with secondary amenorrhea, polyuria, and during endocrine evaluation, patient reported taking desmo­ pressin 0.1-mg tablets twice per day as prescribed by primary care. She reported headaches for several years and occasional blurry vision. She denied galactorrhea. Patient 2 A healthy 23-year-old man presented with right ear fullness and tinnitus over 4 months. He noticed fatigue, decreased li­ bido, polyuria, and polydipsia for 12 months. He underwent excision of a “polyp-like lesion” in the right ear canal. Diagnostic Assessment Patient 1 Initial pituitary magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed thickening of the pituitary infundibulum without mass effect on the chiasm (images not shown).
