Abdominal computed tomography scan revealed no acute intra- abdominal pathology. An abdominal ultrasound demonstrated right and left kidneys measuring 12.5 and 12.4 cm, respectively (normal kidney size is 9-13 cm in females, 10-14 cm in males). Benign renal cysts were identified on ultrasound and were not considered clinic­ ally significant; therefore, no additional investigations were pursued. Treatment Based on these initial investigations, the patient was started on intravenous hydration. It was suspected that she most likely had nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug-induced nephrotoxicity giv­ en her acutely worsening renal function with normal complete blood count, electrolytes, and liver function. Imaging revealed no evidence of obstruction or masses. Therefore, naproxen was discontinued, and she was started on amlodipine and bisoprolol for further optimization of her blood pressure. She was subse­ quently referred to nephrology for outpatient management. Outcome and follow-up Despite these initial interventions, her renal function continued to decline over the following 2 months with her creatinine rising to 3.17 mg/dL (SI: 280 μmol/L).
