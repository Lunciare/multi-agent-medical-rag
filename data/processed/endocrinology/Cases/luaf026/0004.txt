Further evaluation of urinary markers indicated an in­ appropriately high potassium excretion, with a potassium/cre­ atinine ratio of 56.2 mmol/g and a transtubular potassium gradient of 7.4, which are highly suggestive of mineralocortic­ oid activity in the distal convoluted tubule . Diagnostic Assessment As part of the study of hypokalemia , digestive losses were ex­ cluded clinically. Magnesium levels were consistently found to be within normal limits. The possibility of covert diuretic use was also ruled out because all of the patient’s medication was supervised. Genetic conditions such as Bartter, Gitelman, Liddle, and Geller syndrome were also excluded because of the lack of ionic alterations before admission and the absence of relevant family or personal history. Deficiency of 11 β-hydroxylase and/or 17 α-hydroxylase were considered unlike­ ly because of the absence of signs of hyperandrogenism or pre­ the levels of 11-deoxycortisol, deoxycorticosterone, testoster­ one, adrenal androgens, 17-hydroxyprogesterone, or the urin­ ary metabolites 17-hydroxysteroid and 17-ketosteroid.
