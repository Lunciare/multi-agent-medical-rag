Furthermore, on review of the 16 other published case reports of ectopic TSH-secreting pituitary ad­ enomas, 5 included details of a TRH stimulation test. Of these, one patient had an absent response to TRH (stimulated TSH < 2 μU/mL) , 2 had a decreased response to TRH (stimulated TSH < 200% of baseline) , whereas the oth­ er 2 had a positive response (stimulated TSH > 200% of base­ line) . Hence, it is unclear whether the recommended diagnostic cutoffs for the TSH response to TRH in TSH- omas can be extrapolated to ectopic pituitary tissue secreting TSH. The majority of TSH-omas secrete only TSH (72%), with the remainder of tumors usually co-secreting GH or prolactin, since these pituitary hormones all share the Pit1 transcription factor. Co-secretion of gonadotropins with TSH is extremely rare.
