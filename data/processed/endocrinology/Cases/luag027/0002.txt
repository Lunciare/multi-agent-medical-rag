Key Words anaplastic thyroid cancer, dabrafenib, trametinib, BRAF V600E, perforation, adverse events Anaplastic thyroid cancer (ATC) is a rare condition and represents approximately 2% of thyroid cancers. It originates from thyroid follicular cells, which lose their characteristic follicular epithelial differentiation . The median overall survival for ATC is ap­ proximately 6 months from diagnosis . Pure ATC’s prognosis is worse compared to mixed ATC, which can include areas of well-differentiated (DTC) or poorly differen­ tiated thyroid cancer (PDTC). A longer overall survival in patients with mixed ATC compared with pure ATC has been reported in large retrospective series [4-6] and identified as an independent prognostic factor . Nevertheless, the prognosis remains poor, and the disease should be managed similarly to pure ATC through multimodal treatment, which has been shown to im­ prove survival . Although the life expectancy remains poor in most cases, sev­ eral treatment options are available based on the stage of the disease at the time of diagnosis. These options primarily include surgery and chemoradiation for Stages IVA and some IVB cases, while systemic therapies should be considered for Stage IVC
