The lack of similar reported cases emphasizes the importance of real-world experience in recognizing and documenting new or unexpected adverse events. Close monitoring is crucial to iden­ tify both severe and mild complications early. While D+T treat­ ment remains an effective option for ATC, an aggressive cancer with a very poor prognosis that requires urgent intervention, therapy should be as personalized as possible in terms of drug choice, dosage, and vigilant patient monitoring. Learning points • Rapid testing for BRAF V600E should be performed in pa­ tients with pure or mixed ATC because it is an actionable mutation • Systemic treatment with selective kinase inhibitors dabrafe­ nib (BRAF inhibitor) and trametinib (MEK 1/2 inhibitor) is ef­ fective in controlling ATC with a low rate of severe adverse events • Organ perforations, mainly of the trachea, have been docu­ mented with multikinase antiangiogenic drugs (ie, lenvati­ nib, cabozantinib) but, previously, not with dabrafenib and trametinib. • In cases of BRAF mutant ATC, which often extensively invades adjacent vital structures, therapy with dabrafenib and tra­ metinib should be initiated as soon as possible. Still, careful monitoring for potential aerodigestive perforation is needed.
