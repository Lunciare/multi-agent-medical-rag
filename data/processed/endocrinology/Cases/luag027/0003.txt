Moreover, several pathogenic variants involved in ATC tumorigenesis have been described [11-13]. Some of these can be targeted by selective therapies . BRAF V600E is the most common pathogenic variant in ATC (19-48%), which is also found in approximately 60% of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) cases . Recently, the results of the Rare Oncology Agnostic Research (ROAR) trial showed an object­ ive response rate (ORR) of 56%, including three complete re­ sponses, and progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of 6.7 and 14.5 months, respectively, in BRAF V600E-mutant ATC cases treated with the combination of dabra­ fenib (selective inhibitor of BRAF) and trametinib (inhibitor of MEK 1/2) (D+T) . Accordingly, D+T was recently approved as a first-line systemic treatment for BRAF-mutant ATC cases . Compared to multikinase inhibitors (ie, lenvatinib, vandeta­ nib, cabozantinib), D+T treatment has been linked to milder ad­ verse events (AEs), such as fever (47%), anemia (33%), fatigue (33%), nausea (33%), decreased appetite (33%), and rash (28%).
