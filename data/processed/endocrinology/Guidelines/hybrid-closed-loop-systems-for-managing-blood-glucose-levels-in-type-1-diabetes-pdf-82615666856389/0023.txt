The EAG did scenario analyses including a regression analysis (see section 3.6), which linked different baseline HbA1c values to changes in HbA1c. This resulted in ICERs ranging: • from £158,444 for a baseline HbA1c of 57 mmol/mol (7.4%) with a 6 mmol/mol (-0.56 percentage point) reduction • to £50,243 for a baseline HbA1c of 75 mmol/mol (9.0%) with an 11 mmol/mol (-0.97 percentage point) reduction. The committee said that all these analyses were useful to help understand how the ICER would change with different changes in HbA1c. It further noted that the change in HbA1c reported in the NHSE adult study pilot was a good representation of what could be achieved for people with higher HbA1c levels. In the EAG's original analysis, a baseline HbA1c of 79 mmol/mol (9.4%) and a reduction of 16.2 mmol/mol (-1.5 percentage points) showed HCL systems to be cost effective. But the committee said that using this data in the model would be equivalent to restricting HCL system access to people with much higher-than-average HbA1c levels.
