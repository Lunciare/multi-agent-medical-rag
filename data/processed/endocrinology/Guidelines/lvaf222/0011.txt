Most importantly, the number of functioning parathyroid glands remaining in situ postoperatively is a key determinant of the risk of developing postoperative hypocalcaemia (16% for cases with 1-2 pre­ served glands, 6% for 3 glands, and 2.5% for four glands)28-30. All these factors correlate with reduced PTH se­ cretion immediately following surgery. If the PTH concentra­ tion is > 10 pg/mL (1.05 pmol/L) 12 to 24 hours after surgery, progression to permanent HypoPT is very unlikely. However, many patients with lower PTH levels may still recover from postoperative HypoPT13. Notably, in patients with preoperative hypercalcaemia, such as in primary hyperparathyroidism, calcium levels may take several days to normalise after surgery, during which PTH levels may be very low28-31.
