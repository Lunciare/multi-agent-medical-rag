A meta-analysis based on seven observational studies showed an increased risk of breast cancer, especially for women over 50 years old (OR 1.52; 95% CI 1.34-1.72).269 However, data was lacking on previous use of oral contraceptives and concomitant use of MHT. Body mass index (BMI) affects breast cancer risk in women using combined MHT so that obesity (BMI > 30) is associated with a significantly lower attributable risk compared to wom­ en who do not have obesity.263 Oestrogen only treatment. Women who have no uterus can be offered oestrogen-only treatment (see R 2.5). The increase in breast cancer risk with oestrogen alone, particularly conju­ gated equine oestrogen, is non-significant or considerably less compared with combined treatment.270 In fact, the WHI-study showed a non-significant reduced risk of invasive breast cancer after an average of 7.2 years of treatment with oestrogen alone, corresponding to seven fewer cases of breast cancer per 10 000 woman-years and HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.61-1.02.185 Long-term follow-up (>20 years) of the same study showed continued reduced breast cancer incidence with oestrogen alone (HR 0.78; 95% CI, 0.65-0.93),271 as does a recent meta-analysis.138 On the other hand, several ob­ servational studies
