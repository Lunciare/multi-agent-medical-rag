The route of administration of HT appears to be important when considering risk for VTE. Whereas oral HT increases risk, when administered by the transdermal route of administration it does not increase risk above baseline. Oral oestrogens undergo first-pass metabolism in the liver, which results in an increase in coagulation factors, triglycer­ ides and C-reactive protein, adverse changes that may affect cardiovascular and thromboembolic disease risk.194-197 Mostly observational and some RCT data suggest that oral, but not transdermal oestrogen is associated with increased VTE risk.198-200 Additionally, the type of progestogen used for endometrial protection may also impact risk of VTE, with micronized progesterone and pregnane derivatives (in­ cluding dydrogesterone, medrogestone, chlormadinone acet­ ate, cyproterone acetate, and possibly medroxyprogesterone acetate) associated with no increased risk (OR 0.7; 95% CI, 0.3 to 1.9 and OR 0.9; 95% CI, 0.4 to 2.3, respectively) al­ though not all studies give consistent results.
