However, it is important to consider the possibility of peri­ menopause even in the absence of typical symptoms, particu­ larly in younger women. The change in biology and ovarian function is gradual and encompasses the perimenopause, and symptoms may start during this time which may require treatment. R 1.2 We recommend that perimenopause is considered when menstrual irregularity and/or vasomotor symptoms are present, even in younger women (40 to less than 45 years). (Good Clinical Practice) Menstrual irregularity is described within the STRAW criteria (Figure 1) and is a cycle length that falls outside 21-35 days and may vary from month to month. However, cycle length may decrease during the later years of the reproductive phase. R 1.3 We recommend that premature ovarian insufficiency is considered in women under 40 years of age in the presence of menstrual irregularity and/or subfertility and/or vasomotor symptoms.
