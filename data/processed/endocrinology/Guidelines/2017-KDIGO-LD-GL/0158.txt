Selection: Criteria for Acceptable Predonation GFR GFR in the General Population GFR in young men and women of 90 mL/min per 1.73 m2 or greater is generally considered normal.124 GFR between 60 and 89 mL/min per 1.73 m2 is considered to be decreased compared with the usual level for young adults, but does not meet the KDIGO criterion for CKD. GFR declines with age, although the cause of decline is not known and the rate of decline appears widely variable. Most data are based on cross-sectional studies, in which mean GFR is lower in older populations than in young populations. Other kidney functions are also lower in older populations (eg, renal plasma flow, maximal urinary concentration) and kidney struc- ture is altered in older populations (eg, cortical atrophy, global glomerulosclerosis, nephrosclerosis). There is debate about whether abnormalities in kidney function and structure in older people represents normal aging or disease.
