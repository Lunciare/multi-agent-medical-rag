For example, if a transplant program sets the acceptable lifetime post- donation ESKD risk threshold at 5%, and assumes a donation- attributable RR of 3.5 to 5.3 according to sex and race, then the acceptable predonation lifetime ESKD risk threshold would be approximately 1.0-1.5. This strategy enables decision-making based on a more comprehensive and inte- grated assessment of risk factors than is currently practiced, but application of the currently available online tool at this time requires clinician insight and interpretation.37 Cur- rently, there remains uncertainty in lifetime risk estimates, particularly for younger donor candidates, donors from de- veloping countries, and ethnicities other than black or white race. At this time, transplant programs and donor candidates may consider other factors in their acceptance criteria for living kidney donation in addition to quantitative risk esti- mates. We see this work as starting point, and advocate strongly for continued efforts to improve the precision, tai- loring and generalizability of predonation and postdonation risk estimates.
