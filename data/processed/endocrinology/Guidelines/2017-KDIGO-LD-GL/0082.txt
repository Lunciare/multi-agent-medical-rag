Minimizing the lifetime risk of kidney failure in accepted donors is important to safeguard the practice, re- gardless of the degree to which it can be established that donation contributed to the risk of kidney failure. Challenges to determining the postdonation lifetime risk of kidney failure based on current studies include limitations of study follow-up (the largest studies followed most donors for less than 2 decades rather than for their lifetime).30 The risk of kidney failure after donation is nonlinear, and is expected to be higher later (≥10 years) than earlier (<10 years) after donation.31 When the WG was convened, 2 recent studies reported that the risk of kidney failure is higher in donors compared with risk among nondonors with similar base- line demographics.
