Two injectable ultra-rapid-acting analog (URAA) insulin formulations are available that contain excipients that ac- celerate absorption and provide more ac- tivity in the ﬁrst portion of their proﬁle compared with the other RAA (8,9). In- haled human insulin has a rapid peak and shortened duration of action compared with RAA (10) (see also subsection ALTERNA- TIVE INSULIN ROUTES IN PHARMACOLOGIC THERAPY FOR ADULTS WITH TYPE 2 DIABETES). These newer for- mulations may cause less hypoglycemia while improving postprandial glucose excursions and administration ﬂexibility (in relation to prandial intake) compared with RAA (10–12). In addition, longer-acting basal analogs (U-300 glargine or deglu- dec) may confer a lower hypoglycemia risk compared with U-100 glargine in in- dividuals with type 1 diabetes (13,14). Despite the advantages of insulin ana- logs in individuals with type 1 diabetes, the expense and/or complexity of treat- ment required for their use may be pro- hibitive (Table 9.1).
