There is currently no consensus in terms of whether patients with pitNETs should be screened routinely for MEN1. It is reasonable, however, to screen for hyperparathyroidism by checking serum calcium level in all patients diagnosed with a pitNET.40 If hyper- parathyroidism is not present, the likelihood of MEN1 is low.26 Evaluation Clinical manifestations of pitNET in patients with MEN1 are similar to those with sporadic cases. Endocrine testing for pituitary hormonal excess should be performed for all patients with pitNETs whether found incidentally on imaging or based on signs and symptoms (Table 2). Prolactin and IGF-1 levels should be checked to screen for prolactinoma and acromegaly, respectively. If signs or symptoms of hypercortisolemia are present, patients should be screened for Cushing disease with a 1-mg overnight dexametha- sone suppression test, 24-hour urine free cortisol, and/or late-night salivary cortisol levels.41
