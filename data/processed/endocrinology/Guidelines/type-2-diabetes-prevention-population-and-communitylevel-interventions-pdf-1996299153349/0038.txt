Carbohydrate comes from many different kinds of food and drink, including starchy foods such as bread, potatoes and chapatis; fruit; some dairy products; sugar and other sweet foods (Diabetes UK 2010). HbA1c Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) forms when red cells are exposed to glucose in the plasma. The HbA1c test reflects average plasma glucose over the previous eight to 12 weeks. Unlike the oral glucose tolerance test, an HbA1c test can be performed at any time of the day and does not require any special preparation such as fasting. HbA1c is a continuous risk factor for type 2 diabetes. This means there is no fixed point when people are or are not at risk. The World Health Organization recommends a level of 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) for HbA1c as the cut-off point for diagnosing type 2 diabetes in non- pregnant adults.
