However, unlike the evidence regarding female subfertility and thyroid diseas- es, the evidence regarding male subfertility and thyroid diseases is limited. Thyroid Dysfunction and Male Subfertility TH may influence male fertility both prenatally, by in- tervening on Sertoli cell maturing , and postnatally, having an effect on Leydig cell differentiation and ste- roidogenesis . It has been reported that 7.4% of men of subfertile couples may present SCH and 3.7% subclin- ical hyperthyroidism, respectively . Both conditions are believed to reduce mitochondrial activity and increase oxidative stress, thus negatively influencing male fertility. Interestingly, erectile function seems to be dependent on TH concentrations, so that decreased TH concentrations is associated with delayed ejaculation, whereas increased TH concentrations above the normal range are associated with premature ejaculation . A large number of studies have demonstrated adverse effects of hypothyroidism on male fertility in foetal, early neonatal, or even adult life. Increased abnormalities in sperm morphology and motility have been described in case of prolonged OH in adults, though consistency among study results is lacking concerning SCH, possibly due to heterogeneity among study samples .
