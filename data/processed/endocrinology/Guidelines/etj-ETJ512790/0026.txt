RAI and Fertility Considerations Recent reports on a potential negative impact of RAI therapy on ovarian reserve have raised a lot of attention. In 3 prospective studies, serial AMH measurements were performed prior to RAI therapy and regularly thereafter for up to 48 months [61–63]. In a Dutch study that in- cluded 65 women with a mean age of 32 years, AMH lev- els significantly declined during the first 12 months (i.e., −55%) and stabilized thereafter in the single RAI group, while a further decline was demonstrated (i.e., −85% after 48 months) after multiple RAI treatments. The steepest decrease was observed in women aged 35 years and above . Yaish et al. showed comparable results with a nadir at 3 months after treatment and a loss of about 30% from baseline after 12 months. Whether RAI treatment for well-differentiated thyroid cancers was related to low- er birth rates was evaluated in a large US registry-based study of 18,850 women.
