Assessing Thyroid Status with “Pulsatile” T3 Levels on LT3 Replacement May Be Different Fluctuations in serum T3 are relatively narrow within individuals with endogenous thyroid functioning . As discussed in Topic 5, the short half-life of T3 means that dosing even three times a day results in significant hourly fluctuations that are not seen in individuals with an intact HPT axis receiving LT4 alone (Fig. 5). This leads to two problems. First, it is not clear which serum T3 lev- el should be used to assess appropriate dosing – trough or peak – and how this should be compared with “reference levels” from individuals with an intact HPT axis. For ex- ample, if peak levels are above the “reference range” but trough levels are in the lower part of the reference range, is this “overdosing” or “underdosing?” Trough levels are most likely easier to measure, because of the inherent variability in the magnitude and timing of the T3 peak.
