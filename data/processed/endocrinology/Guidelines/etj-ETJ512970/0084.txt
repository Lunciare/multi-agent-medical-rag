DTE might be attractive to participants, as there is considerable interest in DTE expressed by patients and patient advocacy groups, and DTE is preferred by many patients in uncon- trolled studies . Inclusion of DTE would, how- ever, add to cost, trial size, and complexity, and perhaps the need for additional safety monitoring because of the high T3/T4 ratio contained in the DTE. Overencapsula- tion may be the preferred means of masking the study drug to avoid patients potentially identifying DTE based on its aroma or taste. The resultant tablets should be of identical smell and taste, and, if possible, tamper proof. Superiority versus Noninferiority Trial To change the status quo and identify a reason for using LT3, rather than maintaining therapy with LT4, a trial de- signed to show superiority seems desirable. If a noninferior- ity trial was solely examining safety, then it could demon- strate that combination therapy (performed in a controlled manner) does not cause harm to patients.
