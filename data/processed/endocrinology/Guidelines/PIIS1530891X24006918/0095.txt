Clinical Considerations Niacin has been used to reduce TG levels, LDL-C, and ApoB and to increase HDL-C in patients with dyslipidemia, with the goal of reducing MI and ASCVD. Niacin is typically prescribed in doses of 500 to 2000 mg daily and is contraindicated in persons with active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations in liver transaminases, active peptic ulcers, or arterial bleeding. Niacin is not used in com- bination with statins due to increased adverse events.
