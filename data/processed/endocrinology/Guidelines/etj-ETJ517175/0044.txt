However, prevalence, severity, and duration of ICM-in- duced Hypo in neonates remain largely unknown. A sys- temic review, including eleven studies (182 neonates) at- tempted to determine the risk of Hypo after ICM exposure, found inconsistent results for term infants (one study did not show an increased risk, while another did find a trend towards increased TSH levels and decreased FT4 levels) . In contrast, studies including premature infants found trends toward increased serum TSH, decreased T3 and FT4, and the need for LT4 treatment (8.3% of the term infants and 18.3% of the premature infants were treated for ICM- induced Hypo). Unfortunately, all studies included in the systemic review were highly affected by bias . In con- trast, mild, transient, neonatal Hypo was seen following ex- posure to iodine via caesarean section but not via exposure to contrast media .
