Baseline Thyroid Function Should Be Screened before IRT? What Are the Contraindications to IRT? Around one-third of patients with no previous history of thy- roid disorders will develop TD following alemtuzumab [12–14]; therefore, it is important to assess their baseline thyroid function. Small independent studies have suggested that the risk of TD is increased in subjects with established TA . Where thy- roidectomy or radioiodine (RAI) ablation has been performed for GD the risk of recurrence would be expected to be low. How- ever, caution is advised in patients with GD in remission after previous medical therapy, and also those with previous lympho- cytic (autoimmune) or post-partum thyroiditis. In patients on thyroid hormone replacement for hypothyroidism, caution is also advised as fluctuation in the autoimmune state and thyroid status is possible; there is currently no evidence to quantify this risk. Recommendations 1a. We recommend the measurement of thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) in all subjects before IRT. Free-thyroxine (FT4), and free-triiodothyronine (FT3) if available, should be measured where TSH is abnormal (1, ∅∅○○). 1b.
