Discussion and Future Developments The present guidelines provide recommendations and suggestions for the clinical management of TD of autoim- mune etiology triggered by IRT. The commonest cause is alemtuzumab treatment of individuals with active relaps- ing remitting MS, followed by HAART treatment in HIV gorithm toward alemtuzumab-induced thyroid disor- ders, highlighting the differences with the spontaneous form and guiding the clinician through key examinations for correct diagnosis and appropriate clinical manage- ment. Less commonly, TD occurs after BMT/HSCT; however, as different mechanisms of radiation-induced thyroid damage are also involved in the development of TD, it does not represent a central topic for these guide- lines. The prevalence of TD post-alemtuzumab is likely to increase in the near future since alemtuzumab was only licensed for MS in 2013, and thyroid complications are a delayed adverse effect of treatment. However, efforts are being made to mitigate the autoimmunity induced by alemtuzumab, and a wider spectrum of effective treat- ments for MS are being developed (including but not lim- ited to ocrelizumab, cladribine, opicinumab), which ap- pear not to be associated with autoimmunity (although have their own set of side effects) [91–93].
