CCM-aligned programs also improved blood pressure levels and processes of di- abetes care (e.g., screening for complica- tions of diabetes), though there was no impact on cholesterol levels, tobacco use, or weight (17). Multiple studies have ex- amined individual components of the CCM with respect to diabetes manage- ment and have found inconsistent levels of beneﬁt with case management, team- based care, use of electronic patient regis- tries, clinician education, clinician and pa- tient reminders, and patient education and promotion of individual self-manage- ment (18). The inconsistencies in ﬁndings may be driven by heterogeneity of interven- tions, settings, and evaluation strategies. Collaborative, interprofessional teams, which can bring together multiple disci- payors, and community partners, are best suited to provide care for people with chronic conditions such as diabetes and to facilitate individuals’ self-management (Table 1.1) (19–25).
