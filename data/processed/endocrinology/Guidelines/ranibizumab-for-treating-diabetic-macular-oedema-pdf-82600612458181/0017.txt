In this way, a mathematical relationship was assumed between visual acuity in the treated eye and the health-related quality of life of people with diabetic macular oedema. Tests of interaction demonstrated that neither treatment allocation nor duration of treatment at the time of measurement had a significant impact on the relationship between utility and visual acuity category. Therefore, the utility value was derived for each visual acuity category using the whole data set, without any adjustment variables. The results of the regression suggested that, depending on visual acuity in the treated eye, the utility value ranged from 0.860 to 0.547. The utility value for the best health state (visual acuity 86 to 100 letters) resulting from the regression was 0.831; however, this result was considered anomalous, because it was based on relatively few data points and it was thought unrealistic that utility should be worse than in the next-best state, in which vision is inferior.
