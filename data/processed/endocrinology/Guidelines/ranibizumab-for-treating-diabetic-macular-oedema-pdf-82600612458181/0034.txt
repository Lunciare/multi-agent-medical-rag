The analysis applied reduced costs associated with severe visual impairment because fewer people would go blind in both eyes. The analysis assumed that treating the second eye would result in utility gains a quarter the magnitude of those achieved by treating the first eye; this is because the health-related quality of life of people who can see well with both eyes is only a little better than the health-related quality of life of people who can see well with 1 eye.
