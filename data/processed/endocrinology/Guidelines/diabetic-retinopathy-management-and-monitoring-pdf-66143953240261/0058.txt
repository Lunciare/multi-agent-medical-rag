Return to recommendations Treatment strategies for non-centre-involving clinically significant diabetic macular oedema Recommendation 1.6.3 Why the committee made the recommendation There were very few studies on treatment for people with non-centre-involving diabetic macular oedema, making it difficult to determine which is the most effective treatment option for this group (see evidence review G). However, the committee discussed how, in their experience, the use of macular laser for people with non-centre-involving macular oedema is current practice and is important, as it can delay the need for anti-VEGF treatment. They thought a recommendation was important for this group because, without treatment, their disease will progress to centre-involving macular oedema and they will be at higher risk of complications, such as vision loss. The committee's experience was supported by evidence from 1 study with high- to moderate-quality outcomes in the review on thresholds for starting treatment (see evidence review B).
