Page 51 of 61 How the recommendations might affect practice Support for breastfeeding exists but is not consistently available in primary care and community services. There may be some costs associated with improving breastfeeding support services through enhancing face-to-face discussions, virtual or remote contacts, and drop-in group sessions. However, improving breastfeeding rates could bring cost savings to the healthcare system as a whole because breastfeeding is associated with the prevention of breast and ovarian cancer, diabetes and obesity in breastfeeding people, as well as prevention of infections and obesity in babies.
