Adults may retain sufﬁ- cient b-cell function to prevent DKA for many years; such individuals may have remission characterized by decreased in- sulin needs for months or years, eventu- ally become dependent on insulin for survival, and are at risk for DKA (33–35, 59,60). At this later stage of the disease, there is little or no insulin secretion, as manifested by low or undetectable levels of plasma C-peptide. Immune-mediated diabetes is the most common form of diabetes in childhood and adolescence, but it can occur at any age. Autoimmune destruction of b-cells has multiple ge- netic factors and is also related to envi- ronmental factors that are still poorly deﬁned. Although individuals did not clas- sically have obesity when they presented with type 1 diabetes, obesity is increas- ingly common in the general population; as such, obesity should not preclude testing for type 1 diabetes.
