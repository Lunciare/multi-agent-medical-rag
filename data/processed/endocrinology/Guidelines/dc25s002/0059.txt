E Pancreatic diabetes (also termed pan- creatogenic diabetes or type 3c diabetes) includes both structural (e.g., destruction or removal of normal pancreatic tissue) and functional loss of glucose-normalizing insulin secretion in the context of exo- crine pancreatic dysfunction and is com- monly misdiagnosed as type 2 diabetes. The diverse set of etiologies includes pancreatitis (acute and chronic pancre- atic inﬂammation and associated ﬁbrosis leading to loss of functional exocrine and endocrine pancreatic function), trauma or pancreatectomy, neoplasia, cystic ﬁbrosis (addressed later in this section), hemochro- matosis, ﬁbrocalculous pancreatopathy, rare genetic disorders, and idiopathic forms (2); as such, pancreatic diabetes is the pre- ferred umbrella term (116).
