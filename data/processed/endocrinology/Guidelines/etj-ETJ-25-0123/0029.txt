Most of the commonly prescribed drugs act by sequestrating the hormone in the intestinal content, thus considerably decreasing LT4 bioavailability. This mechanism of interference is shared by bile acid sequestrants, such as colesevelam, cholestyramine, and/or phosphate binders, sevelamer hydrochloride or lanthanum, and even antacids containing aluminum hydroxide or sucralfate. These latter, however, may alternatively act by altering gastric acidity (14). Indeed, several studies have demonstrated that patients with decreased gastric acid secretion, owed to the use of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), may need higher LT4 doses to achieve target TSH levels, long-term PPI administration presenting a heightened risk in these cases (47, 78).
