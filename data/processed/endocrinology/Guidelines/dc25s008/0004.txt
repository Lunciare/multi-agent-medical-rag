C Consider combin- ing treatment approaches if appro- priate. E Obesity is deﬁned by the World Health Organization as an abnormal or excessive fat accumulation that presents a risk to health (25). BMI (calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters [kg/m2]) has been used widely to diagnose and stage obesity (overweight: BMI 25–29.9 kg/m2; obesity class I: BMI 30–34.9 kg/m2; obesity class II: BMI 35–39.9 kg/m2; obesity class III: BMI $40 kg/m2); however, BMI should not be relied on as a sole diagnostic and staging tool (19).
