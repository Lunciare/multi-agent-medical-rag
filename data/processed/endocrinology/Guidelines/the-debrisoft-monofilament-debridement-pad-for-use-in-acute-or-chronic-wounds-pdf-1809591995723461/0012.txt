of The Debrisoft pad was used for wound debridement by 25 nurses (62.5%), for hyperkeratosis by 4 nurses (10%), and for both by 11 nurses (27.5%). Thirty-eight of the nurses (95%) said that patients' skin condition improved, whereas 2 (5%) said that it remained the same. Thirty-two of the nurses (80%) reported a positive impact on the wound bed using visual assessment. Thirty-four nurses (85%) reported that after debridement, there was clearer visibility of the wound bed and surrounding skin because of the removal of debris, slough or hyperkeratosis, so they were able to identify clearer wound management objectives. Six out of 40 nurses (15%) said there was no improvement. The time taken to carry out debridement using the Debrisoft pad was 0–2 minutes in 8 patients (20%); 3–5 minutes in 21 patients (52.5%) and 6–10 minutes in 9 patients (22.5%). The overall performance of the Debrisoft pad was rated as 'very good' by 24 nurses (60%), 'good' by 10 nurses (25%), 'fairly good' by 5 nurses (12.5%) and 'poor' by 1 nurse (2.5%).
