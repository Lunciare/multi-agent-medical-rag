Current management 2.5 Debridement is the removal of dead, damaged tissue or haematoma from a wound. Several techniques are used for debridement, depending on the nature of the wound. In the community these are likely to include mechanical, autolytic and biosurgical techniques. Debridement can be carried out with or without analgesia depending on the degree of wound pain, the site, size and severity of the wound as well as the patient's preference. 2.6 The NICE guideline on pressure ulcers states that standard practice in the management of chronic wounds includes wound debridement to remove dead tissue, and that clinicians should recognise the potential benefit of debridement in the management of pressure ulcers. 2.7 The NICE guideline on diabetic foot problems recommends that diabetic foot ulcers can be managed using
