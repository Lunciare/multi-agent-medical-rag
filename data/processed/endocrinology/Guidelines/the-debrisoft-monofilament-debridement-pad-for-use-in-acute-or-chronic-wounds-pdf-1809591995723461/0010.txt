The Debrisoft pad was able to remove most of the coatings in exudating, seropurulent wounds with highly viscous yellow slough (indicating local infection) after a single use. Most of the material removed by debridement became attached to the pad. In dry wounds with serocrusts between the new vital granulation and epithelial tissue, the Debrisoft pad was able to remove the crusts without affecting the new healthy tissue. In wounds with necrotic layers, hyperkeratotic debris and crusts of dried exudate, the Debrisoft pad removed the necrotic layers after a single use and revealed the skin of the lower extremity, showing an almost normal epidermis. For both types of wound, the Debrisoft pad was able to debride without affecting the new healthy tissue, which was undisturbed by the debridement process. 3.7 Johnson et al.
