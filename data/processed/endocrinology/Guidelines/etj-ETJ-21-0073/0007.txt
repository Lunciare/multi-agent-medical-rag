Pediatric Graves’ disease
KEYWORDS: thyroid, treatment, atd, patients, pediatric, disease, dose, rai, hyperthyroidism, thyroid hormone, hormone, tshrab, tsh, thyroidectomy, cbz

Either carbimazole (CBZ) or its active metabolite methimazole (MMI) should be used in young people with GD. Propylthiouracil should not be used (1,ØØØØ). • The initial antithyroid drug (ATD) dose is between 0.15 and 0.5 mg/kg of MMI or between 0.25 and 0.75 mg/kg of CBZ daily. Both drugs are given once daily (1,ØØØO). • Dose titration (DT) approach: with a DT approach, a starting dose of 0.15–0.3 mg/kg MMI or 0.25–0.5 mg/kg CBZ will normalize thyroid hormone concentrations in most patients within the first 4–6 weeks. The dose is then reduced by 25–50% according to prevailing thyroid function tests. Larger doses of ATD up to 0.5 mg/kg MMI or 0.75 mg/kg CBZ can be administered in more severe, symptomatic cases (1,ØØØO). • The treatment regimen may not require adjustment if FT4 or FT3 is relatively high but TSH is normal (1,ØOOO). • Education about GD and especially its treatment is essential to optimize compliance, with attention to the developmental age (1,ØØOO). • Block and replace (BR) approach: a dose of 0.3–0.5 mg/kg MMI or 0.5–0.75 mg/kg CBZ will prevent endogenous thyroid hormone production in most patients.
