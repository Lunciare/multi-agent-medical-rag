As a result, they also agreed that current guidance to measure HbA1c levels every 3 months should be retained but supplemented with new recommendations, to make provision for an initial visit earlier than 3 months after diagnosis and for the use of data from capillary blood glucose monitoring or CGM. This is because combining HbA1c and glucose monitoring data will allow seeing trends quicker than changes in HbA1c levels alone. The target HbA1c level of 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or lower was chosen because: • it can be used to diagnose type 2 diabetes and • staying below this level is recommended to minimise the risk of long-term complications (see recommendation 1.3.31). The committee agreed, using their knowledge and experience, that a first visit to review glucose data should take place after 4 weeks of metformin monotherapy.
