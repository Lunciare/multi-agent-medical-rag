How the recommendations might affect practice Liraglutide and dulaglutide are relatively expensive compared to other possible treatments but recommending them is unlikely to surpass NICE's £1 million threshold for significant resource impact. Empagliflozin is approximately half the price of both liraglutide and dulaglutide. However, there was insufficient evidence to construct a full cost-effectiveness model. The committee indicated that the difference in unit cost per dose is relatively small, especially when considering the low prevalence of type 2 diabetes in children and young people. Similar considerations apply to using insulin at diagnosis where the prevalence of type 2 diabetes combined with a high HbA1c level or high blood ketones is even lower. Increased support from a paediatric diabetic nurse and consultant will be needed when the child or young person starts on liraglutide, dulaglutide or empagliflozin. However, once the child or young person's HbA1c levels are stabilised, this will no longer be required because repeat prescriptions can be made by the GP. Insulin is a last resort in the management of type 2 diabetes in children and young people and the recommendations are not expected to substantially affect practice.
