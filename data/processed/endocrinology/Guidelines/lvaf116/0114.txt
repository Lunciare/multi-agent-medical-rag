Therefore, direct evidence is limited to 2 RCTs comparing limited CGM use in addition to SMBG vs SMBG that included a small number of individuals with T2DM in pregnancy that could be analyzed separately from individuals with T1DM or insulin-requiring gestational diabetes (GDM): the Copenhagen RCT202 and the GlucoMOMS RCT200. The total number of individuals with T2DM in both studies was n = 109 (56 on CGM and 53 on standard therapy). The Copenhagen RCT included n = 123 individuals with T1DM and only 31 in­ dividuals with T2DM (16 with CGM and 15 controls), who were randomized to rtCGM for 6 days at 5 gestational periods during pregnancy. The GlucoMOMS study also included many more T1DM (n = 109) and GDM individuals on insulin (n = 108) than individuals with T2DM (n = 78), who were randomized to wear a CGM for about 6 days every 6 weeks but were blinded to the results. Our meta-analysis combining the T2DM individuals in these trials (n = 109) demonstrated no difference in LGA15. These results were consistent with an­ other published meta-analysis.
