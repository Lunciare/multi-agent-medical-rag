A 2023 retrospective cohort study of CGM use in 102 preg­ nancies complicated by T1DM explored the use of unique CGM parameters244. For LGA, second and third trimester HbA1c, mean glucose, TAR, and TIR were all predictors, with TAR in the third trimester being the strongest. Overnight and fasting glucose levels were not explored. The study provided no comparison of CGM TIR to traditional glu­ cose targets, but, similar to the secondary analyses of CONCEPTT, it showed variation in the strength of associ­ ation of various CGM metrics with selected outcomes across the timeline of pregnancy. A 2024 retrospective cohort analysis of 117 individuals us­ ing CGM during pregnancy complicated by preexisting T1DM and T2DM found that TIR was again associated with LGA and neonatal hypoglycemia but not SGA206. Each 5-percentage point increase in TIR reduced odds of the neo­ natal composite outcome (LGA, SGA, neonatal intensive care unit [NICU] admission, neonatal hypoglycemia, birth trauma, and hyperbilirubinemia) by 28% (odds ratio [OR], 0.72; 95% CI: 0.58-0.89)206.
