Concerns for metformin use in pregnancy are primar­ ily due to the risk of fetal growth restriction in some individuals and long-term fetal programming potential for childhood obes­ ity, with increased fat mass reported in some childhood cohorts but not others135–139. Unlike insulin, metformin has been found to exhibit marked maternal-to-fetal transfer, achieving fetal lev­ els at least as high as maternal levels due to high expression of OCT3 cation transporters in the placenta in the second and third trimesters140. Metformin is concentrated in both fetal and pla­ cental mitochondria 1000-fold. Metformin has pleiotropic ef­ fects, including activating AMP kinase (AMPK) and inhibiting mitochondrial complex 1 respiration, and has antiproliferative and nutrient restriction properties through AMPK and decreased mTOR134,141,142. Notably, it crosses the placenta poorly in the first trimester and does not increase the risk of major malforma­ tions134,143. Pathophysiologic studies providing plausible mech­ anistic data as to how the properties of metformin might result in fetal effects have been recently published in monkeys who share similarities in placental structure with humans.
