The desmopressin dose might need to be increased during the third trimester due to placental vasopressinase activity and the patient should be informed that in case of polyuria and increase of desmopressin, serum sodium concentration should be checked to avoid hyponatraemia. Administration of desmopressin appears to be otherwise safe in pregnancy (19, 215). GH replacement is usually stopped after conception or by the end of the first trimester; the benefit–risk balance for GH in pregnancy cannot be assessed (196, 200). Furthermore, large amounts of GH are secreted by the placenta during pregnancy. One large observational study revealed no relationship between GH replacement therapy regimens and pregnancy outcomes (216). R.2.3. We recommend that women and their partners be provided with education for glucocorticoid stress dose adjustment and measures on how to prevent or manage adrenal crisis during pregnancy. Rationale Symptoms such as persistent nausea, hyperemesis, and excessive fatigue are common in pregnancy.
