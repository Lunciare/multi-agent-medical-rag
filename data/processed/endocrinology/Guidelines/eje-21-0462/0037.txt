Even though pituitary hormone deficiency beyond gonadotropins has been correlated with reduced pregnancy rate and outcome, patients with hypopituitarism can be reassured that in vitro fertilisation (IVF) treatment is often successful (206). Pituitary apoplexy occurring after the application of triptorelin, a GnRH analogue used for OS, has been described (207, 208), possibly due to previously undetected gonadotropinomas. It should be highlighted that although fertility outcomes and pregnancy rates for women with hypopituitarism are generally good, studies have shown that women require multiple OS and/or ART cycles to achieve a live birth and the 'take home baby rate' per cycle remains reduced (197, 201, 209). 5.2. General recommendations: Pregnancy R.2.1. We recommend that pregnant women with known pituitary adenoma, in particular those with a large pituitary adenoma (>1 cm), Cushing’s disease or acromegaly, and those with pituitary deficiencies, should be followed by an endocrinologist and an advanced nurse practitioner where relevant.
