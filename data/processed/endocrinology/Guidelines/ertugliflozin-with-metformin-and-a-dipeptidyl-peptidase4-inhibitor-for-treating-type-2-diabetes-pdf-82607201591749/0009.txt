The ERG noted that although VERTIS-SITA 2 did not include any patients from the UK, it was generally well conducted and representative of patients with type 2 diabetes in the NHS. The primary outcome was change in HbA1c (measured as change in least- squared means from baseline to week 26). Both doses of ertugliflozin showed a statistically significant improvement compared with placebo in the full analysis set, which included all randomised patients who took at least 1 dose of study medication and had at least 1 measurement of the outcome variable. There was also a statistically significant improvement with ertugliflozin in patients with an HbA1c less than 7% (less than 53 mmol/mol) at week 26, and for changes in body weight and systolic blood pressure from baseline to week 26. The committee concluded that ertugliflozin added to treatment with metformin and a DPP-4 inhibitor in people with inadequate glycaemic control on the dual therapy is clinically effective compared with placebo.
