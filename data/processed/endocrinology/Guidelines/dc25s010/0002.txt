Therefore, the recommenda- tions for cardiovascular risk factor modiﬁcation for people with type 1 diabetes are extrapolated from data obtained in people with type 2 diabetes and are similar to those for people with type 2 diabetes. Under the current paradigm of comprehensive risk factor modiﬁcation, cardio- vascular morbidity and mortality have notably decreased in people with both type 1 and type 2 diabetes (1). In addition to the evidence from prospective intervention studies to support comprehensive ASCVD risk factor reduction, a large cohort study conﬁrmed no or only marginally increased mortality, MI, and stroke risk compared with the general population when all major cardiovascular risk factors are managed to goal levels in people with type 2 diabetes (6).
