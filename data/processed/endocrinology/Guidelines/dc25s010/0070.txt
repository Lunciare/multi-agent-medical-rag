The primary efﬁcacy end point was vascular death, MI, stroke, or transient ischemic at- tack. The primary safety outcome was ma- jor bleeding (i.e., intracranial hemorrhage, sight-threatening bleeding in the eye, gas- trointestinal bleeding, or other serious bleeding). During a mean follow-up of 7.4 years, there was a signiﬁcant 12% re- duction in the primary efﬁcacy end point (8.5% vs. 9.6%; P = 0.01). In contrast, major bleeding was signiﬁcantly increased from 3.2% to 4.1% in the aspirin group (rate ra- tio 1.29; P = 0.003), with most of the ex- cess being gastrointestinal bleeding and other extracranial bleeding. There were no signiﬁcant differences by sex, weight, or duration of diabetes or other baseline fac- tors, including ASCVD risk score.
