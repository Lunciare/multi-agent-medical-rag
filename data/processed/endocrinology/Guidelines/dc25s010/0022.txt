In CHIPS, aiming for a dia- stolic blood pressure of 85 mmHg during pregnancy was associated with reduced likelihood of developing accelerated mater- nal hypertension and no demonstrable ad- verse outcome for infants compared with aiming for a higher diastolic blood pres- sure. The mean systolic blood pressure achieved in the more intensively treated group was 133.1 ± 0.5 mmHg, and the mean diastolic blood pressure achieved in that group was 85.3 ± 0.3 mmHg. A similar approach is supported by the Interna- tional Society for the Study of Hyper- tension in Pregnancy, which speciﬁcally recommends use of antihypertensive therapy to maintain systolic blood pressure between 110 and 140 mmHg and dia- stolic blood pressure between 80 and 85 mmHg (54).
