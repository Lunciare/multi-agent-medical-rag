Although rare variants in CETP, LIPC, and LIPG underlie high HDL-C, and the phenotype sometimes has a polygenic basis, there are usually limited clinical consequences. Specific Lifestyle Recommendations for Adults with Dyslipidemia General Advice The effective management of dyslipidemia includes consump- tion of a diet focused on whole, unprocessed foods, such as fruits, vegetables, nuts, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins (such as fish and chicken), and low-fat dairy products (Algorithm Fig. 4). Incorporating nontropical plant oils like olive, canola, and sun- flower oils is advised, whereas tropical oils are to be avoided, as they are typically high in saturated fats and may contain trans fats, if hydrogenated. The addition of stanols in the diet (2 g/day) has been shown to reduce LDL-C levels, but there are no available outcome trials demonstrating a reduction in ASCVD events. 37,38 Certain foods rich in saturated or trans fats should be minimized or avoided, including bacon, sausage, salami, deli meats, red meat, and fried or deep-fried foods.
