In a patient with a history of HTG-associated pancreatitis or an individual whose fasting TG levels remain >885 mg/dL (10 mmol/L) on repeat fasting lipid profiling, without an obvious and treatable secondary cause (including nonadherence to dietary re- strictions), treatment to protect against future pancreatitis epi- sodes must be considered. 29,49 The first-line treatment in these cases includes a low-fat diet, plus strong consideration for use of a fibrate. 29,49 Omega-3 fatty acids are also a consideration, although are somewhat less efficacious. Persistent or refractory HTG would warrant referral to a lipidologist and possibly DNA analysis to rule out FCS. Also, access to new investigational therapies for severe individuals with confirmed FCS, clinicians may consider use of FDA-approved apolipoprotein CIII inhibitors. 33 For those with mild to moderate HTG (TG 150-499 mg/dL; 1.7- 5.6 mmol/L), the primary concern is excess ASCVD risk, and the primary target must be LDL-C.
