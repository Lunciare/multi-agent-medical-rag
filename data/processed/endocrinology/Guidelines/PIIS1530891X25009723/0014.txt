Most cases of hypobetalipoproteinemia are asymptomatic and will be discov- ered incidentally, but symptoms in severe cases will revolve around fat absorption and fat-soluble vitamin transport. Athero- sclerotic disease has not been reported as an issue in individuals with hypobetalipoproteinemia as they age. Cases of hypo- betalipoproteinemia could warrant genetic testing of MTTP, APOB (heterozygous truncating variants), PCSK9 (heterozygous loss-of- function variants), ANGPTL3, and SAR1B. Most cases of abetalipo- proteinemia (due to biallelic variants in MTTP) are clinically obvious in infancy (fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption) but may also be considered when acanthocytosis, retinitis pigmentosa, hepatic steatosis, or abnormally low LDL-C levels are noted.
