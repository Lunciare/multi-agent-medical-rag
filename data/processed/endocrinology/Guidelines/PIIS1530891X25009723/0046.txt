The first case series documenting the potential teratogenic ef- fect of statins in humans was published in 2004. 123 This series reported 70 cases of statin exposure with evaluable birth out- comes, almost all within the first trimester of pregnancy. Adverse outcomes were reported in 30 cases (including 22 structural de- fects and 5 fetal deaths). No adverse outcomes were observed in 20 cases involving pravastatin use, a finding attributed to its hydro- philic properties and low tissue penetration. 124 Two small pro- spective randomized trials examining the use of pravastatin for the prevention of pre-eclampsia in the second trimester of preg- nancy 125,126 and a large trial that randomized 543 women to pravastatin in the third trimester 127 did not find an increase in adverse pregnancy outcomes in pravastatin-treated individuals when compared with those taking a placebo. Available systematic reviews and meta-analyses evaluating statin exposure and preg- nancy outcomes are limited by the paucity of data and based mostly on observational studies. Primary studies may have recall and selection bias, rely on drug dispensing or database linkage, and/or have unaddressed confounders.
