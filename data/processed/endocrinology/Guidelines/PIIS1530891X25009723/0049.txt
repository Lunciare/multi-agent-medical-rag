Management of Hypertriglyceridemia in Pregnant and Lactating Individuals In pregnancy, the goal for management of HTG is preventing TG- induced pancreatitis, a rare but feared complication of dyslipidemia associated with adverse maternal and fetal outcomes, including death (Algorithm Fig. 10). 136-138 TG levels increase during pregnancy more than 3-fold, a change that mirrors the gestational rise of es- trogen levels. 139 The risk of TG-induced pancreatitis is highest during the third trimester of pregnancy when estrogen levels peak and the counter-regulatory effect of placental hormones leads to the development of an insulin-resistant state. 139,140 Insulin resistance diminishes lipoprotein lipase activity and promotes excess pro- duction of TG-rich lipoproteins by the liver. The best approach to avoid the occurrence of TG-induced pancreatitis is prevention. A baseline TG level should be obtained at preconception, and if not obtained then, as early as possible during the first trimester of pregnancy. If fasting TG levels are >150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/dL) in the nonpregnant patient or ≥250 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L) during pregnancy, a search for secondary causes of HTG should ensue.
