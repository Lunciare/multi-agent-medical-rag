This may also expand to individuals with TG in the 500 to 884 mg/dL (5.6-9.9 mmol/L) range in whom the presence of atherogenic lipoproteins predisposes them to ASCVD. 29,49,68 CVD risk factors―hypertension, smoking, diabetes, and chronic stress―should be managed concurrently. 49,68 If ASCVD risk is high or intermediate, medications with proven cardiovascular benefit, such as statins, ezetimibe, and eicosa- pentaenoic acid monotherapy (icosapentyl ethyl) are preferred. 29,49 Because LDL-C may be impossible to determine in an individual treated with statins with persistently elevated TG, non—HDL-C and/or ApoB are alternative tests for treatment thresholds and for monitoring the effects of therapy. 29,67 If ASCVD risk is low, lifestyle advice should be recommended with periodic lipid monitoring every 12 to 24 months. 29,67 Statin treatment can be considered as the individual’s situation evolves. Consideration of TG-lowering therapy for individuals with a history of severe HTG-associated pancreatitis must be individualized.
