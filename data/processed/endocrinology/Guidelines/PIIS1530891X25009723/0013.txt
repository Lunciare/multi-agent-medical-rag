Sitosterolemia/xenosterolemia is further associated with a ten- dency for macrothrombocytopenia and valvular heart disease, while cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis is associated with a variety of neurologic signs and symptoms. 25,26 Diagnostic biochemical testing with measurements of plasma plant sterols or bile alcohols, respectively, is available for both suspected genetic disorders. 27 A DNA test sample is usually derived from whole blood and sent to a dedicated genetic testing laboratory to screen for pathogenic variants in LDLR, APOB, PCSK9 (gain-of-function), LDLRAP1, ABCG5/ G8, LIPA, and APOE by gene sequencing. More than 95% of detected causal variants in FH are found in the LDLR and APOB genes and the rest are rare. 18 The information of positively identified cases can be used for cascade testing of family members to facilitate early-in-life cholesterol lowering for the prevention of ASCVD. 20 Many genetic testing laboratories can also provide a result for polygenic hyper- cholesterolemia. Of all cases submitted for FH genetic testing, the most common response is a high polygenic score for elevated LDL-C, which is associated with a lower cardiovascular risk than mono- genic FH but a higher risk than the general population.
