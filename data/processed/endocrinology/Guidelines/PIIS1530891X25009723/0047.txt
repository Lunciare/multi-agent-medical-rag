However, there may be an increased risk of cardiac anomalies in addition to an increased risk of spontaneous abortion. 128-130 The differential ef- fect of statins on pregnancy outcomes based on lipophilicity re- mains to be established. 130 In view of the limited evidence and signals of concern with first trimester use, it is recommended that statins be stopped at least 4 weeks before conception and that they be avoided during pregnancy unless the patient’s ASCVD risk is very high. In the latter case, statins should be held during the first trimester when the risk of teratogenicity is the highest. If LDL apheresis is available, it may be continued or initiated in in- dividuals with severe hypercholesterolemia and/or ASCVD once statins are discontinued. Bile-acid sequestrants are safe during pregnancy but should be avoided in individuals with HTG 121 ; their cholesterol-lowering effect is modest. Ezetimibe blocks the intestinal absorption of sterols and potentiates the LDL-C—lowering effect of statins.
