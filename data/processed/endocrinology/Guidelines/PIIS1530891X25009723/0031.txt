Management of Adults with Hypertriglyceridemia The approach to an individual with HTG involves clinical assessment, ruling out secondary causes, lifestyle intervention, and medical treatment to reduce the risks of CVD and pancreatitis (Algorithm Fig. 7). 29 When nonfasting TG levels are elevated, ie, >150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L), a fasting lipid profile is recommended. There are frequently contributory secondary causes, the most common being T2D. 67 Clinical assessment should primarily focus on detecting or ruling out characteristic features of severe HTG, including those found in individuals with chylomicronemia syn- dromes or dysbetalipoproteinemia. 29,68 Addressing the secondary causes―dietary factors, medications, and/or medical conditions such as T2D or obesity―will often normalize TG and are critical to the treatment of HTG, even when severe. 29,67 If TG levels are between 500 and 884 mg/dL (5.6-9.9 mmol/L), there is increasing concern for metabolic deterioration predis- posing the individual to the development of acute pancreatitis. 69
