Nongenetic causes of very low HDL- C include renal disease, low estrogen states, hyperandrogenemic states, and paradoxical response to fibrates or thiazolidinediones or their combination. 36 The most common cause of low HDL-C from a genetic disorder is a pathogenic variant in ABCA1, where heterozygous carriers have HDL-C which are typically <50th percentile of normal in the absence of HTG. Tangier disease, with biallelic pathogenic variants in ABCA1 (autosomal recessive) lead to HDL-C levels which are <5th percentile. LCAT deficiency and fish-eye disease result from biallelic variants in LCAT and are associated with low HDL-C but also proteinuria and corneal opa- cification, which should alert the clinician to this rare disease. Investigations of genetic causes of such syndromes are highly specialized and are often performed in a research setting, but these are increasingly used in practice. Most cases of extremely raised HDL-C levels (>90th percentile) are secondary to excessive alcohol intake, antiepileptic treatment, niacin, pharmacologic use of estrogens, or exposure to pesticides. Genetic causes of extremely high HDL-C are rare and either caused by CETP deficiency or deficiency in the lipolytic enzymes that remove TG from HDL, ie, hepatic lipase (LIPC) or endothelial lipase (LIPG).
