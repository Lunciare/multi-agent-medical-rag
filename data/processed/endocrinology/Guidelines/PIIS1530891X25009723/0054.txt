Because of its invasive nature, plasmapheresis is usually limited to the man- agement of refractory chylomicronemia in pregnancy when there is imminent risk of pancreatitis and as a bridge to delivery. 136 Plasmapheresis usually requires multiple sessions and may result in complications. If initiated to prevent pancreatitis, use of plas- mapheresis should be limited to HTG in the chylomicronemia range (≥1000 mg/dL or 11.3 mmol/L) and discontinuing sessions when TG fall to <400 mg/dL (4.5 mmol/L). 48,136,149 The use of parenteral nutrition or plasmapheresis in gestational HTG in in- dividuals without pancreatitis should be individualized. In the individual presenting with HTG-induced pancreatitis, it is critical that a fast be implemented. This approach will clear chylomicrons from circulation. If the individual has diabetes, in- sulin infusion at a starting rate of 0.1 unit per kilogram per hour and intravenous fluids with glucose to maintain euglycemia may be added to the fast. Insulin helps activate lipoprotein lipase and enhances TG clearance.
