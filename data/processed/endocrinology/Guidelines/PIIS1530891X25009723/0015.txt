Typical cases with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS; previously called Frederickson type I hyperlipoproteinemia) present in childhood or young adulthood with grossly lipemic plasma and frequently with abdominal pain/pancreatitis. In such cases a strict low-fat diet will reduce TG levels but rarely normalize them. Monogenic severe HTG is autosomal recessive and therefore not always observed as a trait in a family with the exception of consanguineous situations. The raised TG concentration is always dependent on a clearance defect, with lipoprotein lipase (LPL) being the major lipolytic enzyme responsible for removing TG from blood.
