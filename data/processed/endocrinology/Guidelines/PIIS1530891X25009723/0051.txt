If TG levels are <250 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L) in a low-risk patient in the first trimester of pregnancy, it may not be necessary to recheck TG levels until the start of the third trimester. Any time during pregnancy that TG levels rise above 250 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L), a search for and management of secondary causes should ensue, including the onset of gestational diabetes. With TG ≥500 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L), strict dietary fat re- striction becomes a priority with referral to a dietician for guid- ance on how to implement meal plans that limit fats to no more than 20 to 40 grams per day. 136 These meals should provide adequate calories, lipid-soluble vitamins, and essential fatty acids, particularly when TGs are higher (≥885-1000 mg/dL or 10-11.3 mmol/L) and more aggressive fat restriction is necessary (≤20 g/ day). 136,141 Supplementation with medium chain triglycerides can help address caloric needs and do not add to the
