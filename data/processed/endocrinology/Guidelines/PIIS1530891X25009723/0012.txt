However, as genetic testing becomes more accessible and less costly, it is increasingly being incorporated into the evaluation of individuals with FH and their direct family members (cascade testing). Experts recommend that both genetic and phenotypic diagnoses be pursued when possible given that the two approaches may be complementary and may influence treatment decisions. 18,20 Importantly, to avoid unnecessary genetic testing, secondary causes of hypercholester- olemia should be first excluded and the probability of a diagnosis of FH examined with the use of a likelihood scoring system such as system, 22 the Simon Broome Register criteria, 23 or the U.S. (Make Early Diagnosis to Prevent Early Deaths [MEDPED]) Diagnostic Criteria for FH. 24 The presence of tendon xanthomas with much less extreme LDL-C levels should prompt consideration of sitosterolemia or cerebrotendinous xanthomatosis, which are respectively caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in ABCG5 or ABCG8 and CYP27A1.
