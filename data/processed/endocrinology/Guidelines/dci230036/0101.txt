Measurements of ke- tone bodies are performed both in an ofﬁce/hospital setting and by individuals at home. Additionally, some people follow- ing very-low-carbohydrate (ketogenic) diets for weight loss or diabetes control may check blood or urine ketones at home. Use/Rationale Recommendation: Individuals who are prone to ketosis (those with type 1 diabe- tes, history of diabetic ketoacidosis [DKA], or treated with sodium–glucose cotrans- porter 2 [SGLT2] inhibitors) should mea- sure ketones in urine or blood if they have unexplained hyperglycemia or symptoms of ketosis (abdominal pain, nausea) and implement sick day rules and/or seek medical advice if urine or blood ketones are increased. B (moderate) Ketone bodies are normally present in urine and blood but in very low con- centrations (e.g., total serum ketones <0.5 mmol/L).
