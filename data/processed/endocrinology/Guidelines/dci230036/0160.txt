B (low) Although several islet autoantibodies have been detected in individuals with type 1 diabetes, and these have prognostic value in individuals at high riskof type 1 diabetes, routine measurement of islet autoantibod- ies has limited use outside ofclinical studies (410). Currently islet autoantibodies are not used in routine management of diabe- tes.This section will focus on the pragmatic aspects ofclinical laboratory testing for islet autoantibodies. Diagnosis The clinical onset of type 1 diabetes is re- lated to the loss of the functional b-cell mass. In most people with type 1 diabe- tes, the loss of function is associated with an autoimmune attack (411). This is termed type 1A or immune-mediated dia- betes. As further discussed under Analyti- cal Considerations, quantitative assays of speciﬁc autoantibodies have generally re- placed the islet cell antibody (ICA) test, which is indirect immunoﬂuorescence on frozen sections of human pancreas. Islet autoantibodies comprise autoantibodies to (a) islet cell cytoplasm (ICA), (b) native insulin, termed IAA (412), (c) GADA (413–415), (d) islet antigen-2, IA-2A (414), and IA-2bA (also known as phogrin) (416), and (e) three variants of the ZnT8 transporter (ZnT8A) (417,418).
