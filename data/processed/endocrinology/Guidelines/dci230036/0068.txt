These studies have typically been done in people taking insulin, and the interventions often in- cluded substantial patient education. Stud- ies of is-CGM use in people with type 2 diabetes have shown mixed results for both outcomes (167,173,174). In a large trial of rt-CGM in people with type 1 diabetes showing signiﬁcant reduc- tions in HbA1c in adults (159), improved glucose control was not seen in children (ages 8 to 14 years) or adolescents and young adults (ages 15 to 24 years). These younger participants wore the CGM device signiﬁcantly less than adults of 25 years and up, and consistency of CGM use was highly correlated with lower HbA1c in all participants. A subsequent RCT speciﬁcally targeting adolescents and young adults, which included considerable education and support, showed that those random- ized to rt-CGM had signiﬁcantly reduced HbA1c after 6 months compared with those randomized to BGM (175).
