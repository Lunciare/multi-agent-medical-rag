In con- trast, a subsequent modeling study implies that screening commencing at age 30 or age 45 is highly cost-effective (<$11,000 per QALY gained) (38). Cohort studies sup- port cost-effectiveness of screening (39). Long-term outcome studies are neces- sary to provide evidence to resolve the question of the clinical effectiveness of screening for diabetes (40). Screening and prevention of diabetes based on the Diabetes Prevention Program has been shown to be cost-effective and even cost-saving with metformin (41) and has been endorsed by the Centers for Medi- care & Medicaid Services based on inde- pendent cost-effective analyses. In 2003 the ADA lowered the threshold for “normal” FPG from <6.1 mmol/L (<110mg/dL)to<5.6mmol/L(<100mg/dL) (42).This change remains contentious and has not been accepted by all organizations (21,43). The rationale is based on data that individuals with FPG values between 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL) and 6.05 mmol/L (109 mg/dL) are at increased risk for the development of type 2 diabetes (44,45).
