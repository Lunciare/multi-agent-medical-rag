Some of these need to be updated to include measuring waist circumference and interpreting waist-to-height ratio, which might lead to extra training costs. Healthcare professionals may need extra time to teach older children and young people, and their families and carers, how to measure the waist accurately and calculate waist-to-height ratio. However, the committee agreed that extra costs of training and staff time are unlikely to result in a significant resource impact and are justified by the long-term health benefits associated with a reduction in obesity-related conditions. Page 152 of Return to recommendation Discussing the results with children and young people, and their families and carers Recommendations 1.12.2 to 1.12.5 Why the committee made the recommendations The evidence showed that children and young people and their families or carers were not always keen to accept a referral to overweight and obesity management interventions. The committee therefore highlighted the need to explain the health risks associated with a higher BMI using non-judgemental language, to consider the drivers of overweight and obesity and to advocate for the child's health in proportion to the impact their BMI may have.
