The committee agreed this was an important area for research to investigate whether there are variations in thresholds, as there are in adults, and made a recommendation for research on measurements for assessing health risks in children and young people. The committee noted that although they could not provide different thresholds for BMI, waist-to-height ratios could be used as an indicator of central adiposity for children and young people aged 5 years and over regardless of ethnicity and sex. Studies also suggested that the optimal waist-to-height ratio cut-offs for children and young people ranged from 0.42 to 0.57, with most studies averaging around 0.5. Based on boundary value of 0.5 should be the same for children and young people aged 5 years and over as for adults.
