Studies found that 28% of AUS/FLUS lesions and 58% of those suggestive of follicular or Hurthle cell neoplasm are malignant (73, 74), which prompts surgical treatment. If FNB of the thyroid nodule shows indifferent results (Bethesda 3 or 4), repeated FNB is suggested after 6 months (Table 3). If FNB again is indifferent, it is suggested to discuss about the patient in the multidisciplinary board regarding the subsequent appropriate diagnostic approach (e.g. molecular imaging or diagnostic surgery). Diagnostic hemithyroidectomy is the recommended surgical approach for unifocal lateralized benign lesions. Total or near-total thyroidectomy is recommended in case of lesions in both lobes (e.g. symptomatic nodular goiter). Recommendation 7A: We recommend that benign nodules are followed by serial US and should undergo repeat FNB if suspicious features develop (4S). Suggestion 7B: We suggest hemithyroidectomy or total thyroidectomy for benign nodules, performed by an experienced high-volume pediatric thyroid cancer surgeon, in patients with compressive symptoms, cosmetic concerns, or according to patient/parent preference after counseling of the possible benefits and risks of thyroid surgery (4W).
