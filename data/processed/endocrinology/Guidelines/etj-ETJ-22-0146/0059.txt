The presence of PTC however must be confirmed cytologically or histologically (preoperative FNB or intraoperative frozen section) before total thyroidectomy is performed (4W). B9. Role of surgery for benign thyroid lesions For benign thyroid lesions (thyroid cysts or nodules with Bethesda II on FNB), follow-up is recommended with US after 6–12 months. For a stable lesion, subsequent US follow-up is recommended every 12–24 months, for at least 5 years (8). When there is a significant change in palpable or US characteristics, repeated FNB should be considered (69, 70). When benign nodules cause clinical symptoms (e.g. compression symptoms and cosmetic concerns), surgery may be the preferred choice of treatment (8, 71). When a thyroid nodule is found in a child with a background of Graves’ disease, there is a slightly increased risk for malignancy (72). Indications for FNB however do not change. When surgery is indicated in such children, total thyroidectomy is recommended above hemithyroidectomy.
