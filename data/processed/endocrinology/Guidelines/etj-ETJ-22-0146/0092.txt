Pacini et al. reported more frequent extra-thyroidal extension (ETE) and lymph node metastases (LNM) in radiation-induced thyroid tumors in children diagnosed in the Chernobyl region (134). No significant differences were found between CCS with subsequent DTC and patients with sporadic DTC in the occurrence of surgical complications, recurrence rate, or disease-related death (132, 133). It may be questioned whether treatment and follow-up of DTC in patients after exposure to I-131 or with a history of radiation should be different compared to patients with sporadic DTC, since no differences were found in outcome. However, CCS were found to have more frequent bilateral disease. For this reason, the expert panel agreed that subsequent DTC should minimally be treated with total thyroidectomy. In addition, several medical and psychosocial considerations may be taken into account for CCS (Table 7) (136). Each patient with a history of radiation will have unique characteristics. Caregivers should individually design the optimal treatment and follow- up plan and include the patient and their parents in the decision-making.
