These overall negative results were not unexpected, as blood glucose has subsequently been shown to be a relatively weak CVD risk factor in isolation compared with other CVD risk factors, such as hypertension or hypercholesterolemia. Consequently, even if a wide separation in A1C could be safely obtained, it would take a long time for the CVD beneﬁt to accrue. However, a meta- analysis of individual participant data from UKPDS, ACCORD, ADVANCE, and VADT demonstrated a signiﬁcant reduc- tion in myocardial infarctions and major CVD events but no difference in stroke, heart failure, or mortality between in- tensive and less intensive glycemic man- agement (57). Longer-term epidemiological follow-up has been performed in these studies, and a clear pattern of CVD beneﬁt has emerged (58–60).
