All individuals treated with insulin or who are at high risk of hy- poglycemia as discussed above should be prescribed glucagon. For these individuals, clinicians should routinely review their ac- cess to glucagon, as appropriate glucagon prescribing is very low in current practice (132–134). An individual does not need to be a health care professional to safely ad- minister glucagon. Those in close contact with, or having custodial care of, these in- dividuals (family members, roommates, school personnel, childcare professionals, correctional institution staff, or coworkers) should be instructed on the use of gluca- gon, including where the glucagon product is kept and when and how to administer it. It is essential that they be explicitly edu- cated to never administer insulin to individ- uals experiencing hypoglycemia. Glucagon was traditionally dispensed as a powder that requires reconstitution prior to injec- tion. However, intranasal and ready-to- inject glucagon preparations are now widely available and are preferred due to their ease of administration resulting in more rapid correction of hypoglycemia (135–137).
