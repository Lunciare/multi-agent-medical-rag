C 6.21 Provide structured education on the recognition, prevention, and man- agement of hyperglycemic crisis to all individuals with type 1 diabetes, those with type 2 diabetes who have experi- enced these events, and people at high risk for these events. B Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and the hy- perglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) are serious, acute, and life-threatening hyperglycemic emergencies in individu- als with diabetes (151) that incur sub- stantial morbidity, mortality, and costs (152). Approximately 1% of all hospital- izations in people with diabetes are for hyperglycemic crises. The diagnostic cri- teria for DKA and HHS are summarized in Table 6.8; all criteria must be met to establish these diagnoses. Importantly, approximately 10% of people experiencing DKA present with euglycemic DKA (plasma glucose <200 mg/dL [11.1 mmol/L]); therefore, DKA diagnosis requires either the presence of hyperglycemia or prior history of diabetes (151). Euglycemic DKA requires insulin deﬁciency and can be associated with a variety of factors including reduced food intake, pregnancy, alcohol use, liver failure, and/or SGLT2 inhibitor therapy (153).
