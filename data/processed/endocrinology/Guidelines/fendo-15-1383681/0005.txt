Although it can rarely be caused by a phosphate- deﬁcient diet, nutritional rickets is commonly the result of vitamin D deﬁciency due to insufﬁcient UV exposure and/or insufﬁcient dietary calcium intake, or a combination of both. This is particularly worrisome as nutritional rickets can profoundly affect the well- being of infants, children, and adolescents, with consequences that can persist into adulthood (1, 2). In regions where calcium consumption is typically scant, with limited or absent dairy intake, dietary calcium deﬁciency stands as the primary culprit behind nutritional rickets among children beyond infancy (1, 3). Dietary phosphate deﬁciency is a rare cause of nutritional rickets, but it is infrequent, because phosphate is abundant in natural foods.
