Tumour growth is assessed by sequential CT scan- ning, most commonly at 6- to 12-month intervals and applying RECIST 1.1 criteria to determine disease status. Recently, Tuttle and co-workers proposed that changes in lesion volume, e.g. in lung metastases, may be a more reliable prognostic marker for tumour growth than RECIST and may possibly facilitate the decision when to start MKI therapy. In addition to morphological imaging, important prognostic indicators may be de- rived from FDG-PET/CT. In a pilot study, TC patients with FDG-PET-positive tumour lesions had a signifi- cantly shorter survival than patients with metastatic TC, but no increased glucose uptake . Furthermore, by comparison with RAI avidity, FDG positivity had a more profound negative impact on survival, as confirmed in an independent second study . Tumour burden describes the total tumour mass in the body. Its assessment should be based on imaging in- cluding high-resolution neck ultrasonography, or CT scanning of neck, chest, abdomen, and brain, in addition to assessment for bone metastases by scintigraphy and CT.
