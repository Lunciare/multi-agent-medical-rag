The combination of low FT4 and inap- propriately low TSH should be confirmed on two sepa- rate determinations and after the exclusion of several conditions that could lead to misdiagnosis and are listed in Table 3. In particular, the isolated finding of low FT3 is indicative of nonthyroidal illnesses or deiodinase de- fects rather than CeH. In the absence of any technical problem or interfer- ence, the finding of low FT4 combined with an inappro- priately low or normal TSH accurately delineates the diagnosis of overt forms of CeH, but the diagnosis of milder defects, characterized by FT4 concentrations still within the normal range (mild or hidden CeH), remains problematic.
