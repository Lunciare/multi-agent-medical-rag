In a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies, preconception care for pregnant individuals with preexist- ing diabetes was associated with lower A1C and reduced risks of birth defects, preterm delivery, perinatal mortality, small- for-gestational-age births, and neonatal in- tensive care unit admissions (17). To minimize the occurrence of compli- cations, beginning at the onset of puberty or at diagnosis, all adults and adolescents with diabetes of childbearing potential should receive education about 1) the risks of malformations associated with unplanned pregnancies, even with mild hyperglycemia, and 2) the use of effective contraception at all times when trying to prevent a pregnancy. Preconception counseling using developmentally appro- priate educational tools enables adoles- cents with childbearing potential to make well-informed decisions (4). Preconception counseling resources tailored to adoles- cents are available at no cost through the American Diabetes Association (ADA) (18). Individuals with prediabetes or a history of GDM will need preconception evaluation for as long as they have childbearing poten- tial.
