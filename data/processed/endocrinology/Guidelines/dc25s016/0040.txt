B There is considerable variability in the presentation of DKA and HHS, including euglycemic DKA (deﬁned as plasma glu- cose levels <200 mg/dL [<11.1 mmol/L] in the presence of ketosis and metabolic acidosis), mild to moderate hyperglyce- mia and acidosis, or severe hyperglyce- mia, dehydration, and coma; therefore, individualization of treatment based on a careful clinical and laboratory assessment is needed (70,73,133,134). Management goals include restoration of circulatory volume and tissue perfusion, resolution of ketoacidosis, and correction of electrolyte imbalance and acidosis. It is also essential to treat any correctable un- derlying cause of DKA, such as sepsis, myocardial infarction, or stroke. In criti- cally ill and mentally obtunded individuals with DKA or HHS, continuous intravenous insulin is the standard of care. Successful transition from intravenous to subcutane- ous insulin requires administration of basal insulin 2–4 h before the intravenous insulin is stopped to prevent recurrence of ketoacidosis and rebound hyperglyce- mia while the subcutaneous insulin action rises (71,133,135).
