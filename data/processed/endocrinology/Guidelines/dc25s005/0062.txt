The number of days of fasting is an important factor to consider. In Ramadan fasting, a per- son fasts from dawn to dusk for a lunar month (29–30 days). It is important for the health care professional to compre- hensively assess these risk factors well in advance of fasting date, as some of them are modiﬁable. Some of these factors are related to the nature of the fasting practice, others are related to di- abetes, and others might be due to indi- vidual factors. The International Diabetes Federation along with Diabetes and Ram- adan International Alliance adopted a risk calculator for the various risk factors different countries have been published that assess the validity of the fasting risk score and the ease of use of it (232–235). The accumulation of these risk factors provides a risk score as low, moderate, or high (Table 5.4) (230). While the risks of different religious fasting prac- tices may vary, this risk calculator provides some useful guidance for other religious fasting.
