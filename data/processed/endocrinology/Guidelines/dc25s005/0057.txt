Intermittent fasting is an umbrella term that includes three main forms of re- stricted eating: alternate-day fasting (en- ergy restriction of 500–600 calories on alternate days), the 5:2 diet (energy restric- tion of 500–600 calories on consecutive or nonconsecutive days with usual intake the other ﬁve), and time-restricted eating (daily calorie restriction based on window of time of 8–15 h). Each produces mild to moderate weight loss (3–8% loss from baseline) over short durations (8–12 weeks) with no signiﬁcant differences in weight loss when compared with continuous calo- rie restriction (214,215). A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs exam- ined the most common types of fasting in studies lasting 2–52 weeks. The authors concluded that intermittent energy restric- tion produces small but signiﬁcant reduc- tions in waist circumference and fat-free mass but were otherwise not superior to continuous energy restriction diets (216).
