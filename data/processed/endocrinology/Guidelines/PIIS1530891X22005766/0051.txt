Grade A; BEL 1 R 13.5 The ideal insulin regimen should be personalized to an individual’s needs and glycemic targets, attempting to better emulate physiological insulin replacement to maintain near normoglycemia, to prevent the development and progression of DM complications, while minimizing hypoglycemia and providing ﬂexibility for speciﬁc daily life situations/scenarios such as: exercise, sleep, acute illness, psychological stress, etc. Grade A; BEL 1 R 13.6 Insulin regimens usually should involve the use of insulin analogs for most persons with T1D and include the following approaches: a. MDI, which usually involve 1 to 2 subcutaneous injections daily of basal insulin to suppress ketogenesis and gluconeogenesis and to control glycemia between meals and overnight, and subcutaneous injections of prandial insulin or use of inhaled insulin before each meal to control meal-related glycemic excursions. CGM is the preferred method of glucose monitoring for all individuals with T1D. Grade A; BEL 1 b.
