However, DKA can also occur in persons with T2D.28-31 Worldwide epidemiological studies have reported that between 13% and 80% of individuals with T1D present with DKA. The percentage of adults with T2D who present with DKA at diag- nosis is unknown; however, the number of people with ketosis- prone or atypical T2D has increased. Ketosis-prone T2D most frequently occurs among Blacks of African ancestry or persons who identify as Afro-Caribbean or Hispanic. In contrast to the long-term insulin requirement of autoimmune T1D, many persons with ketosis-prone T2D can discontinue insulin after a few months of therapy and maintain acceptable glycemic control for many years on either diet or noninsulin therapies. At presentation, persons with ketosis-prone T2D have signiﬁcant impairment of both insulin secretion and insulin action; however, at the time of near- normoglycemia remission, insulin secretion and action improve to levels similar to hyperglycemic persons with ketosis-resistant T2D.
