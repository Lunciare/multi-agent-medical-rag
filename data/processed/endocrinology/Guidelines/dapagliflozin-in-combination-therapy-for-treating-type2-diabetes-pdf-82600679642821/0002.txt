It has a UK marketing authorisation 'in adults aged 18 years and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus to improve glycaemic control as: • monotherapy when diet and exercise alone do not provide adequate glycaemic control in patients for whom use of metformin is considered inappropriate due to intolerance • add-on combination therapy with other glucose-lowering agents including glycaemic control'. The subject of this appraisal is the add-on therapy indication. 2.2 The summary of product characteristics lists the following adverse reactions for dapagliflozin: hypoglycaemia (when used with a sulfonylurea or insulin), urinary tract and genital infection, back pain, dysuria, polyuria, dyslipidaemia and elevated haematocrit. Dapagliflozin is not recommended for use in people with moderate to severe renal impairment (patients with a creatinine clearance rate of less than 60 ml/min or an estimated glomerular filtration rate of less than 60 ml/ min/1.73 m 2) because its efficacy is dependent on renal function. Dapagliflozin is also not recommended for use in combination with pioglitazone.
