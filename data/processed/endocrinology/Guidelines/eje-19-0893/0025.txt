Certainly, thyroid hormones have an important role in energy metabolism and hypothyroidism could indeed induce weight gain by means of both an increasing fat mass, due to mild decrease in resting energy expenditure and reduced physical activity, and also fluid retention, due to glycosaminoglycans accumulation (20, 27). However, despite weight gain being a frequent complaint in hypothyroidism (28), it is usually of limited extent (27). In line, treatment of overt hypothyroidism produces only a modest weight loss (usually of less than 10%) (29, 30, 31), indicating that severe obesity is usually not secondary to hypothyroidism. Several studies have shown a positive association between TSH and BMI (32, 33) and some studies suggested that small variations of thyroid hormones, even in the normal range, may promote weight gain (34) or impair weight loss induced by diet (35) or bariatric surgery (36). However, some longitudinal studies suggest that changes in thyroid hormones are side effects of increasing body weight (BW) rather than the cause (37).
