G22 Notwithstanding, PTH levels should not be routinely assessed in obese patients, unless in the presence of signs such as hypercalcaemia, osteoporosis and nephrolithiasis. Still, it should be noticed that an exception for routine measurement of 25OHD and PTH should be made for patients with obesity submitted to bariatric procedures. The prevalence of sHPT was observed to increase up to 50% in patients 2 years after bariatric surgical interventions. In addition, in order to prevent or attenuate bone loss it is recommended to ensure that sufficient 25OHD levels are attained (171), bearing in mind that vitamin D supplements in higher dosages are required to overcome decreased absorption (183). However, the ideal vitamin D replacement therapy and target 25OHD concentrations in the obese or bariatric populations are unknown. Moreover, whether vitamin D supplementation actually protects against weight loss induced bone loss and fractures still needs to be confirmed (184). R.6.6. We recommend not to test routinely other hormones, such as leptin and ghrelin, unless there is suspicion of a syndromic obesity. Reasoning: Serum leptin concentration is increased in patients with simple obesity and correlates with BMI and body fat content (185).
