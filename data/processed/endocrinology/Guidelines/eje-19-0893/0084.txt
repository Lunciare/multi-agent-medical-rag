During pregnancy, a number of endocrine changes induce activation in maternal hypothalamic–pituitary– adrenal (HPA) axis. The foeto-placental unit produces large amounts of steroid and peptide hormones including CRH. The increase in CBG (200), result in a two to threefold increase of plasma cortisol levels and a lesser increase in free cortisol (197). The usual tests are not as easy to interpret during pregnancy. HPA axis activity is reduced in obese pregnancy that could contribute to foetal complications, including macrosomia (201). The systematic assessment of cortisol during pregnancy is not recommended, but obesity can further increase the difficulty to interpret usual tests during pregnancy.
