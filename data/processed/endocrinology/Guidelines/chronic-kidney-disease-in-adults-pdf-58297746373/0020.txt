Commissioners may do this by seeking evidence of practice through clinical audits. Adults with CKD are at a higher risk of heart attacks and strokes. To help reduce the risk they are offered a type of medicine called a statin, which lowers the level of cholesterol (sometimes called lipids) in the blood. If their cholesterol level does not decrease enough, they may change to a higher dose. If the statin causes any side effects, their doctor might ask them to stop taking it for a while to check that they are caused by the statin. Their doctor might discuss reducing the dose or changing to a different statin.
