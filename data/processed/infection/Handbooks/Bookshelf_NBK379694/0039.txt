Consistent and correct use of male condoms Consistent and correct use of male reduces sexual transmission of HIV and condoms reduces sexual transmission other STIs in both vaginal and anal sex by of HIV and other STIs by up to 94%. up to 94%. Use of water- or silicone-based lubricants (as opposed to petroleum-based) helps to prevent condoms from breaking and slipping (1, 2, 3, 4). While fewer data are available on female condoms, evidence suggests that use of female condoms also prevents HIV and STIs (4). Effective condom programming is particularly important for key populations. Unprotected sex and other high-risk behaviours such as substance use often coincide for key populations, in particular adolescents from key populations (5, 6, 7).
