For HIV treatment, key elements of a public health approach include: using simplified drug formularies; using fixed-dose combinations on a large scale for first-line treatment for adults, adolescents and children; providing care and drugs free of user charges at the point of service delivery; decentralizing and integrating services, including task shifting; and using simplified approaches to clinical monitoring. xvi Consolidated guidelines on the use of antiretroviral drugs for treating and preventing HIV infection ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS External contributors to the evidence profiles Boston Children’s Hospital (USA) Boston University Center for Global Health and Development team (USA) Cost-Effectiveness of Preventing AIDS Complications, and Massachusetts General Hospital (USA) Clinton Health Access Initiative Enhance Reviews Ltd Global Evaluative Sciences Global Network of People Living with HIV/AIDS (Netherlands) Grupo Portugues de Activistas sobre Tratamentos de VIH/SIDA Guangdong Provincial Centres for Skin Diseases and STI Control (China) HIV Modelling Consortium Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai (USA) IeDEA Southern Africa Collaboration, IeDEA Southern Africa, West Africa and COHERE collaborations Inserm U897, ISPED Bordeaux (France) International Community of Women Living with HIV/AIDS Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health (USA) London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine (United Kingdom) Médecins Sans Frontières (Switzerland) Paediatric AIDS Treatment for Africa (South Africa) Pangaea Global AIDS Queen Mary University of London (United Kingdom) Royal Tropical Institute (Netherlands) University of Birmingham (United Kingdom) University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC Project-China) University of Sheffield (United Kingdom) WHO thanks all principal investigators who shared the preliminary results of trial data and Development Groups.
