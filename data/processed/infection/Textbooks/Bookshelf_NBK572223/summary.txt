Yu and Saurabh Mehta 154 Nutrition and HIV: Epidemiological Evidence to Public Health Suboptimal vitamin D status is considered by some as the most common medical condition glob- ally (Holick, 2010); however, estimates of deficiency prevalence range widely (6 to 100%) among individuals living with HIV . In the early 1980s, studies identified a nuclear receptor in immune cells (including lymphocytes and monocytes) (Bhalla et al., 1983; Provvedini et al., 1983), which binds specifically to the biologically active vitamin D metabolite. Furthermore, other key laboratory findings (Wang et al., 2004; Liu et al., 2006, 2007; Adams and Hewison, 2008) corroborated the dynamic interaction between vitamin D and the human host response against infectious diseases. Separately, other studies have provided mechanisms of how certain ART pharmacokinet- ics could result in vitamin D deficiency (Dao et al., 2011; Yin and Stein, 2011).
