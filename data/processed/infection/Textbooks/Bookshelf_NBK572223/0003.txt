In studies with HL-60 cell lines, the pres- ence of 1,25(OH)D (prior to HIV infection) delayed the induction of viral production (Kitano et 2 3 al., 1993) and increased HIV virus production . Additionally, 1,25-dihydroxyvi- tamin D downregulated the expression of CD4 surface antigens in HL-60 cells (Schlesinger et al., 1989). Separately, another study found that 1,25(OH)D increased replication of monocyte- 2 3 and lymphocyte-tropic HIV-1 strains (as much as 10,000-fold) in monocyte cell lines (BT4A3.5), peripheral blood monocytes, and mononuclear cells . Finally, another study found that VDR activated the HIV-1 long terminal repeat (LTR) in U937, HeLa, and Cos-1 cells . HIV-1 LTR is transcribed during a key step for viral replication. Separately, human leukemia–lymphoma cell line studies have considered the role of cytokines in the link between vitamin D and HIV. One study indicated that cytokines modulated the inter- action between vitamin D and HIV replication . 1α,25-Dihydroxyvitamin D -stimulated cell differentiation only induced HIV viral expression with tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) . In contrast, vitamin D inhibited HIV expression when other cytokines (gamma interferon, interleukin-6, granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor) were present . Pretreatment of vitamin D among U1 human macrophages of peo- ple living with HIV promoted an innate immune response (TNF release, IκB degradation, NF-κB nuclear translocation) to Mycobacterium tuberculosis exposure compared to no vitamin D treat- ment . IFN-α and -β inhibited HIV production, which was greater among U937 cells with vitamin D relative to those without .
