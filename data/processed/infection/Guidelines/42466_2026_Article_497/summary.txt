RESEARCH ARTICLE Clinical presentation of neurosyphilis – a single-center retrospective data analysis Susanne Dyckhoff-Shen1 · Ilias Masouris1 · Stefanie Völk1 · Konstantin Pusl1 · Ulrich Seybold2 · Matthias Klein1 Received: 8 January 2026 / Accepted: 21 April 2026 © The Author(s) 2026 Abstract Background As cases of neurosyphilis are rising worldwide – especially in people living with HIV (PLWH) – current data 2005 to 2024, were retrospectively analyzed. Probable diagnosis was based on corresponding signs and symptoms, positive syphilis serology, Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) abnormalities, and/or improvement after antibiotic therapy. In asymptomatic patients, diagnosis was based on CSF abnormalities with positive serology. A positive CSF/serum treponemal antibody index confirmed the diagnosis. Results 77 patients with neurosyphilis were identified, the majority being male (95%), with a high proportion of HIV co- symptoms were cognitive deficits, headache, and psychiatric symptoms. CSF abnormalities and additional co-infections were more pronounced among PLWH.
