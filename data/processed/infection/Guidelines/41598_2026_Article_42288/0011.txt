Determination of the parasite density minimum detection limit for fluorescent RPA The temporal detection sensitivity of microscopy, nested PCR, and fluorescent RPA was compared in a mouse infection model. Fluorescence RPA detection was simultaneously performed on 14 sets of Balb/c mouse blood samples with varying parasite densities (Supplementary Table 3). The results (Fig. 2A) showed that this method could detect a minimum parasite load of 0.046 B. microti per microliter (µl) of blood. Blood samples from the three groups with even lower parasite densities showed no amplification curve (similar fluorescence intensity to the negative control). Among the positively detected groups, samples with higher parasite densities exhibited higher fluorescence intensities. Nested PCR detection results (Fig. 2B) revealed clear bands in samples with B. microti loads of 445,200, 89,040, 17,808, and 3,561.6 per µl of mouse blood. Samples with loads of 712.32, 142.46, and 28.49 showed faint bands, while no bands were visible in the remaining groups. Therefore, the nested PCR method was able to effectively detect a minimum B. microti load of 28.49 parasites per µl of blood. Evaluation of different methods for detecting B. microti in infected mouse models Starting on 3 dpi (day 3 post-infection, shortened to 3 dpi)), 10 mice were tested every 3 days using thin blood film smears and microscopic examination. B. microti was not observed in blood smears from any of the 10 mice on 3 dpi, 6 dpi, 9 dpi, or 12 dpi. On 15 dpi, B. microti was observed in 2 blood smears, and by 18 dpi, 8 samples were positive. By 21 dpi, all samples were positive (Table 2). Nested PCR was performed concurrently, with no bands observed on 3 dpi and 6 dpi. On 9 dpi, a band was detected in one blood sample, and by 12 dpi, 7 samples tested positive. By 15dpi, B. microti was detectable in all 10 mice (Supplementary Fig. 5). No upward fluorescence curve was observed in the blood samples of mice on 3 dpi and 6 dpi after fluorescent RPA amplification, with their fluorescence intensity being close to that of the negative control (Fig. 3A). By 9 dpi, four positive samples were detected (Fig. 3B). On 12 dpi, all blood samples from the 10 mice exhibited fluorescence intensity exceeding 200 RFU after isothermal amplification (Fig. 3C).
