The eight included studies compared the effect on mortality of using an inappropriate versus appropriate empiric antibiotic therapy (IEAT versus appropriate empiric A antibiotic therapy, or AEAT) based on subsequent urine culture in vitro susceptibility testing. IEAT was frequent, occurring in a mean of 27.5% across studies and ranging from 10.3%102 to M 50.8%.100 All these studies were observational, and all but one100 were retrospective. Clinicians’ initial choice of empiric antibiotic therapy introduced the bias of confounding by indication, which was either partially or not accoDunted for at all in most studies. Confounding by indication in this context means that sicker patients likely received different antibiotics than less sick patients.
