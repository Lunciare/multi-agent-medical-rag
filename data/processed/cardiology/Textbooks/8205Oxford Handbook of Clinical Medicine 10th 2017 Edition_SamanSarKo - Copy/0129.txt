In practice, the exact axis matters little; what you need to be able to recognize is whether the axis is normal (Ω30° to +90°), left-deviated (<Ω30°), or right deviated (>+90°). There are many ways of doing this. If the QRS in lead I (0°) is predomi- nantly positive (the R wave is taller than the S wave is deep), the axis must be be- tween Ω90° and +90°. If lead II (+60°) is mostly positive, the axis must be between Ω30° and +150°. So if both I and II are positive, the axis must be between Ω30° and +90°—the normal range. When II is negative, the axis is likely to be left-deviated (<Ω30°) and when I is negative, the axis is likely to be right-deviated (>+90°). One way of remembering this is: Lovers Leaving—Left axis deviation—the QRS complexes in I and II point away from each other.
