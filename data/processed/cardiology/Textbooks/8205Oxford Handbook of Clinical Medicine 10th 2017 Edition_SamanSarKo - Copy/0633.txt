Following screening tests (see PROMIS study, 2017, for use of multi-parametric MRI), prostate biopsy is required for diagnosis. This has an inherent risk of complications includ- ing bleeding, infection, and urinary retention. ~1% will require hospital admission. The risks of PSA testing and subsequent biopsy need to be counterbalanced by benefi ts from screening.15 ~1 in 800 men avoid death from prostate cancer as a re- sult of PSA screening. But screening picks up many cancers that will never become fatal. This ‘overdiagnosis’ is thought to occur in ~40% of positive screens with signifi cant risks from treatment including urinary incontinence, erectile dysfunc- tion, and IHD. This balance of risk versus benefi t means that population screening for prostate cancer using PSA is not recommended.
