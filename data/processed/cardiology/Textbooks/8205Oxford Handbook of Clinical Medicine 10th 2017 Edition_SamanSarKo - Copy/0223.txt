Investigations are aimed at determining the underlying cause: • Blood tests: FBC, U&E, CRP, ABG. See table 4.7. • Radiology: CXR. • Microbiology: sputum and blood cultures (if febrile). • Spirometry (COPD, neuromuscular disease, Guillain–Barré syndrome). Management Depends on the cause: Type I respiratory failure: • Treat underlying cause. • Give oxygen (24–60%) by facemask. • Assisted ventilation if PaO2 <8kPa despite 60% O2. Type II respiratory failure: The respiratory centre may be relatively insensitive to CO2 and respiration could be driven by hypoxia. • Treat underlying cause. • Controlled oxygen therapy: start at 24% O2. Oxygen therapy should be given with care. Nevertheless, don’t leave the hypoxia untreated. • Recheck ABG after 20min. If PaCO2 is steady or lower, increase O2 con centration to 28%. If PaCO2 has risen >1.5kPa and the patient is still hypoxic, consider assisted ventilation (eg NIPPV, p813, ie non-invasive positive pressure ventilation). • If this fails, consider intubation and ventilation, if appropriate.
