0022//0055//22001177 1199::0077 sesaesid suoitcefnI Immunization 407 Passive immunity uses pre-formed antibody to protect against infection. It of- fers immediate but short-lived protection. Natural passive immunity occurs in the placental transfer of maternal antibodies to the fetus; acquired passive immunity includes treatment with immunoglobulin eg hepatitis B, rabies, tetanus, varicella- zoster. Active immunity follows exposure to an antigen, which generates an adaptive im- mune response. Natural active immunity occurs following infection. Acquired active immunity is provided by vaccination. Routine vaccinations in the UK are shown in table 9.13. Additional vaccines are off ered to specifi c vulnerable groups (table 9.14). Immunosuppression is a contraindication to live vaccines due to the risk of dis- seminated disease. Includes immunodefi ciency, immunosuppressive treatment, HIV. Inactivated vaccines can be given but the antibody response may be less: aim to give >2wks prior to immunosuppressive therapy when possible (or vaccinate whilst on treatment and considered repeat re-immunization when/if treatment complete).
