Key Points Acute pulmonary edema can result from acute coronary ischemia, decompensation of underlying heart failure, arrhythmia, an acute valvular disorder, or acute volume overload. Patients have severe dyspnea, diaphoresis, wheezing, and sometimes blood-tinged frothy sputum. Clinical examination and chest radiography are usually sufficient for diagnosis; ECG, cardiac markers, and sometimes echocardiography are done to identify cause. Treat the cause and give oxygen and IV furosemide and/or nitrates as needed; inotropic agents to increase cardiac output, try noninvasive ventilatory assistance initially but use tracheal intubation and assisted ventilation if necessary.
