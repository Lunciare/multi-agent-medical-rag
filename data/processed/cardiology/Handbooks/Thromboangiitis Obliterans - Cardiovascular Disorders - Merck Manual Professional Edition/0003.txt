Key Points Thromboangiitis obliterans is inflammatory thrombosis of small and medium-sized arteries and sometimes superficial veins in distal upper and lower extremities. It occurs almost exclusively in males aged 30 to 45 years who smoke. Claudication may occur, and patients may develop ischemic ulceration and gangrene of one or more digits. Diagnose clinically but exclude other causes of ischemia by testing. Smoking cessation is essential; prostacyclin infusion may help prevent amputation, but evidence supporting use of other medications is limited.
