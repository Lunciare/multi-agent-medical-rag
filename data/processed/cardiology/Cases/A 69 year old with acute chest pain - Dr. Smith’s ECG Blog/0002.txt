When chest pain returned, ECG #2 showed clear progression with increased ST elevation and hyperacute ST-T wave changes in leads III and aVF, persistent abnormal ST elevation in lead II, and definitive reciprocal ST depression in aVL. These findings confirmed acute inferior OMI. Subsequent angiography confirmed the culprit lesion. The case highlights the importance of serial ECGs, careful inferior lead assessment, and maintaining a high index of suspicion for OMI in patients with chest pain and known coronary disease.
