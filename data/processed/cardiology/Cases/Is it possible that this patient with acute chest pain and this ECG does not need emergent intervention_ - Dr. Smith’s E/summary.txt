KEYWORDS: posterior, omi, ecg, troponin, posterior omi, depression, leads, inferior, does, pain, chest, symptoms, initial, case, cath

84-year-old male with significant cardiac history (CABG + multiple stents) awoke at 05:00 with chest pressure, diaphoresis, and light-headedness. He arrived to a rural ED ~06:30. Because he was recovering from an influenza-like illness (ILI) and screened positive at triage, his symptoms were initially attributed to viral illness, but an ECG was obtained at 06:49. Initial ECG (06:49) — Why This Does Need Emergent Intervention Despite baseline artifact, findings were consistent with infero-posterior OMI: Sinus bradycardia Convex ST elevation in lead III Reciprocal ST depression in aVL Possible anterior ST depression (V2–V3) → posterior involvement Interpretation: Inferior + posterior occlusive event → inferoposterior OMI until proven otherwise. Key concept: STEMI criteria are insensitive for OMI; failure to meet STEMI thresholds does not mean “no cath”. Early Management Pitfalls Troponin error Initial hs-cTn negative (11 ng/L).
