Do not anchor on ILI Viral illness does not exclude MI and can precipitate plaque rupture. Do not wait for troponin when ECG is diagnostic Early troponin can be negative even in true occlusion. Posterior leads can be falsely negative and can be placed wrong Posterior OMI is best diagnosed using V1–V3 mirror findings. “NSTEMI” label can delay life-saving cath Many NSTEMI patients have an occluded artery; delays lead to preventable myocardial loss. Bottom Line (One Sentence) In a high-risk chest pain patient, convex STE in III with reciprocal STD in aVL plus maximal STD in V2–V3 is infero-posterior OMI until proven otherwise — and requires emergent cath regardless of initial troponin or symptom attribution to ILI.
