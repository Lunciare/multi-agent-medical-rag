Is it possible that this patient with acute chest pain and this ECG does not need emergent intervention_ - Dr. Smith’s ECG Blog
KEYWORDS: posterior, omi, ecg, troponin, posterior omi, depression, leads, inferior, does, pain, chest, symptoms, initial, case, cath


84-year-old male with significant cardiac history (CABG + multiple stents) awoke at 05:00 with chest pressure, diaphoresis, and light-headedness. He arrived to a rural ED ~06:30. Because he was recovering from an influenza-like illness (ILI) and screened positive at triage, his symptoms were initially attributed to viral illness, but an ECG was obtained at 06:49. Initial ECG (06:49) — Why This Does Need Emergent Intervention Despite baseline artifact, findings were consistent with infero-posterior OMI: Sinus bradycardia Convex ST elevation in lead III Reciprocal ST depression in aVL Possible anterior ST depression (V2–V3) → posterior involvement Interpretation: Inferior + posterior occlusive event → inferoposterior OMI until proven otherwise. Key concept: STEMI criteria are insensitive for OMI; failure to meet STEMI thresholds does not mean “no cath”. Early Management Pitfalls Troponin error Initial hs-cTn negative (11 ng/L). This falsely reassured the team. Troponin can be normal in early OMI (especially <2 hours from onset). In the presence of a diagnostic ECG, an initial normal troponin is not actionable for ruling out occlusion. Nitroglycerin Two sprays of SL nitro did not improve pain but caused transient hypotension (SBP <90). Hypotension after nitro in suspected inferior MI raises concern for preload dependence (possible RV involvement). Right-sided leads may be considered clinically, but this does not change the need for cath when OMI is present. Objective Deterioration (Several Hours Later) Repeat troponin returned >600 ng/L. At this point, even without ECG interpretation skills: Persistent chest pain + elevated troponin = high-risk ACS needing urgent invasive strategy. Repeat ECG (11:19) — Reperfusion Pattern But Ongoing Danger Repeat ECG showed evidence of reperfusion: Less ST elevation inferiorly More T-wave inversion in inferior leads Loss of T-wave bulk in V4–V6 But: patient still had pain → reperfusion ECG does not equal “safe” if symptoms persist. Posterior Leads (15-lead ECG at 12:10) — Not Required and Can Mislead A 15-lead ECG was obtained because posterior MI was suspected. Core message from the case: Posterior leads are not necessary to diagnose posterior OMI. Any ST depression in V1–V3 in chest pain is posterior OMI until proven otherwise. Posterior leads can be falsely negative due to reduced amplitudes through back musculature. In this case, V9 was misplaced, further reducing reliability. Best posterior OMI clue: Maximal ST depression in V2–V3 (very high specificity), plus mirror-test morphology.
