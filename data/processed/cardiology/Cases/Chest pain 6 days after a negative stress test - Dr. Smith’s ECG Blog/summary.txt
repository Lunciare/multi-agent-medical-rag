A previously healthy man in his 70s presented to the emergency department with one hour of dull retrosternal chest pain radiating to the left shoulder. He reported no cardiac history and had undergone a stress test six days prior that was reported as normal. He had been given a clean bill of health by his cardiologist. An ECG was obtained at time 0000. No prior ECGs were available. Initial ECG The ECG was triaged as “No STEMI.” The treating physician recognized the ECG as concerning for occlusion myocardial infarction, most notably a hyperacute T wave in lead V2, with lesser changes in leads V3 and V4.
