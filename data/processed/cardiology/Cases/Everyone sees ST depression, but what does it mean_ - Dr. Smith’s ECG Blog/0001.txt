Everyone sees ST depression, but what does it mean_ - Dr. Smith’s ECG Blog
KEYWORDS: ecg, initial, vasospasm, acute, omi, lad, troponin, pain, depression, occlusion, infarction, improvement, coronary, resolved, myocardial


A man in his 70s with a history of hypertension and type 2 diabetes mellitus presented with acute chest pain that awakened him from sleep around midnight. The pain was substernal, non-radiating, and rated 7/10 in intensity. An ECG was obtained at approximately 4:00 AM. Initial ECG (ECG #1) The initial ECG was clearly abnormal, though it did not meet classic STEMI criteria. Key findings: ST depression in inferolateral leads (II, III, aVF, V4–V6) ST elevation and hyperacute T waves in V1, V2 ST elevation and hyperacute T wave in aVL Reciprocal ST depression in III Loss of normal gentle ST elevation in V3 Pattern consistent with a combination of: Precordial swirl South African Flag sign This constellation of findings is diagnostic of acute LAD occlusion, involving: Septal perforators (precordial swirl) A major diagonal branch (South African Flag pattern) This ECG represents acute occlusion myocardial infarction (OMI) and warrants immediate cardiac catheterization, regardless of troponin level or symptom improvement. The Queen of Hearts AI diagnosed OMI with very high confidence (0.99). Initial Management and Misinterpretation The ECG was interpreted clinically as showing only “mild ST depression”. The patient received: Aspirin 325 mg Sublingual nitroglycerin 0.4 mg Documentation stated that the patient’s pain “resolved,” but no short-interval repeat ECG was obtained to confirm electrical resolution. Initial troponin I was mildly elevated (0.049 ng/mL, reference <0.033). Clinical Deterioration At 8:10 AM, repeat troponin I was 3.788 ng/mL, indicating a substantial infarction. A repeat ECG (ECG #2) showed improvement compared to ECG #1, but remained clearly abnormal, raising concern for ongoing ischemia. Despite this: The ECG was again described as showing only ST depression Pain was documented as “2/10” and later “resolved” The distinction between partial improvement and complete resolution was not acted upon Echocardiography Transthoracic echocardiogram demonstrated: Apical akinesis LVEF approximately 45% The pattern could superficially resemble takotsubo, but ECG and clinical context were diagnostic of LAD OMI. Cardiac Catheterization Cardiac catheterization was delayed approximately 8 hours after the initial diagnostic ECG. Findings: Severe LAD vasospasm involving septal perforators and a major diagonal branch Very poor flow in the LAD No acute plaque rupture This was classified as a Type 2 MI (ischemia due to vasospasm rather than atherosclerotic plaque disruption). Intracoronary nitroglycerin was administered around 12:30 PM, resulting in resolution of vasospasm. Troponin continued to rise, reaching 6.607 ng/mL. Final Diagnosis Acute LAD occlusion myocardial infarction due to coronary vasospasm Mechanism:
