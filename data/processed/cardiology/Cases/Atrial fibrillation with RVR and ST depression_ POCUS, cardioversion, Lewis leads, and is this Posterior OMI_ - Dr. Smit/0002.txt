He did not have ACS. Learning Points Not all myocardial infarction is due to ACS; this was a Type II MI. Subendocardial supply–demand ischemia in atrial fibrillation with RVR may be maximal in V1–V4. Atrial fibrillation with RVR may be a consequence, not the cause, of acute illness. Volume status is critical in the management of atrial fibrillation with RVR. If the source of supraventricular tachycardia is unclear, Lewis leads should be used. Additional ECG Interpretation Notes The initial ECG shows diffuse ST depression with subtle ST elevation in aVR, consistent with diffuse subendocardial ischemia. This pattern suggests severe underlying coronary disease or a non-occlusive systemic cause such as sustained tachycardia, hypotension, shock, sepsis, or anemia. Mechanisms by which atrial fibrillation may precipitate subendocardial ischemia include loss of atrial kick and shortening of diastolic filling time, particularly in hypovolemia. Wide “funny-looking beats” in atrial fibrillation may represent either PVCs or aberrantly conducted supraventricular beats (Ashman phenomenon). In this case, treatment was not required, and the abnormal beats resolved with correction of hypovolemia and sepsis.
