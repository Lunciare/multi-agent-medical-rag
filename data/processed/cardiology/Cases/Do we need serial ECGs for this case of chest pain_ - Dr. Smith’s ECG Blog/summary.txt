Do we need serial ECGs for this case of chest pain_ - Dr. Smith’s ECG Blog
KEYWORDS: omi, ecg, inferior, acute, ecgs, hyperacute, serial ecgs, serial, minutes, findings, subtle, posterior, waves, cath, cath lab

A 40-something patient called emergency services for approximately 30 minutes of left-sided chest pain radiating to the right arm and shoulder. Radiation to the right shoulder (and especially to both shoulders) is a high-risk symptom for acute coronary occlusion. Initial Prehospital ECG (22:38) The automated ECG interpretation reported “nonspecific ST-T abnormalities.” Expert interpretation identified acute occlusion myocardial infarction (OMI). Key findings: Hyperacute T waves in inferior leads III and aVF Reciprocal inverted hyperacute T wave in aVL Subtle ST-segment flattening in V2 Pattern consistent with inferior OMI with associated posterior involvement These findings are subtle but diagnostic in the correct clinical context. AI Interpretation The PMCardio Queen of Hearts model identified inferior OMI and recommended an immediate invasive strategy, though it did not explicitly identify posterior involvement.
