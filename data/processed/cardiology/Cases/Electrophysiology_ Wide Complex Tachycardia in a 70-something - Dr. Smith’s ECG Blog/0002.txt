Final Diagnosis The patient was determined to have ventricular tachycardia due to arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) phenocopy, occurring decades after surgical repair of Tetralogy of Fallot. ARVC phenocopy represents an acquired arrhythmogenic substrate caused by chronic right ventricular dilation, fibrosis, and surgical scarring, mimicking genetic ARVC in the absence of familial mutations. Learning Points In older patients with known structural heart disease, regular WCT should be assumed to be VT until proven otherwise Hemodynamic instability mandates immediate cardioversion, without delay for rhythm differentiation A pre-cardioversion ECG is invaluable for retrospective diagnosis Epsilon waves are rare but real, and not exclusive to genetic ARVC Adults with repaired congenital heart disease are at increasing risk for ventricular arrhythmias ARVC phenocopy should be considered in patients with prior right-sided cardiac surgery and VT of right ventricular origin
