A 70-something y.o. male presents to triage with shortness of breath. He reports significant chest pain at the base of his scapula on the right side along with new shortness of breath.

ECG 1 at time zero EARLY REPOLARIZATION ABNORMAL ECG ED final official overread: “early repol vs hyperacute T, minimal changes from previous (previous shown below)” Smith: there is some minimal ST elevation in V2–V6, but does not meet STEMI criteria. Is it normal STE? The computer thinks so, and the physician thinks that is quite possible. However, there is terminal QRS distortion in lead V3. We showed that this NEVER happens in normal ST elevation. There are also subtly hyperacute T-waves in I, aVL, and V2–V6. There is also a very negative (reciprocally inverted) T-wave in lead III, and a down-up T-wave in aVF, both of which are highly suggestive of occlusion of the LAD or D1. Previous ECG many months ago (patient had vertigo) Notice that the T-waves are far smaller, there is none of the inferior findings, and there is a well-formed S-wave in V3 (absence of terminal QRS distortion). Queen of Hearts on first ECG: OMI with High Confidence Queen of Hearts interpretation of this prior ECG: Not OMI with High Confidence These findings were very suspicious for the triage MD and so she took the patient to the critical care area. The patient’s pain resolved spontaneously after 2 sublingual nitroglycerine and another ECG was recorded. ECG 2 at 16 minutes ST ELEVATION CONSISTENT WITH INJURY, PERICARDITIS, OR EARLY REPOLARIZATION Smith: The T-waves are now MUCH smaller. The S-wave is reconstituted. The inferior findings are much less pronounced. The T-wave and STE in V2 are still very suspicious and much different from baseline, but the overall interpretation is that it is consistent with reperfusion. Thus, the LAD has reperfused (“recanalized”) spontaneously. Queen on ECG 2: Not OMI, Low confidence She is also worried about V2. ECG 3 at 21 minutes SINUS RHYTHM WITH FIRST DEGREE AV BLOCK Smith: Look at V2. All findings are now resolved. However, the ST segments are still relatively straight. ECG 4 at 55 minutes SINUS RHYTHM WITH FIRST DEGREE AV BLOCK Overread: previous in the same day, resolution of hyperacute T wave Smith: Upward concavity is restored. Comparison of V1–V3 across ECGs shows terminal QRS distortion in V3 at time zero, restoration of the S-wave at 16 minutes, decrease in T-wave size, and restoration of upward concavity at 21 and 55 minutes.
