Case Summary
KEYWORDS: reperfusion, ecg, waves, consistent, occlusion, chest, showed, pain, changes, initial, reciprocal, chest pain, findings, posterior, heparin


An 84-year-old male (HTN, HLD; never smoker) awoke at 02:30 with new, severe (8/10), pressure-like chest pain radiating across the chest and toward the epigastrium, with shortness of breath. Pain was unrelenting until arrival to the ED (~05:00). Initial vitals were hypertensive (~160/80). Symptoms improved on arrival and fully resolved after nitroglycerin + aspirin 325 mg + clopidogrel 600 mg + heparin infusion. Workup for “can’t miss” diagnoses: CXR: no acute findings CT PE: negative CT abdomen/pelvis: no acute findings Incidental findings: penetrating aortic atherosclerotic ulcer at renal level, bilateral aneurysmal common iliac arteries. Initial hs-troponin T at 06:09: 21 ng/L (within male reference threshold used in the case). Key Question of the Case What does an ECG recorded 4 days earlier in cardiology clinic tell us about the ED ECGs today? Core concept: ACS is dynamic, and symptom improvement before the first ED ECG may reflect spontaneous reperfusion, producing deceptively modest ECG findings (including pseudonormalization). ECG 1 (ED, 05:18) — Chest Pain Improving Main features: High voltage with intraventricular conduction delay (IVCD) ST-T changes: Subtle ischemic ST depression in V3–V6, described as concordant with deep/wide S waves (especially V3; also V4–V6) ST depression in I and aVL looked non-ischemic, consistent with LVH/IVCD repolarization abnormalities Not classic for acute OMI; more consistent with subendocardial ischemia (SEI) in this snapshot AI: Queen of Hearts: negative for OMI Smith notes a newer internal model output consistent with SEI and highlights pseudonormalization of T waves in III and aVF compared with the clinic ECG. Clinical pearl: Even if not diagnostic for occlusion on this frame, sleep-awakening chest pain carries very high pretest probability, and a single ECG can look “too normal” depending on timing. ECG 2 (Clinic, 4 days prior) — “Baseline” but Highly Informative Although recorded when the patient was reportedly without symptoms, the fact that it was obtained and a nuclear stress test was ordered suggests possible antecedent concern. Interpretive significance in the context of today’s presentation: Described as extremely suspicious (bordering on diagnostic) for reperfused inferoposterior occlusion MI Key pattern described: Terminal T-wave inversion in lead III Reciprocal “down-up” T waves in I and aVL Overly upright T waves in V1–V2 consistent with posterior reperfusion (reciprocal changes) Concept: Likely unstable lesion with recurrent occlusion and spontaneous reperfusion (“stuttering” course).
