Medicine ® Narrative Review Autophagy in hepatic macrophages can be regulator and potential therapeutic target of liver diseases A review Jun Ge, MDa,b, Hao Li, PhDa, Jia-Qi Yang, MDb, Yuan Yue, MDb, Sheng-Yu Lu, MDb, Hong-Yun Nie, MDb, Tao Zhang, PhDa, Pei-Ming Sun, PhDa, Hong-Feng Yan, MDa, Hong-Wei Sun, PhDa, Jian-Wu Yang, MDa, Jin-Lian Zhou, MDc, Yan Cui, PhDa,b,* Abstract Hepatic macrophages are a complex population of cells that play an important role in the normal functioning of the liver and in liver diseases. Autophagy, as a maintainer of cellular homeostasis, is closely connected to many liver diseases. And its roles are not always beneficial, but manifesting as a double-edged sword. The polarization of macrophages and the activation of inflammasomes are mediated by intracellular and extracellular signals, respectively, and are important ways for macrophages to take part in a variety of liver diseases. More attention should be paid to autophagy of hepatic macrophages in liver diseases.
