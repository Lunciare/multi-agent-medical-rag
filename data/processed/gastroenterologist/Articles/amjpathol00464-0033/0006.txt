When the entire reverse surface is covered by neoepithelialization, a zone of cell extrusion forms in the center of this surface. Cells in this region showthree types ofchangewhich appearto precede exfoliation- shrinkage necrosis, apoptosis, and intercellular ballooning. The first and second of these changes has been identified in normal exfoliation sites,'2 but intercellular ballooning may be peculiar to the abnormal growth system of these tissue cultures. The development of a recog- nizable zone of exfoliation in these specimens suggests that the normal process is not the result of changes in the supporting lamina propria but, rather, is initiated by contact between cells approaching this point from opposite margins. It also suggests the possibility that cell movement in the mucosal gland and in the neoepithelial layer might be accomplished in a similar fashion.
