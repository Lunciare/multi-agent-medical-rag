Plasticity is facilitated by both the constant establishment of new “memories” and inherent limitations to the maintenance of previously established epigenetic signatures. For example, epigenetic marks are not as faithfully reproduced during cell division as DNA sequences,15 and key determinants of epigenetic states such as nuclear architecture, histone levels and abundance of histone variants and modifications undergo major changes during natural aging.64 Epigenetic inheritance through the germ line faces special obstacles including the erasure and reestablishment of parental imprints and other epigenetic marks in primordial germ cells and after fertilization (see Supplementary Note 9).65 Thus, epigenetic memory is metastable rather than indefinite as it is maintained by dynamic equilibria set up by antagonistic mechanisms that establish/reestablish, reinforce and remove epigenetic signatures (Figure 4). We have previously discussed the key elements of epigenetic regulation. While a detailed review of the biochemical processes that contribute to dynamic establishment, maintenance and removal of epigenetic marks is beyond the scope of this paper, a high-level description of the specific mechanisms is provided in Supplementary Note 12. Environmental control of epigenetic states Homeostasis requires the constant adjustment of cellular behavior such as gene transcription in response to a dynamic environment. Adaptive responses become predictive if “remembered” and facilitate efficient responses to recurring stimuli. However, if adaptability does not keep up with the rate of change, conflict may arise between the actual and perceived environment.
