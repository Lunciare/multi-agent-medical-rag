Chromatin-remodeling complexes Access to DNA for initiation of template-based processes requires rearrangement of nucleosomes by chromatin-remodeling complexes (Figure 1).34 These complexes utilize energy from ATP hydrolysis to disrupt histone-DNA contacts and slide nucleosomes along DNA, alter their spacing, evict entire nucleosomes or replace some of their core histones.35 Chromatin-remodeling complexes contain ATPases homologous to ATP-binding helicases of the DEAD/H box-containing family.35 These ATPases, which are encoded by 27 genes in humans, associate with several other proteins to form complexes that can be divided into four classes: the SWI/SNF (named after yeast mutants that cannot switch mating type or have a sucrose nonfermenting phenotype), ISWI (imitation switch), CHD (chromodomain helicase, DNA-binding) and INO80/SWR1 (Snf2-related CREBBP activator protein) families. Mammalian homologues of these complexes are known by several names;36 here we follow the nomenclature used in ref.35 The subunits that associate with the core ATPases include monomeric β-actin and other actin-related proteins.35 The noncatalytic subunits may provide proximity for coupled reactions or opportunity for diversification of function through combinatorial assembly. The currently favored “loop recapture” model of nucleosome remodeling proposes that the ATPase binds to the nucleosome at a weak DNA-histone contact and utilizes its 3′→5′ translocase activity to “pump” DNA into the nucleosome, thereby disrupting histone-DNA interactions and generating a loop of DNA. Propagation of this loop as a wave displaces DNA relative to the histones and enables nucleosome sliding, thus promoting the formation of open chromatin and transcriptional activation.34–36 Some complexes are also capable of ejection or exchange of octamers,34 and SWR1 (human: SRCAP) may facilitate the selective removal of H2A-H2B and its exchange with H2A.Z-H2B.
