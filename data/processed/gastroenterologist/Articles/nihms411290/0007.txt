Thus, for example, H3R17me2a stands for Histone 3 dimethylated (me2) on aRginine 17 in an asymmetrical fashion, i.e., with both methyl groups occurring on the terminal amino group, leaving the terminal imino group unmodified. The IUPAC-IUBMB one-letter system is used for identifying the amino acid residues. With the aid of modification-specific antibodies and mass spectrometry, the following classes have been identified: acetylation of lysines (Kac), mono-, di- and trimethylation of lysines (Kme1, Kme2, Kme3, respectively), mono- and symmetrical or asymmetrical dimethylation of arginines (Rme1, Rme2s, Rme2a), phosphorylation of serines and threonines (Sph, Tph), monoubiquitylation of lysines (Kub1), SUMOylation (attachment of small ubiquitine-like modifier proteins) of lysines (Ksu), mono- and poly- ADP ribosylation of glutamic acid (Ear1, Earn),17,25 biotinylation of lysines (Kbio),26 proline isomerization (Pcis/Ptrans) and deimination of arginines and methyl-arginines to citrulline.17 Recently, post-translational modification of histone serines and threonines by β- N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) has been added to this list.27 Most of these marks are dynamic and can be established and also removed by defined enzymes17 in response to environmental signals and intracellular pathways activated by them (Figure 4). The most dramatic way to remove modifications is to clip the N terminus of histone 3 by cathepsin L. To most modifications specific biological consequences have also been assigned,17 which depend not only on the type of modification that can be borne by a particular residue (e.g., the mutually exclusive acetylation or methylation of H3K9, which is activating and repressive, respectively), but also on the modified residue carrying the same modification (e.g., trimethylation of H3K4 and H3K27, which is activating and repressive, respectively), and the location of the modification in specific genomic regions such as the association of H3K9me3 with silenced promoters and actively transcribed coding regions. Modifications on particular histone tails can be highly abundant, making interactions and crosstalk between them likely.17 Together, these findings have led to the formulation of the “histone code” hypothesis stating that specific combinations of these marks can predict functional states of the chromatin.28,29 Histone modifications exert their functions either by establishing global changes such as the formation of hetero- or euchromatin or by governing the DNA template-based processes of transcription, DNA repair and replication.
