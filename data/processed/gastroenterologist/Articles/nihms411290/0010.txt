Chromatin-remodeling complexes have diverse and sometimes surprising functions that include transcriptional repression, maintenance of pluripotency and tumor suppression (Supplementary Note 8). Histone chaperones Histone chaperones regulate nucleosome assembly and disassembly required for DNA- templated tasks in an ATP-independent fashion by preferentially binding either histone H3- H4 or H2A-H2B complexes and preventing their nonspecific interactions with DNA.37 They represent structurally diverse groups of proteins (see ref.37) and interact with numerous other chromatin-regulating factors. Important examples include SUPT6H (suppressor of Ty 6 homolog (S. cerevisiae); Spt6), which binds H3-H4 preferentially and stimulates transcription elongation37 together with the H2A-H2B chaperone FACT (facilitates chromatin transcription) complex. FACT also contributes to DNA replication and repair.38 CAF-1 (chromatin assembly factor 1) assembles (H3.1-H4) tetramers from dimers and deposits them onto replicating DNA,39 whereas HIRA (histone regulator A) performs the same function in non-replicating DNA using H3.3-H4 dimers.37 Both CAF-1 and HIRA act synergistically with ASF1 (anti-silencing function 1), which mobilizes parental (H3-H4) tetramers, splits them into dimers and, together with de novo synthesized histones, transfers them to CAF-1 and HIRA.37 Nucleosome assembly is then completed by the addition of H2A-H2B dimers by FACT, nucleoplasmin and NAP-1 (nucleosome assembly protein 1). During disassembly, these steps are performed in reverse. NAP-1 also assists nucleosome sliding by removing H2A-H2B dimers and functions as a chaperone for linker histones. DNA methylation and the role of CpG islands in the regulation of transcription In vertebrates, plants and some invertebrates, postsynthetic modifications can occur not only on histone and nonhistone proteins but also on the DNA. The most common modified DNA base is 5-methylcytosine (5meC; Figure 1),40 which can be further modified by successive oxidation steps to yield 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmeC), formylcytosine (fC) and carboxylcytosine (caC).41 Modified C’s are interpreted as C by enzyme complexes performing DNA-templated tasks but can influence the biological function of the genomic sequences in which they are contained.40 The role of 5meC is to encode stable, heritable transcriptional silencing in cis,10 whereas 5hmeC appears to represent an intermediate toward loss of the 5meC mark and may have specific signaling functions as well.41,42 Most DNA methylation occurs in short palindromic CG sequences (CpG sites), although in embryonic stem (ES) cells, almost 25% of 5meC is associated with CHG or CHH sequences (where H is A, C or T), which disappear during differentiation.43 In human somatic cells, 5meC represents
