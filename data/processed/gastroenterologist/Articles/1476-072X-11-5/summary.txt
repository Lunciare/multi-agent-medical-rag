Grodzinskyetal.InternationalJournalofHealthGeographics2012,11:5 INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL OF HEALTH GEOGRAPHICS RESEARCH Open Access Could gastrointestinal disorders differ in two close but divergent social environments? Ewa Grodzinsky1, Claes Hallert2, Tomas Faresjö3, Elisabet Bergfors1 and Åshild Olsen Faresjö3* Abstract Background: Many public health problems in modern society affect the gastrointestinal area. Knowledge of the disease occurrence in populations is better understood if viewed in a psychosocial context including indicators of the social environment where people spend their lives. The general aim of this study was to estimate the occurrence in the population and between sexes of common gastrointestinal conditions in two neighborhood cities representing two different social environments defined as a “white-collar” and a “blue-collar” city.
