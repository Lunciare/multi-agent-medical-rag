Previous studies have suggested that fecal microbiota trans- plantation (FMT) can potentially treat anxiety- or depression- related diseases due to abundant microbiota diversity. FMT is an old therapeutic approach and an emerging tool used in animal models of gut microbiota research. Li et al used an antibiotic cocktail to deplete the gut microbiota in mice and then colonized the gut via FMT which exposed the microbiota from mice to CUMS. Surprisingly, similar anxiety or depression behaviors were manifested, possibly due to an increase in pro- inflammatory cytokines altered by the gut microbiota. However, certain conditions have been shown to benefit from FMT ther- apies, including recurrent and refractory Clostridium difficile infections, IBS, ulcerative colitis, and autism. Given the importance of the MGB axis in the pathophysiology of depres- sion, the potential use of FMT as a therapeutic strategy for treat- ing neuropsychiatric diseases is being investigated.
