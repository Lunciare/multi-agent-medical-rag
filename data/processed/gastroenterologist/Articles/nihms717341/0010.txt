This was based on the concern that bacterial cholangitis in patients with PSC increased a patient’s risk of mortality, despite limited data to support this contention, until recently. In a 10-year study from two large liver transplant centers, there were 171 patients waitlisted for a liver transplant with PSC, of whom 39% had a history of bacterial cholangitis prior to listing, and 28% had at least one episode of bacterial cholangitis while on the waitlist. To be eligible for liver transplantation, patients must meet specific inclusion and exclusion criteria (Table 2), including the presence of peri-hilar CCA ≤3cm without metastatic disease, and then must undergo neo-adjuvant chemo-radiation and staging to ensure absence of extra-hepatic disease.31 Patients who meet all of these criteria and undergo the protocol at a transplant may then be eligible for exception points upon successful chemo-radiation and tumor staging.31 Unlike CCA, there are no formalized protocols for awarding exception points for patients with PSC who experience recurrent bacterial cholangitis. These exception applications have been reviewed on a case-by-case basis, and, excluding UNOS Region 9 that had a regional policy for awarding exception points, were at the discretion of members of each UNOS region’s regional review board.
