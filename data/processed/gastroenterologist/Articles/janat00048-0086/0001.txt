CUTTS (Accepted 26 April 1988) INTRODUCTION Enteroendocrine cells that show immunoreactivity for cholecystokinin, gastrin, secretin, glucagon, bovine pancreatic polypeptide, motilin, neurotensin, gastric inhibitory peptide, somatostatin and the amine, 5-hydroxytryptamine, have been described previously in the intestinal epithelium of the adult opossum (Krause, Yamada&Cutts, 1985). Someofthesepeptidesmaybeimportantinthedifferentiation and development of the gastrointestinal tract and its associated glands and in regulating digestive functions (Brants & Morisset, 1976; Johnson, 1981, 1987; Mainz, Black & Webster, 1973; Petersen, Solomon & Grossman, 1978; Solomon, Vanier & Morisset, 1983; Werlin & Stefaniak, 1983; Werlin et al. 1987). Studies on the development ofpeptides associated with intestinal enteroendocrine cells generally are limited to a few ofthe fifteen or more known peptides and those studied have been examined primarily in common laboratory animals (Alumets, Hakanson & Sundler, 1983; Huang, Eng & Yalow, 1986; Larsson, 1977; Larsson et al. 1977; Noyer et al. 1980; Sundler etal. 1977; Sundler, Hakanson & Larsson, 1977). In man, such studies have been carried out at mid-gestation or soon after delivery (Alumets, Sundler & Hakanson, 1977; Dubois & Paulin, 1976; Dubois, Paulin & Chayvialle, 1976; Helmstaedter, Muhlmann, Feurle & Forssmann, 1977; Larsson & Jorgenson, 1978; Lehy & Cristina, 1979; Track et al. 1979). The gestation period ofthe North American opossum lasts only 121 days. At birth and until just prior to weaning, the intestinal epithelium of the small intestine is modified for absorption (Krause, Cutts & Leeson, 1977) and the principal intestinal epithelial cells show elaborate apical endocytic complexes, numerous inclusions and largesupranuclearvacuoles. GobletcellsandPanethcellsarenotpresentin significant numbers until weaning. Intestinal epithelial cells of the colon also are modified for absorption during the first two postnatal weeks and show cytological features similar to those ofthe small intestine. Goblet cells are numerous in the colon by the end of the first week ofpostnatal life. In this study, we present the quantitative and distributional changes that occur in amine- and peptide-immunoreactive cells in the intestinal epithelium ofthe small and large intestines of the postnatal opossum. 84 W.
