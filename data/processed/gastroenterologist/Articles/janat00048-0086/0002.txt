MATERIALS AND METHODS Fifteen opossums (Didelphis virginiana) were used, divided according to their snout-rump lengths into the following groups, each containing three animals: 1-5 cm (newborn, lessthan 24hoursold), 2-5 cm(7daysold), 12-5 cm(approximately 74days old, prior to weaning), 20-5 cm (approximately 93 days old, weaning occurring), and 30-0 cm (weaned, free-roving juveniles). Age approximations are based on earlier studies (Cutts, Krause & Leeson, 1978). Opossums 12-5 cm and older were killed by ether inhalation, younger animals were killed by cervical dislocation. The entire gastrointestinal tract was removed as quickly as possible and the small and large intestines subdivided. Segments of small intestine were taken immediately proximal and distal to the pancreatic duct and at four equidistant sites along the remainder of thesmallintestine. Samplesofthecaecum, proximal, middleanddistalcolonwerealso collected. The tissues were fixed in Bouin's solution for 24 hours, dehydrated in ethanol, cleared in xylene and embedded in paraffin.
