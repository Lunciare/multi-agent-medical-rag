Cells immunoreactive for 5-HT, somatostatin and neurotensin are also present in the caecum. 5-HT-immunoreactive cells are present in the intestinal epithelium at 7 days whereas immunoreactivities to somatostatin and neurotensin are not seen until about the 74th postnatal day. Most of the enteroendocrine cells near the luminal surfaces of the small intestine and colon are spindle-shaped with elongated, apical cytoplasmic processes that make contact with the lumen; many ofthe enteroendocrine cells in the intestinal glands also show these features. However, a number of immunoreactive cells in the intestinal glands are round or oval and confined to the basal region ofthe intestinal epithelium. These cells appear to lack an apical cytoplasmic process butoften show aprocess that runsalongthebasementmembrane thatextendsbeneathadjacentcellsintheintestinal epithelium. These features are commonly seen in somatostatin-immunoreactive cells although occasionally othertypesofimmunoreactive cellsshowasimilarmorphology. Some of the spindle-shaped enteroendocrine cells also show basal processes that extend a considerable distance along the base ofthe intestinal epithelium. There is no apparent correlation between the morphology of enteroendocrine cells (open and closedtypes)examinedandaspecificimmunoreactivity, as specificimmunoreactivities occur in both morphological forms.
