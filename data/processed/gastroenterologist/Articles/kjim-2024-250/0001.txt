Transplant recipients more frequently experienced UGI CMV disease in the stomach than non-transplant patients, typically presenting as erosions or mucosal hyperemia. However, non-transplant patients more com- monly experienced UGI CMV disease in the esophagus than transplant recipients, typically presenting as ulcers. The trans- plant group had a significantly higher clinical response than the non-transplant group. Conclusions: UGI CMV disease in transplant patients can be present in the stomach in various forms, including ulcers or erosions. In transplant patients suspected of UGI CMV disease, conducting an esophagogastroduodenoscopy with tissue bi- opsy in any area where even the slightest mucosal abnormality is observed is essential to facilitate a prompt diagnosis.
