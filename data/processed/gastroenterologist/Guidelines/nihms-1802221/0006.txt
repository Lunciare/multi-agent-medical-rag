Inflammatory cytokines are elevated in chronic HCV; eradication of HCV with antiviral agents results in a decrease of these markers.(62,63) Both alcohol-associated liver diseases and NAFLDs are also characterized by elevated systemic inflammatory markers.(64) Further disruption of mediators of the “liver–muscle axis” may result from cirrhosis-related reduction in circulating levels of testosterone and changes in growth hormone secretion and sensitivity.(65) Low testosterone levels have been observed in male patients with cirrhosis and sarcopenia compared with patients who are nonsarcopenic.(66) Testosterone replacement resulted in improvements in total lean body mass,(67) further supporting the role of low testosterone in the development and progression of sarcopenia.
