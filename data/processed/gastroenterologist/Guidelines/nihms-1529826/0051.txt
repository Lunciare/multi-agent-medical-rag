Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Commentary to recommendations 62–64: There is ample evidence indicating that ensuring a quantitatively adequate nutrient intake should be the primary goal (62, 146, 191, 386, 388, 389, 392, 426, 427). If nutritional requirements cannot be met by oral nutrition alone or in combination with ONS then EN is required. EN and has been shown to improve survival and liver function (191, 392, 427). A recent randomized multicenter trial showed no effect on survival or liver function one year after EN using a standard formula for on average not provide data on ONS treatment adherence. Total energy intake during EN was assessed only in a subgroup and exceeded recommended intake by 28% (3292 +/− 781 kcal/d) and raises questions of detrimental effects of overfeeding. In their meta-analysis Ney and co- workers (376) find a mortality reduction in the subgroup analysis of three of the four ONS studies included but not for the whole group of six trials analyzed. Their analysis, however is weakened by the inclusion of one trial aiming for only three days of EN (429) and for the exclusion of two relevant controlled trials (191, 392) for no good reasons. In ten patients with acute HE I-II nasogastric tube feeding using a BCAA-enriched enteral formula was successful with regard to recovery from HE without any complication due to variceal bleeding (217). There is no evidence in the current literature (185, 191, 392, 429– 431) that esophageal varices pose an unacceptable risk to the use of fine bore nasogastric tubes for EN. Regarding the placement of a percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) feeding tube the current European guidelines (432) state that serious coagulation disorders (INR > 1.5, PTT > 50 s, platelets < 50,000/mm3) and severe ascites are contraindications. According to those guidelines no increased morbidity has been observed when a PEG was inserted in the presence of mild to moderate ascites.
