Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Author Manuscript Commentary: The accurate quantitative measurement of nutritional status is difficult in chronic liver disease patients with fluid overload (140, 141) and/or impaired hepatic protein synthesis (e.g. albumin) (63, 64). Sophisticated methods are required such as total body potassium count (16, 142) or in vivo neutron activation analysis (14, 15) or isotope dilution (140). For the nutritional assessment of ASH patients in the VA trials a composite scoring system was used (111–113). This scoring system has been modified repeatedly; one of the later publications of this series also reported the prognostic significance of the absolute CD8+ count and hand grip strength (143). The authors observed a close association between low food intake and high mortality (111). In LC, nutritional status can be assessed using bedside methods, such as the Subjective SGA) combining SGA and anthropometry (145). The RFH-SGA is a strong predictor of morbidity and mortality, but it is time consuming and requires a trained dietician (29, 145, 146). Anthropometry of midarm circumference and triceps skinfold thickness are non- invasive bed-side methods (115, 116) but suffer from high inter-observer variability. Handgrip strength is lower in protein depleted LC patients (14, 147) and is a good predictor of the rate of complications within the next year (28, 148, 149) but is an insensitive measure of fatigue (150). Handgrip strength is better preserved in LC of viral as opposed to alcoholic or cholestatic etiology (14). Handgrip strength appears a valuable tool to measure efficacy of nutritional intervention (151). In LC patients, reactance and resistance readouts from BIA can be used to calculate phase angle or body cell mass as a measure of cell mass and cell function for the nutritional assessment (23, 142, 152, 153).
