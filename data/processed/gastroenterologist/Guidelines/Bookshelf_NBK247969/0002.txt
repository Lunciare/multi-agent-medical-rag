Typically 6 involving distal ileum or colon, the disease can occur anywhere in the gastrointestinal tract. Since the 7 1960s there has been a dramatic change in the prevalence and geographical distribution of the 8 condition. Crohn’s disease was originally recognised in urban areas of Northern Europe and North 9 America, although there are now few parts of the world where it is not found.72 Conservative 10 estimates during the 1990s suggested that the prevalence in the United Kingdom was about 11 75/100,000214 and that this figure may have underestimated the true prevalence by 33%.176 By the 12 end of the century, the prevalence of Crohn’s disease in the north of England was 145/100,000228 and 13 the most recent study from Tayside (Scotland) now indicates a prevalence of 157/100,000263, 14 meaning there are at least 115,000 people in the UK with Crohn’s disease at the present time. 1.2 Aetiology 16 The causes of Crohn’s disease are widely debated, and none have consistently met the criteria 17 necessary to be recognised as the sole or major cause of the condition. Smoking and genetic 18 predisposition are two important factors that are likely to play some role.39 This limited 19 understanding has meant that treatment is largely directed at symptom relief rather than cure, and 20 there is need to distinguish between active treatment of acute disease (inducing remission) and the 21 prevention of relapse (maintaining remission).
