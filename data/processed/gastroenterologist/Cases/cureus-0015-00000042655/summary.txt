Diagnosis requires a combination of clinical and biochemical findings. We present a case of a 20- year-old woman who presented to the Emergency Room with progressive motor decline. She exhibited characteristic neurological symptoms and signs, such as hypomimia, bradyphrenia, bradykinesia, dysarthria, sialorrhea, upper limb dystonia, and wing-beating tremor. Ophthalmological examination revealed corneal deposits known as Kayser-Fleischer rings.
