JOURNAL of MEDICINE and LIFE JML | CASE REPORT Primary segmental omental torsion, mimicking acute appendicitis Paschalis Gavriilidis1* , Salomone Di Saverio2 , Mauro Podda3 , Nicola de’Angelis4 * Corresponding author DOI Paschalis Gavriilidis E-mail: Accepted: 1 January 2024 ABSTRACT Primary segmental omental torsion (PSOT) is a very rare cause of acute abdominal pain, and it may often imitate the omental torsion should be included in the differential diagnosis. Any misdiagnosis may lead to major complications such as intraabdominal abscesses and adhesions. A 63-year-old overweight man with a body mass index (BMI) of 41 Kg/m2 presented to the emergency department on a remote island with acute abdominal pain. His medical history included type 2 diabetes mellitus managed with insulin, essential hypertension, osteoarthritis, and no previous abdominal opera- tions. He reported a sharp pain originating in the epigastrium and the right hypochondrium that started five days prior. Physical examination revealed rebound tenderness and guarding across the abdomen with a positive McBurney sign.
