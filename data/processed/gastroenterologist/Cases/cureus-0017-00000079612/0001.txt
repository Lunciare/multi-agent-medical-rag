Categories: Gastroenterology, Internal Medicine, Hematology Keywords: acute liver failure (alf), coomb's negative, haemolytic anaemia, leipzig score, serum ceruloplasmin, wilson`s disease Introduction Wilson's disease is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by impaired hepatic copper transport. There is copper accumulation in tissues leading to acute liver dysfunction and central nervous system manifestations . The prevalence of Wilson’s disease is 30 per million population which has progressively increased over the recent years . Ceruloplasmin, a copper-binding protein synthesized in the liver, is typically decreased in Wilson's disease and serves as one of the diagnostic markers. We present a case of a 27-year-old male, who was a worker in a chemical factory and had a history of exposure to unknown chemicals. His initial presentation to the hospital was suggestive of acute haemolytic anaemia and pancytopenia. The workup was not suggestive of haematological cause for haemolytic anaemia , and the evaluation for acute liver failure showed normal suggestive of possible Wilson’s disease.
