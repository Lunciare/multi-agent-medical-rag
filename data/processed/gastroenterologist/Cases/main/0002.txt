S. Madokoro et al. T r a u m a C a s e R e p o r t s 4 5(2023)100826 Case report A 25-year-old male patient had a seemingly self-inflicted accident and was transported to our emergency room. He had no relevant family or medical history and was in shock upon arrival. His vital signs were as follows: Glasgow Coma Scale E2V2M4 (total GCS 8), systolic blood pressure of ≤60 mm Hg, and a pulse of 130 beats/min. Blood transfusion, tracheal intubation, and Resuscitative Endovascular Balloon Occlusion of the Aorta (REBOA) were urgently performed. After vital signs stabilized, contrast CT showed grade IV liver injury with active bleeding from the right hepatic artery (Fig. 1). Subsequently, TAE was performed on the right hepatic artery following gauze packing in the emergency room (Fig. 2). The pringle maneuver was not performed in the emergency room because CT revealed the bleeding site, and TAE could be performed immediately. Therefore, he was admitted to the intensive care unit. After admission, interventional radiology (IVR) was repeated because rebleeding was suspected, and TAE was attempted on the right hepatic artery (Fig. 3). The abdomen was temporarily managed with an open abdominal wall to avoid high intra-abdominal pressure and for emergency surgery such as gallbladder ischemia.
