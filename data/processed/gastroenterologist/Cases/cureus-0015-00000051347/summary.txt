Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is a common therapeutic modality used in immune-mediated neuropathy. While the therapeutic benefits are well known, adverse reactions have been reported. One such adverse event, though rare, is transaminitis, which appears to be a transient and a self-limiting adverse reaction. Though most of the cases implicate the stabilizing agent to be the culprit, the exact mechanism is unknown.
