Abstract Wilson's disease is a rare inherited condition that results in an excessive copper buildup in various organs, especially the liver, brain, and other vital organs, leading to cirrhosis, liver failure, neurological problems like involuntary movements, clumsy gait, speech difficulties, and psychological issues, in addition to other symptoms. It is an ATP7B gene mutation-driven autosomal recessive condition. Although the condition is present from birth, symptoms often start to show up between the ages of five and 35, when the body has accumulated enough copper. Wilson's disease may become fatal if an excessive amount of copper is accumulated in the body.
