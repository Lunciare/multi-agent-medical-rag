Deletion breakpoints are usually found in low-copy repeats (LCRs) that flank the 3q29 recurrent deletion region. Nonallelic homologous recombination (NAHR) occurs when LCRs flanking the region misalign during meiosis followed by unequal crossing over between the LCRs. This process can produce gametes with the recurrent deletion or the reciprocal recurrent duplication. Breakpoints of the 3q29 recurrent deletion lie within the flanking LCRs, suggesting that the deletion arises through NAHR. 14 GeneReviews ® Genes of interest in this region. It is not currently known which of the genes in the region is responsible for the phenotype. However, there are several genes of interest: DLG1 and PAK2 are of interest for the neurodevelopmental and psychiatric phenotypes. It is hypothesized that 3q29 deletion phenotypes result from the loss of multiple genes (i.e., loss of both DLG1 and PAK2). A mouse model of the 3q29 deletion has deficits in learning, memory, and social behaviors [Rutkowski et al 2021].
