The individual reported by Brownstein et al had the following additional abnormalities: • Brain MRI: cerebral atrophy and thin corpus callosum • Multiple tortuous venous collaterals and lack of flow in the inferior sagittal sinus • Systemic vasculature: dilated hepatic and celiac arteries, dilated and tortuous intrahepatic arteries and veins Genotype-Phenotype Correlations Current information about genotype-phenotype correlations in Cantú syndrome is limited; no significant clinically relevant genotype-phenotype correlations for ABCC9 or KCNJ8 have been identified. Penetrance Penetrance for Cantú syndrome reported thus far appears to be complete although with variable expression [Grange et al 2019]. In a few families, somatic mosaicism for an ABCC9 variant has been identified in one of the parents, resulting in much milder phenotypic manifestations in that individual [Grange et al 2019; DK Grange, unpublished data]. Nomenclature In the 2023 revision of the Nosology of Genetic Skeletal Disorders [Unger et al 2023], ABCC9-related Cantú syndrome is referred to as osteochondrodysplasia with hypertrichosis (Cantu syndrome), ABCC9-related. Two previously reported conditions, acromegaloid facial appearance (AFA) syndrome and hypertrichosis with acromegaloid facial features (HAFF) syndrome, are now recognized to be reports of individuals with Cantú syndrome with variable and sometimes milder phenotypic features. Prevalence Cantú syndrome has been reported worldwide and in all ethnic groups. The incidence of Cantú syndrome is unknown. More than 100 individuals have been identified with pathogenic variants in ABCC9 or KCNJ8 [Grange et al 2019].
